Licensing Steps

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors regulates general contractor licensing in the state.

Applicants pursuing an Alabama general contractor license must follow the licensing procedures established by the Board and complete the applicable examination requirements.

  1. Determine Your Contractor Classification: Identify the type of general contracting work associated with your intended business activities.
  2. Review Alabama Licensing Requirements: Understand the qualifications, documentation, and requirements associated with your classification.
  3. Prepare Business Information: Gather the business and applicant information required by the licensing authority.
  4. Complete the Licensing Application: Submit the required application materials through the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors.
  5. Prepare for the Business and Law Examination: Study the NASCLA Alabama General Contractors Guide, 3rd Edition.
  6. Prepare for the Trade Examination: Review the technical knowledge applicable to the requested contractor classification.
  7. Complete Required Examinations: Meet the examination requirements established by the Board.
  8. Complete Licensing Requirements: Satisfy the remaining conditions for license issuance.

Book Contents and Study Topics

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first section introduces the important decisions involved in establishing and preparing to operate a contracting business.

Business Planning

Business planning helps contractors establish objectives, identify potential customers, evaluate business needs, and develop an organized approach to starting a construction company. A thoughtful business plan provides direction for daily operations and long-term decisions.

Choosing a Business Structure

Business structure affects how a company is organized and managed. The guide introduces important considerations involved in selecting a business entity, including administrative responsibilities and organizational planning.

Contractor Licensing

Licensing establishes requirements for contractors performing regulated construction activities. Understanding the licensing process helps business owners recognize their professional responsibilities and prepare for the administrative aspects of operating a construction company.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction businesses face risks involving property, employees, project performance, and contractual obligations. The guide addresses insurance fundamentals and the importance of identifying and managing business risk.

Business Management Skills

Managing a construction company requires organizational skills, decision-making, recordkeeping, and communication. Learning these fundamentals can help contractors develop more consistent business practices.

Marketing and Customer Development

Marketing supports business visibility and customer relationships. The guide introduces basic concepts involved in promoting construction services and developing a professional business presence.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section focuses on the practical management responsibilities that arise when operating an active construction business.

Construction Estimating

Estimating is a fundamental construction business skill. Contractors must evaluate labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other expenses to develop project estimates. Understanding estimating principles helps improve financial planning and project preparation.

Bidding

Bidding involves presenting a proposed price and scope of work to a customer or project owner. The guide addresses concepts that support organized bidding and better understanding of project requirements.

Contract Management

Construction contracts establish important responsibilities between project participants. Contractors benefit from understanding agreement terms, documentation, performance obligations, and the role of written communication throughout a project.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling helps coordinate construction activities, labor, material deliveries, and subcontractor work. Effective scheduling supports organized project execution and helps managers monitor progress.

Project Management

Project management brings together planning, budgeting, scheduling, communication, and coordination. The guide introduces management principles that help contractors understand the business side of completing construction projects.

Construction Safety

Safety is an essential part of construction operations. Contractors must understand the importance of workplace hazard awareness, safety responsibilities, and organized jobsite practices.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction activities may involve environmental considerations associated with materials, waste, and site conditions. The guide introduces business responsibilities connected to these issues.

Employee Management

Managing employees requires communication, organization, and familiarity with employment responsibilities. Contractors benefit from learning how employee relationships affect project performance and daily business operations.

Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform important portions of many construction projects. Understanding subcontractor coordination, agreements, scheduling, and communication helps general contractors manage projects more effectively.

Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on clear communication, accurate expectations, and organized documentation. Strong customer service practices support effective project coordination and business reputation.

Part 3: Business Administration and Financial Management

The third section focuses on managing the administrative and financial responsibilities associated with a construction business.

Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors understand business expenses, cash flow, project costs, and the financial position of their companies. These concepts are important for informed business decisions.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides information about business transactions and financial performance. Reviewing basic accounting principles helps contractors understand the importance of accurate records and organized reporting.

Cash Flow

Cash flow management involves monitoring money received and expenses paid. Construction businesses benefit from understanding how project timing, payments, payroll, and operating expenses affect available funds.

Business Taxes

Tax responsibilities are part of operating a contracting business. The guide introduces tax fundamentals and their relationship to financial administration and recordkeeping.

Construction Lien Laws

Lien laws affect certain payment and property-related rights in construction. Understanding their basic purpose helps contractors recognize the importance of proper documentation and payment administration.

Administrative Recordkeeping

Organized records support business operations, financial management, and contract administration. Maintaining clear documentation helps contractors track transactions, project activities, and business responsibilities.

Managing Business Responsibilities

Operating a contracting company involves balancing technical construction work with administrative responsibilities. The guide brings these subjects together to help readers understand the broader management requirements of general contracting.
