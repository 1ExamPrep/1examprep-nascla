Test Information and Study Materials

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first section introduces the essential decisions involved in planning and establishing a construction business.

Business Planning

Business planning helps contractors define objectives, evaluate resources, and organize their approach to providing construction services. Understanding these fundamentals supports informed business decisions.

Choosing a Business Structure

Business structure affects company organization and administration. The guide introduces considerations involved in selecting and managing a business entity.

Contractor Licensing

Licensing establishes professional requirements for regulated construction activities. Contractors benefit from understanding licensing responsibilities and the importance of operating within applicable requirements.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction companies encounter financial and operational risks. Review insurance fundamentals and the role of risk management in protecting business interests.

Business Management

Effective management involves planning, communication, recordkeeping, and decision-making. These skills help contractors organize daily operations and long-term activities.

Marketing

Marketing supports customer development and business visibility. Study the fundamentals of communicating construction services and maintaining professional customer relationships.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section focuses on the practical responsibilities involved in managing active construction projects.

Construction Estimating

Estimating involves reviewing labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other project expenses. Understanding estimating principles supports organized project budgets and proposals.

Construction Bidding

Bidding requires contractors to evaluate project requirements and prepare proposals. Review how estimates, scope, and documentation influence construction bids.

Contract Management

Construction contracts define responsibilities between project participants. Study agreement terms, documentation, communication, and contract administration.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates labor, subcontractors, equipment, and material deliveries. Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors plan and monitor project activities.

Project Management

Project management brings together planning, budgeting, scheduling, and communication. The guide introduces important methods for organizing construction operations.

Construction Safety

Safety is an essential part of construction management. Review workplace responsibilities, hazard awareness, and the importance of organized safety practices.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction work can involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions. The guide introduces administrative responsibilities associated with these issues.

Employee Management

Employee management requires organization, communication, and an understanding of workplace responsibilities. These principles support effective construction operations.

Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform specialized work on many construction projects. Contractors benefit from understanding coordination, agreements, scheduling, and communication.

Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on clear expectations, accurate documentation, and consistent communication throughout a project.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section addresses the financial and administrative responsibilities associated with managing a contracting company.

Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors understand business expenses, project costs, and operating resources. These concepts support informed decisions about company operations.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides organized records of business transactions. Study the importance of accurate documentation and financial reporting.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of money received and expenses paid. Understanding cash flow helps contractors recognize how project payments and operating costs affect available resources.

Tax Fundamentals

Taxes are part of business administration. The guide introduces concepts relevant to understanding financial responsibilities within construction operations.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights. Review lien fundamentals and the importance of contract and payment documentation.

Business Recordkeeping

Organized records support contract administration, financial management, and daily business responsibilities.
