Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Alabama Residential Construction, 4th Edition, is organized into three principal sections addressing business planning and startup, construction business operations, and administrative management.

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

This section introduces the fundamentals of establishing and organizing a residential construction company.

Business Planning

Business planning involves identifying company objectives, organizing resources, and evaluating operational needs. Residential contractors benefit from understanding how effective planning supports company development and construction activities.

Business Structures

Business structure affects company organization and administrative responsibilities. Study the basic considerations involved in choosing an appropriate structure for a contracting business.

Contractor Licensing

Licensing establishes requirements for regulated residential construction work. Understanding contractor licensing principles helps business owners recognize their obligations and operate within applicable requirements.

Insurance and Risk Management

Residential construction businesses face financial and operational risks. Review insurance fundamentals and the importance of identifying potential exposures related to construction projects.

Business Management

Managing a construction business involves planning, communication, decision-making, and recordkeeping. Strong organizational practices support consistent operations and project coordination.

Marketing and Customer Development

Marketing helps contractors communicate their services to prospective homeowners. Review the importance of professional customer relationships and organized business development.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section focuses on the management responsibilities involved in planning and completing residential construction projects.

Construction Estimating

Estimating requires evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other expenses. Understanding estimating principles supports accurate project planning and organized construction proposals.

Bidding

Bidding involves reviewing project requirements and preparing proposed costs. Contractors benefit from understanding the relationship between estimates, project scope, and supporting documentation.

Contract Management

Construction contracts define important responsibilities between contractors, homeowners, and other project participants. Review agreement terms, contract administration, and the importance of written documentation.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates construction activities, workers, subcontractors, equipment, and materials. Effective scheduling helps contractors organize work and monitor project progress.

Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, communication, and coordination. These principles support the effective organization of residential construction operations.

Construction Safety

Safety is an essential component of construction management. Study workplace responsibilities, hazard awareness, and the importance of organized jobsite practices.

Environmental Responsibilities

Residential construction projects may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions. Understanding these issues supports responsible business management.

Employee Management

Employee management requires organization, communication, and an understanding of workplace responsibilities. These skills contribute to consistent construction business operations.

Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform specialized work on many residential projects. Effective subcontractor coordination involves clear agreements, scheduling, and communication.

Customer Relations

Residential contractors frequently work directly with homeowners. Clear expectations, accurate documentation, and professional communication are important throughout the project.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section addresses the financial and administrative responsibilities involved in operating a residential contracting business.

Financial Management

Financial management involves monitoring revenue, expenses, project costs, and available business resources. Understanding these principles supports informed operating decisions.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides an organized record of financial transactions. Accurate information helps contractors monitor payments, expenses, and overall business performance.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of money received and expenses paid. Understanding cash flow helps contractors recognize how customer payments, supplier costs, payroll, and other obligations affect operations.

Tax Fundamentals

Taxes are an important part of construction business administration. The NASCLA guide introduces general tax concepts relevant to operating a contracting company.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens relate to certain payment and property rights. Studying lien fundamentals helps contractors recognize the importance of contracts and payment records.

Business Recordkeeping

Organized records support financial management, project documentation, and daily operations. Good recordkeeping practices help contractors maintain important business information.
