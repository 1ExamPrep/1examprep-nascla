Exam Details

This package supports preparation for the Alabama Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors regulates commercial and industrial general contractors in the state.

Alabama requires prime contractor applicants to complete the Business and Law examination administered through the state’s designated testing provider. The examination addresses the legal, financial, administrative, and business responsibilities connected with operating a contracting company in Alabama.

The Alabama NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management is the state-focused reference included for this portion of the preparation program. Candidates should study contractor licensing, business organization, financial management, contracts, estimating, insurance, employment responsibilities, project administration, safety, and related business practices.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized commercial general building trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies, including the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors. Candidates who pass can send their examination transcript to Alabama through the NASCLA National Examination Database as part of the state application process.

The NASCLA examination contains 125 multiple-choice questions, including 115 scored questions and up to 10 unscored pretest questions. It covers a broad range of commercial construction subjects rather than concentrating on one individual trade.

Major knowledge areas include procurement, contracting, project management, jobsite management, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, roofing, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, plumbing, electrical systems, safety, and code-related construction practices.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create an Alabama contractor license. Applicants must still complete the Alabama licensing process and satisfy the requirements associated with the requested classification, qualifying party, experience, financial standing, business structure, application, and supporting documentation.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates may use permitted references during the examination and must follow the current rules for books, highlighting, tabs, markings, bindings, notes, and other materials.

The Alabama Business and Law examination also relies on an approved state-specific business and law reference. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the Alabama guide, including its table of contents, index, chapters, definitions, financial material, contract subjects, and licensing information.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for preparation. The complete Alabama and NASCLA library contains thousands of pages. Candidates cannot depend on reading full chapters during an examination. They need to recognize the subject, select the correct book, locate the supporting section, and interpret the information efficiently.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books are designed to support reference navigation. Tabs can help identify major chapters, construction divisions, business topics, and code sections. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, procedures, formulas, tables, requirements, and technical concepts.

Candidates should use the references throughout the six-month course-access period. When completing a practice question, locate the supporting material in the appropriate book and review the surrounding section. This method reinforces subject knowledge while building familiarity with the organization of the references.

Timed practice is an important part of open-book preparation. Before opening a book, candidates should identify the likely subject and choose the publication most closely associated with that topic. This approach can reduce unnecessary searching and create a more consistent testing strategy.

Testing policies may restrict loose paper, removable notes, handwritten inserts, temporary tabs, or other additions. Candidates should review the current examination instructions and inspect each reference before test day.

Licensing Steps

  1. Choose the appropriate Alabama classification. Determine which major or specialty classification matches the work the business intends to bid, contract, manage, or perform.
  2. Review the licensing threshold. Alabama generally requires a general contractor license for covered construction projects valued at $100,000 or more, including labor and materials.
  3. Select the qualifying party. Identify the individual whose examination results and construction experience will support the requested license classification.
  4. Prepare the business entity. Confirm the legal name and organizational structure of the company applying for the contractor license.
  5. Submit the license application. File the appropriate application with the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors and include the required business, ownership, experience, and financial information.
  6. Prepare for Alabama Business and Law. Use the Alabama guide and business course to review contractor law, business organization, finance, contracts, insurance, employment matters, and project administration.
  7. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the NASCLA examination application process and receive authorization before scheduling the commercial general building test.
  8. Organize the trade-reference library. Learn the title, subject, table of contents, index, and major chapters of each included publication.
  9. Complete the trade course. Follow the structured lessons and work with the corresponding reference books while reviewing each construction subject.
  10. Practice under timed conditions. Develop a routine for identifying the subject, selecting the correct book, locating the supporting information, and answering the question efficiently.
  11. Pass the required examinations. Complete the Alabama Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination.
  12. Send the NASCLA transcript. Request that the passing examination result be shared with the Alabama Licensing Board through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  13. Document qualifying experience. Provide the work history, project information, references, or other records required to support the requested classification.
  14. Submit financial information. Provide the financial statement and related documentation required for the Alabama contractor application.
  15. Respond to Board requests. Submit corrections or additional information requested during the application review.
  16. Receive approval before contracting. Do not bid or perform work requiring an Alabama general contractor license until the proper license and classification have been issued.

State Requirements

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors licenses and regulates commercial and industrial contractors in major and specialty classifications. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the construction work the company intends to perform.

Under Alabama contractor licensing law, a general contractor license is generally required when the cost of a covered undertaking is $100,000 or more, including labor and materials. The law applies to qualifying construction, alteration, maintenance, repair, rehabilitation, remediation, reclamation, demolition, site work, grading, paving, and related projects.

The applicant must identify a qualifying party who supports the business with the required examination results, experience, and construction knowledge. The qualifying party and business entity must remain connected in the manner required for the license.

Prime contractor applicants must complete the Alabama Business and Law examination. This requirement remains separate from the NASCLA commercial building trade examination.

Alabama accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as a trade-exam pathway. After passing, the candidate can send the NASCLA transcript to the Alabama Licensing Board for use with the license application.

The NASCLA result replaces only the applicable trade-examination component. Applicants must still satisfy Alabama’s business and law, classification, experience, application, financial, and organizational requirements.

Applications must be submitted according to the Board’s filing schedule. The Alabama Licensing Board holds meetings on a quarterly basis, and complete applications must be on file before the applicable meeting deadline.

Applicants may be required to provide a financial statement showing the financial condition of the business. The form and level of financial documentation should correspond to the current application requirements and requested license limitation.

Business names should remain consistent across the contractor application, organizational records, financial documents, and other supporting materials. Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, and other entities should include the records required for their structure.

Alabama also maintains reciprocity relationships with certain states, but reciprocity has separate license-history, classification, application, and examination conditions. A candidate should not assume that NASCLA acceptance and state reciprocity are the same licensing process.

State licensure does not replace local permits, inspections, business licenses, zoning approvals, or project-specific requirements. Contractors remain responsible for complying with the rules imposed by municipalities, counties, building departments, project owners, and other authorities.

Test Information and Study Materials

Separate your study schedule into Alabama Business and Law preparation and NASCLA trade preparation. For the Alabama examination, concentrate on the state-specific guide and review licensing, business organization, contracts, financial management, insurance, employment responsibilities, safety, and project administration.

For the NASCLA examination, organize the technical references by construction discipline. Keep project-management publications together and separate the books covering concrete, masonry, steel, carpentry, roofing, excavation, accessibility, safety, codes, and building systems.

Use the courses with the references open. When a lesson introduces a subject, locate the related chapter, code provision, formula, table, or technical illustration. Reviewing the surrounding material helps connect course instruction to the information available during the examination.

Complete timed practice sessions and confirm each answer in the appropriate book. Track the subjects and references that take the most time so those areas can receive additional review.
