Exam Details

This complete book set supports preparation for two distinct examinations connected with Alabama commercial contractor licensing: the Alabama Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The Alabama Business and Law examination focuses on the responsibilities involved in establishing and operating a contracting business in the state. The required Alabama reference addresses subjects such as contractor licensing, business organization, financial management, estimating, bidding, contracts, employment practices, insurance, bonding, taxation, project administration, safety, and other legal and operational responsibilities.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is a broad technical trade examination. Its subject areas reflect the knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects. Candidates should be prepared to work with questions involving general construction, project management, site work, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, safety, mechanical systems, electrical systems, and environmental responsibilities.

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors determines eligibility for the state licensing examination process. PSI administers the Alabama Business and Law examination. Candidates using a passing NASCLA result for the applicable technical examination requirement must still follow Alabama’s application policies and complete the remaining state requirements.

NASCLA examination results are stored in the National Examination Database. After passing, candidates may send an official transcript to participating licensing agencies. Each agency applies its own licensing rules, so acceptance of the NASCLA examination does not replace a state license application.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing the permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not supply the books.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and reference-navigation skills. The number and size of the books make it difficult to research every answer from the beginning during the examination. Candidates should know which reference applies to each major subject and practice locating information through indexes, chapters, tables, code sections, definitions, and illustrations.

A useful preparation method is to divide the reference set into subject groups. Business and project-management books may be studied together, while technical references can be grouped into areas such as concrete, structural systems, masonry, carpentry, roofing, excavation, safety, accessibility, and building systems. This approach helps candidates associate common question topics with the correct publications.

References brought into the examination must comply with current testing rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate information bulletin before test day. Loose papers, unauthorized inserts, removable notes, and other prohibited materials should not be placed inside the books.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Alabama contractor classification. The classification requested should correspond with the type of commercial construction work the applicant intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying party. The applicant must designate the individual who will qualify the company for the requested classification.
  3. Complete the Alabama license application. The business must provide the requested ownership, organizational, financial, qualifying-party, and classification information.
  4. Submit the required financial documentation. Applicants must satisfy the financial requirements established for Alabama general contractor licensing.
  5. Receive examination eligibility. Eligibility is determined through the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors application process.
  6. Pass the Alabama Business and Law examination. Prime contractor applicants must complete the state-required examination administered through PSI.
  7. Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. A passing NASCLA result may satisfy the applicable technical trade-examination requirement.
  8. Transmit the NASCLA result. Candidates may send their transcript to Alabama through the NASCLA National Examination Database when required.
  9. Complete the state review process. The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors reviews the application, examination results, financial information, and supporting documentation.
  10. Receive the appropriate license before contracting. Applicants must obtain the required Alabama license before performing work that falls within the state’s general contractor licensing law.

State Requirements

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors regulates qualifying commercial general contracting activity in the state. Alabama law applies general contractor licensing requirements to covered projects when the total cost of the undertaking, including labor and materials, reaches the threshold established by state law.

A passing examination score is only one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also submit a complete application, identify the appropriate qualifying party, select the proper classification, provide required financial information, and satisfy the Board’s licensing requirements.

All Alabama prime contractor applicants must take the Business and Law examination administered by PSI. Candidates who pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination may use that result for the applicable technical trade portion, but they must still complete the Alabama Business and Law examination.

Alabama license applications must be submitted according to the Board’s filing and review schedule. Applications should be complete and supported by all required documentation. Missing financial materials, incomplete forms, or unresolved qualifying-party information may delay review.

The Alabama license is issued through the state Board rather than through NASCLA or PSI. NASCLA provides the accredited trade examination, and PSI administers the Alabama examination program, but the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors makes licensing decisions.

Test Information and Study Materials

A complete reference set gives candidates access to the books used for preparation, but the books are most effective when paired with a disciplined study plan. Begin by reviewing the examination subject areas and matching each subject with its primary reference.

Use the Alabama Contractors’ Guide for state business-law preparation. Study contractor licensing responsibilities, business planning, financial management, contracts, insurance, bonding, employment issues, safety, and project administration. Practice locating answers through the guide’s index and chapter structure.

For the NASCLA examination, divide the technical library into manageable categories. Study concrete references together, followed by masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, site work, safety, accessibility, project management, and building systems. This method helps reinforce the relationship between a question topic and the publication most likely to contain the answer.

Practice timed reference searches. Read a sample question, identify the key subject, select a book, locate the relevant section, and confirm the answer. Repeating this process helps develop the speed and familiarity needed for a large open-book examination.
