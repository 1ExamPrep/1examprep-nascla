## Exam Details

This complete book package supports preparation for two parts of the Alabama commercial general contractor examination process: the Alabama Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The Alabama Business and Law examination focuses on the legal, financial, and administrative responsibilities involved in operating a contracting business. Candidates should be prepared to review licensing requirements, business organization, financial management, contracts, estimating, bidding, insurance, bonding, employment responsibilities, project administration, safety, and other business subjects contained in the Alabama NASCLA Business Book.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination covers broad technical knowledge used in commercial building construction. The examination reference library addresses project planning, site operations, safety, codes, accessibility, concrete, reinforcing steel, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, exterior systems, mechanical systems, electrical systems, environmental responsibilities, and construction management.

The NASCLA examination is designed as a trade examination that may be accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Candidates who pass can transmit their results through the NASCLA National Examination Database. Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue an Alabama contractor license. Applicants must still follow the application procedures and licensing requirements established by the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors.

Alabama prime contractor applicants must also complete the required Alabama Business and Law examination. Eligibility for the Alabama examination process is determined through the state licensing application, and the examination program is administered by PSI.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference books to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the reference library.

Although candidates may consult approved books, the size of the reference set makes advance preparation essential. Searching through unfamiliar publications can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should learn which book is associated with each construction subject and practice locating information through indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, charts, and technical tables.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Alabama classification.** The requested contractor classification should correspond with the commercial construction work the applicant intends to perform.
2. **Identify the qualifying party.** The business must designate the individual who will qualify the company for the requested classification.
3. **Complete the Alabama license application.** Applicants must provide the required business, ownership, qualifying-party, classification, and supporting information.
4. **Submit the required financial documentation.** The Alabama application process includes financial responsibility requirements that must be satisfied before licensing.
5. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors determines eligibility through the application process.
6. **Pass the Alabama Business and Law examination.** Alabama prime contractor applicants must complete the state-required business examination administered by PSI.
7. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Candidates must complete the NASCLA examination application and receive authorization before scheduling the trade exam.
8. **Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination.** The examination may be used for the applicable technical trade requirement accepted by Alabama.
9. **Transmit the NASCLA result.** After passing, candidates can send an official transcript to the licensing agency through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
10. **Complete the Alabama licensing review.** The Board reviews the examination results, application, financial information, and other required documentation before issuing a license.

## State Requirements

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors regulates commercial general contracting activity in the state. Alabama licensing law applies to covered general contracting projects when the total cost of the undertaking, including labor and materials, meets the threshold established by state law.

The contractor license is issued by the Alabama Board rather than by NASCLA or PSI. NASCLA administers the accredited trade-examination program, while PSI administers the Alabama examination program. The state Board determines whether an applicant has met the requirements for licensure.

Applicants must submit the appropriate license application, identify a qualifying party, select the proper classification, provide required financial information, and complete the applicable examinations. Passing the NASCLA examination only addresses the accepted technical trade-examination component.

All Alabama prime contractor applicants must complete the Alabama Business and Law examination. The Alabama NASCLA Business Book included in this set is used to prepare for that state-specific examination.

Applications must be filed according to the Alabama Board’s review schedule. Candidates should submit complete and accurate forms with all required supporting materials. Missing documentation, incomplete financial information, or unresolved qualifying-party details can delay the review process.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A complete book set is most valuable when candidates build a repeatable method for locating information. Begin by grouping the references into business and law, management, safety, codes, accessibility, site work, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical systems.

Use the Alabama NASCLA Business Book for state Business and Law preparation. Review the organization of the book and practice locating licensing, contracts, finance, insurance, employment, safety, and project-administration information through the index and chapter headings.

For trade preparation, identify the primary reference for each technical subject. Concrete questions may require ACI 318, the quality concrete guide, or the reinforcing-bar publication. Structural questions may involve the books covering trusses, precast concrete, post-tensioning, steel joists, or steel deck. Code and accessibility questions may require the International Building Code or ICC A117.1.

Timed practice is an important part of open-book preparation. Read a practice question, identify the subject, choose the most appropriate reference, use the tabs or index, and locate the supporting information. Repeating this process develops familiarity with the complete set and helps reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.
