## Exam Details

This online course supports preparation for two examinations associated with the Alabama commercial general contractor licensing process: the Alabama Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The Alabama Business and Law examination addresses the business responsibilities of a licensed contractor. Preparation centers on the Alabama NASCLA Business Book and subjects such as licensing requirements, financial management, contracts, estimating, bidding, business organization, insurance, employment practices, safety, project administration, and other legal and operational responsibilities.

The NASCLA examination serves as a commercial general building trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies, including the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors. Its content reflects the knowledge needed to plan, coordinate, supervise, and complete commercial building projects.

The technical subject areas represented by the listed references include general construction, site work, excavation, concrete, masonry, reinforcing steel, structural systems, carpentry, roofing, steel deck, steel joists, accessibility, safety, exterior insulation and finish systems, green building, stormwater planning, mechanical systems, electrical systems, jobsite management, and project management.

Candidates apply for the NASCLA examination through the NASCLA examination process. After passing, an official result can be transmitted to participating licensing agencies through the NASCLA National Examination Database. A passing score does not automatically create an Alabama contractor license. Candidates must still apply to the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors and satisfy the state’s licensing requirements.

Alabama prime contractor applicants must also complete the Business and Law examination administered through PSI. Eligibility for the Alabama examination process is determined through the state licensing application and review process.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing the permitted reference books to the testing location. The books are not provided by the testing center.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. The reference library contains thousands of pages, and candidates cannot expect to research every question from the beginning during the examination. A successful preparation strategy should combine construction knowledge with the ability to locate information efficiently.

The online course helps candidates learn how the references are divided by subject. A concrete question may require ACI 318, the quality concrete guide, or the reinforcing-bar publication. A structural question may involve the books covering wood trusses, precast concrete, post-tensioning, steel joists, or steel deck. Accessibility and code questions may require ICC A117.1 or the International Building Code.

Students should practice identifying the key words in a question, connecting those words with the correct publication, and locating the relevant chapter, code section, table, definition, or illustration. Timed practice can help candidates develop this sequence until it becomes a consistent exam-day method.

Every reference brought into the testing room must comply with the current candidate rules. Candidates should review the applicable examination bulletin before test day and avoid adding loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Alabama contractor classification.** The requested classification should match the type of commercial construction work the applicant intends to perform.
2. **Review the Alabama application requirements.** Candidates should understand the business, financial, qualifying-party, examination, and supporting-document requirements for the requested license.
3. **Identify the qualifying party.** The company must designate the individual who will qualify the business for the applicable contractor classification.
4. **Complete the Alabama license application.** The applicant must submit accurate business, ownership, classification, qualifying-party, and supporting information.
5. **Provide the required financial documentation.** Alabama contractor applicants must satisfy the financial responsibility requirements that apply to the license application.
6. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors determines eligibility through its application process.
7. **Pass the Alabama Business and Law examination.** Alabama prime contractor applicants must complete the state-required business examination administered through PSI.
8. **Apply for and pass the NASCLA examination.** Candidates must complete the NASCLA application process before scheduling the commercial general building trade examination.
9. **Transmit the NASCLA result when required.** A passing candidate can send an official transcript to a participating licensing agency through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
10. **Complete the state review process.** The Alabama Board reviews the application, examination results, financial information, and supporting documentation before making a licensing decision.

## State Requirements

The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors regulates qualifying general contracting activity in the state. Alabama’s contractor licensing law applies to covered projects when the cost of the undertaking, including labor and materials, reaches the threshold established by state law.

The license is issued by the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors. Neither completion of an online course nor passing the NASCLA examination automatically grants a license. Applicants must complete the state application and meet all applicable licensing requirements.

Alabama prime contractor applicants must complete the Business and Law examination administered through PSI. Candidates who use the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for the technical trade component must still pass the Alabama Business and Law examination.

The applicant must identify the appropriate classification and qualifying party, provide the required financial documentation, and submit all requested business information. Applications must be filed according to the Board’s review schedule and should be complete before submission.

The Alabama Board meets quarterly, and license applications must be on file at least 30 days before a scheduled Board meeting. Incomplete forms, missing financial documentation, or unresolved qualifying-party information can delay the review process.

The NASCLA examination can help candidates satisfy the commercial general building trade-examination requirement in participating jurisdictions. Each jurisdiction maintains its own application rules, so candidates seeking licenses in more than one state must complete the application process required by each licensing agency.
