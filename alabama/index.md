# Alabama NASCLA Exam: How to Get Licensed and How to Prepare

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 12 min read · 10 official sources cited

Alabama splits construction licensing across three state boards. Commercial and public work goes through the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors, electrical work goes through the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors, and home building goes through the Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board. Each one has its own exams, fees and paperwork, so the first job is knowing which board you answer to.

The good news is that NASCLA exams fit into two of those three systems. The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam can stand in for the trade portion of a commercial license, and the NASCLA electrical exams are an option for electrical contractor and journeyman candidates. This page walks you through each path, the Alabama Business and Law exam you still have to pass, the money and paperwork the boards ask for, and the study packages we offer for each exam. ## Does Alabama Accept the NASCLA Exam?

Yes. NASCLA lists the Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors among the agencies that accept the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. The board's testing page explains that every applicant takes a trade exam plus the Business and Law exam. For commercial building work, a passing NASCLA score can fill the trade slot. You then send your transcript to Alabama through NASCLA's National Examination Database.

The NASCLA exam does not replace the Alabama Business and Law exam, the financial statement, the references or the board's approval. Think of it as one box checked, not the license itself.

On the electrical side, NASCLA lists the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board as a participating agency for two exams: Electrical Contractor (Master Electrician) and Journeyman Electrician. The board's application lets you choose between its own exam given by Prov and the NASCLA version. Contractor candidates who take the NASCLA route still need the Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law exam before a license is issued.

Residential home builders follow a separate system. The Home Builders Licensure Board uses its own Business and Law and Skills exams through Prov, so ask that board before you count on a NASCLA score for a residential license. You can confirm the current agency lists on [NASCLA's participating agencies page](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies) and the [electrical participating agencies page](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies_Electrical).

## Who Issues an Alabama General Contractor License

The [Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors](https://genconbd.alabama.gov/) in Montgomery licenses contractors on commercial, industrial and public projects. Under the Code of Alabama, you need this license when the cost of an undertaking, labor and materials included, is $100,000 or more. Swimming pool work has a lower $5,000 trigger. Subcontractors also need a license once their own contracts reach that minimum.

The [Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors](https://aecb.alabama.gov/) licenses electrical contractors and journeyman electricians statewide. Its contractor application also points out that an electrical contractor taking on a job over $50,000 must hold a license from the General Contractors Board as well.

The [Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board](https://hblb.alabama.gov/how-to-get-licensed/) regulates residential construction and remodeling. Its applicant FAQ applies the license to residential jobs where labor and materials exceed $10,000. If you do both homes and commercial buildings, plan on holding more than one license. Each board keeps its own roster, so check the right one when you verify a competitor or a sub.

## Alabama License Types and the Exam Each One Needs

Match your work to the right license first, then pick your exams. Here is how the main paths line up.

- Prime general contractor (commercial building): a trade exam, which can be the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam, plus the Alabama Business and Law exam through PSI.
- Subcontractor license from the General Contractors Board: trade and Business and Law exams as the board requires for your classification. Check with the board for your trade.
- Electrical contractor (Master Electrician): either the Alabama Electrical Contractor exam or the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam, plus the Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law exam.
- Journeyman electrician: either the Alabama Journeyman exam or the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician exam.
- Unlimited home builder: the Alabama Home Builders Business and Law exam and the Skills exam, both through Prov. Limited and roofer licenses follow different rules set by that board.
- Not sure which applies? Call the board before you register for anything, because the boards treat most fees as nonrefundable and testing for the wrong license costs you time and money.

## How to Get Your Alabama General Contractor License, Step by Step

This is the commercial path most NASCLA candidates follow. The board meets quarterly, so timing your paperwork matters.

1. Choose your classification and name a qualifier. The qualifier is the person whose exam scores and experience back the license.
2. Gather your financial statement. It must be audited, reviewed or compiled by a CPA under generally accepted accounting principles and be less than one year old.
3. Collect three reference forms from licensed general contractors, architects, engineers or other parties the board accepts, covering commercial work in the classifications you want.
4. Get proof of current liability insurance with the board listed as certificate holder, plus a Secretary of State entity printout if you are an LLC or corporation.
5. Submit the prime application with the $300 fee by cashier's check or money order, at least 30 days before a board meeting.
6. Apply for the NASCLA exam through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org, get pre-approval, and schedule with PSI.
7. Pass the NASCLA exam and send your transcript to the Alabama board through the National Examination Database.
8. Pass the Alabama Business and Law exam with PSI and submit your passing scores to the board.
9. Wait for board approval, then keep your license current each year in your renewal month.

## Inside the Alabama NASCLA Exam: Format and Testing Rules

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam has 115 scored questions and a 330 minute limit, and it may include unscored pretest items. You need 70 percent to pass, which PSI's Alabama bulletin lists as 81 correct answers. It is open book. You bring the approved references, and highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs are allowed. Removable sticky tabs and loose papers are not. Topics run from contracts, estimating and project management to concrete, masonry, steel, wood, roofing, codes and OSHA safety.

PSI runs the Alabama general contractor exams at centers that include Birmingham (Vestavia Hills), Huntsville, Mobile and Montgomery. Under the PSI bulletin, your eligibility lasts one year from the date the state receives your application. You can retest as often as you need during that year, paying the fee each time. If the year runs out, you reapply to the state. Cancel at least two days ahead to keep your fee, and bring a government photo ID that matches your registration name.

The NASCLA electrical exams are open book too. Prov's 2026 bulletin for the electrical board lists the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam at 110 questions in 4.5 hours and the NASCLA Journey-level exam at 110 questions in 5 hours. Each exam includes 10 unscored questions. The bulletin cites the 2023 National Electrical Code, allows only highlighting and permanent tabs, and gives you 90 days from board approval to test. Confirm the passing score and code edition in the current bulletin before you book.

## The Alabama Business and Law Exam

Every prime general contractor takes the Alabama Business and Law exam, even with a NASCLA score in hand, and reciprocity does not waive it either. According to PSI's bulletin, it has 50 questions, a 120 minute limit and a 70 percent passing score. It is open book, and you may use a silent, nonprinting calculator.

The approved reference is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Alabama General, 3rd Edition. NASCLA's bookstore still carries the 3rd Edition for general contractors and a separate Alabama Residential Construction 4th Edition for home builders. Expect questions on business organization, contracts, estimating, lien law, labor law, taxes, insurance and project management.

Electrical contractors take a different test, the Electrical Contractor Business and Law exam through Prov. The bulletin lists 40 questions in 2 hours and an $86 fee paid to Prov. Home builders take the Home Builders Business and Law exam through Prov, which the board scores at 70 to pass. That board also says scores stay valid for three years from the test date.

## Financial, Experience and Insurance Requirements in Alabama

The General Contractors Board sets a floor of $10,000 in both net worth and working capital for every applicant. Under board rule 230-X-1-.02, your bid limit tops out at 10 times the lower of those two figures, unless you qualify for an unlimited license. A line of credit from a qualifying bank can add to working capital. A personal or parent company financial statement with a signed guaranty can raise your bid limit by one step.

The board reviews your work history and references to judge your experience in each classification you request. It does not publish a fixed number of years on the pages we reviewed, so expect it to look closely at commercial projects you supervised.

Electrical rules are more specific. Electrical contractor applicants need at least four years or 8,000 hours of supervisory electrical construction experience. Journeyman applicants need 8,000 hours. For both, approved electrical schooling or apprenticeship can count for 1,000 hours per year, up to 2,000 hours.

Home builders seeking an unlimited or limited license send a credit report to the board, and roofers post a $10,000 license bond. Commercial applicants must show current liability insurance with the application.

## Alabama Contractor License Costs and Fees

These are the published figures we found on official pages. Fees change, so confirm with the board before you pay. Remember that study materials, notary costs, CPA fees for your financial statement and insurance premiums come on top of these.

- General Contractors Board prime application: $300. Subcontractor application: $150.
- PSI exam fee for general contractor exams: $96 per exam, or $106 for certain classifications such as Building Construction (Commercial). You pay again for each retake.
- Annual renewal with the General Contractors Board: $200 for primes and $100 for subcontractors.
- Electrical Contractor exam application: $165. Journeyman exam application: $115.
- Electrical Contractor license after passing: $150. Journeyman license: $35. Electrical reciprocity processing: $315.
- Electrical Contractor Business and Law exam through Prov: $86.
- Home Builders application package: $25, plus a $100 nonrefundable processing fee and the license fee.

## Reciprocity and Working Outside Alabama

The General Contractors Board has reciprocity with Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina and Tennessee. You need a license in good standing in a comparable classification, held for at least one year, and the same qualifier must stand behind both licenses. The trade exam can be waived, but the Business and Law exam cannot.

The Electrical Board accepts contractor licenses from Mississippi, Tennessee, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, Ohio and Louisiana, and both contractor and journeyman licenses from Texas. It no longer has reciprocity with Georgia. You still pass the Business and Law exam through Prov first.

A NASCLA score travels further than any single agreement. Your result sits in the National Examination Database, and you can send it to any participating state that accepts that exam. If you plan to work in several Southern states, that is often the simplest way to avoid repeating a trade test. Each state still sets its own business, law and financial rules.

## After You Pass: Renewal and Continuing Education

General Contractors Board licenses renew each year, and your renewal month depends on the first letter of your company name. Companies starting with A renew in December, B in November and C in October, with later letters spread from March through September. Renewals sent more than 45 days early may come back. If you let a license lapse for more than a year past expiration, you start over with a new application, the trade exam and the Business and Law exam. Details are on the board's [renewal page](https://genconbd.alabama.gov/licensees/renewal-information/).

The Electrical Board offers online renewal for contractors and journeymen. It has announced that 2027 is a continuing education year, with 14 hours required for electrical contractors. Check its [continuing education page](https://aecb.alabama.gov/ce) for the full rule and approved providers.

Once licensed, keep your financial statement, insurance and qualifier information current. A change in any of them can affect your bid limit or your license status.

## Alabama NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Does Alabama accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. The Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors is a NASCLA participating agency, so a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor score can satisfy the trade exam. You still need the Alabama Business and Law exam and board approval.

### Do I need a license for a job under $100,000 in Alabama?

For commercial and public work, the General Contractors Board license applies at $100,000 or more in labor and materials, or $5,000 for swimming pools. Residential work over $10,000 falls under the Home Builders Licensure Board. Other rules and local licenses can still apply.

### How many questions are on the Alabama Business and Law exam?

PSI's bulletin lists 50 questions with 120 minutes to finish and a 70 percent passing score. It is open book, using the NASCLA Alabama General 3rd Edition guide.

### What financial statement does Alabama require for a prime license?

The board wants an audited, reviewed or compiled statement prepared by a CPA, no more than a year old. You need at least $10,000 in both net worth and working capital, and your bid limit is generally capped at 10 times the lower of the two.

### Can I use the NASCLA exam for an Alabama electrical license?

Yes. The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor and Journeyman Electrician exams as an alternative to its own. Electrical contractor candidates must also pass the Electrical Contractor Business and Law exam through Prov.

### How much experience do I need for an Alabama journeyman electrician license?

The board asks for 8,000 hours of documented electrical experience. Approved schooling or apprenticeship can count for up to 2,000 of those hours, at 1,000 hours per year.

### How long is my Alabama PSI exam eligibility good for?

PSI's bulletin says eligibility lasts one year from the date the state receives your application. You can retest during that year by paying the fee again, and after it ends you reapply.

### Does Alabama have reciprocity for general contractors?

Yes, with Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina and Tennessee. The trade exam can be waived if you held a comparable license for at least a year, but the Business and Law exam is never waived.

### Which Business and Law book do I need for Alabama?

General contractors use the NASCLA Alabama General 3rd Edition guide. Home builders use the Alabama Residential Construction 4th Edition. We sell both as plain books, tabbed bundles and highlighted and tabbed copies.
