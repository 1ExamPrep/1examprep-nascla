## Exam Details

The **NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians** is a standardized electrical trade examination developed through the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies.

It evaluates the knowledge and skills associated with installing, maintaining, and repairing electrical systems according to applicable technical standards and codes.

Alabama is among the participating jurisdictions that recognize the NASCLA journeyman electrician examination as part of their electrical licensing framework.

- **Exam Name:** NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians
- **Examination Organization:** National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is an **open book test**. Candidates may use authorized reference materials during testing under the applicable PSI and NASCLA examination rules.

An open-book electrical examination requires more than knowing where a code book is located. Candidates must interpret electrical questions, understand technical terminology, perform calculations, and identify the correct code requirements.

Becoming familiar with NEC chapters, articles, tables, definitions, and installation requirements is an important preparation strategy.

Authorized examination references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed according to the testing rules. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary removable tabs and unauthorized additional papers are prohibited.

**Not every rental reference included in a study package is necessarily authorized inside the examination room.** Only materials listed for the applicable examination may be brought into testing.

Practice navigating the National Electrical Code and locating information about conductors, circuit protection, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, and equipment installation.

For theory questions, work through electrical formulas and relationships before searching for supporting information. For safety questions, build familiarity with OSHA requirements and electrical hazard prevention concepts.

Consistent reference-navigation practice and technical review help students become more comfortable with the format and demands of open-book electrical testing.

## State Requirements

The **Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors** oversees statewide electrical contractor and journeyman electrician licensing.

Applicants for Alabama's statewide journeyman electrician examination must generally demonstrate at least 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience involving wiring, equipment, or electrical systems used for lighting, power, or controls.

Qualifying educational or apprenticeship training may be considered toward applicable experience requirements under the Board's rules.

Applicants must complete the required application process and submit supporting documentation for review. Approval to take the examination and issuance of a license are separate parts of the licensing process.

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides a standardized examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. Alabama recognizes the NASCLA journeyman electrician examination within its licensing framework.

Passing the examination alone does not automatically provide an Alabama journeyman electrician license. Applicants must also satisfy the state's applicable experience, application, and licensing requirements.
