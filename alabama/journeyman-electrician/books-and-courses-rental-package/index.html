Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is designed around the knowledge and technical skills used in journeyman-level electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work. The examination program was developed through an occupational analysis with input from electrical subject matter experts.

The examination covers multiple areas of electrical knowledge rather than relying exclusively on memorized National Electrical Code provisions. Candidates should be prepared to interpret electrical requirements, solve calculations, understand theory, recognize safety requirements, navigate reference books, and apply electrical principles to practical situations.

Major areas associated with the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include subjects such as branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, electrical equipment, and specialty installations. Electrical theory, calculations, safety, troubleshooting, and plans should also receive dedicated study time.

Alabama currently bases its electrical examinations on the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code. The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors has announced that testing will transition to the 2026 National Electrical Code beginning January 1, 2027. Candidates preparing under the current testing cycle should therefore work with the 2023 NEC included in this package.

Passing the examination is one component of Alabama licensure. The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors separately reviews applicant qualifications and issues statewide Journeyman Electrician licenses.

Licensing Steps

Alabama statewide Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Passing an electrical examination does not automatically issue the license; candidates must complete the Board's qualification and application process.

  1. Confirm your eligibility for the Journeyman Electrician examination. Review Alabama's experience requirements and determine whether qualifying electrical education or apprenticeship training may be applied toward the required experience.
  2. Submit the examination application. Provide the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors with the required application, identification, supporting documents, and examination fee.
  3. Receive Board approval. The Board reviews applicant qualifications before authorizing the candidate to proceed with examination.
  4. Prepare for the electrical examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, safety, calculations, wiring methods, troubleshooting, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the applicable examination. Follow the examination provider's current rules concerning scheduling, identification, reference materials, and testing procedures.
  6. Apply for the Alabama Journeyman Electrician license. After successfully completing the examination requirement, submit the applicable licensing form to the Board.
  7. Complete the license-issuance requirements. Pay the applicable licensing fee and provide any documentation required by the Board.
  8. Maintain the license. Follow Alabama's current renewal and continuing-education requirements after the license is issued.

State Requirements

The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors regulates statewide Electrical Contractor and Journeyman Electrician licenses. Alabama defines a Journeyman Electrician as a person who has the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge required to install electrical wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or control systems within the applicable scope.

A statewide Alabama Journeyman Electrician applicant must demonstrate a minimum of 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience. The experience must involve the installation of electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for lighting, power, or controls. Qualifying experience may involve commercial, industrial, or residential electrical work.

Alabama also allows qualifying electrical education or apprenticeship training to substitute for part of the experience requirement. An applicant may substitute one year of approved electrical education or apprenticeship training for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, up to a maximum substitution of 2,000 hours toward the 8,000-hour requirement.

Applicants using education or apprenticeship credit must provide appropriate documentation to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. The Board determines whether the submitted education, training, and experience meet its licensing standards.

Applicants must submit the required examination application and supporting documentation before testing. The Board reviews the submitted materials and determines whether the applicant is qualified to sit for the examination.

The Alabama Board currently lists a $115 Journeyman Electrician examination fee. After successfully completing the examination, the current Journeyman Electrician license fee is $35. These government fees are separate from the price of this Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep rental package.

After passing the applicable examination, Journeyman Electrician applicants complete the Board's licensing application process. Alabama uses its EC-1 application for Electrical Contractor and Journeyman Electrician licensing, while the additional EC-2 business information form applies to active Electrical Contractors.

An Alabama Journeyman Electrician works under a master or state-certified electrical contractor and must be capable of performing electrical work according to plans, specifications, and applicable electrical rules and regulations.

Alabama's statewide electrical license should also be distinguished from local licensing requirements. State law recognizes electrical licensing through the Board as well as certain county or municipal licensing structures. Candidates should understand the licensing requirements that apply to the location and scope of work they intend to perform.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Alabama NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine technical knowledge with frequent use of the reference library. The online course and highlighted and tabbed rental books give candidates a structured way to review key electrical subjects during the 6 months of course access.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice locating rules involving services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, equipment, and special installations. Learn the organization of the NEC instead of depending entirely on memorized article numbers.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside the Code. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, loads, and common electrical calculations. Understanding the principle behind a problem makes it easier to select the correct formula and interpret the result.

Safety preparation should incorporate OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Learn which source applies to construction environments, general workplace requirements, and electrical-specific safety practices.

Testing and troubleshooting should also receive consistent attention. Review voltage, resistance, current, continuity, circuit behavior, test instruments, and systematic fault diagnosis. Journeyman-level electrical knowledge includes understanding why a system is not functioning correctly as well as knowing how it should be installed.

NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References provides additional support for calculations, formulas, conversions, and commonly used field information.

Use the tabs and highlighting as study tools, not substitutes for understanding. Work practice-oriented questions, identify the topic, navigate to the applicable reference, locate the supporting information, and then solve or confirm the answer. Repeating this process builds both electrical knowledge and reference familiarity.
