Exam Details

The NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination contains 110 questions and allows 5 hours of testing time. The examination includes 10 experimental questions. Experimental questions are not scored and do not count toward the candidate's overall examination score.

The published subject areas for the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician exam are:

  • Interpreting Plans & Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Selects and Installs Cabinets, Boxes, Fittings, and Conduit: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

Wiring and protection is the largest published content category with 18 questions. The installation of cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit represents another 16 questions, while general code requirements accounts for 15. These areas make strong NEC navigation and code familiarity especially important.

Electrical theory and principles accounts for 10 questions, while testing and troubleshooting accounts for 7. Candidates should be prepared to combine practical electrical knowledge with the ability to find supporting information in their approved references.

The examination is administered by Prov as part of the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board licensing examination program. Candidates must first complete the appropriate application process with the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board and receive authorization before scheduling the examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination is an open-book, timed test. Alabama's current Candidate Information Bulletin states that the electrical licensing examinations administered by Prov are open book and timed.

An open-book format changes the way candidates should prepare. Memorizing every code section is not necessary, but understanding electrical concepts and learning how to locate information quickly is essential. A candidate who knows where to look can use the references strategically instead of spending excessive time searching through unfamiliar books.

Practice identifying the central subject of each question before reaching for a reference. For example, an installation question may point directly toward the National Electrical Code, while a workplace safety question may require OSHA or NFPA 70E. Fire alarm questions may direct you toward NFPA 72, while formula-based questions may be easier to approach with Ugly's Electrical References or the electrical theory textbook.

The current testing guidance encourages candidates to become familiar with the authorized references before exam day. Repeated use of the books during study sessions can help you learn chapter structures, terminology, indexes, tables, article organization, and common lookup paths.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Candidates seeking licensure through examination begin by completing the required application and demonstrating that they meet the Board's experience requirements.

  1. Review the Journeyman Electrician qualification requirements. Candidates must meet Alabama's required experience standards before being approved for the examination.
  2. Complete the Electrical Journeyman Examination Application. The application must be submitted to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors with the required supporting documentation.
  3. Provide qualifying experience documentation. Applicants must document the electrical work experience required by the Board through the applicable employment affidavits, employment records, apprenticeship documentation, or approved education records.
  4. Receive approval to test. After the Board reviews and approves the application, the candidate receives information needed to schedule the examination.
  5. Schedule the examination through Prov. Approved candidates use Prov's testing system to select an available testing date and location.
  6. Take the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination. Candidates should prepare using the authorized references and become familiar with the exam's subject areas before test day.
  7. Complete the Journeyman licensing process. After successfully completing the applicable examination, candidates submit the required licensing materials to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

The examination is one component of the licensing process. Meeting the Board's experience, documentation, application, and licensing requirements is also necessary to obtain an Alabama Journeyman Electrician license.

State Requirements

Applicants for the Alabama statewide Journeyman Electrician examination must document a minimum of 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience. The experience must demonstrate the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge necessary to install electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for lighting, heat, power, or controls.

The Board recognizes qualifying commercial, industrial, and residential electrical experience. Applicants must also be capable of performing electrical work according to plans and specifications and in accordance with applicable standards and regulations.

Alabama permits a limited education or apprenticeship substitution toward the 8,000-hour experience requirement. One year of education in an approved electrical curriculum or approved apprenticeship program may substitute for 1,000 hours of electrical experience. A maximum of 2,000 hours may be satisfied through this substitution.

Applicants using education or apprenticeship experience toward the requirement must provide appropriate documentation for Board approval. Employment and experience documentation may include notarized affidavits and related employment records as required by the current application.

An Alabama Journeyman Electrician performs electrical work under a master electrician or state-certified electrical contractor. The Journeyman must possess the technical knowledge needed to install electrical wiring, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or controls and perform work according to plans, specifications, and applicable rules.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan should include both subject review and reference navigation. The NASCLA Journey-level Electrician exam gives candidates five hours to complete 110 questions, so efficient use of time can be just as important as knowing the material.

Start with the National Electrical Code because several of the largest exam categories depend heavily on NEC knowledge. Learn how the code is organized, including chapters, articles, definitions, tables, and the index. Practice translating the language used in a question into the terminology used by the NEC.

Give focused attention to wiring and protection, cabinets and boxes, fittings and conduit, and general code requirements. Together, these areas represent a substantial portion of the published examination content. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, raceways, boxes, equipment, and related installation requirements.

Electrical theory should be studied as a practical skill rather than a memorization exercise. Work through problems involving current, voltage, resistance, power, and circuit relationships. Become familiar with the formulas and reference tables you may need to locate during the examination.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction requirements, OSHA general industry requirements, and NFPA 70E. Understanding which safety reference applies to a particular question can save time during the examination.

NFPA 72 should be included in study sessions involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Even though some subject areas contain fewer questions than wiring and code categories, a balanced study plan should include every published content area.

Timed practice can help bring these skills together. Work through groups of questions while using the same books you plan to use for exam preparation. Track how long it takes to find information, identify the subjects that slow you down, and revisit those sections until navigation becomes more comfortable.

One useful technique is to practice without knowing in advance which book contains the answer. Read the question, identify the subject, choose the likely reference, and then locate the applicable information. This strengthens the same decision-making process required in an open-book testing environment.
