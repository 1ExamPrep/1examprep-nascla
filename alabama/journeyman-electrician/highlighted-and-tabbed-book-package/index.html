Exam Details

The NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination administered for Alabama contains 110 questions and allows 5 hours of testing time. Ten of the questions are experimental items and are not counted toward the candidate's overall examination score.

The examination content is divided among several electrical knowledge areas:

  • Interpreting Plans & Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Cabinets, Boxes, Fittings, and Conduit: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

Wiring and protection, general code requirements, and the installation of cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit represent significant portions of the examination. Candidates should be comfortable locating requirements within the NEC as well as applying electrical principles to practical installation questions.

The exam is administered through the Alabama electrical licensing examination program handled by Prov. Candidates must receive the appropriate approval before scheduling the examination.

Open Book Test

The Alabama licensing examination bulletin identifies the NASCLA National Electrical Journeyman examination as an open-book, timed examination. Open-book testing makes reference navigation a critical exam skill. Candidates should not expect the books to replace subject knowledge; the time limit requires you to recognize the subject of a question and locate the appropriate information efficiently.

Permanent tabs can make major chapters, articles, subjects, and frequently used sections easier to identify. Highlighting can also help important provisions stand out during review. The most effective way to use a prepared reference library is to study with the exact books repeatedly. Candidates should bring their books to practice sessions in the same organized condition they plan to use during the examination.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Candidates seeking a statewide Journeyman Electrician license through examination begin by submitting the required examination application and supporting documentation to the Board.

  1. Document the required electrical experience. Applicants for the statewide Journeyman examination must document qualifying electrical experience through the forms and supporting records required by the Board.
  2. Submit the examination application. The application must include the required supporting documentation for the Board's review.
  3. Receive authorization to test. After approval, the candidate receives scheduling information for the examination.
  4. Schedule the approved examination. Alabama's electrical licensing examination program is administered through Prov.
  5. Prepare for and take the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination. Study should combine technical review with repeated practice locating information in the approved references.
  6. Complete the licensing process after passing. Candidates who successfully complete the applicable examination follow the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors' licensing process to obtain their Journeyman Electrician credential.

Passing an examination is one part of the Alabama licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy the Board's application and experience requirements and complete the applicable licensing forms.

State Requirements

Applicants for the Alabama statewide Journeyman Electrician examination must document a minimum of 8,000 hours of electrical experience demonstrating the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge needed to install electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment for lighting, heat, power, or controls.

The current Alabama examination application also requires documentation showing qualifying electrical-industry employment. The Board's application materials call for supporting employment affidavits and related documentation. Electrical experience may involve commercial, industrial, or residential work.

Alabama permits an education or apprenticeship substitution toward part of the experience requirement. One year of approved electrical education or an approved apprenticeship program may substitute for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, with a maximum substitution of 2,000 hours toward the required 8,000 hours. Documentation of the qualifying education or apprenticeship must be submitted for Board approval.

A Journeyman Electrician in Alabama performs electrical work under a master electrician or state-certified electrical contractor and must be capable of performing work according to plans, specifications, and applicable standards and regulations.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan for the Alabama NASCLA Journey Level Electrician Exam should include both knowledge review and timed book-navigation practice. With 110 total questions and a five-hour testing period, candidates benefit from developing a method for deciding when a question can be answered from knowledge and when a reference lookup is necessary.

Begin with the 2023 NEC. Become comfortable with the code's organization, Article 100 definitions, chapter structure, indexes, tables, and commonly tested electrical subjects. Practice identifying keywords in a question and translating those keywords into NEC terminology. A candidate who recognizes the correct code concept can usually search more efficiently than someone who begins reading broad sections without a clear target.

Give additional attention to wiring and protection because it represents 18 questions on the examination. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding concepts, conductor requirements, and other related NEC provisions. The exam also assigns 16 questions to selecting and installing cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit, making wiring-method navigation another major preparation area.

Electrical theory deserves focused study rather than simple memorization. Practice applying formulas and principles to voltage, current, resistance, power, and other electrical relationships. Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications can be useful companions during this portion of preparation.

Safety preparation should include knowing which reference applies to the situation. OSHA 29 CFR 1926 focuses on construction safety, while 29 CFR 1910 covers general industry requirements. NFPA 70E adds electrical safe-work-practice material. Becoming familiar with the scope and layout of each safety reference can save valuable time when a question requires a specific source.

Plans and specifications, testing and troubleshooting, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, and communication systems should also remain part of a balanced study schedule. Do not concentrate only on the largest categories. Smaller content areas can still represent important points on a timed examination.

Once you have reviewed the major subjects, shift toward mixed practice. Work problems from different categories without first being told which book contains the answer. This more closely develops the decision-making process needed during an open-book examination: identify the subject, select the correct reference, navigate to the relevant section, interpret the information, and answer efficiently.
