Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is one of three examinations offered through the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA developed the examination content through an occupational analysis involving electrical subject matter experts from across the United States.

The examination focuses on the knowledge and skills expected of a journeyman electrician rather than the business-management responsibilities associated with an electrical contractor or master-level contractor examination. NASCLA's published Journeyman Electrician content domains include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

These subject areas reflect the broad range of knowledge journeyman electricians use in the field. Preparation should therefore include more than memorizing NEC article numbers. Candidates benefit from understanding electrical theory, calculations, installation requirements, safety practices, equipment operation, troubleshooting methods, and how different sections of the electrical code work together.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program consists of open-book electrical trade examinations using authorized reference materials under the applicable examination rules.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and reference familiarity. The ability to bring approved books into an examination does not eliminate the need to understand electrical concepts. Candidates still need to recognize what a question is asking, determine which reference applies, find the correct section, interpret the requirement, and choose the best answer while managing exam time.

Reference navigation should therefore become part of your study routine. Practice using indexes, tables of contents, NEC article organization, tables, definitions, informational notes, and cross-references. When working through a practice question, first identify the electrical topic and then decide where the information is most likely located before beginning your search.

The more frequently you use the actual references during preparation, the more familiar their organization becomes. This can be especially valuable for questions involving wiring methods, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, motors, special occupancies, communications, fire alarms, or safety requirements.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an examination credential, not a license by itself. Alabama licenses Journeyman Electricians through the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors, and candidates must satisfy the Board's application and licensing requirements.

  1. Review the Alabama Journeyman Electrician qualification requirements. Determine whether your electrical experience meets the Board's eligibility requirements before applying for examination approval.
  2. Submit the Electrical Journeyman Examination Application. Alabama requires candidates to apply to the Board for the applicable examination process.
  3. Prepare for the electrical examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, safety, wiring methods, troubleshooting, equipment, special occupancies, and other journeyman-level subjects.
  4. Schedule the authorized examination. Follow the scheduling instructions provided after your examination application and eligibility are processed.
  5. Take and pass the applicable examination. Follow all examination-center rules concerning identification, approved references, calculators, prohibited materials, and testing procedures.
  6. Apply for Alabama Journeyman Electrician licensure. After successfully satisfying the examination requirement, complete the applicable Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors licensing application.
  7. Work within the scope of the Journeyman Electrician license. Alabama Journeyman Electricians perform qualifying electrical work under a master electrician or state-certified electrical contractor.

State Requirements

The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors regulates electrical contractor and Journeyman Electrician licensing in Alabama. The Board distinguishes between an Electrical Contractor and a Journeyman Electrician.

An Electrical Contractor possesses the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge necessary to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical installations and may conduct business involving electrical power or control systems. A Journeyman Electrician performs electrical installation work under a master electrician or state-certified electrical contractor and must be capable of completing work according to plans, specifications, codes, and applicable regulations.

Alabama requires candidates to complete an examination application before proceeding through the traditional examination pathway. After successful examination completion, Journeyman Electrician applicants use the applicable Board licensing application to obtain their license.

Because NASCLA examination passage is not itself a license or certification, candidates must still meet the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors' requirements. NASCLA examination participation can provide a standardized electrical examination pathway, while Alabama remains responsible for issuing and regulating the actual state license.

Alabama currently references the 2023 National Electrical Code for its electrical examination program. Candidates studying for current examinations should therefore practice code navigation using the 2023 NEC rather than an older edition.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective NASCLA Journeyman Electrician exam preparation should combine code study with practical electrical knowledge. A strong study routine should cover the NEC, theory, calculations, troubleshooting, safety, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, and plans and specifications.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Learn how the NEC is organized rather than trying to memorize every rule. Practice using Article 100 definitions, locating requirements through the index, identifying chapters associated with specific equipment or occupancies, and following references from one code section to another.

Wiring and protection is a major journeyman-level knowledge area. Study services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor protection, overcurrent devices, grounding, bonding, and related requirements. Work through practice problems that require both calculation and code navigation.

Wiring methods and materials should also receive substantial attention. Become familiar with conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, installation conditions, conductor fill, ampacity considerations, and installation rules. Rather than studying each topic in isolation, practice recognizing which NEC article applies to a particular installation scenario.

Electrical theory provides the foundation for many journeyman questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, and common electrical calculations. Understanding the underlying principles makes it easier to solve unfamiliar questions instead of relying only on memorized formulas.

Testing and troubleshooting should be included in your preparation as well. Journeyman electricians need to understand how electrical systems behave, how measurements are interpreted, and how problems can be isolated logically. Study the relationships among voltage, current, resistance, continuity, equipment operation, and circuit conditions.

Safety preparation should extend beyond a few memorized OSHA rules. The OSHA construction standards, OSHA general industry standards, and NFPA 70E help candidates study jobsite safety, electrical hazards, safe work practices, and workplace responsibilities. Understanding the purpose behind a safety requirement can make it easier to identify the correct response to scenario-based questions.

Special occupancies and equipment can involve code requirements that candidates do not encounter every day. Regular practice with these sections can prevent unfamiliar NEC articles from consuming too much time during an open-book examination. Fire alarm and signaling systems should also be reviewed using NFPA 72 and applicable NEC requirements.

Interpreting plans and specifications is another important journeyman skill. Practice identifying circuit information, symbols, equipment, locations, and installation requirements from electrical plans. Journeyman-level work frequently requires translating plans and specifications into safe, code-compliant installations.
