Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a timed exam with 100 questions. The minimum passing score is 70, and the time allowed is 300 minutes. The exam also includes 10 non-scored “experimental” questions that do not count toward the final score.

The exam content outline is organized across key areas that reflect real job responsibilities and code use. The published content outline includes the following subject areas and item counts:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications (6)
  • Basic Electrical Safety (8)
  • Electrical Theory & Principles (10)
  • Testing and Troubleshooting (7)
  • General Code Requirements (15)
  • Wiring & Protection (18)
  • Wiring Methods & Materials (16)
  • Equipment for General Use (10)
  • Special Occupancies & Special Equipment (8)
  • Special Conditions & Communication Systems (2)

That mix matters for your study plan: it’s not only about memorizing code rules. You’ll also need to interpret scenarios, apply safety requirements, and solve practical problems that connect theory, protection, and installation methods to real-world decisions.

Open Book Test

This examination is OPEN BOOK. That means your performance depends heavily on how efficiently you can navigate your references. Your references will not be available in the examination center, so you must bring your own copies.

Reference materials may be tabbed/indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs (including Post-It notes) are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Reference material may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed, but it must be otherwise unmarked (not written in) and may not contain additional papers (loose or attached).

The exam rules also allow a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the testing center. In addition, a physical diagram/blueprint packet may be handed out onsite at the testing center (when applicable for the exam administration).

Open-book doesn’t mean “easy”—it means “find it fast.” Your goal in preparation should be to build repeatable habits: where definitions live, which chapters typically hold wiring method rules, how to confirm conductor ampacity or protection requirements quickly, and how OSHA and NFPA safety standards show up in practical jobsite scenarios.

Licensing Steps

Alabama issues statewide journeyman licenses by examination through the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board process. The Board’s journeyman examination application materials describe experience documentation requirements and the submission items needed to be approved to sit for the exam.

  • Document qualifying experience: Applicants must document (work affidavits) a minimum of 8,000 hours of experience showing qualifications, training, and technical knowledge to install electrical wiring, apparatus or equipment, light, heat, or power.
  • Education substitution option: One year of education in an approved electrical curriculum or apprenticeship program may substitute for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, up to a maximum of 2,000 hours of the 8,000-hour requirement, with appropriate documentation subject to Board approval.
  • Submit the application package: The Board’s instructions list required items, including the examination application and work documentation.
  • Provide affidavits and supporting tax documentation: Applicants may need notarized affidavits of employment from employer(s) plus corresponding W-2 forms or 1099s, or documentation through a Department of Labor Registered Apprentice program, as applicable.
  • Wait for approval and scheduling instructions: Once the application is approved, the applicant is notified by email with scheduling information.

For the NASCLA pathway, NASCLA’s candidate bulletin describes a pre-approval process through NASCLA before scheduling the examination. Once approved, the candidate receives instructions to schedule with the testing provider, with exam eligibility tied to the approval and scheduling timeframes described in the bulletin.

State Requirements

Alabama’s journeyman examination application materials outline several key requirements candidates should plan for as part of the statewide process:

  • Experience requirement: A minimum of 8,000 hours of qualifying experience is required, supported by work affidavits.
  • Approved training documentation: If claiming education or apprenticeship substitution, applicants must provide documentation for Board review and approval.
  • Exam selection and fee reference: The Board’s journeyman application materials list exam-type selections and reference an $115.00 exam fee for the Alabama journeyman exam option and the NASCLA portable journeyman exam option on the application materials.
  • Supporting documentation: The application instructions reference affidavit and documentation requirements and include proof of citizenship/legal presence documentation steps as part of compliance with Alabama Code requirements listed on the application materials.

Because application review and documentation can take time, many candidates find it helpful to start prep early and build a steady study routine while completing the paperwork side. That way, once you receive exam scheduling instructions, you can focus on readiness instead of rushing through a last-minute cram.

Test Information and Study Materials

Because the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician exam is open book and timed, your study approach should be built around two complementary skills:

  • Knowledge and interpretation: Understanding what the question is actually asking—especially with code language, exception handling, and installation scenarios.
  • Reference navigation and pace: Finding the answer efficiently without burning time. This includes knowing which reference to use first, how to confirm an answer, and when to move on and return later if needed.

A practical study routine often includes:

  • NEC navigation drills: Practice locating answers using the index, chapter structure, and common pathways (definitions → general requirements → wiring methods → protection → special occupancies).
  • Safety cross-training: Pair NFPA 70E concepts with OSHA references so you can recognize how safety expectations appear in workplace scenarios.
  • Theory refreshers: Strengthen fundamentals—especially topics that show up in troubleshooting and calculations—so you’re not relying solely on open-book lookups.
  • Timed sets: Train your pacing so 100 questions in 300 minutes feels manageable, not rushed. The goal is steady progress, accurate lookups, and controlled decision-making.
