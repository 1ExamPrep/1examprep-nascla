Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination for Master Electrician and Unlimited Electrician candidates contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours of testing time. Ten of the questions are experimental items.

The published examination content areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory & Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • General Equipment Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

Several of the largest exam categories rely heavily on the National Electrical Code. General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each account for 17 questions, while Wiring Methods and Materials represents another 16 questions. General Equipment Use adds 13 questions, making NEC familiarity a major component of preparation.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 11 questions, while Safety represents 9 questions and Project Design and Management represents 8. Candidates should therefore build a balanced preparation plan rather than concentrating exclusively on code lookup.

Alabama currently offers both a NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination and an Alabama Electrical Contractor examination. Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination path must also satisfy the applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. Subjects include business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, labor laws, licensing laws, lien laws, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, and safety and environmental topics.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book, timed examination. The applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination is also open book and timed.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important exam skill. Candidates have access to approved books, but the testing time limit means that searching every reference from the beginning for every question can consume valuable time. Preparation should combine trade knowledge with efficient navigation.

Begin by learning which book is most appropriate for each major subject. National Electrical Code questions will generally involve the NEC, while electrical theory problems may require the theory textbooks or Ugly's Electrical References. Workplace safety questions may involve OSHA or NFPA 70E. Project management and contractor Business and Law questions require familiarity with a different group of references.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. The Electrical Contractor classification applies to professionals who possess the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge required to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical installations involving wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or controls. Alabama also uses the term Master Electrician in connection with this contractor-level classification.

  1. Review the Electrical Contractor qualification requirements. Candidates should determine whether their electrical experience satisfies Alabama's contractor-level requirements.
  2. Complete the Electrical Contractor examination application. Submit the current examination application and required supporting documentation to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
  3. Document qualifying electrical experience. Applicants must provide the employment documentation, affidavits, or other supporting records required by the Board.
  4. Receive authorization to test. The Board reviews the application before the candidate proceeds with the applicable examination.
  5. Schedule the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Approved candidates follow the current testing procedures for the examination they have selected.
  6. Complete the Alabama Business and Law requirement. Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination route must satisfy the applicable Business and Law examination requirement.
  7. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the applicable examination requirements, candidates submit the licensing forms and supporting documentation required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

Successfully completing an examination is one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy Alabama's qualification, documentation, application, and licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Alabama Electrical Contractor applicants must meet the requirements established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Contractor-level applicants are expected to possess the training, experience, and technical knowledge necessary to perform and supervise electrical construction responsibilities within the scope of the license.

The Alabama Board currently references the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code for electrical examinations. The Board has announced that testing will transition to the 2026 National Electrical Code beginning January 1, 2027. This rental package contains the 2023 NEC.

Applicants should use the current forms required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors throughout the examination and licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation routine should combine technical review with repeated reference navigation. Because this examination involves a substantial collection of books, candidates should begin using the references well before their examination date rather than trying to learn their organization immediately before testing.

Start with the 2023 NEC. Become familiar with its organization, chapters, articles, definitions, tables, and index. Practice identifying key terms in electrical questions and translating those terms into the terminology used by the NEC.

Give particular attention to General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, and Wiring Methods and Materials. Together, these categories make up a substantial portion of the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Review subjects such as branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, equipment, and related installations.

Electrical theory should also receive dedicated preparation time. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, and other electrical principles. Work calculations regularly so that you understand both the formulas and the reasoning behind their application.

Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications as part of your theory-focused study. Becoming familiar with these resources can help you recognize where supporting information and commonly used formulas are located.

Safety preparation should incorporate OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn the scope of each reference and practice determining which book applies to different safety-related situations.

Project management and energy topics should not be overlooked. Master Electrical Contractor candidates are preparing for responsibilities that involve planning, coordination, supervision, and project-level decision-making in addition to technical electrical work.

Business and Law preparation should include both the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management and the Alabama supplement. Review business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, employment topics, project management, risk management, licensing requirements, liens, and other contractor responsibilities.

As your study progresses, incorporate mixed practice sessions. Instead of studying one book at a time, work through questions from several categories. Determine which reference you need, locate the relevant information, and continue to the next problem. This helps develop the reference-selection process required during open-book testing.
