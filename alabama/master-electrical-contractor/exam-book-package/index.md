## Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours of testing time. Ten questions are experimental items and do not count toward the candidate's score.

The published examination subject areas include:

- **Project Design and Management:** 8 questions
- **Safety:** 9 questions
- **Electrical Theory & Principles:** 11 questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 17 questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 17 questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 questions
- **General Equipment Use:** 13 questions
- **Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions:** 8 questions
- **Communication Systems:** 1 question
- **Experimental Items:** 10 questions

General code requirements and wiring and protection each account for 17 questions, while wiring methods and materials accounts for another 16. These categories make familiarity with the National Electrical Code especially important. General equipment use, electrical theory and principles, safety, project design and management, and specialized electrical installations also represent meaningful portions of the examination.

Alabama currently offers the NASCLA Electrical Contractors Exam and the Alabama Electrical Contractor Exam through Prov. Candidates choosing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor route must also satisfy the applicable Alabama Business and Law examination requirement before licensure is issued.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. Its subject areas include business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, labor laws, licensing laws, lien laws, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, safety, and environmental topics.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination and the applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination are open-book and timed examinations. Candidates should prepare by learning how each approved reference is organized and which books apply to particular subject areas.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who can combine technical knowledge with efficient reference navigation. Looking up every question from the beginning can consume valuable testing time. The National Electrical Code will be particularly important for subjects involving wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, general equipment, special occupancies, and general code requirements. Project management and Business and Law preparation require familiarity with a different group of books. Candidates should become comfortable moving between technical electrical references and contractor-management material so they can quickly identify where relevant information is located.

## Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Candidates seeking licensure through examination must complete the appropriate application process and receive approval before scheduling the required examination.

1. **Review the Electrical Contractor qualification requirements.** Applicants should confirm that their electrical experience satisfies the Board's contractor-level requirements.
2. **Complete the Electrical Contractor Examination Application.** Submit the current application and required supporting documentation to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
3. **Document qualifying electrical experience.** Applicants must provide the employment, affidavit, or other documentation required by the Board.
4. **Receive authorization to test.** The Board reviews the candidate's application and qualifications before examination approval is issued.
5. **Schedule the applicable examination.** Approved candidates follow the current testing instructions for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination.
6. **Complete the required Business and Law examination.** Alabama requires the applicable Business and Law examination before the Electrical Contractor license is issued.
7. **Complete the licensing process.** After satisfying the examination requirements, candidates submit the applicable Electrical Contractor licensing forms and required documentation.

Candidates should use the current forms required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors when applying. The Board maintains separate examination and licensing stages, so successfully completing an examination is only one part of obtaining the license.

## State Requirements

Applicants seeking an Alabama Electrical Contractor license by examination must satisfy the experience and application requirements established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Qualifying applicants must demonstrate substantial electrical construction experience appropriate to contractor-level responsibilities.

The Board requires applicants to document qualifying work experience through the applicable forms and supporting records. Electrical Contractor experience may involve planning, layout, supervision, installation, and other electrical construction responsibilities consistent with the scope of contractor-level work.

Alabama currently uses the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code for electrical examinations. The Board has announced that testing will move to the 2026 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code beginning January 1, 2027. This book package contains the 2023 NEC.

Candidates should make sure their examination application is approved before attempting to schedule the trade examination. After successfully completing the required examinations, the applicant must complete the appropriate licensing forms with the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A successful study plan should combine subject review with consistent book-navigation practice. This examination uses a large reference library, so candidates should avoid waiting until test day to become familiar with the books.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Learn how it is organized and practice locating information through the index, chapters, articles, tables, and definitions. General code requirements, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials represent a substantial portion of the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination.

Review conductor requirements, branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, wiring methods, equipment, motors, special occupancies, and other common electrical installation subjects. The goal is not simply to remember individual sections, but to understand where major subjects are found.

Electrical theory should receive dedicated study time. Work through voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and related calculations. Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications can support preparation in this area.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E. Candidates should know which reference is appropriate for a particular safety question and become familiar with the structure of each book.

Project design and management should also be included in the study schedule. A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge supports preparation involving project planning, coordination, processes, and management concepts. These subjects are important for professionals working at the contractor level because managing electrical work involves more than technical installation knowledge.

The energy standard should be reviewed alongside electrical and project-design material. Become familiar with its general organization and where applicable energy-related requirements can be found.

For the Business and Law examination, study the Alabama supplement together with the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. Focus on business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, licensing, employment topics, liens, project management, risk management, and safety responsibilities.

As the examination approaches, incorporate timed practice. Work through mixed questions and force yourself to determine which book contains the information before beginning a search. This creates a more realistic open-book testing experience.

Track subjects that consistently take too long to locate. Return to those chapters and references during later study sessions. Repeated navigation can help you develop a mental map of each book and make your preparation more organized.
