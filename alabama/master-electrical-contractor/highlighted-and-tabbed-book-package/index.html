Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours of testing time. The examination includes 10 experimental questions that are not scored.

The published examination subject areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory & Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • General Equipment Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

General code requirements and wiring and protection each account for 17 questions, while wiring methods and materials accounts for another 16. These categories make familiarity with the 2023 NEC especially important. Electrical theory and principles, general equipment use, safety, project design and management, and specialized installations also represent important portions of the examination.

For Alabama candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination path, candidates must also complete the applicable Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. The published content areas include business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, labor laws, licensing laws, lien laws, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, safety, and environmental topics.

Open Book Test

The Alabama NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination and the applicable Business and Law examination are open book and timed. This makes reference-book familiarity a major part of effective preparation.

Open-book testing does not mean candidates can simply search through every book for every answer. The time limit makes efficient navigation important. Strong preparation combines practical electrical knowledge with the ability to recognize the subject of a question, select the correct reference, locate the relevant section, and interpret the information accurately.

Highlighted and tabbed books can make this process more organized. During study sessions, learn what each reference is best suited for. Become comfortable moving between the NEC, electrical theory resources, OSHA material, NFPA 70E, project management references, energy standards, and business-law materials.

Practice identifying important terms in each question. An NEC installation question may send you to a specific article, while a workplace safety question may require OSHA or NFPA 70E. A project management question may require a different reference entirely. Knowing where to begin can reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Candidates pursuing licensure through examination must complete the Board's application process and receive approval before scheduling the required examination.

  1. Meet the experience requirements. Applicants must document qualifying electrical experience that satisfies Alabama's contractor-level licensing requirements.
  2. Complete the Electrical Contractor Examination Application. Submit the required application and supporting documentation to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
  3. Document qualifying work experience. Applicants must provide the required employment records, affidavits, or other supporting documentation showing qualifying electrical experience.
  4. Receive approval to test. The Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination authorization is issued.
  5. Schedule the required examination. Approved candidates follow the current testing instructions for the Alabama Electrical Contractor examination path.
  6. Complete the required testing. Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination must satisfy the applicable trade and Business and Law examination requirements.
  7. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the examination requirements, candidates follow the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors licensing process.

State Requirements

Applicants seeking an Alabama statewide Electrical Contractor license through examination must document a minimum of 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience. Qualifying experience involves electrical construction activities and must demonstrate the knowledge and responsibility expected at the contractor level.

The qualifying experience may involve commercial, industrial, or residential electrical construction. Applicants are required to document their experience using the forms and supporting records required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

Alabama also allows qualifying education or approved apprenticeship training to replace a portion of the experience requirement. One year of qualifying education or apprenticeship may substitute for 1,000 hours of electrical experience, up to a maximum substitution of 2,000 hours. Supporting documentation must be provided when education or apprenticeship credit is used.

Electrical Contractor candidates should review the current Alabama application requirements carefully and make sure all experience documentation is complete before submitting the examination application.

Test Information and Study Materials

The size of this reference library makes organization especially important. Begin by learning what type of information belongs in each book. The NEC should become a major navigation focus for code-based questions, while electrical theory books support calculations and principles. OSHA and NFPA 70E should be incorporated into safety review, and project management references should be used when studying planning and management topics.

Pay particular attention to the NEC-heavy categories. General code requirements, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials represent a substantial portion of the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Practice locating requirements for conductors, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, equipment, motors, special occupancies, and other installation subjects.

Electrical theory also deserves dedicated practice. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and other electrical relationships. Understanding both the formulas and their practical applications can make calculation-based questions easier to approach.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E electrical safety concepts. Become familiar with the organization and purpose of each reference so you can determine which book applies when a safety-related question appears.

Project design and management requires a different approach. Review the terminology and concepts found in the project management references and understand how planning, scheduling, coordination, documentation, and project execution relate to electrical contracting.

For the Business and Law portion, study the Alabama supplement together with the NASCLA Contractors Guide. Review contractor business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, employment requirements, licensing, liens, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, and safety responsibilities.

Timed practice should become part of the final stages of preparation. Work through mixed questions while using the same references you have studied. Rather than being told which book contains the answer, practice identifying the subject and selecting the proper reference yourself.

This type of study helps develop the decision-making process required during an open-book examination. Read the question carefully, identify the topic, select the appropriate reference, locate the applicable information, and move forward efficiently.
