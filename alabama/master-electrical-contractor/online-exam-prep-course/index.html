Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination for Master Electrician and Unlimited Electrician candidates contains 110 questions. Candidates are allowed 4.5 hours to complete the examination. Ten of the questions are experimental items and are not included in the final score.

The published NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination subject areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory & Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • General Equipment Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

Several of the largest examination categories are heavily connected to the National Electrical Code. General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each account for 17 questions, while Wiring Methods and Materials represents another 16 questions. General Equipment Use adds 13 questions. Together, these categories make NEC familiarity one of the most important parts of preparation.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 11 questions. Candidates should understand the electrical relationships and calculations commonly used in professional electrical work rather than depending only on memorized formulas. Safety represents another important category, while Project Design and Management addresses responsibilities beyond field installation.

Alabama currently offers both the NASCLA Electrical Contractors Exam and the Alabama Electrical Contractor Exam. Candidates selecting the NASCLA route must also satisfy the applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement before the Electrical Contractor license can be issued.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. Published subjects include Business Organization, Contracts, Estimating, Financial Management, Labor Laws, Licensing Laws, Lien Laws, Payroll and Employment Taxes, Project Management, Risk Management, and Safety and Environmental topics.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book, timed examination. The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination is also open book and timed.

Open-book testing creates a specific preparation challenge. Candidates have access to approved references, but the examination time limit makes it impractical to research every question without having a working knowledge of the material. Effective preparation combines subject understanding with the ability to locate supporting information efficiently.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should become one of the most familiar books in your study library. Practice navigating chapters, articles, tables, definitions, and the index. Learn to identify the key terminology in a question and connect that terminology with the language used by the NEC.

Safety questions may require familiarity with OSHA construction regulations or NFPA 70E. Project-management questions may direct you toward project-management references, while electrical-theory problems may require formulas and principles contained in the electrical textbooks and field references.

The online exam prep course helps reinforce this process by encouraging candidates to recognize the subject of a question before searching for an answer. Knowing where to begin is a major part of efficient open-book testing.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. An Electrical Contractor is a professional who possesses the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge needed to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical installations involving wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or controls. Alabama also uses the term Master Electrician in connection with this contractor-level classification.

  1. Review the Electrical Contractor qualification requirements. Candidates should determine whether their electrical experience meets the requirements established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
  2. Complete the Electrical Contractor examination application. Candidates seeking licensure through examination must submit the appropriate application and required supporting documentation.
  3. Document qualifying electrical experience. The Board requires applicants to demonstrate appropriate electrical construction experience through the documentation required with the application.
  4. Receive authorization for examination. The application is reviewed before a candidate proceeds through the applicable testing process.
  5. Complete the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Candidates choosing the NASCLA examination route take the approved national electrical contractor examination.
  6. Complete the Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination. Alabama requires the Business and Law examination before an Electrical Contractor license is issued through the NASCLA examination path.
  7. Complete the licensing application process. After satisfying the applicable examination requirements, candidates complete the licensing forms required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

Passing the examination does not by itself create an Alabama Electrical Contractor license. Candidates must also satisfy the Board's application, experience, documentation, and licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Alabama requires Electrical Contractor applicants to meet the qualification standards established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Contractor-level applicants must possess the training, experience, and technical knowledge necessary to plan, lay out, supervise, and perform electrical construction responsibilities within the scope of the license.

Applicants must provide appropriate experience documentation as part of the examination application process. Electrical Contractor experience may include commercial, industrial, or residential work involving contractor-level electrical responsibilities.

The Board currently references the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code for its electrical examinations. The Board has announced that Alabama will begin testing on the 2026 National Electrical Code on January 1, 2027. Candidates preparing under the current 2023 examination reference list should study with the 2023 NEC.

Candidates pursuing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor route should also prepare for the Alabama-specific Business and Law requirement. Contractor licensing includes responsibilities beyond electrical installation, including business organization, contracts, estimating, project management, financial matters, risk management, employment topics, licensing requirements, and other business-related subjects.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Alabama NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor Online Exam Prep Course is designed to support organized study across the major examination categories. Candidates should combine course review with regular use of the applicable reference books.

Begin by developing strong NEC navigation skills. The largest portions of the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination include General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, and Wiring Methods and Materials. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, conductors, raceways, boxes, wiring methods, equipment, motors, and other commonly encountered electrical subjects.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside the code. Practice working with voltage, resistance, current, power, and circuit relationships. Develop an understanding of how formulas relate to real electrical installations rather than relying entirely on memorization.

Safety preparation should incorporate both OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand which reference applies to different types of workplace and electrical safety questions.

Project Design and Management should receive dedicated study time. Master Electrical Contractor candidates may encounter questions involving project organization, planning, coordination, and management principles. Understanding these concepts reflects the broader responsibilities associated with contractor-level electrical work.

Business and Law preparation should include the NASCLA Contractors Guide and the Alabama supplement. Study contractor business organization, estimating, contracts, financial management, labor requirements, payroll and employment taxes, licensing, liens, project management, risk management, and safety responsibilities.

A useful study strategy is to work through individual categories before combining them. Start with NEC-heavy material, then dedicate separate sessions to theory, safety, project management, energy standards, and Business and Law. Once you are comfortable with the individual areas, move into mixed practice.

Mixed practice helps simulate one of the most important parts of an open-book examination: determining which reference contains the information. Read each question, identify its subject, choose the appropriate reference, and locate the supporting material.

Timed practice should become part of your preparation as the examination approaches. The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination gives candidates 4.5 hours for 110 questions. Practice working steadily while avoiding excessive time on individual questions.

Use the online exam prep course as a structured companion to your reference books. Review concepts, practice trade-focused material, identify weaker areas, and return to the books whenever additional reference familiarity is needed.
