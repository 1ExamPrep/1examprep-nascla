Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination for Master Electrician and Unlimited Electrician candidates contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours of testing time. Ten of the questions are experimental items.

The published examination subject areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory & Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • General Equipment Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each represent 17 questions, while Wiring Methods and Materials represents another 16. General Equipment Use accounts for 13 questions. These areas make familiarity with the National Electrical Code especially important for candidates preparing for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 11 questions. Safety, Project Design and Management, special electrical installations, and communication systems are also part of the published examination outline. Candidates should prepare across the complete range of subjects instead of concentrating only on code lookup.

The Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors currently offers the NASCLA Electrical Contractors Exam and the Alabama Electrical Contractor Exam. Candidates pursuing Alabama licensure through the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination must also satisfy the Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. Its subject areas include business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, labor laws, licensing laws, lien laws, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, and safety and environmental topics.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. The Electrical Contractor classification applies to professionals with the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge necessary to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical installations involving wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or controls. The term may also be used interchangeably with Master Electrician.

  1. Determine your eligibility. Review the current Alabama Electrical Contractor qualification requirements and make sure your electrical experience meets the applicable standards.
  2. Prepare the examination application. The Application Service included with The 1 Package provides assistance with the application process.
  3. Provide required documentation. Applicants must submit qualifying electrical experience and other supporting documentation required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
  4. Receive approval for testing. The Board reviews the application before the candidate proceeds through the applicable examination process.
  5. Prepare for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Use the included course and highlighted and tabbed reference books to study the published examination subjects.
  6. Complete the applicable Alabama Business and Law examination. Candidates using the NASCLA examination path must satisfy this requirement before licensure is issued.
  7. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the applicable examination requirements, complete the licensing steps required by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.
  8. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation.
  9. Obtain the EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included to support the business setup process.
  10. Review contractor compliance responsibilities. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to help customers understand requirements relevant to operating an Alabama contracting business.

State Requirements

Alabama Electrical Contractor applicants must meet the qualification and application requirements established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Contractor-level applicants are expected to possess the training, experience, and technical knowledge appropriate for planning, supervising, and performing electrical construction responsibilities.

The Alabama Board currently references the 2023 NFPA 70 National Electrical Code for electrical examinations. Beginning January 1, 2027, the Board has announced that electrical examinations will transition to the 2026 National Electrical Code. The 2023 NEC is included in this package.

Candidates following the NASCLA Electrical Contractor path must also complete the applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement. Business and Law preparation is important because operating an electrical contracting business includes responsibilities involving contracts, estimating, financial management, project management, licensing, employment matters, risk management, and related business functions.

Passing the examinations is one component of the licensing process. Applicants must also complete the required application and licensing documentation before an Alabama Electrical Contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should combine the included online course with regular use of the highlighted and tabbed rental references. Begin with the examination outline and divide your study time among code, theory, safety, equipment, project management, and Business and Law topics.

The National Electrical Code should receive significant attention. Practice locating information involving branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, equipment, motors, special occupancies, and other electrical installation subjects.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside NEC preparation. Practice working with voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and related calculations. Use the electrical textbooks and field reference materials to reinforce concepts and develop familiarity with commonly used formulas.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Understanding the scope of each reference can make it easier to determine where safety-related information should be located during an open-book examination.

Project Design and Management should also be part of the study schedule. Contractor-level professionals are responsible for more than installation work, so preparation should include planning, coordination, organization, project processes, and management concepts.

For Business and Law preparation, work with the Alabama supplement and the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. Review business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, licensing, employment matters, project management, risk management, and other contractor responsibilities.

The included 1 year of course access provides an extended period for structured preparation. Use the course together with the rental books so that study material and reference-navigation practice reinforce each other.

As your preparation progresses, introduce mixed and timed practice. Read a question, identify its subject, determine which reference is most appropriate, locate the information, and move on efficiently. This helps build a repeatable approach for open-book testing.
