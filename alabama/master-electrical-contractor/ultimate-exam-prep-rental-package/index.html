Exam Details

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination for Master Electrician and Unlimited Electrician candidates contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours of testing time. The examination includes 10 experimental questions.

The published examination subject areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory & Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • General Equipment Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each represent 17 questions, while Wiring Methods and Materials represents 16. General Equipment Use accounts for another 13 questions. These categories make familiarity with the National Electrical Code a major part of preparation for the NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination.

Electrical Theory and Principles represents 11 questions, Safety represents 9, and Project Design and Management represents 8. A balanced study plan should therefore include code navigation, theory and calculations, workplace safety, equipment knowledge, and contractor-level project responsibilities.

Alabama currently offers the NASCLA Electrical Contractors Exam and the Alabama Electrical Contractor Exam. Candidates who select the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination route must also satisfy the applicable Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination requirement before the Electrical Contractor license is issued.

The Alabama Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination contains 40 questions and allows 2 hours. Its published subjects include business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, labor laws, licensing laws, lien laws, payroll and employment taxes, project management, risk management, and safety and environmental topics.

Licensing Steps

Alabama Electrical Contractor licensing is administered by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. The Board describes the Electrical Contractor classification as applying to professionals with the qualifications, training, and technical knowledge to plan, lay out, and supervise electrical installations involving wiring, apparatus, equipment, lighting, heating, power, or controls. The term may also be used interchangeably with Master Electrician.

  1. Determine eligibility for the Electrical Contractor examination. Review the current Alabama requirements and make sure your qualifying electrical experience meets the standards established by the Board.
  2. Complete the current examination application. Candidates must submit the required Electrical Contractor examination application and supporting materials.
  3. Document qualifying electrical experience. Provide the employment records, affidavits, or other documentation required for review.
  4. Receive examination approval. The application is reviewed before the candidate proceeds with the applicable examination process.
  5. Complete the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Candidates choosing the NASCLA examination route must successfully complete the applicable national trade examination.
  6. Complete the Alabama Business and Law requirement. Alabama requires the applicable Electrical Contractor Business and Law examination before the license is issued through the NASCLA route.
  7. Complete the licensing process. After meeting the required examination conditions, submit the appropriate licensing documents to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors.

The Application Service included with this Ultimate package provides support with the application process. Licensing approval remains subject to the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors and its current eligibility, documentation, examination, and licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Alabama Electrical Contractor applicants must meet the qualification requirements established by the Alabama Board of Electrical Contractors. Test Information and Study Materials

The Alabama NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination requires candidates to prepare across several technical and professional areas. A productive study plan should combine course review, direct use of the rental references, electrical problem solving, and repeated book-navigation practice.

Begin with the National Electrical Code. General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, and Wiring Methods and Materials are among the largest published examination categories. Become familiar with the NEC's organization and practice locating information through chapters, articles, definitions, tables, and the index.

Study subjects such as branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, conductor requirements, raceways, boxes, equipment, motors, special occupancies, and other installation-related areas. Rather than concentrating entirely on memorizing section numbers, develop an understanding of where major subjects are located.

Electrical theory also deserves focused review. Practice working with voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and other electrical relationships. Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications provide resources for reinforcing calculations and electrical concepts.

Safety preparation should include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Become familiar with the purpose and organization of each reference so that you can recognize which source applies to workplace safety and electrical safety questions.

Project Design and Management should be included in the study schedule. Contractor-level responsibilities extend beyond installation, and candidates should understand concepts involving planning, organization, coordination, project processes, and management.

Business and Law preparation should include both the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management and the Alabama supplement. Study business organization, contracts, estimating, financial management, employment topics, licensing, liens, project management, risk management, and contractor safety responsibilities.

The included 1 year of course access gives candidates time to build a structured preparation routine. Use the course alongside the rental books rather than treating the references and course material as separate resources. When reviewing a subject, locate the corresponding material in the applicable book and become familiar with how that information is organized.

As your preparation progresses, introduce mixed practice sessions. Read each question, identify the topic, choose the most appropriate reference, locate the supporting information, and continue efficiently. Timed practice can help develop the pace needed for an open-book examination.
