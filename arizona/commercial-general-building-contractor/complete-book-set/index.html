Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors contains 115 questions. Candidates are allowed 5-1/2 hours to complete the exam, and the minimum passing score is 81 correct answers.

The exam content outline includes the following subject areas:

  • General Requirements
  • Site Construction
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Wood
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection
  • Doors, Windows, and Glazing
  • Finishes
  • Mechanical and Plumbing Systems
  • Electrical Systems
  • Procurement and Contracting Requirements

The exam may include questions based on listed references, trade knowledge, and general construction industry practices. Code questions are based on the code edition listed for the examination. For non-code references, candidates may study from later editions as they become available, but edition differences can affect how a topic is presented, so candidates should prepare from the references required for their exam whenever possible.

The exam measures broad commercial construction knowledge rather than a single specialty trade. Candidates should understand how a commercial project is planned, estimated, contracted, scheduled, managed, built, inspected, and closed out. The questions may require practical construction knowledge, code lookup, reference navigation, plan interpretation, project coordination, and management judgment.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should practice pacing. With 115 questions and 5-1/2 hours, candidates have less than three minutes per question on average. Some questions may be answered from knowledge, while others require looking up information in the correct reference. A strong preparation plan includes learning when to answer immediately, when to look up a question, and when to mark a question and return later.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The references are not provided at the testing center.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. They must otherwise be unmarked and may not contain additional papers, whether loose or attached. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the examination.

References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are the type of tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes or Post-It style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Candidates may use a silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. A physical diagram or blueprint packet may also be issued at the testing center for use during the exam.

Because this is an open-book exam with a large reference set, organization is a major part of preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the book titles, table of contents, indexes, topic sections, charts, tables, definitions, and code provisions before exam day. The goal is to reduce search time and increase confidence when moving from a question to the correct book.

Licensing Steps

Arizona contractor licensing is handled by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants pursuing a commercial general building license should review the current Arizona classification requirements and choose the exam path that matches the license classification they plan to obtain.

Arizona applicants applying for the B-1 or B-2 General Commercial classifications may elect to take the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as an option in place of the Arizona-specific trade examination. The NASCLA exam and the Arizona ROC General Commercial exam differ in content and structure, so candidates should select the exam that best supports their licensing and business goals.

After passing the NASCLA commercial general building exam, candidates may use NASCLA’s National Examination Database to transmit exam results to participating agencies that accept the exam. Arizona has accepted this NASCLA exam option for the B-1 and B-2 General Commercial classifications, with transcript timing requirements applying to the application process.

Passing the NASCLA exam does not automatically issue an Arizona contractor license. Applicants must still satisfy Arizona Registrar of Contractors licensing requirements, which may include the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, qualifying party information, business entity documentation, financial responsibility, bonding, background-related forms, and other state-required application documents.

Once the required examination and application requirements are complete, the applicant submits the contractor license application to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for review. The license is issued only after the state approves the application for the applicable classification.

State Requirements

Arizona uses contractor license classifications to define the work a contractor may legally contract for and perform. The B-1 General Commercial Contractor classification is connected to general commercial building work, while the B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor classification is connected to smaller commercial projects within the state’s classification limits.

The NASCLA commercial general building exam may be used as an alternative trade exam option for Arizona B-1 and B-2 General Commercial applicants. Candidates should apply for the classification that matches the work they intend to perform and should operate only within the scope of the license issued by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Commercial general building contractors coordinate multiple trades and construction disciplines. A general building license can involve project management responsibilities, subcontractor coordination, code compliance, safety responsibilities, scheduling, procurement, contract administration, and quality control. The exam reflects this broad role by covering both construction trade knowledge and project management topics.

Some projects may require additional permits, inspections, specialty contractors, design professionals, utility coordination, or local jurisdiction approvals. Exam preparation is one part of the larger licensing and compliance process. Contractors should follow Arizona licensing rules and project requirements before bidding, contracting, or performing work.
