Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a trade examination designed for commercial general building contractor licensing in participating jurisdictions. The exam contains 115 scored questions, and candidates are allowed 330 minutes to complete the test. The passing score is 70%, which requires 81 correct answers.

The exam is broad because commercial general building contractors are responsible for coordinating many areas of construction. Candidates should be ready for questions involving project management, procurement, contracts, scheduling, estimating, safety, jobsite controls, accessibility, site construction, structural systems, building envelope work, interior systems, and trade coordination.

The exam may include questions based on code requirements, construction standards, trade references, general industry practices, and practical contractor judgment. Because the reference set is large, preparation should include learning which book supports which topic. A concrete question may point to ACI 318, the concrete construction guide, the reinforcing bar book, post-tensioning material, or a general construction reference. A structural steel question may point to steel joists, steel deck, precast concrete, or the International Building Code.

Project management questions may involve jobsite administration, schedules, procurement, submittals, RFIs, change orders, closeout, communication, and contract responsibilities. Site construction questions may include excavation, pipe installation, grading, drainage, stormwater controls, erosion control, and jobsite safety. Trade coordination questions may involve concrete, masonry, metals, wood systems, roofing, gypsum, EIFS, mechanical and electrical systems, and commercial construction sequencing.

Code and standard questions may require candidates to navigate the 2021 International Building Code, ICC A117.1 accessibility standard, ACI concrete provisions, EIFS standard, and other listed references. Safety questions may require OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 navigation, including fall protection, scaffolds, ladders, excavation, personal protective equipment, tools, equipment, material handling, and hazard recognition.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam is an open-book test using approved references. Candidates bring their own allowed reference books to the testing center, and the books must comply with testing rules. References may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session begins.

During the exam, candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index inside the books. Any markings, notes, highlighting, underlining, or indexing must be completed before exam day. This makes a highlighted and tabbed book set valuable because the references are already organized for study and timed lookup practice.

Reference materials may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary removable tabs, including Post-it style tabs or other removable markers, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Loose papers and additional attached papers are not allowed with approved references. Downloaded references may be brought into the testing center only when properly bound, such as spiral bound or placed in a binder after being hole-punched.

Open book does not replace preparation. Candidates still need construction knowledge, code familiarity, safety awareness, management understanding, and the ability to choose the correct reference quickly. With a large NASCLA book set, the biggest challenge is often not whether the information exists. The challenge is finding the right information fast enough to answer accurately within the time limit.

Licensing Steps

Arizona contractor licensing is handled by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Candidates using the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam should begin by confirming the Arizona classification they intend to apply for and whether the NASCLA exam satisfies the trade examination requirement for that classification.

The person who satisfies the examination requirement is commonly the qualifying party for the license. The qualifying party is connected to the contractor license application and is responsible for demonstrating knowledge in the classification. Passing the NASCLA exam is an examination step, not a license by itself.

A practical preparation path begins with reviewing the licensing goal, gathering the correct reference set, studying the NASCLA content areas, and practicing open-book lookup under timed conditions. After passing the NASCLA exam, candidates may need to transmit or report their passing result through the applicable NASCLA process and then complete the Arizona Registrar of Contractors application requirements.
