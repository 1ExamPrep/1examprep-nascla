Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Arizona recognizes NASCLA examination results for applicable classifications through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions. Candidates must answer at least 81 questions correctly to pass. The examination provides 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, for completion.

The examination covers a broad range of commercial construction topics, including:

  • Planning and estimating
  • Financial and project management
  • Construction safety
  • Site construction
  • Concrete construction
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Wood foundations and framing
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors, windows, and glazing
  • Finishes
  • Specialty equipment and conveyance systems

The examination requires candidates to move between practical construction knowledge, technical standards, building codes, safety regulations, and project-management concepts. Contractors with extensive field experience can still benefit from structured exam preparation because licensing examinations often test specific terminology, technical requirements, and reference-based information.

A successful study plan should include practice identifying the correct reference for a question, locating information efficiently, and interpreting tables, code sections, definitions, diagrams, and technical requirements under timed conditions.

Licensing Steps

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors, or AZ ROC, administers contractor licensing in Arizona. Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create an Arizona contractor license. Applicants must complete the applicable Arizona licensing requirements for their requested classification.

  1. Choose the appropriate Arizona contractor classification. Determine which commercial or dual building classification matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify a qualifying party. Arizona requires the applicant to identify a qualifying party who has the necessary experience, knowledge, and skills for the requested classification and is regularly employed by the licensee.
  3. Complete the required examination process. The qualifying party must satisfy the applicable trade examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies. NASCLA examination results may be submitted for classifications for which Arizona accepts the NASCLA examination.
  4. Complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules requirement. The qualifying party must complete Arizona's Statutes and Rules Training Course and Exam, commonly referred to as the SRE.
  5. Complete required background checks. Arizona requires background checks for the persons named on the contractor license application.
  6. Establish the business entity. Arizona contractor licenses may be issued to qualifying sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLCs, or corporations. LLCs and corporations must be properly registered and in good standing with the Arizona Corporation Commission before submitting the license application.
  7. Obtain the required contractor license bond. Arizona requires applicants to provide proof of the appropriate contractor license bond based on classification and anticipated Arizona gross volume.
  8. Submit the license application. Complete the Arizona Registrar of Contractors application and provide the required qualifying-party documentation, examination results, identification, background information, bond, supporting documents, and applicable fees.

State Requirements

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors regulates contractor licensing throughout Arizona. A contractor license belongs to the licensed business entity rather than to the qualifying party personally. Eligible applicants may operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, or corporation.

Every applicant must identify a qualifying party who possesses the experience, knowledge, and skills necessary to supervise or perform the work authorized by the requested classification. The qualifying party may be the sole proprietor, a partner, an LLC member, a corporate officer, or an employee who meets Arizona's requirements.

Arizona also requires the qualifying party to complete the applicable examinations. The Arizona Statutes and Rules Training Course and Exam is an online, open-book requirement. A trade examination is also required unless the qualifying party qualifies for an approved waiver or satisfies the requirement through an accepted examination such as NASCLA for an applicable classification.

Arizona recognizes NASCLA examination scores for applicable general building classifications, including B-1, B-2, KB-1, and KB-2 applications. Candidates should select the classification that corresponds to the scope of work their company intends to perform.

The B-1 General Commercial Contractor classification permits qualifying commercial construction, alteration, and repair work within the scope established by AZ ROC. Certain work involving electrical, plumbing, air conditioning systems, boilers, swimming pools, spas, and water wells must be subcontracted to appropriately licensed contractors.

The B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor classification applies to qualifying commercial projects of $2,000,000 or less, including labor and materials, within the scope established for that classification.

Applicants must also obtain the contractor license bond required for their license type and anticipated gross volume. Arizona's commercial general contractor bond amounts increase according to anticipated annual Arizona gross volume, so the required amount depends on the applicant's specific circumstances.
