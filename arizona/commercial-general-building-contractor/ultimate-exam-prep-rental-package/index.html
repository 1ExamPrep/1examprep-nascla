Exam Details

This Ultimate package supports candidates preparing for the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Arizona requirements connected with contractor licensure. Arizona contractor licenses are issued and regulated by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Arizona accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as an alternative to the state-specific trade examination for qualifying applicants in the B-1 General Commercial Contractor and B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor classifications. The NASCLA result addresses the applicable trade-examination requirement, but it does not replace the complete Arizona licensing process.

The NASCLA examination evaluates commercial general building knowledge across a broad range of construction divisions. Candidates should prepare for subjects involving procurement, contracting, project management, jobsite management, site work, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, roofing, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, electrical systems, safety, and code-related construction practices.

The broad exam outline makes balanced preparation important. A candidate with extensive experience in one construction area may still need additional review in subjects outside that specialty. Someone with a strong framing background, for example, may need more preparation involving concrete standards, steel erection, accessibility, roofing calculations, stormwater controls, mechanical systems, or project administration.

Arizona also requires applicants to complete the state examination or training requirements applicable to the selected license classification and application pathway. The person serving as the qualifying party is responsible for completing the required examinations unless an approved waiver applies.

The business course helps candidates review Arizona-focused contractor responsibilities while the trade course addresses the technical commercial construction subjects represented by the NASCLA references. Using both courses creates a coordinated preparation plan for the business and trade portions of the licensing process.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates may use permitted reference books during the examination and must comply with the current rules governing tabs, highlighting, markings, bindings, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for detailed study. The complete commercial general building library contains thousands of pages, and candidates cannot depend on reading entire chapters during the examination. Effective preparation requires learning which reference applies to each subject and how the books are organized.

The highlighted and tabbed rental references are designed to support efficient navigation. Tabs can help identify major chapters, construction divisions, code provisions, and technical subjects. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, procedures, requirements, formulas, tables, and important reference points.

Candidates should work with the books throughout the one-year access period. When completing a practice question, first identify the subject and select the reference most likely to contain the answer. After finding the supporting material, review the surrounding section to understand the topic in context.

Timed practice is especially useful for open-book preparation. Candidates can track how long it takes to select a book, locate the information, interpret the requirement, and answer the question. Topics that consistently require more time can be added to a focused review schedule.

Testing policies may limit loose paper, removable inserts, temporary notes, handwritten additions, or certain types of tabs. Candidates should review the current NASCLA examination instructions and inspect every book before the scheduled test.

Licensing Steps

  1. Choose the appropriate Arizona classification. Determine whether the B-1 General Commercial Contractor, B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor, or another classification matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the legal business entity. Decide whether the applicant will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, or corporation.
  3. Select the qualifying party. Identify the individual whose experience and examination qualifications will support the contractor license.
  4. Review the classification requirements. Confirm the examinations, experience, bond, and supporting documents required for the selected Arizona classification.
  5. Complete Arizona statutes and rules preparation. Use the Arizona business course and state-specific guide to review contractor laws, rules, business responsibilities, and licensing subjects.
  6. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the applicable NASCLA registration process and obtain authorization before scheduling the commercial general building examination.
  7. Organize the trade references. Become familiar with each book’s title, subject, table of contents, index, major chapters, and technical tables.
  8. Complete the trade course. Follow the structured lessons and use the related reference books while reviewing each commercial construction topic.
  9. Attend the live classes. Use instructor-led sessions to reinforce challenging concepts, improve reference navigation, and maintain a consistent study schedule.
  10. Complete timed practice. Practice selecting the correct reference, locating supporting information, and answering questions efficiently.
  11. Pass the required examinations. Complete the NASCLA examination and the applicable Arizona statutes and rules requirement.
  12. Provide the NASCLA transcript. Arrange for the passing result to be shared with the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for an eligible classification.
  13. Complete background checks. Submit the required background information for the individuals identified in the Arizona application.
  14. Obtain the contractor bond. Secure the bond required for the selected license classification and anticipated business volume.
  15. Prepare business documentation. Gather the organizational records, identification, qualifying-party information, and disclosures required for the application.
  16. Submit the Arizona application. File the completed application with the required examination records, background documentation, bond, fees, and supporting materials.
  17. Respond to application requests. Provide corrections or additional documentation requested during the Arizona Registrar of Contractors review.
  18. Receive approval before contracting. Do not offer or perform work requiring an Arizona contractor license until the license and proper classification have been issued.

State Requirements

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors issues separate commercial, residential, and dual contractor licenses. Applicants should select the classification that matches the type and scope of work the business intends to perform.

The B-1 General Commercial Contractor classification applies to broad commercial construction work within the scope established by Arizona licensing rules. The B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor classification applies to qualifying commercial projects within the limitations assigned to that classification.

Arizona accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination in place of the state-specific trade examination for qualifying B-1 and B-2 applicants. A NASCLA transcript used for the Arizona application must meet the acceptance period established for that pathway.

The NASCLA examination replaces only the applicable trade examination. Applicants must still complete Arizona’s statutes and rules requirement unless an approved waiver applies. New applicants generally complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam training and assessment provided through the state process.

The person acting as the qualifying party must satisfy the experience and examination requirements for the selected classification. The qualifying party is connected to the licensed business and is responsible for the construction knowledge and supervision associated with the classification.

Arizona applications also require background checks for the individuals identified by the licensing process. Applicants must provide the requested identification, business records, disclosures, and supporting information.

A contractor bond is required before license issuance. The applicable bond amount depends on the license classification and other factors established by Arizona requirements. The bond must be issued in the correct legal business name.

The legal name on the application should match the business-entity records, bond, identification, and related licensing documents. Corporations and limited liability companies should complete the appropriate entity formation or registration process before submitting the contractor application.

Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to support candidates with organizing the licensing submission. Examination results, experience approval, classification decisions, waiver determinations, and final license issuance remain under the authority of the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

An Arizona contractor license does not replace local permits, inspections, zoning approvals, tax registrations, or business-license requirements. Contractors remain responsible for complying with rules imposed by cities, counties, building departments, project owners, and other authorities.
