The NASCLA result addresses the applicable trade-examination requirement, but it does not replace the complete Arizona licensing process.

The NASCLA examination evaluates commercial general building knowledge across a broad range of construction divisions. ## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates may use permitted reference books during the examination and must comply with the current rules governing tabs, highlighting, markings, bindings, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for detailed study. The complete commercial general building library contains thousands of pages, and candidates cannot depend on reading entire chapters during the examination. Effective preparation requires learning which reference applies to each subject and how the books are organized.

The highlighted and tabbed rental references are designed to support efficient navigation. Tabs can help identify major chapters, construction divisions, code provisions, and technical subjects. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, procedures, requirements, formulas, tables, and important reference points.

Candidates should work with the books throughout the one-year access period. When completing a practice question, first identify the subject and select the reference most likely to contain the answer. After finding the supporting material, review the surrounding section to understand the topic in context.

Timed practice is especially useful for open-book preparation. Candidates can track how long it takes to select a book, locate the information, interpret the requirement, and answer the question. Topics that consistently require more time can be added to a focused review schedule.

Testing policies may limit loose paper, removable inserts, temporary notes, handwritten additions, or certain types of tabs. Candidates should review the current NASCLA examination instructions and inspect every book before the scheduled test.

## State Requirements

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors issues separate commercial, residential, and dual contractor licenses. Applicants should select the classification that matches the type and scope of work the business intends to perform.

The B-1 General Commercial Contractor classification applies to broad commercial construction work within the scope established by Arizona licensing rules. The B-2 General Small Commercial Contractor classification applies to qualifying commercial projects within the limitations assigned to that classification.

Arizona accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination in place of the state-specific trade examination for qualifying B-1 and B-2 applicants. A NASCLA transcript used for the Arizona application must meet the acceptance period established for that pathway.

The NASCLA examination replaces only the applicable trade examination. Applicants must still complete Arizona’s statutes and rules requirement unless an approved waiver applies. New applicants generally complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam training and assessment provided through the state process.

The person acting as the qualifying party must satisfy the experience and examination requirements for the selected classification. The qualifying party is connected to the licensed business and is responsible for the construction knowledge and supervision associated with the classification.

Arizona applications also require background checks for the individuals identified by the licensing process. Applicants must provide the requested identification, business records, disclosures, and supporting information.

A contractor bond is required before license issuance. The applicable bond amount depends on the license classification and other factors established by Arizona requirements. The bond must be issued in the correct legal business name.

The legal name on the application should match the business-entity records, bond, identification, and related licensing documents. Corporations and limited liability companies should complete the appropriate entity formation or registration process before submitting the contractor application.

Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to support candidates with organizing the licensing submission. Examination results, experience approval, classification decisions, waiver determinations, and final license issuance remain under the authority of the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

An Arizona contractor license does not replace local permits, inspections, zoning approvals, tax registrations, or business-license requirements. Contractors remain responsible for complying with rules imposed by cities, counties, building departments, project owners, and other authorities.
