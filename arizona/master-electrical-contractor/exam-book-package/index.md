## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is also identified as the Master Electrician/Unlimited Electrician examination. It contains 100 scored questions, requires a minimum passing score of 75, and allows 270 minutes of testing time. In addition to the scored questions, the exam includes 10 non-scored experimental questions that do not count toward the candidate’s final score.

The exam content outline includes the following subject areas:

- Project Design and Management
- Safety
- Electrical Theory and Principles
- General Code Requirements
- Wiring and Protection
- Wiring Methods and Materials
- Equipment for General Use
- Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions
- Communication Systems

The exam questions are based on the listed references, trade knowledge, and general industry practices. Code questions are based only on the code editions listed for the examination. For non-code references, candidates may use earlier or later editions, but they do so at their own risk if differences between editions affect an answer.

The NASCLA electrical contractor exam places strong emphasis on the National Electrical Code and electrical field knowledge. Candidates should be comfortable using the NEC to answer questions about wiring methods, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment installation, special occupancies, special equipment, and communication systems. Electrical theory and principles may include calculations, circuits, voltage, current, resistance, power, conductors, and related practical applications.

Project design and management topics may include planning, coordination, scheduling, project documentation, leadership, quality, risk, procurement, and professional responsibilities. Safety topics may include OSHA construction standards, OSHA general industry standards, electrical safety work practices, hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, jobsite controls, and safe work procedures.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center. The references are not provided at the testing center.

The approved references for the examination include:

- National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023
- Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations
- Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910 - OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards
- A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge, Project Management Institute, 2021, 7th edition
- ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022, Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings - I-P edition
- NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition
- NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
- Ugly's Electrical References, 2023 Edition
- Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications

The examination also includes a physical diagram or blueprint packet handed out onsite at the testing center. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator during the examination.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. They must otherwise be unmarked and may not contain additional papers, whether loose or attached. References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes or Post-It style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Because this is an open-book exam, preparation should include more than reading. Candidates should practice using each reference under timed conditions. The NEC, OSHA standards, NFPA 70E, and Ugly’s Electrical References can be especially important for quick lookup. Project management and business references should also be reviewed because the examination includes project design and management content in addition to electrical code questions.

## Licensing Steps

Arizona contractor licensing is handled by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants pursuing the C-11 Electrical Commercial or CR-11 Electrical Residential/Commercial classification must follow the current Arizona licensing process for the classification they plan to obtain.

Arizona applicants applying for the C-11 or CR-11 Electrical classification have a choice of trade exams. They may elect to take either the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors or the Arizona ROC Electrical Commercial trade exam. Candidates should choose the exam path that best matches their licensing goals and business needs.

The qualifying party must complete the applicable examination requirements for the license classification. Arizona contractor applicants may also need to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, which covers state-specific contractor laws and rules. After the required exams are completed, the applicant submits the contractor license application and supporting documents to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

The application process may include business entity information, qualifying party information, experience documentation, bonding, financial responsibility, background-related forms, and other required documents. Candidates should apply under the classification that matches the work they intend to perform.

The NASCLA exam result may be used through the NASCLA National Examination Database to send transcripts to participating state agencies that accept the exam. Passing the NASCLA exam does not automatically issue an Arizona contractor license. The license is issued only after the applicant meets Arizona’s licensing requirements and receives approval from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

## State Requirements

Arizona uses contractor license classifications to define the type of work a contractor may legally contract for and perform. The C-11 classification is connected to commercial electrical work, while the CR-11 classification is a dual residential and commercial electrical classification.

The Arizona electrical commercial scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of wiring, related electrical material, and equipment used in generating, transmitting, or utilizing electrical energy under the scope of the classification. It includes electrical work under 600 volts, including certain overhead electrical wiring for signs and street decorations, underground electrical distribution systems serving private properties, outside electrical construction, and wiring in or on buildings under the applicable scope.

The CR-11 classification is useful for contractors who intend to perform electrical contracting work in both residential and commercial settings. Because license scope matters, applicants should make sure their planned work fits the classification they are pursuing before scheduling the exam or submitting an application.

Electrical contracting is highly regulated because of the safety risks involved. Candidates should expect the licensing process to focus on trade competency, code knowledge, safety responsibilities, business qualifications, and state regulatory compliance. The NASCLA electrical contractor exam supports the trade competency portion of the process, while the Arizona Registrar of Contractors determines the license application requirements and final approval.

Contractors should operate only within the scope of the license issued to them. Some projects may involve additional permits, inspections, utility coordination, specialty work, or local requirements. Exam preparation is one part of the larger licensing and compliance process for electrical contractors in Arizona.

Electrical theory preparation should include Ohm’s law, power formulas, series and parallel circuits, voltage drop, conductor properties, grounding concepts, transformers, motors, and practical NEC applications. Understanding theory helps candidates answer calculation-based and concept-based questions more efficiently.

Project design and management study should include planning, schedules, documentation, quality control, contracts, procurement, risk, communication, and project coordination. Electrical contractors are not tested only as technicians; they are also tested as business and project leaders responsible for safe, compliant, and organized work.

Strong exam preparation includes organizing references before test day. Highlight important sections, add permanent tabs, and use indexes to build lookup speed.
