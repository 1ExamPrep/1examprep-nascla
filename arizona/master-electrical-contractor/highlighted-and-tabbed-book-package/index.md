## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors contains 100 questions. The minimum passing score is 75, and candidates are allowed 270 minutes to complete the exam. A physical diagram or blueprint packet is handed out onsite at the testing center for use during the exam.

The exam content areas include project design and management, safety, electrical theory and principles, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, general equipment use, special occupancies, special equipment and special conditions, and communication systems.

The content breakdown includes 8 items on project design and management, 9 items on safety, 11 items on electrical theory and principles, 17 items on general code requirements, 17 items on wiring and protection, 16 items on wiring methods and materials, 13 items on general equipment use, 8 items on special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions, and 1 item on communication systems.

Project design and management questions may involve planning, coordination, scheduling, job documentation, resource awareness, project communication, and management practices used in electrical contracting. Safety questions may involve OSHA construction standards, OSHA general industry standards, electrical workplace safety, personal protective equipment, arc flash awareness, shock protection, ladders, tools, equipment, and hazard recognition.

Electrical theory and principles questions may involve current, voltage, resistance, power, circuits, grounding, bonding, conductor behavior, overcurrent conditions, load relationships, and electrical calculations. General code requirement questions may involve NEC organization, definitions, general installation rules, conductor rules, grounding and bonding, equipment requirements, and code navigation.

Wiring and protection questions may include branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, conductor sizing, disconnecting means, and protection of electrical systems. Wiring methods and materials questions may involve raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor installation, installation methods, and materials used in electrical work.

General equipment use may include motors, transformers, panels, disconnects, electrical equipment installation, working clearances, wiring to equipment, and equipment-specific NEC requirements. Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions may include locations or systems with additional rules, including hazardous locations, healthcare, emergency systems, renewable energy, or other code-regulated conditions. Communication systems are a smaller portion of the exam but still require awareness of applicable NEC requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book test using approved references allowed by the testing provider. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own reference materials to the examination center. References may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session begins.

During the exam, candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index inside the reference books. Any markings, notes, highlighting, underlining, or indexing must be completed before the exam begins. Candidates should arrive with their references already organized and ready to use.

Reference materials may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary removable tabs, including Post-it style tabs or other removable markers, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. Loose papers and additional attached papers are not allowed with approved references. Downloaded references may be brought into the testing center when they are properly bound, such as spiral bound or placed in a binder after being hole-punched.

Open-book electrical exams reward preparation and organization. The books can help during the exam, but candidates still need electrical knowledge, code familiarity, calculation practice, safety awareness, and the ability to identify which reference applies to the question. Highlighting and tabs help candidates move through the references with purpose instead of searching without a plan.
