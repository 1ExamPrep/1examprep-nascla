## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors contains 100 scored questions. Candidates must earn a minimum passing score of 70, and the time allowed for the exam is 270 minutes. In addition to the scored questions, the exam includes 10 non-scored experimental questions that do not count toward the candidate’s final score.

The exam content outline includes the following subject areas:

- Theory
- Personal and Jobsite Safety, including OSHA and NFPA 70E
- Project Design, Estimating, and Management
- Grounding and Bonding
- Special Systems and Special Equipment Installations
- Tools and Test Equipment
- Circuit Protection and Installation
- Wiring Methods and Practices
- Equipment for General Use
- Energy Efficiency and Management

The exam may include questions based on the listed reference materials, general trade knowledge, and industry practices. Code questions are based only on the specific code editions listed for the exam. For non-code references, candidates may use earlier or later editions as they become available, but edition differences can affect answers, so candidates should study from the correct listed materials whenever possible.

The definition used for the NASCLA residential electrical contractor exam focuses on competency in performing electrical installations, service, repair, and maintenance typically encountered in a permanent dwelling unit. Candidates should be ready for questions involving dwelling-unit electrical systems, service and feeder concepts, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, boxes, devices, equipment, safety, calculations, and residential code requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The references are not provided at the testing center.

The approved references for this exam include:

- National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023
- Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations
- NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition
- NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
- Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023 Edition
- NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2022 Edition
- Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
- ANSI Z535.4-2023, Product Safety Signs and Labels
- International Residential Code, 2024

The examination may also include a physical diagram or blueprint packet handed out onsite at the testing center. Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator during the exam.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. They must otherwise be unmarked, and candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the exam. Additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not permitted with approved references.

References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are the type of tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes or Post-It style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins.

For this exam, preparation should include repeated practice with the NEC, OSHA, NFPA 70E, NFPA 72, the IRC, Ugly’s Electrical References, and the theory material. Since all listed books may be used during the test, candidates should organize the full package in a way that supports fast lookup and clear exam-day navigation.

## Licensing Steps

Arizona contractor licensing is handled by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Applicants pursuing the R-11 Electrical Residential classification should follow the current Arizona licensing process for the classification they plan to obtain.

Arizona applicants applying for the R-11 Electrical Residential classification have a choice of trade exams. They may elect to take either the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors or the Arizona ROC Electrical Residential exam. Candidates should compare the available exam paths and choose the one that best supports their licensing and business goals.

The qualifying party must complete the applicable examination requirements for the license classification. Arizona contractor applicants may also need to complete the Arizona Statutes and Rules Exam, which covers state-specific contractor laws and rules.

After the required exams are completed, the applicant submits the contractor license application and supporting documents to the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. The application process may include business entity information, qualifying party information, experience documentation, bonding, financial responsibility, background-related forms, and other state-required documents.

Passing the NASCLA residential electrical exam does not automatically issue an Arizona contractor license. The exam result supports the trade-exam portion of the process, but the license is issued only after the applicant meets Arizona’s licensing requirements and receives approval from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Wiring methods and practices may include raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, supports, terminations, bends, fill, protection from damage, and installation workmanship.

Special systems and special equipment may involve fire alarm and signaling code topics, residential safety systems, appliances, HVAC equipment connections, pools or similar special installations when applicable, low-voltage systems, and other residential electrical equipment. NFPA 72 should be studied for fire alarm and signaling questions, while the IRC should be reviewed for residential code coordination.
