## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is an open book test using approved reference materials. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center.

For this Arizona R-11 NASCLA residential electrical package, the included references support preparation with Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition, NFPA 72 National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2016, Ugly's Electrical References, and Photovoltaic Systems, 3rd Edition, 2011. Candidates should follow the current NASCLA and PSI reference rules for the exam version they schedule.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index references during the exam. Additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not permitted with approved references.

References may be tabbed or indexed with permanent tabs only. Permanent tabs are tabs that would tear the page if removed. Temporary tabs, removable sticky tabs, and loose papers are not permitted and must be removed before the examination begins. Downloaded references may be brought into the testing center only when bound, such as spiral-bound or placed in a binder after being hole-punched.

Candidates may use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator in the examination center. A physical diagram or blueprint packet may be issued onsite at the testing center when applicable to the exam administration.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA residential electrical exam rewards candidates who combine residential electrical experience with organized reference use. Since the exam contains 100 questions and allows 270 minutes, candidates should practice using their references before exam day. The goal is not to read full code chapters during the test. The goal is to know which book to use, which article or section to open, and how to confirm an answer efficiently.

The National Electrical Code is central to preparation. Candidates should practice locating definitions, branch circuit rules, feeder and service provisions, grounding and bonding requirements, conductor ampacity tables, adjustment and correction factors, raceway rules, box fill requirements, overcurrent protection rules, equipment requirements, and photovoltaic system requirements. NEC questions often require candidates to follow cross-references, notes, exceptions, and table instructions carefully.

Grounding and bonding is one of the largest subject areas on the exam. Candidates should review grounding electrodes, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, grounding paths, service bonding, bonding of metal parts, and residential grounding requirements. This is an area where both code knowledge and accurate navigation are important.

Circuit protection and installation should receive significant study time. Candidates should review overcurrent protection, branch circuit sizing, conductor protection, load conditions, disconnecting means, panelboards, breakers, fuses, equipment ratings, and installation rules. Electrical exam questions may include calculations or conditions that require candidates to apply the correct code rule rather than rely on memory.

Wiring methods and practices is another major content area. Candidates should study cable wiring methods, raceways, boxes, fittings, support and securing requirements, conductor protection, installation locations, transition points, bending rules, and common residential wiring conditions. Practicing with the highlighted and tabbed NEC can help candidates build speed in this subject area.

Personal jobsite safety should be studied through OSHA and related electrical safety knowledge. Candidates should review electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, tools, excavation concerns, material handling, hazard communication, and general construction safety practices. Safety questions may involve recognizing hazards or finding requirements in OSHA Part 1926.

NFPA 72 supports fire detection and alarm system preparation. Candidates should review initiating devices, notification appliances, circuits, power supplies, system layout concepts, testing concepts, and fire alarm terminology. Even candidates with strong general wiring experience may need extra study time in this area because fire alarm language can be specific.

Photovoltaic Systems, 3rd Edition, 2011 supports solar electric preparation. Candidates should review photovoltaic modules, arrays, conductors, grounding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, inverters, mounting concepts, system sizing, wiring methods, and safety considerations. The NEC should also be used for code-based photovoltaic requirements.

Ugly's Electrical References is useful for reviewing calculations, common electrical formulas, quick reference tables, conversions, wire information, and field terminology. Candidates should use it during study sessions to strengthen calculation speed and reinforce practical electrical reference habits.

A practical study method is to work through the content outline one subject at a time. Start with wiring methods and practices, circuit protection and installation, grounding and bonding, equipment for general use, theory, personal jobsite safety, project design, estimating, measurement, special systems, special equipment, tools, and energy management. During each study session, candidates should use the highlighted and tabbed books under timed conditions to build speed and confidence.
