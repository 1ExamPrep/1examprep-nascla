Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is a nationally developed electrical trade examination offered through NASCLA's Accredited Examination Program.

Arizona applicants pursuing the R-11 Residential Electrical classification may choose this examination instead of the Arizona Registrar of Contractors' state-specific residential electrical trade examination.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors
  • Arizona License Classification: R-11 Electrical (Residential)
  • Examination Program: National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA)
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Examination Format: Open-book, multiple-choice
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%
  • Additional Arizona Requirement: Arizona Statutes and Rules Training Course and Examination

The examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with residential electrical installation, maintenance, safety, calculations, code compliance, and related construction activities.

Important subjects include electrical theory, conductors and cables, grounding and bonding, service equipment, branch circuits, wiring methods, protective devices, electrical safety, and residential code requirements.

Candidates should also understand practical electrical calculations and the relationship between electrical equipment, building construction, and safe installation practices.

This product focuses on the NASCLA residential electrical examination, not the separate NASCLA examination for Master or Unlimited Electrical Contractors. These examinations have different scopes and reference requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is an open book test. Candidates may use authorized reference materials in accordance with PSI examination rules.

Open-book electrical examinations require more than memorizing code sections. Candidates must understand the installation condition, identify the appropriate reference, locate the requirement, and apply the information correctly.

For example, a question involving conductor sizing may require an understanding of ampacity, insulation ratings, overcurrent protection, and installation conditions. Grounding and bonding questions require recognizing the relevant electrical components and their safety functions.

The 2023 National Electrical Code provides an important foundation for reviewing residential electrical installation requirements. NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace, while NFPA 72 provides requirements for fire alarm and signaling systems.

The International Residential Code supports study of electrical installations within the broader context of residential construction.

Candidates should practice navigating reference indexes, chapter headings, electrical tables, definitions, formulas, and technical diagrams.

The examination reference list effective August 1, 2025, identifies the 2023 NEC, 2024 NFPA 70E, 2022 NFPA 72, and 2024 International Residential Code among its approved publications.

Authorized reference editions and examination-room preparation rules apply to the testing session. Consistent practice with these materials helps candidates become familiar with their organization and use.

Licensing Steps

Using the NASCLA residential electrical examination toward Arizona R-11 licensing involves completing both the examination process and the Arizona Registrar of Contractors' application requirements.

  1. Choose Your Classification: Identify Arizona R-11 Electrical (Residential) as the classification matching your intended contracting activities.
  2. Select the NASCLA Examination: Choose the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors as your trade examination pathway.
  3. Review Qualifying Requirements: Understand Arizona's applicable experience and qualifying-party requirements.
  4. Prepare for the Examination: Study electrical codes, wiring systems, theory, safety, calculations, and residential construction requirements.
  5. Attend Live Virtual Training: Follow scheduled instruction to reinforce technical subjects.
  6. Complete Self-Study: Review the nine references and practice locating electrical information.
  7. Apply Through NASCLA: Complete the NASCLA examination application process through its National Examination Database.
  8. Schedule the Examination: Follow PSI's examination scheduling procedures after receiving the necessary authorization.
  9. Complete Arizona Statutes and Rules: Satisfy the separate state-specific licensing training and examination requirement.
  10. Submit Licensing Documentation: Arrange for examination results and complete Arizona's applicable licensing application procedures.

Passing the NASCLA examination provides a trade examination result. An Arizona contractor license is issued only after the state's complete licensing requirements have been satisfied.

State Requirements

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors regulates residential electrical contracting under the R-11 Electrical classification.

The R-11 classification authorizes the installation and repair of residential electrical systems within the scope established by Arizona licensing regulations.

Arizona also provides a combined CR-11 Electrical classification and a commercial C-11 Electrical classification. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is associated with the residential trade scope rather than unlimited commercial electrical contracting.

Applicants must meet the licensing requirements associated with their selected classification, including qualifying-party qualifications, appropriate examinations, business registration information, and applicable administrative requirements.

Arizona recognizes the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors as an alternative to the state-specific R-11 trade test.

Contractor licensing also involves compliance with applicable construction codes, safety requirements, business responsibilities, and the permitted scope of work.

The NASCLA examination program allows candidates to request examination transcript transmission to participating jurisdictions through its National Examination Database. Each receiving jurisdiction retains responsibility for its own licensing decisions.

Understanding the difference between a passed trade examination and an issued contractor license is an important part of planning a professional electrical contracting career.

Reference Books

The Arizona NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination (R-11) Live Virtual + Self Study Training program is organized around the following nine reference publications.

  • National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023
    Provides electrical installation requirements covering wiring methods, conductors, service equipment, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, branch circuits, and other electrical systems.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    Contains federal construction safety regulations addressing electrical hazards, protective equipment, workplace safety, ladders, tools, and construction operations.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th
    Supports knowledge of contractor business operations, estimating, project management, contract administration, financial responsibilities, and construction business practices.
  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    Provides electrical workplace safety practices addressing shock hazards, arc-flash risk, protective measures, and establishing electrically safe working conditions.
  • Ugly's Electrical References
    A practical electrical reference covering common formulas, electrical calculations, conductor information, wiring configurations, conversions, and technical trade information.
  • NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2022
    Addresses fire alarm and signaling systems, including equipment, installation, performance, inspection, testing, and maintenance requirements.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    Explains electrical theory and its relationship to code-based applications, supporting understanding of circuits, electrical quantities, and practical calculations.
  • ANSI Z535.4-2023, Product Safety Signs and Labels
    Establishes standards for communicating product hazards through safety signs, signal words, symbols, colors, and labeling practices.
  • International Residential Code (IRC), 2024
    Provides construction requirements for applicable residential buildings, including electrical, structural, mechanical, plumbing, and building safety provisions.

These publications provide a combination of code requirements, electrical theory, safety, contractor management, signaling systems, and residential construction knowledge.

The references support study of the different technical subjects associated with residential electrical contracting and the NASCLA examination.
