## Exam Details

The **NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors** is a nationally developed electrical trade examination offered through NASCLA's Accredited Examination Program.

Arizona applicants pursuing the R-11 Residential Electrical classification may choose this examination instead of the Arizona Registrar of Contractors' state-specific residential electrical trade examination.

- **Examination:** NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors
- **Arizona License Classification:** R-11 Electrical (Residential)
- **Examination Program:** National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA)
- **Testing Provider:** PSI
- **Examination Format:** Open-book, multiple-choice
- **Scored Questions:** 100
- **Time Allowed:** 270 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%
- **Additional Arizona Requirement:** Arizona Statutes and Rules Training Course and Examination

The examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with residential electrical installation, maintenance, safety, calculations, code compliance, and related construction activities.

Important subjects include electrical theory, conductors and cables, grounding and bonding, service equipment, branch circuits, wiring methods, protective devices, electrical safety, and residential code requirements.

Candidates should also understand practical electrical calculations and the relationship between electrical equipment, building construction, and safe installation practices.

This product focuses on the **NASCLA residential electrical examination**, not the separate NASCLA examination for Master or Unlimited Electrical Contractors. These examinations have different scopes and reference requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is an **open book test**. Candidates may use authorized reference materials in accordance with PSI examination rules.

Open-book electrical examinations require more than memorizing code sections. Candidates must understand the installation condition, identify the appropriate reference, locate the requirement, and apply the information correctly.

For example, a question involving conductor sizing may require an understanding of ampacity, insulation ratings, overcurrent protection, and installation conditions. Grounding and bonding questions require recognizing the relevant electrical components and their safety functions.

The **2023 National Electrical Code** provides an important foundation for reviewing residential electrical installation requirements. NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace, while NFPA 72 provides requirements for fire alarm and signaling systems.

The International Residential Code supports study of electrical installations within the broader context of residential construction.

Candidates should practice navigating reference indexes, chapter headings, electrical tables, definitions, formulas, and technical diagrams.

The examination reference list effective August 1, 2025, identifies the 2023 NEC, 2024 NFPA 70E, 2022 NFPA 72, and 2024 International Residential Code among its approved publications.

Authorized reference editions and examination-room preparation rules apply to the testing session. Consistent practice with these materials helps candidates become familiar with their organization and use.

Establishes standards for communicating product hazards through safety signs, signal words, symbols, colors, and labeling practices.
- **International Residential Code (IRC), 2024**
Provides construction requirements for applicable residential buildings, including electrical, structural, mechanical, plumbing, and building safety provisions.
