## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers a standardized trade examination for residential electrical contractors. The residential examination is intended for professionals who demonstrate competency in electrical installation, service, repair, and maintenance work typically encountered in permanent dwelling units.

Arizona applicants pursuing the R-11 Electrical Residential classification may use the NASCLA Accredited Residential Electrical Contractor Examination instead of the Arizona-specific trade examination. The qualifying party must still complete the remaining requirements connected with the Arizona contractor license application.

The qualifying party is the individual whose experience and examination qualifications support the contractor license. This person must demonstrate the applicable electrical background and remain associated with the licensed business in the capacity recognized by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

The examination preparation program may address electrical theory, branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, dwelling-unit calculations, motors, equipment, fire alarm systems, workplace safety, residential construction, labeling, and contractor business practices.

Questions may require candidates to interpret National Electrical Code provisions, complete calculations, identify proper installation methods, select suitable materials, recognize electrical hazards, or determine how residential construction requirements affect an electrical installation.

Fire alarm preparation may involve initiating devices, notification appliances, circuits, system documentation, testing, inspection, maintenance, and residential alarm requirements. Candidates should learn when a question belongs in NFPA 72 rather than the National Electrical Code.

The International Residential Code supports study of one- and two-family dwelling construction. Electrical contractors benefit from understanding residential building assemblies, wall and ceiling spaces, openings, penetrations, fireblocking, access, structural components, and other conditions that influence electrical installations.

The NASCLA business, law, and project management reference supports preparation involving business organization, estimating, contracts, scheduling, financial management, insurance, safety, workforce responsibilities, and project administration.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national electrical license. It provides an accredited trade-exam result that may be accepted by participating licensing agencies. The applicant must still apply for and receive the Arizona R-11 contractor license before offering or performing regulated contracting work in Arizona.

## State Requirements

The Arizona R-11 classification applies to residential electrical contracting within the scope established by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Contractors planning to perform commercial electrical work may need a commercial or dual electrical classification.

The proposed qualifying party must demonstrate the experience, knowledge, and skills required for the R-11 license. Experience records may need to describe residential wiring, service work, equipment installation, troubleshooting, supervision, and the duration of relevant electrical experience.

Arizona accepts the NASCLA Accredited Residential Electrical Contractor Examination as an alternative to the applicable state trade exam for R-11 applicants. The examination result satisfies the trade-exam component but does not replace the remaining licensing requirements.

The legal business entity applying for the contractor license must be properly formed and identified. The company name, ownership records, qualifying-party information, EIN documents, contractor bond, and license application should remain consistent throughout the process.

Arizona contractor applicants must complete applicable background requirements and provide the identification, experience, examination, business, and disclosure information required for state review.

A contractor license bond is required. The applicable bond amount depends on the residential license category and anticipated annual gross volume of work. Residential contractors must also satisfy Arizona’s residential financial-protection requirements.

Passing the examination does not authorize the applicant to operate as a licensed contractor. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors must review and approve the complete application before the R-11 license is issued.

After licensing, the contractor must operate within the authorized classification, maintain the required bond, renew the license, and keep business and qualifying-party information current. Changes involving ownership, business structure, address, bond, or qualifying party may require additional filings.
