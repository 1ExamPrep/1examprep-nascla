Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial contractor trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. Arkansas participates in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination program for applicable contractor licensing purposes.

The current NASCLA examination contains 115 scored questions. Candidates must answer at least 81 questions correctly, equivalent to a 70% passing score. Candidates are allowed 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, to complete the examination.

The current NASCLA examination content outline includes:

  • General Requirements: 25 questions
  • Site Construction: 15 questions
  • Concrete: 6 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 6 questions
  • Wood: 5 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 5 questions
  • Doors, Windows, and Glazing: 4 questions
  • Finishes: 5 questions
  • Mechanical and Plumbing Systems: 6 questions
  • Electrical Systems: 3 questions
  • Procurement and Contracting Requirements: 31 questions

This distribution demonstrates why the NASCLA exam requires preparation across both technical construction and contractor-management subjects. Procurement and Contracting Requirements and General Requirements account for a substantial portion of the examination, while candidates must also be prepared for code, structural, sitework, building-system, and trade-specific questions.

Questions can require candidates to interpret construction practices, technical standards, codes, tables, definitions, management concepts, and safety requirements. Field experience is useful, but candidates should also practice working with the specific types of written references used by the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board regulates commercial contractor licensing in Arkansas. Passing the NASCLA commercial examination does not automatically issue an Arkansas contractor license. Applicants must complete the state licensing process and satisfy the requirements associated with the classification they are pursuing.

  1. Determine the appropriate Arkansas contractor classification. Select the classification that corresponds with the commercial construction work the business intends to perform.
  2. Prepare for the required examination process. Candidates pursuing an applicable commercial building classification may use the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors to satisfy the accepted trade examination requirement.
  3. Complete Arkansas Business and Law requirements. Arkansas requires a Business and Law test for new commercial contractor licensing applicants unless an applicable accepted alternative applies.
  4. Identify the qualifying party. Arkansas contractor license applications must be supported by a qualifying party who has satisfactorily completed examinations required by the Board.
  5. Prepare the commercial contractor application. Complete the application for the appropriate license type and provide the requested business and qualifying-party information.
  6. Provide required financial documentation. Arkansas commercial contractor licensing includes financial qualification requirements based on the requested license and classification.
  7. Submit the application for Board review. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board reviews the application and supporting documentation before issuing the applicable contractor license.

NASCLA examination portability can reduce the need to repeat the same commercial trade examination when pursuing licenses in multiple participating jurisdictions. Each jurisdiction still maintains its own licensing application, state-specific testing, financial, business, and classification requirements.

State Requirements

An Arkansas commercial contractor license is generally required when a contractor performs covered work valued at $50,000 or more, including labor and materials, on a commercial project. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board identifies projects other than single-family residences as commercial projects for this licensing requirement.

Arkansas requires a Business and Law test for new commercial contractor license applicants. The state-specific business and law component is separate from the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor trade examination. The NASCLA Arkansas, 8th Edition included in this rental package supports Arkansas Business and Law preparation.

Arkansas applications may be filed under recognized business structures including an individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another business organization recognized by the Arkansas Secretary of State. Each license application must be supported by a qualifying party who has completed the examinations required by the Board.

Financial requirements also apply. For example, current Arkansas commercial contractor rules establish minimum business-related net worth requirements by classification and distinguish between restricted and unrestricted commercial licenses. Candidates should plan for examination preparation and the financial qualification portion of the licensing process as separate requirements.

A NASCLA passing score is an examination credential rather than a contractor license. Candidates must still obtain the appropriate Arkansas license before performing work for which state contractor licensing is required.
