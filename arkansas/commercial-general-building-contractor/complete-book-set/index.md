## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **open book test**. Candidates may bring the permitted reference materials identified for the examination into the testing center.

PSI permits examination reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. References may also be tabbed or indexed using permanent tabs. Temporary tabs such as Post-It notes are not permitted and must be removed before the examination begins.

Reference books must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. The approved references are not supplied at the examination center, so candidates who plan to use them are responsible for bringing their own books.

Open-book testing makes reference-navigation practice especially important. Candidates should become familiar with each book's index, table of contents, chapter structure, terminology, and common technical tables. Knowing which book is likely to contain an answer can save significant time compared with searching through several references for every question.

A practical study strategy is to identify the subject first, select the most relevant reference, and then use the index or chapter structure to narrow the search. Repeating this process during preparation can help candidates become more efficient with the large NASCLA reference library.

## Licensing Steps

The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board regulates commercial contractor licensing in Arkansas. Passing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, but candidates must still complete Arkansas-specific licensing requirements.

1. **Determine the appropriate Arkansas commercial classification.** Applicants should select the classification that corresponds with the type of construction work they intend to perform.
2. **Complete the Arkansas commercial contractor application.** Commercial contractor applicants must submit the required application and supporting documentation to the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board.
3. **Identify the qualifying party.** Arkansas contractor license applications must be supported by a qualifying party who completes any examination required by the Board.
4. **Complete the applicable trade examination requirement.** Arkansas participates in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor program and accepts the NASCLA examination for applicable licensing purposes.
5. **Complete the Arkansas Business and Law examination.** Arkansas commercial license applicants are required to complete the state Business and Law test unless an applicable Board-recognized alternative applies.
6. **Submit required financial and supporting documentation.** Commercial licensing requirements include financial qualification and other supporting information required by the Board for the requested license.
7. **Complete Board review.** The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board reviews the application and determines whether all requirements have been satisfied before issuing a license.

A NASCLA examination result is not a contractor license. NASCLA provides an accredited trade examination that participating state agencies can accept in place of their own trade examination. The applicant must still apply directly to the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board and meet all Arkansas requirements.

## State Requirements

Arkansas requires a commercial contractor license when a contractor performs covered work valued at **$50,000 or more**, including labor and materials, on a commercial project. For licensing purposes, the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board identifies commercial projects as projects other than single-family residences.

A Business and Law test is required for a new Arkansas commercial contractor license. The Arkansas Business and Law examination is separate from the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor trade examination.

Arkansas requires commercial license applications to be filed under a recognized business form, such as an individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another business organization recognized by the state. Each license application must be supported by a qualifying party who has completed the examination requirements established by the Board.

The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board evaluates contractor qualifications using factors that include construction experience, business capability, financial condition, and other licensing considerations established under Arkansas law and Board rules.

Applicants pursuing a commercial contractor license should prepare for both sides of the licensing process: the applicable trade qualification and the Arkansas-specific Business and Law requirement. This complete book set supports both by including the commercial NASCLA references and the Arkansas edition of the NASCLA Contractors Guide.
