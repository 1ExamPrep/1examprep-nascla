Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board is a participating agency in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination program.

The NASCLA commercial examination is a comprehensive test of commercial building knowledge. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved examination references into the testing center subject to current examination rules.

Open-book testing changes the way candidates should prepare. Instead of attempting to memorize every code provision or technical requirement, candidates should learn how the references are organized and practice locating information quickly.

Reference materials used at PSI examination centers may generally be highlighted, underlined, annotated, and indexed before the examination session when permitted for the applicable test. Permanent tabs are allowed where specified by PSI rules, while temporary tabs that can be removed without damaging the page are not permitted for Arkansas contractor examinations. Candidates may not add highlighting, notes, or indexing to their references during the examination session.

A highlighted and tabbed book set can help candidates establish an organized reference system before exam day. Tabs are most useful when candidates understand what each section contains and have repeatedly practiced using the books during study sessions.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Accredited Examination does not automatically issue an Arkansas contractor license. Applicants must complete the licensing requirements administered by the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board.

  1. Determine the correct Arkansas contractor classification. Applicants should identify the classification that matches the construction work their business intends to perform.
  2. Complete the applicable contractor license application. Applicants must submit the required information and documentation to the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board.
  3. Satisfy the trade examination requirement. For classifications that accept the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination, an approved NASCLA result may be used to satisfy the applicable trade examination requirement.
  4. Complete the Arkansas Contractor Business and Law Examination. Arkansas requires this examination in addition to applicable trade-specific testing, including NASCLA.
  5. Provide the required supporting documentation. Applicants must satisfy the Board's current licensing requirements for the requested classification and license type.
  6. Complete Board review. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board determines whether the applicant has satisfied the applicable licensing requirements.

The NASCLA examination is designed to improve contractor mobility among participating jurisdictions, but every state maintains its own licensing process. An applicant using a NASCLA score in Arkansas must still meet Arkansas-specific requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Preparing with a large NASCLA reference library works best when candidates create a structured study system. Start by grouping books according to subject. Codes and standards can form one group, management references another, and specialty trade publications another.

Practice choosing the correct book before searching for an answer. If a question concerns construction safety, begin with OSHA. Accessibility questions may direct you toward ICC A117.1. General commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Structural concrete questions can involve ACI 318, while practical concrete questions may be addressed in the concrete construction references.

The same approach applies to masonry, roofing, steel construction, wood trusses, excavation, gypsum systems, EIFS, reinforcing steel, and mechanical or electrical systems. Learning which reference covers each topic can reduce unnecessary searching.

Timed practice is especially useful for the NASCLA exam. With 115 questions and 330 minutes available, candidates need to balance knowledge-based answers with reference research. Highlighting and tabs can support this process, but candidates should repeatedly use the prepared books before test day so that the reference organization becomes familiar.
