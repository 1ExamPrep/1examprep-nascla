## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is designed as a standardized trade examination for commercial general building contractors. Participating licensing agencies can accept a passing NASCLA examination result instead of requiring candidates to repeat separate commercial building trade examinations in each participating jurisdiction.

The NASCLA commercial examination contains **115 scored questions**. Candidates must answer at least **81 questions correctly** to pass. Candidates receive **330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes**, to complete the examination.

The examination covers commercial construction knowledge across multiple areas, including:

- General construction requirements
- Planning and estimating
- Procurement and contracting
- Project management
- Construction safety
- Site construction
- Concrete construction
- Masonry
- Structural metals
- Wood construction
- Thermal and moisture protection
- Doors, windows, and glazing
- Interior and exterior finishes
- Mechanical and plumbing systems
- Electrical systems

Questions can require both practical construction knowledge and research within technical references. Candidates should be prepared to interpret code provisions, construction procedures, terminology, project-management concepts, safety requirements, tables, specifications, and other information commonly encountered in commercial contracting.

The breadth of the exam makes a balanced study plan important. Contractors with extensive field experience may already understand many construction practices but still need focused review of formal code requirements, technical standards, management principles, and subjects outside their normal specialty.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **open book test**. Candidates may bring permitted reference materials into the examination center in accordance with current testing-provider rules.

The open-book format makes reference-navigation skills a major part of effective preparation. Candidates should not approach the test expecting to research every question from the beginning. With more than one hundred scored questions and a large reference library, searching inefficiently can consume valuable examination time.

Practice should include identifying the subject of a question before opening a book. Once the subject is clear, candidates should determine which reference most likely contains the answer and then use the book's index, table of contents, chapter organization, definitions, or technical tables to narrow the search.

Current NASCLA examination rules allow approved references to be used in the testing center subject to applicable preparation and security requirements. Candidates should become familiar with those rules before test day and prepare their references only in ways permitted by the examination provider.

Repeated practice with the books is useful even when candidates already understand the underlying construction topic. The objective is to build familiarity with where information is located so the reference library can function as a practical exam tool rather than a collection of unfamiliar manuals.

## Licensing Steps

The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board regulates applicable contractor licensing in Arkansas. Passing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination does not automatically issue an Arkansas contractor license. Candidates must complete the state licensing process for the classification they intend to hold.

1. **Determine the appropriate Arkansas contractor classification.** Applicants should identify the classification that corresponds with the scope of commercial construction work they intend to perform.
2. **Review Arkansas licensing requirements.** The applicant must satisfy the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board requirements applicable to the requested commercial contractor license and classification.
3. **Complete the applicable trade examination requirement.** Arkansas is a participating NASCLA jurisdiction and accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for applicable commercial building licensing purposes.
4. **Complete the Arkansas Business and Law requirement.** Arkansas administers a separate Business and Law examination for general contractors. The Arkansas-specific NASCLA Contractors Guide supports preparation for this requirement.
5. **Prepare the required licensing documentation.** Applicants must submit the information and supporting documents required by the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board for their application.
6. **Submit the contractor license application.** The completed application must be submitted to the appropriate Arkansas licensing authority for review.
7. **Complete Board review and approval.** A contractor may operate under the applicable licensed classification after satisfying the Board's licensing requirements and receiving approval.

NASCLA examination results are intended to reduce repeated trade testing in participating jurisdictions. They do not replace state applications, state Business and Law examinations, financial requirements, or other licensing requirements imposed by Arkansas.

Timed practice is also valuable. Candidates should work on deciding when an answer can be selected from knowledge and when a reference search is necessary. When research is required, selecting the correct book before searching helps make better use of the available exam time.
