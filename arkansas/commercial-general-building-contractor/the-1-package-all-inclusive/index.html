Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial contractor trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies, including the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board.

The examination contains 115 scored questions. Candidates must answer at least 81 questions correctly to achieve the required passing score. Candidates are allowed 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, to complete the examination.

The NASCLA commercial examination covers major commercial construction knowledge areas such as:

  • Planning and estimating
  • Financial and project management
  • Construction safety
  • Site construction
  • Concrete construction
  • Masonry
  • Structural metals
  • Wood foundations and framing
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors, windows, and glazing
  • Finishes
  • Specialty equipment and conveyance systems

The exam requires candidates to apply practical construction knowledge while also using technical references, codes, safety regulations, construction-management books, and specialty trade manuals. Contractors with substantial field experience may still benefit from focused exam preparation because the test covers formal standards, terminology, procedures, and subjects that may extend beyond a candidate's daily specialty.

Preparation should include both content review and reference-navigation practice. Candidates should learn which book is most likely to address a particular topic and become comfortable using indexes, tables of contents, code chapters, diagrams, technical tables, and other reference tools.

Licensing Steps

The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board regulates commercial contractor licensing in Arkansas. Passing the NASCLA examination can satisfy an applicable trade examination requirement, but a passing score is not itself an Arkansas contractor license.

  1. Select the appropriate Arkansas contractor classification. The applicant should identify the classification that matches the type of commercial construction work the company intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying party. Arkansas license applications must be supported by a qualifying party who has satisfactorily completed the examination required by the Board.
  3. Complete the NASCLA trade examination requirement. Arkansas accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for applicable commercial licensing purposes.
  4. Complete Arkansas Business and Law requirements. Arkansas commercial contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable state Business and Law examination requirement.
  5. Prepare the contractor license application. Applicants must provide the required business, classification, qualifying-party, financial, and supporting information.
  6. Provide required financial documentation. Arkansas establishes financial requirements based on the commercial contractor classification and license type requested.
  7. Submit the application for Board review. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board evaluates the application before issuing the requested license and classification.

The Application Service included with The 1 Package provides support with the contractor application process while candidates continue preparing for the examination and organizing the business.

State Requirements

Arkansas contractor licenses are issued under the business name and structure shown on the application. Arkansas recognizes applications filed as an individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another business organization recognized by the Secretary of State.

Each contractor license application must be supported by a qualifying party who has satisfactorily completed the examination required by the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board. For applicants using the NASCLA commercial examination, the NASCLA trade result addresses the applicable commercial trade examination component, while Arkansas-specific licensing requirements remain separate.

Arkansas commercial contractor licensing also includes financial qualification requirements. Current rules establish minimum business-related net worth requirements by classification. For a Building classification, the minimum business-related net worth is $50,000, and new applicants must have at least one-half of the required minimum net worth in cash.

Arkansas distinguishes between restricted and unrestricted commercial licenses for financial reporting purposes. Applicants should ensure that the financial documentation submitted with the application corresponds with the license type they are pursuing.
