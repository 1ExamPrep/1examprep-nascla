Arkansas NASCLA Exam and Commercial Contractor License Guide

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 12 min read · 9 official sources cited

If you plan to bid commercial work in Arkansas, you will deal with the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board, and sooner or later you will hear about the NASCLA exam. This page explains where the Arkansas NASCLA exam fits, what the Board still expects from you, and how to prepare for the two tests that matter most: the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam and the Arkansas Business and Law exam.

Does Arkansas accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. The Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board is listed by NASCLA as a participating agency for the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. NASCLA's agency list notes that Arkansas gives the exam in lieu of candidates showing appropriate experience on the application. In plain terms, a passing NASCLA result is a way to support the trade qualification side of a commercial application when your documented experience alone is not enough.

What the NASCLA exam does not do is replace the rest of the Arkansas process. You still file a full commercial application with the Board, you still meet the financial standard for the classification you request, you still post a bond, and the person qualifying the company still passes the Arkansas Business and Law exam unless a recognized alternative applies. A NASCLA pass report is a test result, not a license.

Because the NASCLA listing is short, we recommend calling the Board before you schedule. Ask how they want a NASCLA transcript tied to your application and which classification you should request. That one phone call can save you weeks.

Who issues the Arkansas commercial contractor license

Commercial contractor licenses in Arkansas come from the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board, whose pages now live on the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing site. The Board also handles residential builder and remodeler licensing, but this page focuses on the commercial side because that is where the NASCLA exam applies.

The Board's rule of thumb is simple. You need an Arkansas commercial contractor license before you bid or perform work on a commercial project worth $50,000 or more, counting both labor and materials. For licensing purposes, any project other than a single-family residence counts as commercial. That means apartments, retail, offices, churches, schools and industrial work can all trigger the requirement once the contract reaches that amount.

Some trades also need a separate state trade license or certification from another agency, such as plumbing, electrical, HVACR, gas fitting, fire sprinklers and asbestos. The commercial application asks you to attach a copy of that credential when you request one of those specialties. The NASCLA commercial exam does not satisfy those separate trade licenses.

Arkansas contractor license types and which exam each needs

The commercial application splits work into major classifications and specialties. Major classifications listed on the current application include Heavy Construction; Municipal and Utility; Highway, Railroad and Airport; Building; Light Building; Mechanical; and Electrical. The application ties major classifications to five years of experience and specialties to one year, documented through reference forms.

The Building classification is the one most NASCLA candidates are after, since the NASCLA exam is a general commercial building test. Light Building and many specialties are narrower. If your reference forms do not show the experience the Board wants for Building, the NASCLA exam is the tool Arkansas recognizes to fill that gap.

Arkansas also issues commercial licenses in two financial tiers. A restricted license comes with a cap on project size and accepts a CPA compiled financial statement, while an unrestricted license has no project size cap and requires a reviewed or audited statement. The current application and the renewal page state different caps for the restricted tier, so ask the Board which figure applies before you choose.

  • Building (commercial and residential): five years of experience on the application, or support it with a NASCLA pass.
  • Every new commercial applicant: the Arkansas Business and Law exam, unless the reciprocal form or another Board alternative applies.
  • Specialties needing a state trade license (plumbing, electrical, HVACR and others): that agency's own exam and license.

How to get an Arkansas commercial contractor license, step by step

Here is the order most applicants follow. Your situation may differ, so use this as a checklist and confirm the details with the Board.

  1. Pick the classification that matches the work you plan to contract for, and decide between a restricted or unrestricted license.
  2. Name your qualifying party. The person who sits for the Business and Law exam must be the sole owner, a full-time paid employee, or an officer, member or partner active in daily operations.
  3. If you are using the NASCLA route, apply for the NASCLA exam at ned.nascla.org, wait for pre-approval, then schedule with PSI and pass it.
  4. Register for the Arkansas Business and Law exam with PSI, study the NASCLA Arkansas guide, and pass. Send the passing score to the Board yourself by fax or mail.
  5. Order a CPA financial statement less than one year old, or arrange a bond in lieu of the financial statement.
  6. Collect three reference forms that document experience for each classification you request. References must be no more than 90 days old.
  7. Buy the $10,000 contractor surety bond, and get a workers' compensation certificate if you have one or more employees.
  8. Mail the complete application with the $100 filing fee so it arrives at least three weeks before a Board meeting.
  9. Finish any missing items within 90 days of filing. After that, the Board requires a new application and a new fee.

The NASCLA commercial exam itself

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions and a 330 minute time limit, which is five and a half hours. You need 70 percent to pass, which works out to 81 correct answers. PSI may add a small number of unscored pretest questions, and the time limit already allows for them.

The exam is open book, but the books are not supplied. You bring the approved references yourself. PSI allows highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs that you put on before test day. Sticky notes and other removable tabs are not allowed, and you cannot tuck loose pages into the books. The scored content leans heavily on project management: Procurement and Contracting Requirements carry 31 questions and General Requirements carry 25, with the rest spread across site work, concrete, masonry, metals, wood, moisture protection, openings, finishes, mechanical, plumbing and electrical.

To sit for it, you apply through NASCLA's National Examination Database at ned.nascla.org. NASCLA publishes a $65 application fee and says it usually processes applications in about seven business days. Once approved, you get one year of eligibility and up to three attempts. PSI sends a candidate ID and scheduling link, and you can test at any PSI site that offers the exam. After you pass, you buy a transcript for each state agency at $45 each, and NASCLA sends it electronically.

The Arkansas Business and Law exam

Every new Arkansas commercial applicant must pass the state Business and Law exam unless an alternative applies. It is a separate test from the NASCLA trade exam, and passing one does not count for the other.

According to the Board's testing notice, the Arkansas Business and Law exam has 50 multiple-choice questions, a two-hour limit, and is open book. The only reference allowed is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Arkansas Edition. NASCLA's store currently lists the 8th Edition. You may highlight, underline, annotate and add permanent tabs before the exam, but you cannot write in the book during the test, and temporary tabs are banned. The Board's notice asks you to put tabs on at least a day before you test.

PSI runs the exam at its test centers. The published exam fee is $84, and it is non-refundable and non-transferable. You register on PSI's Arkansas page or by phone, using your name exactly as it appears on your photo ID. Arrive 30 minutes early with a government-issued photo ID and your guide. Results come back right away, but PSI does not automatically file them with the Board, so fax or mail your passing score to the Board yourself to avoid delays.

The Arkansas edition of the guide pairs general business, contract and project management material with Arkansas licensing and law content. Because the time limit is tight for an open-book test, tabs and highlighting pay off here more than almost anywhere else.

Experience, financial, insurance and bond requirements in Arkansas

Experience is documented through three reference forms. References cannot come from a supplier or banker unless that person actually saw your work, cannot be related to or affiliated with your owners or employees, and must be under 90 days old. Each reference has to speak to the classification you request.

Financial strength matters a great deal in Arkansas. A restricted license needs a CPA compiled report with a balance sheet. An unrestricted license needs a reviewed or audited statement from an independent CPA, with footnotes. Either way, the statement must be dated within the last year, and half of the required net worth must be cash in the bank. The application gives the Building classification as an example with a $50,000 net worth requirement, half in cash, and lists $5,000 for a specialty. Other classifications follow Board rules, so confirm your figure. The Board also accepts a surety bond in lieu of a financial statement, which is separate from the contractor bond.

Every licensee posts a $10,000 contractor surety bond under Arkansas Code 17-25-401. The company name and tax ID on the bond must match your Secretary of State registration exactly, and the bond must come from an Arkansas-licensed agent or a direct surety. If you have one or more employees, you must provide a current workers' compensation certificate.

Arkansas contractor license costs and fees

Here are the published figures we found. Always check the Board and NASCLA sites for current amounts before you pay.

  • Arkansas commercial application filing fee: $100, non-refundable, paid to the Board.
  • Arkansas Business and Law exam through PSI: $84, non-refundable.
  • NASCLA exam application through ned.nascla.org: $65 per application.
  • NASCLA transcript sent to the Board: $45 per agency.
  • Contractor surety bond: $10,000 bond amount (your premium depends on the surety).
  • Late renewal fee listed by the Board: $50 if the renewal is not complete by the expiration date.

Reciprocity and working in other states

Arkansas has reciprocal agreements with Alabama, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina and Tennessee. A qualifier or company holding a current license in one of those states can file the Board's reciprocal form instead of a passing Business and Law score. Alabama, Mississippi and Tennessee licenses must have been held for at least one year, and North Carolina reciprocity is limited to listed equivalent classifications. You still file a full Arkansas application with every other document, and the verifying state must complete its part of the form within 90 days of notarization.

Your NASCLA pass is also portable. Results do not expire, and you can send a transcript to any participating state that accepts the exam. If you plan to work across several states, passing NASCLA once can support applications in each of them, though each state keeps its own business, financial and law requirements.

After you pass: approval, renewal and keeping your license

Once your application is complete, it goes before the Board at a scheduled meeting. Meeting dates are posted on the Board's site, and your packet must be in at least three weeks ahead. The Board looks at experience, financial condition, references and test results before it approves the license.

Arkansas ties your license expiration date to the date of the financial statement you submitted, which is different from many states. At renewal, you file either a current CPA financial statement or a bond in lieu of one, plus the renewal fee. If everything is not in by the expiration date, the Board charges a $50 late fee. Renewal is available online, but missing documents are sent separately by fax or email.

Keep your qualifier in place. If your qualifying party leaves, the company must file a qualifier change and the new person may need to pass the Business and Law exam or use the reciprocal form.

Arkansas NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does Arkansas accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. NASCLA lists the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board as a participating agency, and the Board uses a NASCLA pass in place of appropriate experience shown on the application. You still need to meet every other Arkansas requirement.

Do I still need the Arkansas Business and Law exam if I pass NASCLA?

Yes, in most cases. The Business and Law exam is a separate state requirement for new commercial applicants. The main exception is the reciprocal form for qualifying licensees from Alabama, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina and Tennessee.

How many questions are on the Arkansas Business and Law exam?

The Board's testing notice lists 50 multiple-choice questions with a two-hour time limit. It is open book, and the only allowed reference is the NASCLA Arkansas guide.

When do I need a commercial contractor license in Arkansas?

You need one before bidding or performing commercial work valued at $50,000 or more, including labor and materials. Any project other than a single-family home counts as commercial.

What is the net worth requirement for an Arkansas Building license?

The commercial application uses Building as an example with a $50,000 net worth requirement, and half of it must be cash in the bank. Other classifications differ, so confirm your number with the Board.

How much is the Arkansas contractor bond?

Arkansas requires a $10,000 contractor surety bond. It is separate from any bond you post in lieu of a financial statement.

Which edition of the Arkansas guide do I need?

NASCLA's store currently sells the Arkansas 8th Edition of the Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. Confirm the edition named in your PSI registration before you buy or tab a book.

Can I highlight and tab my books for both exams?

Yes. Both PSI exams allow highlighting and permanent tabs applied before test day. Temporary tabs like sticky notes and loose papers are not allowed.
