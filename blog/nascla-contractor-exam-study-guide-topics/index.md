# What Topics Are Covered in the NASCLA Contractor Exam Study Guides?

The NASCLA contractor exam study guides cover key construction topics including procurement and contracting, general construction requirements, site construction, concrete, metals, mechanical and plumbing systems, wood and composites, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, masonry, doors, windows, glazing, and electrical systems. These cover the full scope of the NASCLA commercial general building contractor exam.

## Introduction to the NASCLA Contractor Exam

### Exam Purpose and States Accepting It

The NASCLA contractor exam is designed to provide a standardized assessment for commercial general building contractors. It is accepted in multiple states, including Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Nevada. Passing this exam is a crucial step to obtaining a contractor license in these states.

### Open-Book Exam Format Overview

This exam is open book, allowing candidates to use approved reference materials that meet PSI candidate bulletin regulations. Approved materials may be highlighted and permanently tabbed to help locate answers quickly during the timed exam period.

### Passing Requirements

To pass, candidates must correctly answer at least 70% of the scored questions within the 330-minute time limit. The exam includes 125 total questions , 115 scored and 10 unscored pilot questions to validate future test items.

## Overview of NASCLA Exam Topics

### Procurement and Contracting Requirements

This category covers laws, contracts, licensing requirements, bidding procedures, safety regulations, and risk management. It's fundamental since contractors must understand how to manage contracts and legal obligations on projects.

### General Requirements

Topics include project management principles, construction plans and specifications, surveying basics, building codes, safety regulations compliance, and environmental considerations.

### Site Construction

Covers land development, earthwork, grading, site utilities installation, drainage systems, erosion control, and temporary structures. Mastery here ensures proper site preparation and infrastructure groundwork.

## Detailed Topic Breakdown

### Concrete

This includes concrete mixes, placement, curing processes, reinforcing steel installation, formwork, and finishes. The study guide emphasizes quality control and structural integrity of concrete components.

### Metals

Covers structural steel, welding procedures, metal framing, fasteners, corrosion protection, and metal detailing. Understanding metal specifications and installation methods is critical for commercial structures.

### Mechanical and Plumbing Systems

Topics span HVAC installation, piping systems, plumbing codes, equipment requirements, and mechanical equipment setup. These ensure compliance with safety and functionality standards for mechanical systems.

### Wood, Plastics, and Composites

This includes framing, sheathing, wood treatment, plastic components, engineered wood products, and composite materials used in construction. Knowledge here supports environmentally sound and cost-effective material use.

### Thermal and Moisture Protection

Guides candidates through insulation types, vapor barriers, waterproofing, roofing systems, flashing, and coatings. Proper thermal and moisture protection is essential for durability and energy efficiency.

### Finishes

Covers interior and exterior finishes such as drywall, painting, flooring, wall coverings, and ceilings. Candidates learn about installation techniques and finish materials characteristics.

### Masonry

Focuses on brick, concrete block, stone masonry, grout, mortar types, reinforcement, and waterproofing of masonry. These topics align with standard construction practices for commercial projects.

### Doors, Windows, and Glazing

Includes door and window types, frames, hardware, installation methods, and glass types used in commercial construction. Candidates must understand performance standards and code compliance.

### Electrical Systems

Topics include electrical wiring, circuits, grounding, lighting, electrical safety, and code requirements. Though a separate NASCLA electrical exam exists, basic electrical knowledge appears in the general contractor exam as well.

## Study Materials and Approved References

### Official Reference Books

The NASCLA exam requires candidates to bring certain approved reference books, such as the International Building Code (IBC), National Electrical Code (NEC), and state-specific amendments. These form the backbone of the open-book exam resources approved for use.

### Permissible Markings and Tabs

PSI regulations allow candidates to apply highlight markings and permanent tabs in their reference materials, but no handwritten notes or additional printed materials may be added. This helps candidates quickly locate important sections during the test.

### Using Highlighted and Tabbed Books in the Exam

Using highlighted and tabbed books significantly reduces time spent searching through codes and standards. This strategy is encouraged by NASCLA prep experts to improve exam efficiency and confidence.

## Study Strategies for the NASCLA Exam

### Tabbing and Indexing Methods

Organizing your reference materials with color-coded tabs and an indexed table of contents enables quick cross-referencing of topics. Many candidates create personalized guides focusing on high-frequency topics like structural concrete or contract laws.

### Time Management During Open-Book Exam

Even with open books, time is tight. Practicing quick lookups and understanding common question formats can ensure you spend less time hunting for answers and more time answering accurately.

### Practice Question Types and Traps

The NASCLA exam uses multiple-choice questions, sometimes with scenario-based problem solving. Watch out for tricky wording or answers that seem correct but overlook code specifics , practicing with real or simulated tests helps develop this skill.
