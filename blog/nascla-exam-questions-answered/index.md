# NASCLA Exam: 15 Questions People Ask, Answered

November 23, 2026 7 min read Max Ace

## Key takeaways

- The NASCLA Commercial General Building exam has 115 scored questions in 330 minutes, is open book and needs 70% to pass.
- It is accepted in 16 states and the U.S. Virgin Islands in place of their own commercial trade exam.
- You apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org and must be approved before you can schedule with PSI.
- Passing replaces the trade exam only; each state still has its own application and usually a business and law exam.

The NASCLA exam is a standardized commercial general building contractor test with 115 scored questions in 330 minutes. It is open book and needs 70 percent to pass. Sixteen states and the U.S. Virgin Islands accept it in place of their own trade exam. Here are the 15 questions contractors search most about it.

## What is the NASCLA exam?

NASCLA is the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies. Its best-known test is the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Instead of taking a different trade exam in every state, you pass this one exam and send the score to each participating state where you want a license. That makes it popular with contractors who bid work across state lines. NASCLA also accredits some electrical and residential exams, but when people say "the NASCLA exam" they usually mean the commercial general building one.

## Which states accept the NASCLA exam?

Sixteen states plus the U.S. Virgin Islands accept the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of their own commercial trade exam. They are Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia and West Virginia. The list can change, and each state decides which license classes the score covers, so check NASCLA's participating agencies page and your state board before you apply.

## How do you apply for the NASCLA exam?

You apply through NASCLA's online system at ned.nascla.org. You create an account, fill out the application, pay the NASCLA fee and wait for approval. Once you are approved, you get the information you need to book your seat with PSI, the testing vendor. You do not have to be licensed in any state to take the exam, but you still need to meet each state's own rules when you apply for the license itself. Start the NASCLA application early, since approval has to come before scheduling.

## How do you schedule and take the NASCLA exam?

After NASCLA approves you, schedule with PSI online or by phone. You pick a PSI test center and a date from what is open. On exam day, bring a government photo ID that matches your registration and the approved reference books. Leave phones, notes and bags in your car or a locker. The exam is on a computer and multiple choice. Plan to arrive early, because checking in and getting your books inspected takes time before the clock starts.

## Where can I take the NASCLA exam?

At a PSI test center. PSI has centers across the country, so you do not have to test in the state where you want a license. A contractor in Texas could sit the exam in Dallas and then send the score to Georgia or Virginia. Pick a center close to home, and check its hours and parking before test day. Because the exam runs more than five hours, a center that is easy to reach makes a long day less stressful.

## How many questions are on the NASCLA exam?

The NASCLA Commercial General Building exam has 115 scored questions. They are multiple choice and come from a published list of construction references. Topics include:

- Contract documents, plans and specifications
- Concrete, masonry and steel
- Carpentry, roofing and finishes
- Site work, excavation and safety
- Project management and scheduling

The candidate information bulletin shows how many questions come from each area. Use it to plan your study time.

## How long is the NASCLA exam?

You get 330 minutes, or five and a half hours. That sounds like plenty of time for 115 questions, but it works out to under three minutes per question, and many questions need a lookup in a thick reference book. Set a pace before you start. A checkpoint every 20 questions helps you see if you are falling behind. If a question takes too long, mark it, move on and come back in a second pass.

## How hard is the NASCLA exam?

NASCLA is hard mostly because of time and the size of the books. The construction ideas are familiar to most contractors, but the questions want the exact answer from a specific reference, and you have to find it fast. People who know the trade but have not practiced looking things up often run out of time. People who tab their books and take timed practice tests find it very passable. The math questions, like concrete volumes and load figures, also catch people who have not practiced them.

## How hard is the NASCLA electrical exam?

The NASCLA accredited electrical exams are built on the National Electrical Code and the references each state allows, so they feel like other open-book electrical licensing exams. Expect code lookups, load calculations, conductor sizing, grounding and motor questions. Like the commercial building exam, time is the main enemy. Tab the code book, learn its layout and drill calculations until they feel routine. Each state lists its own references, so match your books to your state's bulletin before you start.

## How much does the NASCLA exam cost?

There are two parts to the cost. NASCLA charges an application fee when you apply, and PSI charges an exam fee when you schedule your seat. On top of that, budget for the reference books, a prep course or practice exams, travel to the test center and each state's own license application and fees. Fees change, so check the current amounts on NASCLA's and PSI's sites before you apply, and plan for a retake fee just in case.

## How long is the NASCLA exam good for?

Your passing score stays on file with NASCLA, and you can request a transcript to send to each participating state when you apply there. Each state decides how old a score it will accept, so a score that one state takes may be too old for another. If you plan to use it in several states, apply to them soon after you pass. Check each board's rules on score age before you count on an older result.

## How long should you study for the NASCLA exam?

Most working contractors need six to ten weeks. The reference list is long, so spend the first weeks getting to know each book's layout and tabbing it. Then work practice questions topic by topic, writing down the page for every miss. In the last two weeks, take full-length timed practice exams. If you are new to commercial work, or the math feels rusty, give yourself the longer end of that range.

## How do you study for the NASCLA exam?

Study with the same books you will bring in. Get the current reference list from the bulletin, buy or rent the right editions, and tab them so each chapter is one flip away. Highlighted and tabbed sets save a lot of time. An online course or AI tutor can explain why an answer is right and where it lives in the book. Keep an error log of the pages you miss, and drill those sections until you can find them without thinking.

## How do you pass the NASCLA exam?

Pass by managing the clock. Answer the questions you know first and flag the long lookups for later. Never leave a question blank, because there is no penalty for a wrong answer. Use each book's index instead of flipping through chapters. Keep your pace near three minutes per question. And take at least two full-length timed practice exams before test day, so 330 minutes feels normal instead of rushed.

## Does passing NASCLA give you a license?

No. Passing replaces the trade exam only. You still apply for a license with each state, meet its experience, financial and insurance rules, and pay its fees. Most states also require their own business and law exam, often written from NASCLA's Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management in the edition for that state. Plan for that second exam from the start, since it is part of getting licensed in almost every participating state.

## Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to a few more questions people ask about the exam and license.

### Is the NASCLA exam open book?

Yes. You can bring the approved references listed in the candidate bulletin. Tabs and highlighting are usually allowed.

### What score do you need to pass the NASCLA exam?

You need 70 percent. Your score report shows pass or fail and how you did in each content area.

### Do I need a license to take the NASCLA exam?

No. You apply through NASCLA and can take the exam before you hold any state license. Each state's own licensing rules still apply afterward.

### Can I use NASCLA in Florida?

Yes. Florida has accepted the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of its trade exam since March 1, 2019. You still pass Florida Business and Finance.

### Who gives the NASCLA exam?

PSI gives the exam at its test centers, after NASCLA approves your application.

### Does NASCLA have a business and law book?

Yes. NASCLA publishes the Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, with an edition for each state.
