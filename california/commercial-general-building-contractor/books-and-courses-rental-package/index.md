## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination used by participating contractor licensing agencies. It is designed to evaluate knowledge associated with commercial general building construction and project administration.

The examination covers multiple construction divisions rather than concentrating on one specialty. Candidates should be prepared to work with material related to general requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, openings, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, plumbing, electrical systems, construction safety, project management, procurement, and contracting.

The breadth of the examination makes a balanced preparation plan important. A candidate with strong experience in one construction area may still need additional review in subjects such as accessibility, concrete standards, steel erection, stormwater controls, roofing calculations, project administration, mechanical systems, or electrical coordination.

Passing the NASCLA examination allows candidates to send their examination transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database to participating licensing agencies. Each licensing agency applies its own rules and determines whether the examination can be used for a specific classification or licensing pathway.

California recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for a limited General Building trade-exam waiver pathway. The waiver applies to qualifying applicants who have held a general building contractor license, or a comparable license, in good standing in another state for the previous five years and who apply for the California B General Building classification.

The NASCLA result does not automatically issue a California contractor license. Applicants must still complete the Contractors State License Board application process and meet all applicable experience, examination, business, background, bonding, insurance, and licensing requirements. California applicants who do not qualify for the NASCLA waiver pathway must complete the examinations required by the Contractors State License Board.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted reference books to the testing location and following the current rules for tabs, highlighting, markings, bindings, notes, and other materials.

Open-book testing does not mean candidates can search every answer without preparation. The reference library contains thousands of pages. Efficient testing requires candidates to recognize the construction subject, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the applicable section, and interpret the information within the available time.

The highlighted and tabbed rental references help candidates create a practical navigation system. Tabs can identify important chapters, code divisions, and construction topics. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, requirements, procedures, tables, formulas, and technical concepts.

Candidates should use the books throughout the six-month access period. This approach reinforces technical knowledge while helping candidates remember where important information is located.

Testing rules can limit removable notes, loose papers, temporary tabs, handwritten inserts, or other additions. Candidates should review the current NASCLA candidate bulletin before the examination and inspect every reference to ensure that the books comply with test-center requirements.

## State Requirements

California contractor licensing is administered by the Contractors State License Board. The CSLB issues licenses in multiple classifications, including the B General Building classification.

California began recognizing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for reciprocity purposes in 2025. This recognition does not create a general examination waiver for every NASCLA candidate. It provides a specific pathway for contractors who satisfy California’s reciprocity conditions.

To request the NASCLA trade-examination waiver, an applicant must apply for the California B General Building classification, have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination, and have held a comparable general building contractor license in good standing in another state for the previous five years.

The applicant must submit the California original contractor license application, include a signed request for the NASCLA trade-exam waiver, arrange for verification of the qualifying out-of-state license, and share the NASCLA transcript with California.

The NASCLA waiver applies to the qualifying trade-examination requirement. It does not remove the applicant’s responsibility to meet California’s remaining licensing conditions. The CSLB may require additional documentation and retains authority over the final examination-waiver and licensing decision.

Applicants who do not qualify for the waiver must follow the regular California examination process. The applicable California state examination requirements depend on the license classification and application pathway.

California contractor applicants must also address qualifying experience, business structure, fingerprinting, background review, contractor bond, workers’ compensation obligations, application fees, and other issuance requirements. A passing examination result alone does not authorize a person or business to contract in California.

Local governments may impose separate business-license, building-permit, zoning, inspection, tax, and registration requirements. A California contractor license does not replace approvals required by cities, counties, building departments, project owners, or other authorities.
