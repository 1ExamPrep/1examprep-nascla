## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It addresses knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects.

The examination covers a broad range of subjects represented by the books in this set. Candidates should be prepared to review jobsite management, project management, estimating, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast concrete, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, exterior insulation systems, stormwater controls, green construction, and mechanical and electrical systems.

After passing, a candidate may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official result to participating licensing agencies. Each agency applies its own licensing laws, classifications, financial requirements, experience standards, examinations, and application procedures.

California’s Contractors State License Board recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for qualifying out-of-state applicants seeking a waiver of the B – General Building trade examination. The waiver is not automatic. The applicant must request it as part of the California application and satisfy the conditions established by CSLB.

A qualifying applicant must hold an out-of-state commercial building contractor license in good standing for the required period and have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. CSLB retains authority over the waiver decision and may require additional information or examinations.

The NASCLA examination does not replace California’s Law and Business examination when that examination is required. It also does not replace experience verification, fingerprinting, bonding, insurance, application, business-entity, personnel, or license-issuance requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete book library.

Open-book testing requires strong subject recognition and reference-navigation skills. The candidate must read the question carefully, identify the construction topic, choose the most relevant publication, and locate the supporting information within the available examination period.

A concrete question may require ACI 318, the quality concrete guide, or the reinforcing-bar reference. A structural-system question may involve the wood-truss guide, precast erection manual, post-tensioning publication, steel-deck manual, or steel-joist digest. Accessibility and general code questions may require ICC A117.1 or the International Building Code.

Candidates should practice directly with the listed editions. Page numbers, chapter organization, code provisions, definitions, and technical requirements can change between editions. Using the correct books during preparation helps the candidate build familiarity with the same reference structure expected for the examination.

All materials brought into the examination room must comply with the current testing rules. Candidates should review the applicable NASCLA candidate bulletin before test day and avoid loose papers, unauthorized inserts, removable notes, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine whether the California NASCLA waiver pathway applies.** The pathway is intended for qualifying out-of-state contractors who have passed the NASCLA commercial building examination and meet CSLB’s licensing and experience conditions.
2. **Confirm the appropriate California classification.** The comparable California classification is B – General Building Contractor.
3. **Review the qualifying experience requirement.** California applicants must document the experience required for the requested contractor classification.
4. **Complete the NASCLA examination process.** Candidates must apply for, schedule, and pass the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
5. **Maintain the eligible out-of-state license.** The contractor must hold the qualifying license in good standing for the period required by CSLB.
6. **Submit the California contractor license application.** The applicant must provide the required business, personnel, classification, experience, and supporting information.
7. **Request the NASCLA trade-examination waiver.** The application must include the required signed request for waiver consideration.
8. **Share the NASCLA result with California.** The applicant must use the NASCLA account and examination database to transmit the official result to CSLB.
9. **Complete the California Law and Business examination when required.** A NASCLA trade-exam waiver does not automatically waive the California Law and Business examination.
10. **Complete the remaining issuance requirements.** These may include fingerprinting, bonding, insurance documentation, fees, and other materials required for the applicant’s business structure and license.

## State Requirements

The California Contractors State License Board licenses and regulates contractors operating in the state. A contractor must hold the appropriate California license before performing work that requires licensure. An out-of-state license or NASCLA examination result does not independently authorize contracting in California.

The California B – General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves structures built for the support, shelter, and enclosure of persons, animals, property, or movable items when the construction requires the use of at least two unrelated building trades or crafts.

California began recognizing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for an expanded reciprocity and trade-waiver pathway in 2025. The pathway allows qualifying contractors licensed outside California to request waiver consideration for the B – General Building trade examination.

The applicant must have passed the NASCLA examination and held the eligible out-of-state commercial building contractor license in good standing for at least five years. The applicant must also complete California’s original-license application and submit a signed request for the NASCLA examination waiver.

The official NASCLA result must be shared with California through the NASCLA examination database. CSLB evaluates the application and determines whether the trade-examination waiver is approved. The Board retains the right to require an examination when appropriate.

Applicants must still satisfy California requirements that are not covered by the waiver. These may include the Law and Business examination, qualifying experience, business and personnel disclosures, fingerprinting, contractor bond, workers’ compensation documentation, application fees, and initial license requirements.

California residents who do not qualify through the out-of-state NASCLA waiver pathway generally must complete the California state-specific B – General Building trade examination. Candidates should choose study products that correspond with the examination and licensing pathway that applies to their circumstances.
