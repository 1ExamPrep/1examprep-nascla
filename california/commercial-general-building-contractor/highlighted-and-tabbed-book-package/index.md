## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It addresses the broad knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects.

The examination covers subjects represented throughout this book set, including construction jobsite management, project management, estimating, contracts, safety, concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, wood trusses, steel deck, steel joists, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, exterior insulation and finish systems, stormwater planning, green building, and mechanical and electrical systems.

Passing candidates can transmit an official examination result to participating licensing agencies through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The NASCLA result may satisfy a participating jurisdiction’s technical trade-examination requirement, but every licensing agency maintains its own application, experience, financial, business, and state-examination requirements.

California recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for qualifying applicants seeking a waiver of the B – General Building trade examination. The applicant must meet the California Contractors State License Board’s waiver conditions, complete the original license application, request the waiver, and share the NASCLA examination result with California.

The California waiver pathway applies to qualifying applicants who hold a comparable out-of-state general building contractor license in good standing and have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. CSLB reviews the application and determines whether the trade-examination waiver is approved.

A NASCLA result does not automatically issue a California contractor license. Applicants must complete all remaining licensing requirements, including any required California Law and Business examination, experience verification, fingerprinting, bonding, insurance documentation, application forms, and license-issuance steps.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing approved reference books to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete reference library.

Open-book testing requires strong reference-navigation skills. Candidates must read each question, determine the primary construction subject, choose the most appropriate publication, locate the supporting information, and select the best answer within the available testing period.

The size of the library makes advance preparation essential. A question about structural concrete may require ACI 318, while a question about reinforcing-bar placement may rely on Placing Reinforcing Bars. Steel construction questions may involve the steel-deck manual or the steel-joist technical digest. Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial code questions may rely on the International Building Code.

The highlighted and tabbed format helps candidates move more efficiently between major chapters and subject areas. Highlighting identifies useful information, and permanent tabs create clear reference points throughout the books. Candidates should still practice using indexes, tables of contents, definitions, code sections, and technical tables before examination day.

All books taken into the examination room must comply with current testing rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate information bulletin before test day and avoid adding loose pages, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## State Requirements

The California Contractors State License Board regulates contractors operating in California. An out-of-state license or passing NASCLA result does not independently authorize a contractor to perform work in the state. The applicant must receive the appropriate California license before performing work that requires licensure.

The B – General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal contracting business involves structures built for the support, shelter, and enclosure of persons, animals, property, or movable items when construction requires at least two unrelated building trades or crafts.

Qualifying out-of-state contractors who have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination may request a waiver of the California B – General Building trade examination. The applicant must have held a comparable out-of-state general building contractor license in good standing for the previous five years.

The contractor must complete California’s Application for Original Contractor License and include a signed and dated statement requesting the NASCLA Commercial General Builders examination waiver. The applicant must also request that the official NASCLA examination result be shared with California.

CSLB reviews the license verification, application, experience documentation, NASCLA result, and waiver request. Approval is determined by California, and the Board may require additional information when reviewing an application.

The waiver applies to the eligible trade examination and does not automatically waive the California Law and Business examination. Applicants must also complete all other requirements that apply to the individual, qualifying person, and business entity.

California residents who do not qualify under the NASCLA waiver pathway generally follow the state-specific examination process for the B – General Building classification. Candidates should select preparation materials that match the examination pathway applicable to their licensing situation.
