Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It is designed to measure knowledge associated with planning, managing, supervising, and completing commercial building projects.

The examination covers subjects represented throughout the listed reference library. Candidates may encounter questions involving jobsite management, project planning, cost control, contracts, safety, building codes, accessibility, concrete, reinforcing steel, precast systems, post-tensioning, structural metals, wood trusses, masonry, carpentry, roofing, excavation, environmental controls, exterior insulation systems, and building systems.

The online exam prep course helps candidates break this broad content into focused study categories. Instead of treating the examination as one large subject, students can review management, safety, codes, site work, structural systems, building-envelope topics, specialty construction, and mechanical and electrical coordination separately.

After passing the NASCLA examination, candidates can transmit their official examination result to participating licensing agencies through the NASCLA National Examination Database. Each licensing agency applies its own rules, and candidates must complete the application process required by the state where they are seeking a license.

California recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for certain out-of-state applicants applying for the B – General Building classification. A qualifying applicant must have passed the NASCLA examination and held a comparable out-of-state general building contractor license in good standing for the previous five years.

The applicant must complete California’s original contractor license application, submit the required verification of the existing out-of-state license, include a signed request for the NASCLA trade-examination waiver, and share the NASCLA result with California through the examination database.

CSLB reviews the application and determines whether the applicant qualifies for waiver of the B – General Building trade examination. California retains the authority to require the trade examination when appropriate. The applicant must still complete the California Law and Business examination unless a separate waiver applies.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing all permitted reference books to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the reference library.

The open-book format allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not remove the need for detailed preparation. The library contains thousands of pages across numerous construction books, codes, standards, manuals, and technical guides. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the references may spend too much time searching and leave questions unanswered.

A practical open-book method begins by identifying the subject of the question. The candidate then selects the reference most likely to contain the answer and uses the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, or technical tables to locate the relevant material.

For example, a structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question about jobsite concrete quality may be found in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. A reinforcing-bar placement question may require Placing Reinforcing Bars. A steel construction question could involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders.

Accessibility questions may rely on ICC A117.1, while broader building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, while safety questions may be answered through 29 CFR Part 1926.

The online course helps students practice these distinctions and build a repeatable navigation process. Candidates should use the listed reference editions during preparation because page numbers, code requirements, terminology, and book organization can change between editions.

All materials used in the testing room must comply with the current NASCLA and testing-provider rules. Candidates should review the current candidate bulletin before preparing their books and avoid loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine whether the NASCLA pathway fits your licensing goal. California uses the NASCLA commercial building examination as part of a trade-exam waiver pathway for qualifying out-of-state contractors.
  2. Confirm the applicable California classification. The related California classification is B – General Building Contractor.
  3. Review the California reciprocity requirements. The applicant must hold a comparable out-of-state general building license in good standing for the required period.
  4. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Candidates must complete the NASCLA application and receive approval before scheduling the commercial building examination.
  5. Study the examination references. Use the online course with the listed books to review trade content and practice open-book navigation.
  6. Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. The passing result is placed in the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  7. Complete the California original license application. Submit the required business, personnel, classification, and experience information to CSLB.
  8. Provide verification of the out-of-state license. The licensing agency that issued the existing license must complete the required verification documentation.
  9. Request the NASCLA examination waiver. Include a signed and dated statement requesting waiver of the B – General Building trade examination.
  10. Share the NASCLA result with California. Use the NASCLA account and database to transmit the official examination result to CSLB.
  11. Complete the California Law and Business examination when required. The NASCLA waiver applies to the qualifying trade examination, not automatically to Law and Business.
  12. Complete the remaining issuance requirements. These may include fingerprinting, bonding, insurance documentation, fees, and other materials required for the license and business structure.

State Requirements

The California Contractors State License Board licenses and regulates contractors working in the state. A NASCLA examination result or an out-of-state contractor license does not independently authorize the holder to contract in California.

The California B – General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves structures built for the support, shelter, and enclosure of persons, animals, property, or movable items when construction requires at least two unrelated building trades or crafts.

California permits qualifying out-of-state contractors to request a waiver of the B – General Building trade examination when they have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination and meet CSLB’s reciprocity conditions.

The applicant must have held a comparable general building contractor license in good standing for the previous five years. The existing license must be verified by the licensing entity that issued it.

The contractor must complete California’s Application for Original Contractor License and submit a signed request for the NASCLA examination waiver. The applicant must also authorize the NASCLA examination result to be shared with California through the NASCLA National Examination Database.

CSLB evaluates the application, license verification, experience information, waiver request, and NASCLA result. The Board determines whether the applicant qualifies for the trade-examination waiver and retains the right to require an examination.

The waiver does not automatically remove the California Law and Business examination requirement. Applicants must also satisfy the remaining California licensing requirements, which may include qualifying experience, fingerprinting, contractor bonding, workers’ compensation documentation, business disclosures, application fees, and license-issuance requirements.

California applicants who do not qualify for the NASCLA waiver pathway generally follow the state-specific examination process for the B – General Building classification. Candidates should select exam-preparation materials that match the pathway applicable to their licensing situation.
