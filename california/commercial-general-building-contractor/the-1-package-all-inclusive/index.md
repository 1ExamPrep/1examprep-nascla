## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. California's Contractors State License Board recognizes the NASCLA commercial examination for a limited B-General Building trade-exam waiver pathway.

The California NASCLA pathway is not a replacement for every California licensing requirement. To qualify for the B-General Building NASCLA trade-exam waiver, an applicant must be seeking the California B-General Building classification, must have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building examination, and must have served as the qualifier of an equivalent out-of-state general building contractor license in good standing for at least the previous five years.

The applicant must also arrange for the out-of-state licensing agency to verify the qualifying license and must have the NASCLA examination transcript released to California. CSLB reviews the application and determines whether the applicant satisfies the waiver requirements.

The NASCLA examination covers a wide range of commercial construction subjects, including:

- General construction requirements
- Planning and estimating
- Project and financial management
- Construction safety
- Site construction
- Concrete construction
- Masonry
- Metals
- Wood construction
- Thermal and moisture protection
- Doors, windows, and glazing
- Finishes
- Mechanical and plumbing systems
- Electrical systems
- Procurement and contracting requirements

California applicants who qualify for the NASCLA trade-exam waiver must still complete the California Law and Business examination unless an independent waiver applies. The Law and Business examination addresses California-specific construction law and business-management topics and is separate from the NASCLA commercial trade examination.

## Licensing Steps

California contractor licensing is administered by the Contractors State License Board. Contractors pursuing the B-General Building classification through the NASCLA pathway must meet both the general CSLB licensing requirements and the specific NASCLA trade-exam waiver criteria.

1. **Confirm eligibility for the NASCLA pathway.** The applicant must be seeking the California B-General Building classification, have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building examination, and have been the qualifier of an equivalent general building contractor license in another state in good standing for at least the previous five years.
2. **Meet California experience requirements.** The qualifying individual must generally document at least four years of qualifying journey-level, foreman, supervising employee, contractor, or owner-builder experience within the ten years immediately before the application.
3. **Submit the Application for Original Contractor License.** Applicants must complete the appropriate CSLB application and identify the qualifying individual for the license.
4. **Request out-of-state license verification.** The applicable licensing authority must verify the equivalent general building contractor license and its good-standing history.
5. **Request the NASCLA transcript release.** The applicant must have the NASCLA examination results shared with California.
6. **Request the B-General Building NASCLA trade-exam waiver.** CSLB evaluates whether the applicant satisfies the requirements for waiver of the California B trade examination.
7. **Complete the California Law and Business examination.** The trade-exam waiver does not eliminate the California Law and Business examination unless the applicant independently qualifies for another examination waiver.
8. **Complete remaining CSLB licensing requirements.** Applicants must satisfy the applicable bond, fingerprinting, business, and other licensing requirements before the license can be issued.

## State Requirements

The California Contractors State License Board regulates contractor licensing throughout the state. Contractors performing covered construction work generally must hold the appropriate CSLB license when the total contract cost, including labor and materials, is **$1,000 or more**, or when the project requires a building permit or uses additional workers.

The California **B-General Building Contractor** classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves structures requiring the use of at least two unrelated building trades or crafts. A B-General Building Contractor may also perform or subcontract work within the scope established by California law.

To qualify for a California contractor license, the qualifying individual must generally be at least **18 years old** and possess the experience and skills required to manage the daily activities of a construction business, including field supervision.

California generally requires at least four years of qualifying experience in the classification within the ten years immediately preceding the application. Experience may include work at the journey level or service as a foreman, supervising employee, contractor, or owner-builder when properly documented.

California's NASCLA reciprocity pathway is specifically designed for experienced out-of-state general building contractors. The applicant must have held an equivalent general building contractor license in good standing for at least the previous five years and must have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building examination.

The NASCLA waiver applies to the B-General Building **trade examination**. It does not eliminate the standard California Law and Business examination requirement for applicants who do not independently qualify for a separate examination waiver.
