Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Candidates who pass can request that their examination transcript be sent through the NASCLA National Examination Database to agencies that accept the exam.

The examination is used as a trade-exam pathway and does not create a contractor license by itself. Every participating jurisdiction applies its own application, experience, business, financial, insurance, background, classification, and state-examination requirements.

The NASCLA commercial building examination contains 125 multiple-choice questions. This total includes 115 scored questions and up to 10 unscored pretest questions. Candidates should prepare for broad coverage across commercial construction rather than expecting the examination to focus on a single trade.

Major knowledge areas include general construction requirements, procurement, contracting, project management, jobsite management, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, doors and windows, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, plumbing systems, electrical systems, safety, and code-related construction practices.

The variety of subjects makes balanced preparation important. A candidate with extensive experience in framing or concrete may still need additional review in accessibility standards, steel erection, roofing calculations, stormwater controls, project administration, mechanical systems, or construction safety.

The included trade course and live classes help candidates organize these topics into a practical study sequence. The reference books can be used alongside lessons and practice questions so candidates become familiar with both the subject matter and the location of information in the approved publications.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references to the testing location and complying with current examination rules governing books, tabs, highlighting, bindings, markings, and other materials.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for preparation. The complete reference library contains thousands of pages, and there may not be enough time to search several books for every question. Candidates should learn which reference applies to each major subject before test day.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books help create an organized navigation system. Tabs can make important chapters, code sections, construction divisions, and technical subjects easier to locate. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, procedures, formulas, requirements, tables, and reference points.

Candidates should practice using indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, code sections, diagrams, and technical tables. When completing practice questions, first identify the subject, then select the reference most likely to contain the answer. After finding the information, review the surrounding section to understand the topic in context.

Current testing rules may restrict loose paper, temporary notes, removable inserts, handwritten additions, or certain types of tabs. Candidates should inspect every rental reference before the examination and follow the rules provided for the scheduled testing appointment.

State Requirements

California contractor licensing is administered by the Contractors State License Board. The CSLB issues licenses in multiple classifications, including the B General Building classification.

A B General Building contractor generally works on structures intended for the support, shelter, or enclosure of persons, animals, personal property, or movable property when the project involves at least two unrelated building trades or crafts. The classification also allows the contractor to perform or supervise work within the scope established by California law.

California recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination through a reciprocity pathway approved in 2025. The pathway is not an automatic waiver for every person who passes the NASCLA examination.

To request the B General Building trade-exam waiver through NASCLA, an applicant must have passed the NASCLA commercial building examination and must have held a comparable general building contractor license in good standing in another state for the previous five years.

The applicant must submit the original California contractor license application, include a signed and dated statement requesting the NASCLA examination waiver, arrange for verification of the existing out-of-state license, and request that the NASCLA transcript be sent to California.

The waiver applies only to the qualifying trade-examination requirement. The applicant remains responsible for California’s other licensing conditions, including any required law and business examination, qualifying experience, background review, fingerprinting, contractor bond, workers’ compensation responsibilities, business information, and application fees.

The Contractors State License Board retains authority over the application and examination-waiver decision. Applicants who do not meet the NASCLA reciprocity requirements must complete the regular California examination process for the classification requested.

State licensure does not replace local business licenses, building permits, planning approvals, inspections, zoning requirements, or project-specific authorizations. Contractors remain responsible for complying with the requirements imposed by cities, counties, building departments, project owners, and other authorities.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin by organizing the references by construction discipline. Keep project-management publications together and separate the books covering concrete, masonry, structural steel, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, excavation, safety, codes, and building systems.

Use the trade course with the rental books open. When a lesson introduces a subject, locate the related chapter, code provision, table, formula, or technical illustration. Reviewing the surrounding section can make the material easier to recognize when it appears in a practice question.

Complete timed practice that requires both technical knowledge and reference navigation. Before opening a book, decide which publication is most likely to contain the answer. This helps reduce time spent searching unrelated references.

Use live classes to reinforce difficult topics, improve pacing, and maintain a consistent preparation schedule. After each session, return to the applicable books and complete additional practice while the material is fresh.

Study across the entire commercial building outline rather than concentrating only on familiar construction activities. Balanced preparation should include project management, safety, concrete, masonry, steel construction, wood framing, roofing, accessibility, excavation, mechanical systems, electrical systems, stormwater controls, procurement, and contracting.
