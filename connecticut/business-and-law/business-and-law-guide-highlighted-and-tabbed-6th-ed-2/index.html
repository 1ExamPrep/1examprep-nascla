Test Information and Study Materials

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first section introduces the decisions involved in organizing and establishing a construction business. It addresses business planning, choosing a business structure, understanding licensing and insurance, and developing basic management and marketing skills.

Business Planning

Business planning helps contractors define objectives, identify necessary resources, and organize company operations. Understanding business planning principles supports informed decisions about construction services and company development.

Business Structures

Choosing a business structure affects company organization and administrative responsibilities. Study the considerations involved in establishing an appropriate structure for a contracting business.

Licensing and Registration

Licensing and registration establish requirements for regulated construction work. Contractors benefit from understanding which credentials apply to their activities and the importance of maintaining appropriate authorization.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction operations involve financial, contractual, and workplace risks. Understanding insurance and risk management concepts helps contractors recognize potential exposures and business responsibilities.

Business Management and Marketing

Effective business management requires planning, organization, communication, and consistent decision-making. Marketing helps contractors present their services and build professional customer relationships.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section focuses on the activities involved in operating a construction business and coordinating construction projects.

Construction Estimating

Estimating requires evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor expenses, and other project costs. Understanding estimating principles supports organized budgets and construction proposals.

Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities among contractors, customers, and other project participants. Study agreements, documentation, contract administration, and the importance of clear communication.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates employees, subcontractors, materials, equipment, and construction activities. Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize work and monitor progress.

Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and communication. These skills help construction professionals coordinate resources and manage project responsibilities.

Construction Safety

Safety is an important part of construction operations. Study workplace hazard awareness, employee responsibilities, and organized jobsite management practices.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction activities may involve environmental considerations involving materials, waste, and site conditions. Understanding these principles supports responsible project administration.

Employee Management

Managing employees involves communication, supervision, and understanding workplace responsibilities. These principles support organized construction company operations.

Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform specialized work on many projects. Effective coordination requires scheduling, documentation, clear agreements, and consistent communication.

Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on accurate expectations, clear project communication, and organized documentation.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section addresses financial and administrative responsibilities associated with operating a construction company.

Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors monitor business income, expenses, project costs, and available resources. Understanding these concepts supports informed management decisions.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides organized records of financial transactions. Accurate records help contractors monitor project costs and understand company financial performance.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of money entering and leaving a business. Contractors benefit from understanding how project payments, payroll, materials, and operating expenses affect available funds.

Tax Fundamentals

Taxes are part of construction business administration. Understanding general tax concepts and maintaining accurate records are important management responsibilities.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights. Studying lien principles helps contractors understand the importance of agreements, payment administration, and recordkeeping.
