About NASCLA

What is NASCLA?

NASCLA is the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies. It brings state licensing boards together and runs an accredited examination program, so a contractor can pass one standardized exam and use the result when applying for a license in each state that accepts it.

What is the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors?

It is a standardized commercial building exam that participating states accept in place of their own trade exam. It covers commercial building construction knowledge such as regulations, best practices and terminology. Passing it once lets you send the same score to every accepting state where you want to work.

Which states accept the NASCLA commercial building exam?

Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Each board sets its own rules for how the score is used, and the list can change, so check NASCLA's participating agencies page and your state board before you apply.

Does NASCLA have electrical exams too?

Yes. NASCLA also accredits trade examinations for electrical licenses, such as master electrical contractor, journeyman electrician and residential electrical contractor exams, in the states that participate. Which electrical exams a state accepts is listed on that state's pages on this site and on NASCLA's own site.

What is the Business and Law exam?

Most states also require a business and law exam alongside the trade exam. It is usually written from NASCLA's Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management in the edition for your state. We carry each state edition, with tabs or highlighted and tabbed, under Business and Law on the state pages.

The commercial exam

How many questions are on the NASCLA commercial exam?

115 scored questions, with a time limit of 330 minutes, and you need 70 percent to pass. The exam can also include a small number of unscored pretest questions, so the total you see may be higher.

Is the NASCLA exam open book?

Yes. You bring the approved reference books. Because every answer is somewhere in the references, the skill being tested is finding it quickly, which is why tabbed and highlighted books save so much time.

How long do I get per question?

330 minutes for 115 scored questions works out to a little under three minutes per question, and a bit less if your exam includes unscored pretest items. Timed practice is the best way to learn that pace before exam day.

How do I register for the NASCLA exam?

Apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org. You must be pre-approved by NASCLA before you can schedule a seat.

Can I use older editions of the reference books?

Use the editions named in the current NASCLA candidate information. Questions are written to specific editions, and older books can have different page numbers and content. Our book packages are matched to the current list.

Licensing

Does passing the NASCLA exam give me a license?

No. Passing replaces the trade exam in states that accept it. You still apply to each state board and meet its other requirements, which can include a business and law exam, experience, financial statements, insurance and fees.

Can I use one NASCLA score in more than one state?

Yes, that is the point of the program. Your passing record is kept in NASCLA's national database, and you can have it sent to each accepting state where you apply.

Do states have extra rules for NASCLA scores?

Some do. For example, Florida accepts NASCLA scores for its Residential, Building and General Contractor classifications, Utah accepts the exam toward its experience requirement for general contractors, and West Virginia accepts scores from exams passed after November 1, 2013. Always confirm the current rule with the state board.

Who decides whether I am eligible to test or get licensed?

NASCLA approves you to sit the exam, and each state licensing board decides on your license. Buying prep materials does not affect eligibility.
