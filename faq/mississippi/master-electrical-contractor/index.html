The exam

Does passing the NASCLA examination create a national license?

No. Candidates must apply for and satisfy the licensing requirements of each jurisdiction where they intend to perform regulated electrical contracting work.

Are all the books highlighted and tabbed?

Yes. Every included reference is professionally highlighted and tabbed to support organized study and efficient open-book examination navigation.

Does renting the books guarantee a passing score or contractor license?

No. Examination and licensing results depend on the candidate’s experience, preparation, technical understanding, performance, complete application, and approval by the applicable licensing authority.

How should I study with the prepared books?

Review every tab, study the highlighted sections, practice NEC navigation, complete calculations, learn the purpose of each reference, and perform timed lookup exercises.

Does purchasing the books guarantee a passing score?

No. Examination results depend on the candidate’s electrical experience, preparation, technical understanding, reference-navigation skills, and performance on test day.

Does passing the examination automatically issue a Mississippi contractor license?

No. Passing the trade examination is one part of the licensing process. The state application, Business and Law examination, supporting documentation, and final approval requirements must also be completed.

How should I study with the reference books?

Learn the purpose of every book, review the indexes and tables of contents, practice NEC navigation, complete calculations, and perform timed reference searches.

Can I bring the books into the examination room?

Candidates must follow the current examination bulletin for permitted references, editions, preparation methods, and testing-center requirements.

Licensing

Can I use a NASCLA examination result for Mississippi licensing?

A passing NASCLA result may satisfy the trade-examination requirement when accepted by Mississippi, but the applicant must still complete the state application and remaining licensing requirements.
