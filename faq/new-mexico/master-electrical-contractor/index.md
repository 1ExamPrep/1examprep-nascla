## The exam

### Does Passing NASCLA Automatically Give Me a New Mexico EE98 License?

No. Passing the NASCLA examination can satisfy the accepted trade-examination component, but candidates must still complete the applicable New Mexico qualifying-party, Business and Law, licensing, and application requirements.

Study the electrical concepts first and practice using the references throughout your preparation. Learn the NEC structure, indexes, tables, common articles, and supporting references. Practice timed lookups and review missed questions so you become more comfortable choosing the correct book and locating information efficiently.

### Are all rental books highlighted and tabbed?

### Does passing the NASCLA examination automatically issue a New Mexico contractor license?

No. Passing the examination can satisfy an accepted examination component, but applicants must still complete New Mexico's Qualifying Party, Business and Law, contractor application, bonding, business-registration, and other licensing requirements.

### Does a New Mexico Qualifying Party need to pass Business and Law?

Yes. Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy the applicable classification examination requirements and the New Mexico Contractor Business and Law requirement.

### Can the NASCLA electrical reference books be highlighted and tabbed?

The NASCLA open-book format permits candidates to use approved references prepared in accordance with current examination rules. This package is highlighted and tabbed to support organized study and navigation.

### How many questions are on the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam?

The examination contains 100 scored questions. NASCLA also uses 10 non-scored experimental questions for examination development. Experimental questions do not count toward the candidate's final score.

### What score is required to pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam?

The minimum passing score for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is 70.

### How much time is allowed for the NASCLA electrical exam?

## Licensing

### Does New Mexico accept the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination?

Yes. The New Mexico Construction Industries Division participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway.

### What Is Contractor Compliance Guidance?

Contractor Compliance Guidance provides assistance understanding requirements and responsibilities associated with operating a New Mexico contracting business.

### How much experience is required for the EE-98 classification?

The EE-98 classification requires four years of qualifying experience. New Mexico requires qualifying-party experience to meet the documentation and recency requirements established by the Construction Industries Division.

### How recent must Qualifying Party experience be?

New Mexico requires the qualifying experience to be within the ten years immediately preceding the Qualifying Party application.

### Does New Mexico require Business and Law?

Yes. A qualifying-party applicant must satisfy the Contractor's Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable trade-examination requirement. New Mexico also permits an approved Business and Law course as an alternative to the examination under the state's licensing process.

### What New Mexico license is associated with the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam?

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the trade exam for the EE-98 electrical contractor license pathway.

### Does New Mexico require prior licensing to use the NASCLA pathway?

Yes. New Mexico's current NASCLA participating-agency requirements state that candidates must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year.

### Who regulates electrical contractors in New Mexico?

Electrical contractor licensing is administered by the New Mexico Construction Industries Division within the Regulation and Licensing Department. Electrical-code enforcement and electrical licensing matters are handled through the Division's Electrical Bureau.

### Does New Mexico require Business and Law in addition to the trade examination?

Yes. Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy the New Mexico Contractor Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable classification examination requirement.

### Is the EE-98 contractor classification the same as the EE-98J journeyman certificate?

No. The EE-98 contractor classification is associated with the licensed contracting business and Qualifying Party structure, while EE-98J is an individual residential and commercial electrical journeyman certificate.

### Does New Mexico accept the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam?

Yes. The New Mexico Construction Industries Division is a participating NASCLA agency and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 licensing pathway, subject to New Mexico's additional licensing requirements.

### Does New Mexico require previous licensing for the NASCLA acceptance route?

New Mexico's NASCLA participating-agency information states that candidates using this pathway must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for one year.

## More questions

### Is there an additional requirement for using the NASCLA exam in New Mexico?

Yes. Candidates using New Mexico's NASCLA acceptance pathway must provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year.

### How long should I allow for my rental package order?

Please allow up to 15 business days for book & course rental package orders.

### What is the New Mexico EE-98 classification?

EE-98 is New Mexico's Residential and Commercial Electrical classification covering electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less within the scope established by state classification rules.

### Does New Mexico require a qualifying party?

Yes. New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualified business entities that employ or are owned by one or more qualifying parties certified for the classification in which the contractor intends to operate.

### Is EE-98 the same as an individual journeyman electrician certificate?

No. The EE-98 contractor classification and the EE-98J journeyman certificate are separate credentials. New Mexico regulates contractor licensing and journeyman certification under different requirements.

### Can a NASCLA electrical exam result be used in other states?

Yes. A passing NASCLA electrical examination result can be used with participating state agencies that accept the applicable examination, subject to the individual licensing requirements of each jurisdiction. The NASCLA examination itself is not a national license.

### Why combine live virtual training with self-study?

Live instruction provides structured technical review, while self-study gives candidates additional time to repeat calculations, practice NEC navigation, review safety references, and reinforce difficult subjects between scheduled classes.

### What areas are most heavily tested?

General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each contain 17 scored questions. Wiring Methods and Materials contains 16 questions, and Equipment for General Use contains 13. Electrical Theory, Safety, Project Design and Management, Special Occupancies and Conditions, and Communication Systems make up the remaining scored examination.

### Can a NASCLA electrical exam result be used outside New Mexico?

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program is accepted by participating licensing agencies in multiple jurisdictions. Passing candidates can transmit examination results through the NASCLA National Examination Database. Each jurisdiction maintains its own licensing requirements, so the NASCLA examination is not a national electrical license.
