## Licensing Steps

Florida contractor licensing requirements depend on the construction classification, the work performed, and the responsible licensing authority.

Florida distinguishes between state-certified contractors and contractors operating under applicable local licensing or registration programs.

1. **Identify Your Contractor Classification:** Determine the construction activities associated with your intended work.
2. **Determine the Licensing Authority:** Identify whether your classification requires state certification or an applicable local credential.
3. **Review Qualification Requirements:** Understand the experience, business, documentation, and examination requirements.
4. **Prepare Application Information:** Organize the company and applicant records needed for licensing.
5. **Complete the Application:** Follow the procedures established by the appropriate licensing authority.
6. **Prepare for Business and Law:** Study the Florida Counties NASCLA Contractor's Guide, 2nd Edition, when designated for your examination.
7. **Use Flash Cards for Review:** Reinforce important business and law concepts through repeated study.
8. **Practice Reference Navigation:** Use the permanent tabs to locate chapters and highlighted information.
9. **Complete Required Examinations:** Take the examinations associated with your contractor classification.
10. **Complete Licensing Requirements:** Meet the remaining conditions established by the responsible authority.

### Contractor Licensing

### Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights. Understanding lien concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of written agreements and payment documentation.

### Tax Laws

Tax Laws account for six examination questions. Review construction business tax concepts, financial documentation, and responsibilities associated with managing business obligations.

### OSHA and Construction Safety

Construction safety involves workplace hazard awareness, employee responsibilities, and organized jobsite procedures. Understanding these principles supports responsible project management.

### Labor Laws

Labor Laws account for six examination questions. Review employment-related responsibilities, payroll concepts, workers' compensation, and unemployment compensation.

### Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities between contractors, customers, and other project participants. Study agreement terms, contract administration, documentation, and communication.

### Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and coordination. These responsibilities help contractors organize construction activities and monitor progress.

### Estimating and Bidding

Estimating involves evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor expenses, and project costs. Bidding uses this information to develop construction proposals.

### Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors understand business income, expenses, project costs, and available resources. These principles support informed business decisions.

### Risk Management

Risk management involves identifying financial, operational, and contractual exposures. Study insurance concepts and the responsibilities associated with managing construction business risks.
