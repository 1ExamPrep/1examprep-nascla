Licensing Steps

Florida contractor licensing requirements depend on the classification of construction work, the licensing authority, and whether the credential is issued at the state or local level.

Florida county contractor examinations are associated with applicable local licensing programs. Candidates must follow the requirements established by the authority responsible for their intended work.

  1. Identify Your Contractor Classification: Determine the construction activities you intend to perform.
  2. Determine the Licensing Authority: Identify whether your classification requires state certification or an applicable local credential.
  3. Review Qualification Requirements: Understand the experience, business, documentation, and examination requirements for your classification.
  4. Prepare Your Application: Organize the information requested by the responsible licensing authority.
  5. Complete Application Procedures: Submit the necessary documents through the applicable licensing process.
  6. Prepare for Business and Law: Study the Florida Counties NASCLA Contractor's Guide, 2nd Edition, when assigned as your examination reference.
  7. Install Your Reference Tabs: Apply the matching tabs securely and practice locating important chapters.
  8. Prepare for Trade Testing: Review the technical construction requirements of your classification.
  9. Complete Required Examinations: Take the applicable Business and Law and trade examinations.
  10. Complete Licensing Requirements: Meet the remaining conditions for your credential.

Business and law preparation complements technical construction knowledge by addressing the responsibilities involved in managing a contracting company.

State Requirements

Florida regulates construction contracting through state and local licensing frameworks. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Construction Industry Licensing Board administer state construction contractor licensing.

Florida distinguishes between state-certified contractors and contractors whose registration or licensing is tied to an authorized local jurisdiction.

The applicable requirements depend on the work performed and the contractor classification.

Important contractor business responsibilities include:

  • Understanding applicable licensing requirements.

  • Reviewing insurance and risk management responsibilities.
  • Preparing estimates, bids, and construction agreements.
  • Managing employees, subcontractors, and customers.
  • Understanding workplace safety and employment responsibilities.
  • Maintaining financial records and addressing tax obligations.
  • Understanding Florida construction lien concepts.

Test Information and Study Materials

Business Organization

Business organization covers decisions involved in establishing and managing a construction company. Review business structures, administrative responsibilities, and basic management practices.

Contractor Licensing

Licensing establishes requirements for performing regulated construction work. Study the importance of identifying the appropriate contractor classification and following applicable procedures.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights. Understanding lien concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of written agreements, payment documentation, and administrative requirements.

Tax Laws

Tax subjects include business-related financial obligations and the importance of accurate reporting. Review the concepts addressed in the NASCLA guide concerning contractor taxes and financial administration.

OSHA and Construction Safety

Construction safety involves workplace hazard recognition, employee responsibilities, and organized jobsite practices. Understanding safety management principles is important for business and law preparation.

Labor Laws

Labor laws address responsibilities involving employees and workplace administration. Review employment-related concepts, workers' compensation, payroll responsibilities, and unemployment compensation.

Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities between contractors, customers, and other project participants. Study agreement terms, contract administration, documentation, and communication.

Project Management

Project management combines scheduling, budgeting, coordination, and supervision. These principles help contractors organize work and monitor construction project activities.

Estimating and Bidding

Estimating involves reviewing labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other expenses. Bidding applies that information to construction proposals and project requirements.

Financial Management

Financial management involves monitoring revenue, expenses, project costs, and available resources. Understanding accounting and cash flow fundamentals supports organized construction business operations.

Risk Management

Risk management involves recognizing potential financial, operational, and contractual exposures. Study insurance concepts and the responsibilities associated with managing construction business risk.

Part 3: Business Administration

Understanding these responsibilities helps contractors maintain organized records and recognize the importance of sound administrative practices.
