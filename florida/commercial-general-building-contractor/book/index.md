## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is administered through PSI Services LLC. The examination measures construction knowledge associated with commercial general building contractor responsibilities.

- **Examination:** NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
- **Exam Sponsor:** National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA).
- **Testing Provider:** PSI Services LLC.
- **Examination Format:** Computer-based, multiple-choice examination.
- **Scored Questions:** 115.
- **Additional Questions:** 10 unscored pretest questions.
- **Total Questions Administered:** 125.
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 81 correct answers out of 115 scored questions.
- **Time Limit:** 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes.
- **Reference Format:** Open book.
- **Examination Approval:** NASCLA preapproval before PSI scheduling.

The examination covers general requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood, thermal and moisture protection, doors and glazing, finishes, mechanical and plumbing systems, electrical systems, and procurement and contracting.

Candidates who pass may request that their NASCLA examination transcripts be transmitted to participating licensing agencies through the NASCLA National Examination Database.

## Open Book Test

The **NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an open book test**. Candidates may bring authorized reference materials into the PSI examination center.

Open book testing requires effective time management because the examination covers numerous construction subjects and uses an extensive reference library.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary tabs and handwritten notes are prohibited.

References cannot contain loose papers or additional attached materials. Candidates may use an approved silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator.

A physical diagram and blueprint packet is provided at the examination center for applicable questions.

Practice identifying which reference applies to a question before searching for a specific answer. Familiarity with book organization, indexes, tables, and technical terminology can reduce unnecessary lookup time.

## Licensing Steps

The NASCLA examination and Florida contractor licensure involve separate administrative processes.

1. **Review licensing goals:** Determine whether the NASCLA examination aligns with your intended Florida Certified General, Building, or Residential Contractor classification.
2. **Understand the examination:** Review NASCLA's commercial general building contractor content outline.
3. **Apply for NASCLA approval:** Submit the examination application through NASCLA's registration system.
4. **Prepare for testing:** Study the approved references, practice construction calculations, and review project management concepts.
5. **Schedule with PSI:** After NASCLA approval, register for an available examination appointment.
6. **Complete the NASCLA examination:** Take the computer-based open book examination.
7. **Arrange examination records:** Use the NASCLA National Examination Database to transmit results to the appropriate licensing authority.
8. **Complete Florida requirements:** Satisfy the applicable Florida Business and Finance examination and Florida Building Code examination requirements for NASCLA endorsement.
9. **Submit the Florida application:** Complete the applicable contractor certification by endorsement application and supporting qualification requirements.

Florida uses the CILB 10 certification by endorsement application for eligible NASCLA applicants.

NASCLA examination success does not itself issue a contractor license. Florida certification depends on satisfying the applicable state requirements and approval by the Construction Industry Licensing Board.

## State Requirements

Florida regulates general, building, and residential contractors through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Construction Industry Licensing Board.

Florida recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as substantially similar to its Division I trade knowledge examinations for General, Building, and Residential Contractor classifications.

Applicants using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must also pass the **Florida Business and Finance examination** and attest to having passed an examination covering the **Florida Building Code**.

Florida's endorsement process also requires applicants to satisfy the remaining qualifications under Section 489.115, Florida Statutes.

These requirements include the appropriate professional qualifications, application documentation, and applicable financial responsibility and background conditions.

Florida's Certified General Contractor classification covers a broad range of construction activities under the scope authorized by state law.

Certified Building and Residential Contractors operate under separate statutory scopes. The NASCLA examination does not expand the authority granted by a particular Florida contractor license.

For professionals interested in working across multiple jurisdictions, NASCLA's standardized examination can support applications in states that recognize its results, while each state maintains its own licensing requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA examination evaluates twelve subject categories comprising 115 scored questions.

**General Requirements – 25 Questions:** Review building codes, safety, accessibility, plan reading, construction procedures, and general building requirements.

**Site Construction – 15 Questions:** Study grading, excavation, soil conditions, site drainage, utilities, and construction site preparation.

**Concrete – 6 Questions:** Review concrete materials, reinforcement, placement, curing, structural requirements, and construction practices.

**Masonry – 4 Questions:** Study brick, concrete masonry units, mortar, reinforcement, and masonry installation.

**Metals – 6 Questions:** Review structural steel, connections, erection procedures, steel decking, and related construction methods.

**Wood – 5 Questions:** Study framing methods, wood materials, structural connections, trusses, and construction details.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection – 5 Questions:** Review insulation, waterproofing, roofing, vapor protection, and building envelope components.

**Doors, Windows, and Glazing – 4 Questions:** Study openings, glazing materials, installation requirements, and applicable building safety provisions.

**Finishes – 5 Questions:** Review drywall, plaster, ceilings, flooring, coatings, and finishing methods.

**Mechanical and Plumbing Systems – 6 Questions:** Review HVAC, piping, drainage, mechanical equipment, and coordination with building construction.

**Electrical Systems – 3 Questions:** Study basic electrical system components and construction coordination.

**Procurement and Contracting Requirements – 31 Questions:** Review contracts, purchasing, project administration, scheduling, construction management, and related business responsibilities.

Procurement and Contracting Requirements is the largest examination category. Combined with General Requirements, these two categories represent 56 of the 115 scored questions.
