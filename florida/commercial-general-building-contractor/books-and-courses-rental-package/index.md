## Exam Details

This package supports preparation for the Florida Business and Finance examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Florida contractor licensing is regulated by the Construction Industry Licensing Board within the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. Florida accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination through its certification-by-endorsement pathway for qualifying applicants seeking certification as a general, building, or residential contractor.

An applicant using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must also pass the Florida Business and Finance examination and attest to having passed an examination covering the Florida Building Code. Passing NASCLA by itself does not create a Florida contractor license or replace the additional requirements imposed by the Construction Industry Licensing Board.

The NASCLA examination addresses the trade portion of commercial general building knowledge. The included reference set covers project management, jobsite management, general construction requirements, site work, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, roofing, openings, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, electrical systems, safety, procurement, and contracting.

The Florida Business and Finance examination focuses on the business side of operating a contracting company. Preparation may involve business organization, financial management, accounting, contracts, estimating, payroll, taxes, insurance, workers’ compensation, project administration, lien responsibilities, and other contractor business subjects.

Florida candidates pursuing the regular certified general contractor examination route generally complete the Business and Finance, Contract Administration, and Project Management examination parts. Candidates using NASCLA for endorsement follow the requirements established for that application pathway rather than assuming that a NASCLA result automatically replaces every Florida examination component.

## State Requirements

The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board regulates certified and registered construction contractors. A certified contractor may work throughout Florida within the scope of the approved license, while a registered contractor operates within the local jurisdictions recognized by the registration.

A certified general contractor license permits work within the scope established by Florida law and Board rules. Applicants should select the correct license category based on the construction services they intend to offer.

Florida’s NASCLA pathway is an endorsement process rather than an automatic trade-exam substitution for every applicant. Applicants seeking endorsement with a NASCLA result must satisfy Florida’s conditions, including passing the Florida Business and Finance examination and meeting the Florida Building Code examination requirement.

Applicants for certified contractor licensure are generally required to demonstrate four years of qualifying experience or an accepted combination of education and experience. The application must document the type, duration, and level of construction responsibility required for the selected license.

Applicants must also satisfy background-screening, financial-responsibility, insurance, and application requirements. The Board reviews the complete application before issuing a license. Passing examinations alone does not guarantee approval.

A licensed contractor may qualify a business organization when the applicable qualifying-agent requirements are satisfied. The contractor and business remain responsible for compliance with Florida licensing laws, permits, building codes, supervision requirements, and financial obligations.

Local governments may impose business-tax, permit, zoning, inspection, and registration requirements. State certification does not replace permits or approvals required for an individual project.
