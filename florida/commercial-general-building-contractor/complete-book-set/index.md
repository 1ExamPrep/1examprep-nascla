## Exam Details

This complete reference package supports two important parts of the Florida licensing pathway: the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Florida Business and Finance examination.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It measures knowledge used to plan, manage, supervise, and complete commercial general building projects.

Its broad content is represented by the references in this package. Candidates may encounter questions involving project management, jobsite administration, estimating, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, wood trusses, steel decking, steel joists, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, stormwater controls, exterior insulation systems, green building, and mechanical and electrical systems.

After passing the NASCLA examination, candidates may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official examination transcript to participating licensing agencies. Each agency maintains its own application, experience, financial, business, and licensing requirements.

The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board accepts the NASCLA commercial building examination in lieu of the applicable Florida Division I trade examination through the endorsement process. The NASCLA examination is considered substantially similar to Florida Division I trade-knowledge examinations, including General Contractor, Building Contractor, and Residential Contractor classifications and related specialties.

A NASCLA examination result does not replace the Florida Business and Finance examination. Applicants using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must pass the Florida Business and Finance examination and affirm that they have completed an examination covering the Florida Building Code.

Florida construction examinations are administered through the state’s approved examination process. ## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates must obtain and bring the permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not supply the complete NASCLA book library.

The open-book format allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for study. The size of the reference library makes it impractical to research every question from the beginning during the examination. Candidates should know which book covers each major subject and practice locating information efficiently.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question involving reinforcing-bar placement may rely on Placing Reinforcing Bars. A concrete quality question may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. Steel construction questions may involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may rely on the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and jobsite safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Florida’s Business and Finance examination also permits approved bound references under the current examination rules. Permitted reference materials may contain highlighting and underlining, but handwritten or typewritten notes are not allowed. Candidates should review the current Florida candidate information booklet and reference list before preparing books for the examination.

All materials brought to either examination must comply with the current testing rules. Candidates should avoid loose pages, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or materials that are not securely bound when binding is required.

12. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** These may include fingerprints, insurance, business-entity qualification, application fees, and other documentation required by the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board.

## State Requirements

The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board regulates certified and registered construction contractors in the state. A contractor must hold the appropriate Florida license before performing work that requires state licensure.

A Florida Certified General Contractor has an unlimited scope regarding the type of contracting work the license holder may perform, subject to applicable laws, codes, permits, and local requirements. A Certified Building Contractor has a more limited scope involving commercial and residential buildings within the restrictions established for that classification.

Florida accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination through the endorsement process. The NASCLA result may satisfy the applicable Division I trade-knowledge examination requirement, but it does not independently issue a Florida license.

Applicants using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must pass the Florida Business and Finance examination. They must also affirm that they have taken an examination covering the Florida Building Code. The state may require supporting documentation as part of the endorsement review.

Florida applicants must demonstrate financial stability and responsibility. Required materials may include credit reports for the applicant and the qualifying business entity. Additional documentation may be necessary when a credit report does not satisfy the Board’s standards.

Applicants must also meet the experience requirements for the requested license. Florida generally requires four years of qualifying experience or an approved combination of education and experience for certified contractor applicants.

The licensing process may also require electronic fingerprints, public liability and property-damage insurance, workers’ compensation coverage or an applicable exemption, business-entity qualification, and payment of application and licensing fees.

Passing the NASCLA examination and purchasing this book set do not guarantee endorsement or license approval. The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board reviews each application and determines whether the applicant has met all legal and administrative requirements.
