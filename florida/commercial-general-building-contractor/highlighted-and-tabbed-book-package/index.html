The Florida Business and Finance examination focuses on the financial, administrative, legal, and operational responsibilities involved in running a contracting business. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete book library.

The Florida Business and Finance examination also relies on approved reference materials. Candidates should use the Florida Business Book Set during preparation and become familiar with the organization of each publication before test day.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who can recognize the subject of a question and quickly identify the correct reference. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages, so searching through every book during the examination is not a practical strategy.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question involving reinforcing-bar placement may be answered through Placing Reinforcing Bars. A steel construction question could involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial code questions may rely on the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, while safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

The highlighting and permanent tabs in this package support faster navigation, but candidates should still practice using indexes, tables of contents, definitions, chapter headings, code sections, and technical tables. Familiarity with the books can help reduce unnecessary searching and support better time management.

All references taken into the examination room must comply with the current testing rules. Candidates should avoid loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials and review the applicable candidate information before the examination.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Florida contractor classification. Candidates should determine which certified contractor classification matches the work they intend to perform.
  2. Review the Florida application requirements. Applicants should understand the experience, examination, financial, business, and supporting-document requirements connected with the requested license.
  3. Prepare for the Florida Business and Finance examination. Use the included Florida Business Book Set to review the state-focused business, financial, and administrative subjects.
  4. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Study the listed commercial construction references and practice open-book navigation under timed conditions.
  5. Apply for the required examinations. Complete the appropriate registration and approval processes before scheduling.
  6. Pass the Florida Business and Finance examination. This examination addresses the business and financial responsibilities of operating a contracting company in Florida.
  7. Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. The NASCLA result addresses the technical commercial construction examination component.
  8. Submit the appropriate Florida contractor application. Include the required business, qualifying-party, experience, financial, and examination information.
  9. Provide the required supporting documentation. This may include credit reports, insurance information, fingerprints, experience records, and business-entity materials.
  10. Complete the Florida licensing review. The licensing authority reviews the application and supporting materials before issuing a license.

State Requirements

Florida contractor licensing is administered through the state’s construction licensing process. Applicants must select the appropriate classification and satisfy the requirements connected with that license.

The Florida Business and Finance examination remains an important part of the process for candidates using the NASCLA commercial building examination. The Florida Business Book Set included in this package supports preparation for that state-focused examination.

The NASCLA examination addresses commercial building trade knowledge but does not replace Florida’s application, financial responsibility, qualifying experience, business-entity, fingerprinting, insurance, or license-issuance requirements.

Applicants should provide complete and accurate application materials. Missing documentation, incomplete financial information, unresolved business details, or examination issues may delay the licensing review.

Experience requirements depend on the Florida contractor classification being requested. Candidates should document their education and construction experience according to the applicable application instructions.

Financial responsibility is also part of the Florida licensing process. Applicants may be required to provide personal and business credit information along with other financial documents connected with the requested license.

Additional requirements may include electronic fingerprints, contractor insurance, workers’ compensation coverage or an applicable exemption, business qualification, and payment of application and licensing fees.

Passing an examination and purchasing this book set do not guarantee license approval. Applicants must complete the state process and receive the appropriate license before performing work that requires Florida contractor licensure.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin by separating the Florida Business and Finance references from the NASCLA technical library. This helps create a focused plan for each examination instead of mixing state business topics with commercial trade subjects.

For Florida Business and Finance preparation, review the organization of each included publication and practice locating information involving accounting, contracts, financial management, employment, insurance, and business operations.

For NASCLA preparation, divide the technical books into manageable groups. Create separate groups for project management, safety, building codes, accessibility, concrete, masonry, structural systems, carpentry, roofing, excavation, exterior systems, environmental controls, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Practice identifying the correct reference before opening a book. Read a sample question, isolate the key subject, select the publication most likely to contain the answer, and use the index, tabs, or table of contents to locate the applicable section.

Timed exercises can help build speed and confidence. The goal is to recognize the topic, select the right book, find the supporting information, and evaluate the available answers without spending unnecessary time in unrelated references.

Review missed questions carefully. An incorrect response may result from a content gap, a misunderstood term, a poor reference choice, or inefficient navigation. Identifying the reason helps candidates improve future study sessions.
