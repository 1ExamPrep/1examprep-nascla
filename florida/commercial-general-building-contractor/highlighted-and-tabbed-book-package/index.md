## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete book library.

The Florida Business and Finance examination also relies on approved reference materials. Candidates should use the Florida Business Book Set during preparation and become familiar with the organization of each publication before test day.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who can recognize the subject of a question and quickly identify the correct reference. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages, so searching through every book during the examination is not a practical strategy.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question involving reinforcing-bar placement may be answered through Placing Reinforcing Bars. A steel construction question could involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial code questions may rely on the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, while safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

The highlighting and permanent tabs in this package support faster navigation, but candidates should still practice using indexes, tables of contents, definitions, chapter headings, code sections, and technical tables. Familiarity with the books can help reduce unnecessary searching and support better time management.

All references taken into the examination room must comply with the current testing rules. Candidates should avoid loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials and review the applicable candidate information before the examination.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Begin by separating the Florida Business and Finance references from the NASCLA technical library. This helps create a focused plan for each examination instead of mixing state business topics with commercial trade subjects.

For Florida Business and Finance preparation, review the organization of each included publication and practice locating information involving accounting, contracts, financial management, employment, insurance, and business operations.

For NASCLA preparation, divide the technical books into manageable groups. Create separate groups for project management, safety, building codes, accessibility, concrete, masonry, structural systems, carpentry, roofing, excavation, exterior systems, environmental controls, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Practice identifying the correct reference before opening a book. Read a sample question, isolate the key subject, select the publication most likely to contain the answer, and use the index, tabs, or table of contents to locate the applicable section.

Timed exercises can help build speed and confidence. The goal is to recognize the topic, select the right book, find the supporting information, and evaluate the available answers without spending unnecessary time in unrelated references.

Review missed questions carefully. An incorrect response may result from a content gap, a misunderstood term, a poor reference choice, or inefficient navigation. Identifying the reason helps candidates improve future study sessions.
