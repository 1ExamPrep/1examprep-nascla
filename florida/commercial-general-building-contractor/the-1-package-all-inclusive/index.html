Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial building trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing jurisdictions. Florida provides an endorsement pathway for qualifying general, building, and residential contractor applicants who have passed the NASCLA examination.

For Florida applicants using NASCLA for endorsement, passing the NASCLA examination alone does not complete the Florida examination requirements. Florida also requires applicants using this pathway to pass the Florida Business and Finance examination and affirm that they have completed an examination covering the Florida Building Code.

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation administers contractor licensing through the Construction Industry Licensing Board. Florida construction examinations include Business and Finance as well as applicable Contract Administration, Project Management, and Trade Knowledge examination components for candidates following the standard state examination route.

Candidates using the NASCLA endorsement pathway should prepare specifically for the requirements that apply to endorsement rather than assuming the NASCLA examination replaces every Florida licensing examination. The combination of NASCLA materials and Florida-specific references in this package supports a broader preparation approach.

Study should include commercial construction methods, building systems, safety, project administration, contracts, accounting, business practices, and Florida-specific contractor requirements. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open book test. Candidates may use the permitted references designated for the examination under the testing provider's rules.

Florida contractor examinations are also reference-based examinations, with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation publishing specific reference lists for each construction examination category. Candidates should prepare with the references that correspond to the examination they are taking and bring only materials permitted for that examination session.

Open-book testing requires a different preparation strategy than a closed-book exam. Candidates need to understand the material, but they also need to know where specific information can be found. Searching several large technical books for every question can use valuable testing time.

Licensing Steps

Florida Certified General Contractor licensing is administered by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation through the Construction Industry Licensing Board. Applicants using NASCLA must follow the applicable Florida endorsement process.

  1. Determine the appropriate Florida license category. Applicants pursuing Certified General Contractor licensure should make sure the license classification matches the scope of contracting work they intend to perform.
  2. Complete the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. Florida allows qualifying general, building, and residential contractor endorsement applicants to use the NASCLA examination in lieu of proving successful completion of another state's substantially equivalent licensing examination.
  3. Complete the Florida Business and Finance examination. NASCLA endorsement applicants must pass Florida's Business and Finance examination.
  4. Satisfy the Florida Building Code examination requirement. Applicants using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must affirm that they have completed an examination covering the Florida Building Code.
  5. Meet Florida experience and education requirements. Certified contractor applicants generally must document four years of qualifying experience or an approved combination of college and experience.
  6. Complete the required background screening. Florida contractor applicants are subject to fingerprinting and background-check requirements.
  7. Provide financial and credit information. Endorsement applicants must demonstrate financial responsibility and stability using the documentation required by the Construction Industry Licensing Board.
  8. Submit the applicable Florida application. NASCLA applicants pursuing endorsement use the appropriate Construction Industry Licensing Board endorsement application and provide the required supporting documentation.

State Requirements

Florida contractor licensing is regulated by the Construction Industry Licensing Board under the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Certified General Contractor applicants must satisfy examination, experience or education, financial, background, and application requirements established by Florida law and Board rules.

Applicants seeking a Certified General Contractor license are generally required to demonstrate four years of qualifying experience or an acceptable combination of college education and construction experience. The precise experience documentation depends on the qualification method used in the application.

Florida also requires applicants to demonstrate financial responsibility and stability. For NASCLA endorsement applicants, the licensing process includes credit-report requirements for the applicant and applicable business entities. Background screening through fingerprinting is also part of the licensing process.

NASCLA applicants should understand the distinction between passing a national trade examination and obtaining a Florida contractor license. The NASCLA examination can be used within Florida's endorsement process, but applicants must still satisfy the other requirements of Florida law, including Florida-specific Business and Finance and Building Code requirements.
