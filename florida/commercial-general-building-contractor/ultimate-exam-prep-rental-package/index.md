## Exam Details

This Ultimate package supports preparation for the Florida Business and Finance examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Florida construction contractor licensing is regulated by the Construction Industry Licensing Board within the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized commercial general building trade examination accepted by participating state licensing agencies. Florida recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination through its certification-by-endorsement process for qualifying applicants seeking licensure as a Certified General Contractor, Certified Building Contractor, or Certified Residential Contractor.

Applicants using the NASCLA endorsement pathway must also pass the Florida Business and Finance examination and attest to having passed an examination covering the Florida Building Code. A passing NASCLA result does not automatically issue a Florida contractor license or replace the other application, experience, background, financial, and insurance requirements established by the Construction Industry Licensing Board.

The NASCLA trade examination addresses commercial construction knowledge across multiple divisions. The included references support preparation in project management, jobsite management, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, roofing, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, accessibility, mechanical systems, electrical systems, construction safety, procurement, and contracting.

The Florida Business and Finance examination focuses on the financial and administrative side of operating a contracting business. Preparation may include accounting, financial statements, estimating, contracts, payroll, taxes, insurance, workers’ compensation, lien responsibilities, personnel management, project administration, and other contractor business subjects.

Candidates pursuing Florida’s standard Division I examination route generally complete Business and Finance, Contract Administration, and Project Management examination components. Candidates using NASCLA through endorsement must follow the specific requirements of that licensing pathway and should not assume that the NASCLA examination replaces every Florida requirement.

## State Requirements

The Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board licenses and regulates construction contractors. Certified contractors may work throughout Florida within the scope of their license, while registered contractors generally operate within the local jurisdictions connected with their registration.

The NASCLA pathway is available through Florida’s certification-by-endorsement process for applicants seeking licensure as a Certified General Contractor, Certified Building Contractor, or Certified Residential Contractor. The NASCLA examination is treated as a trade-knowledge examination for this pathway.

Applicants relying on NASCLA must also pass the Florida Business and Finance examination and attest to passing an examination covering the Florida Building Code. These requirements remain separate from the NASCLA commercial building examination.

Florida contractor applicants are generally required to demonstrate four years of qualifying experience or an approved combination of college education and construction experience. The experience documentation must correspond to the license category requested and show the appropriate level of construction responsibility.

Applicants must also satisfy fingerprinting and background-screening requirements. Financial responsibility and financial stability must be demonstrated through the records required by the Board, including applicable credit reports.

Required insurance must be in place before the license is activated. Depending on the applicant’s circumstances, this may include workers’ compensation coverage or an approved exemption, along with general liability and property-damage insurance.

A licensed contractor may qualify a business organization when the applicable qualifying-agent requirements are met. Both the qualifying contractor and the business remain responsible for compliance with Florida licensing laws, permits, supervision requirements, building codes, and financial obligations.

Local governments may impose separate business-tax, permit, zoning, inspection, and registration requirements. State certification does not replace the permits or project approvals required by a county, municipality, building department, or other authority.
