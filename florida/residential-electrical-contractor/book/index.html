Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors is a national examination administered by PSI Services LLC through NASCLA's accredited examination program.

The examination evaluates the technical knowledge associated with residential electrical installation, maintenance, repair, and service.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors.
  • Examination Sponsor: National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA).
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services LLC.
  • Examination Format: Computer-based, multiple-choice examination.
  • Scored Questions: 100.
  • Additional Questions: 10 unscored experimental questions.
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70 correct answers out of 100 scored questions.
  • Time Limit: 270 minutes, or 4 hours and 30 minutes.
  • Reference Format: Open book.
  • Examination Approval: NASCLA preapproval before PSI scheduling.
  • Florida Licensing Connection: Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor certification by endorsement.

The examination covers electrical theory, workplace safety, project design, grounding and bonding, special systems, testing equipment, circuit protection, wiring methods, general-use equipment, and energy efficiency.

Passing results can be transmitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database to participating licensing agencies.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the PSI examination center.

Open book testing requires effective preparation because candidates must answer technical questions while managing their examination time.

The examination includes 100 scored questions and 10 unscored experimental questions within the designated testing period.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent reference tabs are permitted, but temporary tabs and handwritten notes are prohibited.

Reference books cannot contain additional loose or attached papers. Candidates must bring their own authorized reference materials.

PSI permits a silent, battery-operated, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator without an alphabetic keyboard.

For questions involving construction plans, the testing center provides a physical diagram and blueprint packet.

Effective preparation includes practicing code lookups, understanding electrical terminology, and recognizing which reference contains the applicable information.

ACE encourages candidates to strengthen these skills through technical review and practice-oriented preparation. AI tutoring platforms and online practice resources are intended for studying rather than use during the proctored examination.

Licensing Steps

Preparing for the NASCLA Residential Electrician examination and obtaining a Florida contractor license involve separate processes.

  1. Identify your licensing goal: Review the NASCLA examination classification and the Florida Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor endorsement pathway.
  2. Evaluate your qualifications: Determine whether your existing electrical contractor license and professional experience satisfy the applicable Florida endorsement requirements.
  3. Apply for NASCLA approval: Submit the examination application through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  4. Study the examination outline: Review residential electrical theory, wiring methods, safety requirements, grounding, and circuit protection.
  5. Prepare with authorized references: Practice navigating the National Electrical Code and the other designated examination books.
  6. Schedule with PSI: After receiving NASCLA approval, arrange your examination appointment through PSI.
  7. Complete the examination: Take the computer-based residential electrical contractor examination.
  8. Transmit examination results: Use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send your passing transcript to an accepting licensing agency.
  9. Complete Florida endorsement requirements: Submit the applicable electrical specialty contractor endorsement application and supporting documentation to Florida DBPR.

NASCLA examination results satisfy only the examination component accepted by a participating licensing authority. Professional licensure requires completion of the applicable state process.

Florida State Requirements

Florida regulates electrical contractors through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) and the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

The state recognizes Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor as an electrical specialty classification.

Florida's licensing framework distinguishes between Certified Electrical Contractors, who hold broader electrical contracting authority, and specialty electrical contractors whose authorized work is limited to their classification.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is recognized by Florida for endorsement applications involving the Certified Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor classification.

Florida's NASCLA endorsement pathway requires the applicant to hold an existing license from another jurisdiction with a scope substantially similar to the Florida classification being requested.

Applicants pursuing initial Florida electrical contractor licensure without a qualifying license from another jurisdiction must follow Florida's applicable examination and licensing process.

Florida also requires applicants to satisfy the applicable endorsement qualifications and provide the documentation required by the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

The scope of a Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor credential differs from unrestricted electrical contracting authority. Contractors must perform work within the classification authorized by their Florida license.

Understanding these distinctions helps candidates connect NASCLA examination preparation with their professional licensing goals.

Reference Books

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination uses an authorized reference library covering electrical codes, workplace safety, residential construction, business administration, and electrical theory.

  • NFPA 70 – National Electrical Code (NEC), 2020 or 2023 Edition
    Provides electrical installation requirements involving services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, conductors, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, and electrical equipment. The corresponding NEC Handbook is also an authorized alternative.
  • Code of Federal Regulations – 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA Construction Industry Regulations, 2024 Edition
    Addresses construction workplace safety, electrical hazards, protective practices, equipment operation, and jobsite requirements.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition
    Provides information about contracting responsibilities, project management, business operations, construction administration, and related professional practices.
  • NFPA 70E – Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024 Edition
    Addresses electrical workplace safety, shock hazards, arc flash considerations, and practices intended to reduce electrical injury risks.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023 Edition
    Provides electrical formulas, reference tables, calculations, wiring information, and practical technical data useful for electrical examination preparation.
  • NFPA 72 – National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2022 Edition
    Provides requirements relating to fire alarm systems, notification equipment, signaling components, and associated electrical installations.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications, Mike Holt
    Explains electrical principles and their relationship to National Electrical Code applications, supporting understanding of electrical calculations and circuit behavior.
  • ANSI Z535.4-2023 – Product Safety Signs and Labels
    Provides requirements for product safety signs, labels, hazard communication, and safety information associated with equipment.
  • 2024 International Residential Code (IRC)
    Addresses construction requirements for one- and two-family dwellings, including residential systems and building provisions relevant to electrical installation coordination.

NASCLA identifies these references for examination preparation and authorized open book use. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of each reference and the subjects it addresses.

Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Residential Electrician examination evaluates ten subject categories. Understanding each category helps candidates organize study sessions and prioritize technical review.

Electrical Theory – 9 Questions: Review Ohm's law, electrical power, voltage, current, resistance, circuit relationships, and basic electrical calculations.

Personal and Jobsite Safety – 8 Questions: Study electrical hazards, safe work practices, OSHA construction requirements, and NFPA 70E electrical safety principles.

Project Design, Estimating and Management – 8 Questions: Review construction plans, electrical materials, project coordination, estimating concepts, and professional electrical contracting responsibilities.

Grounding and Bonding – 14 Questions: Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, grounding connections, and electrical fault-current paths.

Special Systems and Special Equipment Installations – 7 Questions: Review electrical requirements involving specialized residential equipment and related installations.

Tools and Test Equipment – 7 Questions: Study meters, testing instruments, diagnostic procedures, and tools commonly used in electrical installation and maintenance.

Circuit Protection and Installation – 16 Questions: Review overcurrent protective devices, circuit ratings, equipment protection, branch circuits, and installation requirements.

Wiring Methods and Practices – 17 Questions: Study conductor selection, wiring materials, cable assemblies, raceways, boxes, installation methods, and related NEC provisions.

Equipment for General Use – 13 Questions: Review switches, receptacles, luminaires, appliances, motors, panels, and other commonly installed electrical equipment.

Energy Efficiency and Management – 1 Question: Review basic electrical energy management and efficiency concepts relevant to residential installations.

Wiring Methods and Practices, Circuit Protection and Installation, and Grounding and Bonding account for a combined 47 scored questions, making these subjects important priorities for examination preparation.

Practice interpreting simple residential electrical diagrams and recognizing the relationship between connected equipment and required circuit provisions.

Electrical Calculations and Testing Equipment

Electrical theory and calculations are important foundations for understanding residential wiring systems.

Practice applying Ohm's law, determining electrical power, and evaluating basic circuit relationships.

Review conductor calculations, residential load concepts, circuit sizing, and the use of reference tables.
