Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes standardized examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors. Florida participates in the program through the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around knowledge used in residential electrical contracting, including National Electrical Code requirements, electrical theory, calculations, safety, residential wiring methods, equipment, construction conditions, and related technical responsibilities.

Residential electrical preparation should include subjects such as:

  • Electrical theory and practical calculations
  • National Electrical Code organization and navigation
  • Residential services and service equipment
  • Feeders and branch circuits
  • Dwelling-unit load calculations
  • Conductor sizing and ampacity
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Residential wiring methods and materials
  • Boxes, raceways, cables, and enclosures
  • Electrical equipment and appliances
  • Construction-industry safety
  • Electrical workplace safety
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Residential construction requirements
  • Product safety signs and labels
  • Contractor business and project management

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The current application fee is $65. Applications are generally processed within seven business days, and an approved application is valid for one year. Candidates currently receive up to three opportunities to take and pass the examination during that eligibility period.

After NASCLA approval, candidates receive scheduling information from PSI. Candidates can schedule the examination through an available PSI testing location offering the applicable NASCLA examination.

Passing NASCLA does not itself issue a Florida electrical contractor license. The examination result can satisfy an accepted technical examination component for an eligible endorsement applicant, while Florida continues to evaluate experience, existing licensure, financial responsibility, and other applicable licensing requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies the Residential Electrical Contractor examination as one of the open-book electrical trade examinations within its Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should know how to identify the subject of a question, select the correct book, locate the applicable provision, formula, definition, table, or safety requirement, and apply that information accurately.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Florida Residential Electrical Contractor license. Florida uses the accepted NASCLA examination as part of an endorsement pathway administered through the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

  1. Confirm the Florida residential electrical classification. Review the scope of the Residential Electrical Contractor specialty and determine whether it matches the work you plan to perform.
  2. Confirm endorsement eligibility. Florida endorsement based on a substantially similar examination requires an applicant to hold an out-of-state license associated with a comparable licensing scope.
  3. Review Florida experience requirements. Applicants must satisfy one of the experience pathways established for electrical contractor certification.
  4. Apply for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Submit the NASCLA examination application through the National Examination Database.

  9. Complete remaining Florida examination requirements. Florida state electrical certification examinations include technical/safety and business components, and endorsement applicants must satisfy the applicable business examination requirement associated with their pathway.
  10. Receive Board approval. Florida certification is issued only after the Board determines that the applicant satisfies all applicable requirements.

State Requirements

The Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board regulates electrical contractors and specialty electrical contractors. Certified electrical contractor licenses are statewide, allowing the license holder to perform contracting work anywhere in Florida within the authorized classification.

Florida's endorsement process allows an applicant who holds a substantially equivalent out-of-state license to seek Florida certification without repeating an examination that the Board has determined to be substantially similar. Endorsement remains subject to Board approval and Florida's additional licensing requirements.

For endorsement based on a substantially similar examination, applicants must demonstrate that they satisfy an applicable experience pathway. Florida identifies several possible pathways involving qualifying management, supervisory, contractor, technical education, professional engineering, or other approved electrical experience.

Applicants must also satisfy applicable personal and business financial-responsibility requirements. Florida's endorsement application includes credit-report requirements and, when a business is being qualified, may require business financial information and supporting documentation establishing the applicant's relationship to the business.

Florida differentiates between certified and registered electrical contractors. A certified contractor may work throughout Florida within the scope of the license. A registered contractor is generally associated with local competency and may work only in jurisdictions where the registration is recognized.

The Residential Electrical Contractor classification is a specialty credential and does not provide the same unrestricted scope as Florida's Certified Electrical Contractor classification. Contractors should perform only the work authorized by the license they hold.

Florida also administers its own state electrical certification examinations through a computer-based testing format. Those examinations are divided into technical/safety and business portions. Endorsement applicants using an accepted substantially similar examination must still complete the business requirement applicable to their licensing pathway.

Electrical contractors must also comply with applicable insurance, permitting, inspection, code, business-qualification, and renewal requirements after licensure. Passing an examination is only one component of operating legally as a licensed electrical contractor in Florida.
