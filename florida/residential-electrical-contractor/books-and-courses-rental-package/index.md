## State Requirements

The **Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board** regulates electrical contractors and specialty electrical contractors. Certified electrical contractor licenses are statewide, allowing the license holder to perform contracting work anywhere in Florida within the authorized classification.

Florida's endorsement process allows an applicant who holds a substantially equivalent out-of-state license to seek Florida certification without repeating an examination that the Board has determined to be substantially similar. Endorsement remains subject to Board approval and Florida's additional licensing requirements.

For endorsement based on a substantially similar examination, applicants must demonstrate that they satisfy an applicable experience pathway. Florida identifies several possible pathways involving qualifying management, supervisory, contractor, technical education, professional engineering, or other approved electrical experience.

Applicants must also satisfy applicable personal and business financial-responsibility requirements. Florida's endorsement application includes credit-report requirements and, when a business is being qualified, may require business financial information and supporting documentation establishing the applicant's relationship to the business.

Florida differentiates between **certified** and **registered** electrical contractors. A certified contractor may work throughout Florida within the scope of the license. A registered contractor is generally associated with local competency and may work only in jurisdictions where the registration is recognized.

The Residential Electrical Contractor classification is a specialty credential and does not provide the same unrestricted scope as Florida's Certified Electrical Contractor classification. Contractors should perform only the work authorized by the license they hold.

Florida also administers its own state electrical certification examinations through a computer-based testing format. Those examinations are divided into **technical/safety and business portions**. Endorsement applicants using an accepted substantially similar examination must still complete the business requirement applicable to their licensing pathway.

Electrical contractors must also comply with applicable insurance, permitting, inspection, code, business-qualification, and renewal requirements after licensure. Passing an examination is only one component of operating legally as a licensed electrical contractor in Florida.
