Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies its accredited Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an open-book electrical trade examination using authorized references under the applicable testing rules.

Because the examination is open book, effective reference navigation should be part of regular preparation. Candidates should know how to identify the subject of a question, determine which reference is most appropriate, find the relevant code provision, table, formula, definition, or technical requirement, and apply that information correctly.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Florida Residential Electrical Specialty Contractor certification. Florida uses the NASCLA examination as part of an endorsement pathway.

  1. Confirm the Florida endorsement pathway applies. Florida recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Exam as substantially similar to the Florida Residential Specialty technical examination.
  2. Hold an appropriate out-of-state license. Endorsement based on the NASCLA examination requires an applicant to hold a license from another jurisdiction that is similar in scope to the Florida classification being requested.
  3. Review Florida experience qualifications. Applicants must satisfy the experience or education requirements applicable to Florida electrical contractor certification.
  4. Prepare for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Study the NEC, electrical theory, calculations, safety, residential construction, fire alarm systems, business and project management, and the other reference topics associated with the examination.
  5. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the current examination procedures and authorized-reference rules.
  6. Complete the Florida Business examination requirement. Florida's current endorsement determination recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Exam as the Residential Specialty technical examination, while applicants must still complete the Florida Business examination for licensure.
  7. Submit the Florida endorsement application. Provide the examination, license, experience, financial, and other supporting documentation required by the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Florida reviews the applicant's qualifications before issuing certification.
  9. Obtain certification before contracting statewide. A Florida certified specialty electrical contractor license authorizes work statewide within its approved scope.

State Requirements

The Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board regulates electrical contractors and certified specialty electrical contractors. Certified licenses are statewide, allowing a properly licensed contractor to work within the authorized classification throughout Florida.

The Florida Residential Electrical Contractor specialty scope includes installation, repair, alteration, addition to, replacement of, or design of electrical wiring, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, and related electrical components in one-, two-, three-, or four-family residences not exceeding two stories in height, including accessory-use structures associated with those residences.

The electrical service within the Residential Electrical Contractor specialty scope is limited to a single-phase, 400-ampere single service.

Florida recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Exam as substantially similar to the Residential Specialty technical examination for endorsement. Applicants using this substantially similar examination pathway must also hold an out-of-state license associated with a comparable scope and satisfy Florida's certification requirements.

Florida law provides several possible experience and education pathways for electrical contractor certification. These include at least three years of qualifying management experience in the trade within the preceding six years, with permitted educational equivalency; four years of qualifying supervisory or contractor experience within the preceding eight years; or six years of qualifying comprehensive training, technical education, or supervisory experience within the preceding 12 years. Additional qualifying pathways exist for certain professional engineers and combinations of eligible experience and education.

Applicants for Florida certification must also satisfy applicable character, financial responsibility, credit, insurance, business, and application requirements. The Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board reviews the application and supporting documentation before certification is issued.

Florida's NASCLA endorsement pathway should not be confused with automatic reciprocity. The Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board does not treat NASCLA examination passage alone as automatic authorization to perform electrical contracting work in Florida.
