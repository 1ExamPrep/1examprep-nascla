Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized trade examinations for participating licensing agencies. Electrical examination categories include Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor.

Florida currently accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for endorsement toward the Florida Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor classification. NASCLA acceptance serves as a trade-examination waiver pathway for an eligible endorsement applicant. It does not automatically issue a Florida license.

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The current application fee is $65. Approved applications are valid for one year from the approval date, and candidates currently receive up to three examination attempts during that eligibility period.

NASCLA examination applications are generally processed within seven business days. Once approved, candidates receive scheduling information from the testing provider. The examination may be scheduled at a PSI location offering the applicable NASCLA test.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Florida Residential Electrical Contractor certification. Florida uses the accepted NASCLA examination as part of an endorsement pathway administered through the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

  1. Review the Florida Residential Electrical Contractor classification. Confirm that its residential specialty scope matches the work you intend to perform.
  2. Confirm eligibility for endorsement. Florida's NASCLA pathway requires an applicant to hold a license from another jurisdiction that is similar in scope to the Florida specialty classification being requested.
  3. Review Florida qualification requirements. Applicants must satisfy the experience, financial responsibility, insurance, and other requirements applicable to certification.
  4. Apply for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Submit the NASCLA examination application through the National Examination Database.
  5. Prepare using the examination references. Study residential electrical code, theory, safety, calculations, construction requirements, fire alarm systems, and contractor business topics.
  6. Practice reference navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed package while working technical questions and locating information throughout the reference library.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the current testing-provider procedures and authorized-reference rules.
  8. Complete the Florida endorsement application. Submit the examination and licensing documentation required by the Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.
  9. Complete remaining Florida requirements. The Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before issuing certification.

State Requirements

The Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board regulates certified and registered electrical contractors and specialty electrical contractors. Residential electrical contracting is one of the specialty classifications recognized by Florida.

The Florida Residential Electrical Contractor specialty authorizes qualifying electrical work involving wiring, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, and related electrical components in one-, two-, three-, or four-family residences not exceeding two stories in height. The scope also extends to accessory-use structures associated with those residences.

The electrical service installed or worked on under the Residential Electrical Contractor specialty is limited to single-phase, 400-ampere single service. Candidates planning to perform work outside this residential specialty scope should review the requirements for Florida's broader electrical contractor classifications.

Florida's acceptance of the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is specifically an endorsement pathway. The applicant must already hold a license from another jurisdiction that is similar in scope to the Florida classification being requested. An initial applicant who has never been licensed in another jurisdiction cannot rely solely on the NASCLA endorsement route in place of Florida's applicable examination pathway.

Endorsement is not automatic reciprocity. The Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board evaluates the applicant's existing license, examination record, experience, financial responsibility, and other required documentation before determining whether certification should be issued.

Florida distinguishes between certified and registered electrical contractors. A certified contractor is licensed for statewide practice within the authorized classification. A registered contractor works based on local competency requirements and is limited to the jurisdictions for which the registration is issued.

The Residential Electrical Contractor classification is a specialty electrical credential. It does not provide the same scope as Florida's unlimited Certified Electrical Contractor classification, which covers broader electrical and alarm work.

Applicants using an endorsement pathway must also satisfy applicable financial-responsibility and insurance requirements and provide the supporting documentation required by the Board. Businesses may be subject to additional financial documentation depending on the structure and license being requested.
