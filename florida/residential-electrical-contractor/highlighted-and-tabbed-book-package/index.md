## State Requirements

The **Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board** regulates certified and registered electrical contractors and specialty electrical contractors. Residential electrical contracting is one of the specialty classifications recognized by Florida.

The Florida Residential Electrical Contractor specialty authorizes qualifying electrical work involving wiring, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, and related electrical components in **one-, two-, three-, or four-family residences not exceeding two stories in height**. The scope also extends to accessory-use structures associated with those residences.

The electrical service installed or worked on under the Residential Electrical Contractor specialty is limited to **single-phase, 400-ampere single service**. Candidates planning to perform work outside this residential specialty scope should review the requirements for Florida's broader electrical contractor classifications.

Florida's acceptance of the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is specifically an **endorsement pathway**. The applicant must already hold a license from another jurisdiction that is similar in scope to the Florida classification being requested. An initial applicant who has never been licensed in another jurisdiction cannot rely solely on the NASCLA endorsement route in place of Florida's applicable examination pathway.

Endorsement is not automatic reciprocity. The Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board evaluates the applicant's existing license, examination record, experience, financial responsibility, and other required documentation before determining whether certification should be issued.

Florida distinguishes between **certified** and **registered** electrical contractors. A certified contractor is licensed for statewide practice within the authorized classification. A registered contractor works based on local competency requirements and is limited to the jurisdictions for which the registration is issued.

The Residential Electrical Contractor classification is a specialty electrical credential. It does not provide the same scope as Florida's unlimited Certified Electrical Contractor classification, which covers broader electrical and alarm work.

Applicants using an endorsement pathway must also satisfy applicable financial-responsibility and insurance requirements and provide the supporting documentation required by the Board. Businesses may be subject to additional financial documentation depending on the structure and license being requested.
