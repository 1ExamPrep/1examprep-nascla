## Exam Details

The **NASCLA Accredited Residential Electrical Contractor Examination** is part of NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor.

The residential examination focuses on electrical knowledge and practical requirements associated with residential electrical contracting. Candidates should develop familiarity with applicable code provisions, electrical system design concepts, calculations, equipment requirements, and safe installation practices.

**Major examination preparation areas include:**

- **National Electrical Code:** Residential electrical requirements, definitions, articles, tables, and applicable installation provisions.
- **Residential Wiring:** Branch circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, wiring methods, cables, raceways, and equipment connections.
- **Electrical Services and Feeders:** Service arrangements, feeder conductors, electrical loads, and related protection requirements.
- **Grounding and Bonding:** Grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, bonding methods, and electrical safety concepts.
- **Conductor Sizing:** Ampacity, conductor selection, installation conditions, and technical calculations.
- **Overcurrent Protection:** Circuit breakers, fuses, protective devices, and associated electrical requirements.
- **Electrical Theory:** Voltage, current, resistance, power, alternating current, and circuit relationships.
- **Electrical Calculations:** Ohm's law, power formulas, loads, conductor calculations, and practical electrical mathematics.
- **Construction Safety:** OSHA requirements concerning electrical work, equipment, tools, and construction hazards.
- **Workplace Electrical Safety:** Safe electrical work practices and hazard-awareness concepts addressed by NFPA 70E.
- **Fire Alarm Systems:** Fire alarm and signaling concepts supported by NFPA 72.
- **Residential Construction:** Building conditions and requirements addressed by the International Residential Code.
- **Business and Project Management:** Contractor operations, contracts, estimating, safety management, and business responsibilities.
- **Product Safety:** Safety signs, labels, hazard communication, and related technical standards.

The examination requires candidates to connect electrical knowledge with code requirements. A question involving a dwelling-unit circuit may require understanding both the electrical principle and the applicable NEC provision.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized trade examination, but each participating licensing jurisdiction determines how an examination result applies to its licensing requirements.

Passing NASCLA does not create a national contractor license or automatically authorize electrical work in Florida.

## Open Book Test

The **NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination** is an **open-book test**. Candidates should prepare by developing familiarity with the technical references and learning how to locate relevant information efficiently.

Open-book testing requires more than having publications available. Candidates must understand the question, identify the applicable subject, select the appropriate reference, and accurately interpret the provision or calculation method.

The **2023 National Electrical Code** is an important foundation for preparation. Candidates should understand its organization, including definitions, articles, chapters, technical tables, exceptions, and cross-references.

Residential electrical questions may involve general electrical requirements together with provisions specific to dwelling units, services, feeders, branch circuits, equipment, and wiring methods.

Safety questions may require distinguishing between construction safety requirements addressed by OSHA and electrical workplace safety practices addressed by NFPA 70E.

**Recommended open-book preparation strategies include:**

- **Identify the Electrical Subject:** Determine whether a question involves calculations, wiring methods, grounding, protection, safety, or residential construction.
- **Select the Appropriate Reference:** Choose the publication most relevant to the technical subject.
- **Learn Code Organization:** Practice using indexes, articles, definitions, chapter headings, and technical tables.
- **Review Conditions:** Read exceptions, notes, definitions, and qualifying language before selecting an answer.
- **Practice Calculations:** Become comfortable identifying the required formula and applying it correctly.
- **Use Repeated Navigation:** Locate important provisions several times to strengthen reference familiarity.
- **Improve Examination Pace:** Practice answering technical questions efficiently without unnecessary searching.

Candidates should use the current NASCLA examination instructions to determine which reference materials, editions, annotations, tabs, and other items are permitted during testing.

Consistent reference-navigation practice can help candidates develop a more organized approach to residential electrical examination questions.

## State Requirements

Florida electrical contractor certification is regulated by the **Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board** within the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

The Board administers requirements associated with electrical and specialty electrical contractor certification, including examination and endorsement pathways.

Florida accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for endorsement toward the applicable Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor classification when an applicant satisfies the endorsement requirements.

The applicant must hold a license from another jurisdiction that is similar in scope to the Florida certification requested.

Florida's residential electrical specialty scope includes qualifying electrical wiring and related work in one-, two-, three-, and four-family residences not exceeding two stories in height, together with associated accessory-use structures.

The electrical service within this specialty scope is limited to a **single-phase, 400-ampere single service**.

Florida's electrical contractor certification requirements include professional qualifications and good moral character. Statutory qualification pathways recognize appropriate management experience, supervisory or contractor experience, technical education, comprehensive training, and certain qualifying professional engineering experience.

Recognized experience pathways include three years of proven management experience within the preceding six years, four years of supervisory or contractor experience within the preceding eight years, and six years of qualifying training, education, or supervisory experience within the preceding twelve years, subject to the applicable statutory conditions.

Florida also recognizes certain qualifying military electrical experience and permitted combinations of education and experience.

The licensing authority determines whether the applicant satisfies the applicable statutory and endorsement requirements.

Passing the NASCLA examination is not equivalent to receiving Florida certification. Applicants must complete the endorsement process and receive the appropriate licensing approval before performing work requiring state certification.

**These physical reference books are not included with the online course and can be purchased separately.**

- **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023**
The primary electrical code reference covering residential wiring, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment, and electrical installations.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
A construction safety reference addressing workplace hazards, electrical work, equipment, tools, and occupational safety requirements applicable to construction activities.
- **NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th**
A contractor reference covering business organization, financial concepts, contracts, estimating, project administration, employment responsibilities, and safety management.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
An electrical safety standard addressing workplace safety practices and methods for reducing exposure to electrical hazards.
- **Ugly's Electrical References**
A practical electrical reference containing frequently used formulas, calculations, tables, conversions, and technical information useful during electrical theory review.
- **NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2022**
A fire alarm and signaling reference addressing detection, initiating devices, notification appliances, signaling functions, and related system requirements.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
A study reference connecting fundamental electrical theory, including voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and calculations, with practical National Electrical Code applications.
- **ANSI Z535.4-2023, Product Safety Signs and Labels**
A product safety standard addressing signs, labels, hazard communication, and related safety information.
- **International Residential Code (IRC), 2024**
A residential construction code addressing requirements associated with one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses, including building conditions that may interact with electrical installations.

Each publication supports a different subject. The NEC provides core electrical requirements, OSHA and NFPA 70E address safety, and the theory references support calculations and technical understanding.

NFPA 72 supports fire alarm study, while the International Residential Code provides broader residential construction context.
