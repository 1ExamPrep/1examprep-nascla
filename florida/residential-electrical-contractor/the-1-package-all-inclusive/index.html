Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized electrical examinations that participating licensing agencies may administer or accept. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

Florida's Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for endorsement toward the Florida Residential Specialty category. Florida's endorsement matrix identifies the NASCLA Residential Electrical examination as substantially similar to the Florida Residential Specialty technical examination.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination focuses on knowledge associated with residential electrical installation, service, repair, maintenance, electrical code compliance, safety, calculations, wiring methods, circuit protection, grounding and bonding, equipment, and related residential electrical responsibilities.

  • Construction-industry safety

Florida's recognition of the NASCLA examination applies to the technical examination component for eligible endorsement candidates. Applicants must still satisfy Florida's applicable business examination and licensing requirements. Endorsement requires Board approval and is not automatic reciprocity.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. Effective preparation should therefore include both technical understanding and repeated reference navigation.

All rental books included in The 1 Package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting can help direct attention toward important material during study, while tabs provide visible navigation points throughout larger codes, standards, manuals, and technical references.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial attention. Practice using Article 100 definitions, the index, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Residential study should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, load calculations, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, receptacles, equipment, appliances, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside code navigation. Candidates should understand voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, circuit relationships, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and practical calculations.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction safety, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system preparation. The International Residential Code provides residential construction context, while ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide and Contractors Manual 2025 add contractor business and business-law study to the package. Candidates preparing for Florida endorsement should treat technical examination preparation, Florida business requirements, licensing documentation, and business setup as related but separate parts of the overall process.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Florida contractor license. Eligible applicants must complete Florida's endorsement process through the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

  1. Confirm the Florida Residential Specialty classification. Determine whether the residential specialty scope matches the electrical work you plan to perform.
  2. Confirm endorsement eligibility. The NASCLA pathway requires an applicable license in another jurisdiction with a similar scope of work.
  3. Review Florida experience requirements. Applicants must meet an experience pathway established under Florida electrical contractor licensing law.
  4. Complete the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Prepare with the supplied references and course and pass the accepted examination.
  5. Complete the Florida business examination requirement. Florida recognizes NASCLA as substantially similar to the Residential Specialty technical examination, but the Florida business examination remains applicable.
  6. Complete the endorsement application. Submit the required out-of-state license verification, experience documentation, financial information, and other application materials.
  7. Use the included Application Service. The 1 Package includes Application Service to support organization of the licensing application process.
  8. Establish the business entity. Business Formation for an LLC or Corporation is included with The 1 Package.
  9. Obtain the EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included for the business entity.
  10. Review contractor compliance requirements. Use the included Contractor Compliance Guidance to better understand the requirements associated with operating the Florida contracting business.
  11. Receive Board approval before contracting. The applicable Florida contractor credential must be issued before performing work that requires that license.

State Requirements

The Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board regulates electrical and specialty electrical contractor licensing in Florida. Florida provides several routes to certification, including examination and endorsement pathways.

For the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor pathway, Florida recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical examination as substantially similar to the Florida Residential Specialty technical examination. Candidates using this pathway must hold a qualifying out-of-state license with a scope similar to the Florida license requested.

Florida specifically states that endorsement is similar to reciprocity but is not automatic. The Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board reviews each application and determines whether all applicable requirements have been met.

An applicant who has never been licensed in another jurisdiction cannot use the NASCLA endorsement route as a substitute for Florida's initial examination pathway. Initial applicants must follow the applicable Florida examination process.

Florida also offers a separate 10-year endorsement option for applicants who have held a similar license in another state or U.S. territory for at least 10 years when that license has been active within the required recent period. This is a separate endorsement option from examination-based endorsement.

Financial responsibility is part of the Florida Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor endorsement process. Applicants are required to provide personal credit information. When qualifying a business, Florida may also require business financial documentation such as a business credit report, financial statement, banking documentation, and verification of the applicant's role in the business.

The Florida Residential Specialty credential is more limited in scope than the unrestricted Certified Electrical Contractor license. License holders must perform work only within the classification authorized by the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

Formation of an LLC or Corporation does not itself provide authority to perform electrical contracting. The business entity, qualifying contractor, licensing status, local requirements, permits, insurance, and other applicable obligations must all be addressed before regulated contracting work is performed.
