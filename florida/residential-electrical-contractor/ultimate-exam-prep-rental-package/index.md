## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized trade examinations that participating agencies may administer or accept. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and **Residential Electrical Contractors**.

Florida's Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board currently recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for endorsement toward an applicable Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor classification. Florida specifically identifies the accepted NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an endorsement option for eligible applicants.

Passing the NASCLA examination is not itself a Florida contractor license. Florida requires applicants to satisfy the state's endorsement requirements, including holding an applicable license from another jurisdiction with a scope similar to the Florida classification being requested. The Board reviews the complete application before granting certification.

NASCLA describes the Residential Electrical Contractor examination as covering the knowledge and skills associated with electrical installation, service, repair, and maintenance typically encountered in permanent dwelling units. Current content domains include:

- Electrical theory
- Personal and jobsite safety
- Project design, estimating, and management
- Grounding and bonding
- Special system and equipment installations
- Tools and test equipment
- Circuit protection and installation
- Wiring methods and practices
- Equipment for general use
- Residential electrical code applications

Preparation should therefore combine National Electrical Code study with electrical theory, calculations, practical installation knowledge, OSHA safety, NFPA 70E electrical safety, fire alarm systems, residential construction knowledge, and contractor business subjects.

## State Requirements

The **Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board** is responsible for licensing and regulating electrical contractors in Florida. It oversees Certified Electrical Contractors and Certified Specialty Electrical Contractors along with other electrical and alarm-system license categories.

Florida recognizes the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for endorsement to the applicable Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor classification. NASCLA's Florida participating-agency information specifically states that endorsement requires the candidate to hold a license in another jurisdiction and that the other jurisdiction's license must have a scope similar to the Florida license being requested.

For an initial applicant who has never been licensed before, Florida requires the applicant to follow the Florida examination pathway. The NASCLA endorsement route is not a substitute for Florida's initial examination requirement in that situation.

Florida also offers endorsement based on an examination that the Board considers substantially similar to the Florida examination. Applicants using that pathway must demonstrate that they hold an out-of-state license and passed an accepted substantially similar examination for the applicable category.

Florida's experience requirements for endorsement can be satisfied through several different pathways. These can include qualifying management experience in the trade, supervisory or contractor experience, comprehensive training and technical education combined with supervisory experience, and other education or professional engineering pathways recognized under Florida's electrical contractor licensing laws.

Financial responsibility is also part of the endorsement process. Applicants for Certified Specialty Electrical Contractor endorsement must provide personal credit information. When qualifying a business, additional business financial information may be required, including a business credit report, financial statement, bank documentation, and verification of the applicant's relationship to the business.

Applicants must also provide proof of the applicable out-of-state license and demonstrate that the examination and license support the endorsement category being requested. Endorsement is similar to reciprocity but is **not automatic**; approval is determined by the Florida Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board.

Florida distinguishes between certified and registered electrical contractors. A certified contractor may operate statewide within the authorized scope of the certification, while a registered contractor is generally tied to local competency requirements and may operate only where that registration is recognized.

The residential specialty classification is limited in scope compared with Florida's unrestricted Certified Electrical Contractor credential. Contractors must perform only work authorized by the specific Florida license they hold.
