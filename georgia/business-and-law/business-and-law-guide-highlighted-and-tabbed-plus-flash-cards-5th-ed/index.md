### Choosing a Business Structure

Business structure affects company ownership, organization, and administrative responsibilities. Understanding these concepts helps contractors recognize important considerations when establishing a contracting business.

### Contractor Licensing

Contractor licensing establishes requirements for performing regulated construction work. Contractors must understand the classification associated with their trade and the responsibilities involved in maintaining appropriate credentials.

### Insurance and Risk Management

Construction businesses encounter financial, contractual, and operational risks. Insurance and risk management principles help contractors identify potential exposures and understand important business protection concepts.

### Business Management and Marketing

Business management requires planning, organization, communication, and recordkeeping. Marketing helps contractors communicate their services and build professional customer relationships.

The second section addresses the responsibilities involved in managing construction projects and operating a contracting company.

### Construction Estimating

Estimating involves evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor expenses, and other project costs.

Accurate estimates support organized budgets and help contractors understand the resources required for construction work.

### Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities between contractors, customers, and other project participants.

Contract management involves reviewing agreements, maintaining documentation, communicating expectations, and administering project obligations.

### Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates employees, subcontractors, equipment, materials, and construction activities.

Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize work and monitor project progress.

### Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and communication.

These responsibilities help contractors coordinate construction activities and manage available resources.

### Construction Safety

Construction safety involves identifying workplace hazards and understanding important jobsite responsibilities.

Safety awareness is relevant to plumbing, conditioned air, electrical, low voltage, and utility construction operations.

### Environmental Responsibilities

Construction activities may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions.

Understanding these responsibilities supports informed project administration and responsible construction practices.

### Employee Management

Construction businesses depend on effective coordination among employees, supervisors, and managers.

Understanding workplace communication and management responsibilities supports organized company operations.

### Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform specialized work on many construction projects.

Effective coordination involves clear agreements, organized schedules, documentation, and communication among participants.

### Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on clear expectations, consistent communication, and organized project documentation.

These principles help contractors develop professional approaches to customer service and project administration.

### Part 3: Business Administration

The third section focuses on the financial and administrative responsibilities involved in operating a construction contracting business.

### Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors monitor business income, expenses, project costs, and available resources.

Organized financial practices support informed decisions about construction projects and daily business operations.

### Construction Accounting

Accounting provides a structure for recording financial transactions and monitoring company activity.

Accurate records help contractors understand project expenses, company performance, and administrative responsibilities.

### Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of money received and expenses paid.

Contractors benefit from understanding how project payments, payroll, materials, and operating expenses affect available funds.

### Tax Fundamentals

Business taxes are an important part of contractor financial administration.

Understanding general tax concepts and maintaining organized records supports responsible construction business management.

### Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights.

Understanding lien concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of contracts, payment documentation, and financial administration.
