The first section of the NASCLA guide introduces the decisions involved in establishing and organizing a contracting business.

Business planning helps contractors establish company objectives, understand available resources, and organize operating activities.

Business structure affects ownership, organization, and administrative responsibilities.

Understanding different structures helps contractors recognize the considerations involved in establishing a professional contracting company.

### Contractor Licensing

### Insurance and Risk Management

Contracting businesses encounter financial, operational, and contractual risks.

Insurance and risk management principles help contractors identify potential exposures and understand important business protection concepts.

### Business Management and Marketing

Business management requires planning, communication, organization, and accurate records.

### Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section addresses the responsibilities involved in operating a construction business and managing projects.

### Construction Estimating

Estimating involves evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor expenses, and other project costs.

Accurate estimates help contractors prepare organized budgets and understand the resources required for construction projects.

### Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities among contractors, customers, and other project participants.

Contract management involves understanding agreements, maintaining documentation, and administering project obligations.

### Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates employees, subcontractors, equipment, material deliveries, and construction activities.

Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize work and monitor progress.

### Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and communication.

These responsibilities help contractors coordinate resources and manage construction activities.

### Construction Safety

Construction safety involves recognizing workplace hazards and understanding jobsite responsibilities.

Safety awareness is relevant to plumbing, conditioned air, electrical, low voltage, and utility construction operations.

### Environmental Responsibilities

Construction activities may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions.

Understanding these responsibilities supports informed project administration and responsible construction practices.

### Employee and Subcontractor Relationships

Construction businesses depend on effective coordination among employees, subcontractors, supervisors, and managers.

Clear agreements, organized schedules, and consistent communication help support professional project operations.

### Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on accurate expectations, consistent communication, and organized documentation.

Understanding these concepts helps contractors develop effective customer management practices.

### Part 3: Business Administration

The third section focuses on financial and administrative responsibilities involved in operating a contracting business.

### Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors monitor company income, expenses, project costs, and available resources.

Organized financial practices support informed decisions about business operations.

### Construction Accounting

Accounting provides a structure for recording financial transactions and monitoring company activity.

Accurate records help contractors understand project expenses and business performance.

### Cash Flow Management

Cash flow involves the timing of money received and expenses paid.

Contractors benefit from understanding how project payments, employee wages, materials, and operating expenses affect available funds.

### Tax Fundamentals

Business taxes are an important part of contractor financial administration.

Understanding general tax concepts and maintaining accurate records supports responsible construction business management.

### Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights.

Studying lien concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of agreements, payment documentation, and administrative responsibilities.
