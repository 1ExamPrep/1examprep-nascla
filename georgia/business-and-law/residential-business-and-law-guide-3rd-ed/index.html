The examination is administered by PSI Services for the Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors.

Open Book Test

The Georgia Business and Law Examination is an open book test administered by PSI Services.

The NASCLA Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors, 3rd Edition is the authorized reference for this examination.

Open-book testing requires more than locating information. Candidates must recognize the business or legal principle involved in a question, find relevant material, and apply the information correctly.

For example, questions concerning construction agreements may involve contract administration. Other questions may address taxes, financial management, licensing, estimating, employee responsibilities, or lien laws.

PSI permits authorized reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent reference tabs are permitted, while temporary tabs and loose notes are not allowed.

The reference must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain additional papers.

Licensing Steps

Georgia residential and general contractor licensing is administered through the Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors.

The licensing process depends on the contractor classification and whether the applicant seeks an individual or qualifying agent credential.

  1. Identify Your Contractor Classification: Determine whether your intended work falls under Residential Basic, Residential Light Commercial, General Contractor, or General Contractor Limited Tier licensing.
  2. Review Georgia Licensing Requirements: Understand the qualifications, experience, financial responsibilities, and documentation requirements associated with your classification.
  3. Prepare Your Application: Organize the business and applicant information required by the Georgia State Licensing Board.
  4. Submit the Application: Complete the applicable licensing application process.
  5. Obtain Examination Approval: The Board determines applicant eligibility for the required examinations.
  6. Prepare for Business and Law: Study the Georgia NASCLA Contractor's Guide, 3rd Edition.
  7. Prepare for Trade Testing: Review the construction knowledge associated with your specific contractor classification.
  8. Schedule Your Examinations: Follow the applicable PSI examination registration and scheduling procedures after approval.
  9. Complete Required Testing: Take the Georgia Business and Law Examination and the applicable trade examination.
  10. Complete the Licensing Process: Satisfy the remaining requirements established by the Georgia State Licensing Board.

The Business and Law Examination focuses on the administrative responsibilities involved in operating a construction business, while the applicable trade examination evaluates construction knowledge associated with the classification.

Test Information and Study Materials

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first section of the Georgia NASCLA guide introduces the decisions involved in establishing and organizing a construction business.

Business Planning

Business planning helps contractors identify company objectives, understand operating expenses, evaluate available resources, and organize business activities.

A structured business plan provides direction for company operations and supports informed decision-making.

Business Organization

Business organization involves selecting an appropriate company structure and understanding the administrative responsibilities associated with operating a contracting business.

Review business structures and how organizational decisions affect construction company management.

Contractor Licensing

Contractor licensing establishes requirements for performing regulated construction work. Applicants must understand their classification and the importance of meeting applicable licensing requirements.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction businesses encounter risks involving projects, property, employees, and contractual responsibilities.

Insurance and risk management concepts help contractors recognize potential business exposures and understand methods of managing professional responsibilities.

Business Management and Marketing

Effective management involves planning, communication, recordkeeping, and consistent decisions.

Marketing helps construction businesses communicate their services and build professional relationships with prospective customers.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section addresses the responsibilities involved in managing construction projects and operating a construction company.

Estimating and Bidding

Estimating and Bidding is one of the largest subject areas on the Georgia Business and Law Examination.

Estimating requires evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other project expenses.

Bidding involves using project information and estimates to prepare construction proposals. Understanding these principles helps contractors develop organized approaches to project pricing.

Contract Management

Construction contracts establish the responsibilities of contractors, customers, and other project participants.

Study contract terms, documentation, agreements, performance responsibilities, and contract administration.

Contract management helps contractors recognize the importance of clear communication and organized project records.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates construction activities, employees, subcontractors, equipment, and material deliveries.

Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize work and monitor construction project progress.

Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and communication.

These responsibilities help contractors coordinate construction activities and manage available resources.

Construction Safety

Construction safety involves recognizing workplace hazards and understanding important jobsite responsibilities.

Review safety management principles and the role of organized workplace practices in professional contracting.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction work may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions.

Understanding environmental responsibilities supports informed project management and construction operations.

Employee and Subcontractor Relationships

Construction businesses depend on effective communication and coordination among employees, subcontractors, and managers.

Study the responsibilities involved in supervising personnel, coordinating specialized work, and maintaining professional relationships.

Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships involve clear communication, accurate expectations, and organized project documentation.

Understanding these concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of consistent communication throughout construction projects.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section focuses on financial and administrative responsibilities associated with operating a construction company.

Financial Management

Business Organization and Financial Management is one of the largest examination categories.

Financial management helps contractors monitor income, expenses, project costs, and available resources.

Understanding these concepts supports informed business decisions and organized financial operations.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides a structure for recording financial transactions and monitoring business activity.

Accurate records help contractors understand project expenses, company performance, and administrative responsibilities.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of money received and expenses paid.

Contractors benefit from understanding how project payments, payroll, equipment, materials, and operating expenses affect available funds.

Tax Laws

Tax Laws account for five examination questions.

Understanding business tax concepts and maintaining financial records are important responsibilities for construction contractors.

The NASCLA guide provides material supporting review of contractor tax fundamentals.

Labor Laws

Labor Laws also account for five examination questions.

Review employment responsibilities, payroll practices, and important principles associated with managing construction employees.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights.

Studying lien law concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of contracts, payment administration, and accurate project documentation.
