Licensing Steps

Georgia residential and general contractor licensing is administered through the Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors.

The licensing process depends on the contractor classification and whether an applicant seeks an individual or qualifying agent credential.

  1. Identify Your Contractor Classification: Determine whether your intended work falls under Residential Basic, Residential Light Commercial, General Contractor, or General Contractor Limited Tier licensing.
  2. Review Georgia Licensing Requirements: Understand the qualifications, experience, financial responsibilities, and documentation associated with the classification.
  3. Prepare Application Information: Organize the applicant and business records required by the Board.
  4. Submit Your Application: Complete the applicable Georgia contractor licensing application.
  5. Obtain Examination Approval: Follow the eligibility procedures established by the Georgia licensing board.
  6. Prepare for Business and Law: Study the Georgia NASCLA Contractor's Guide, 3rd Edition.
  7. Practice Reference Navigation: Use the highlighted passages and permanent tabs to locate important information.
  8. Prepare for Trade Testing: Review the technical construction topics associated with your contractor classification.
  9. Complete Required Examinations: Take the Business and Law Examination and applicable trade examination.
  10. Complete Licensing Requirements: Meet the remaining conditions established by the Georgia State Licensing Board.

Business and law preparation complements technical construction knowledge by addressing the administrative responsibilities involved in operating a contracting company.

Test Information and Study Materials

Business Organization

Business organization involves establishing and managing a construction company. Contractors must understand the importance of selecting a business structure, organizing administrative responsibilities, and maintaining professional operations.

These concepts help candidates recognize how organizational decisions influence business management and financial responsibilities.

Licensing Requirements

Contractor licensing establishes qualifications for performing regulated construction work. Applicants must understand the classifications and requirements established by the Georgia State Licensing Board.

Studying licensing concepts helps candidates recognize the importance of meeting professional and administrative responsibilities.

Estimating and Bidding

Estimating and Bidding is one of the largest categories on the Georgia Business and Law Examination.

Construction estimating involves reviewing labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor expenses, and other project costs.

Bidding involves applying those estimates to construction proposals. Understanding these principles helps contractors develop organized approaches to project pricing.

Construction Contracts

Construction contracts establish responsibilities between contractors, customers, and other project participants.

Contract management includes reviewing agreement terms, maintaining documentation, communicating expectations, and administering construction obligations.

Understanding contract principles supports organized project management and professional customer relationships.

Project Management

Project management combines planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and communication.

Contractors must coordinate resources and monitor construction activities to maintain organized operations.

Studying project management principles helps candidates understand the responsibilities involved in coordinating construction work.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights.

Understanding lien concepts helps contractors recognize the importance of accurate agreements, payment administration, and project documentation.

Project Management and Lien Law account for six questions on the Georgia Business and Law Examination.

Tax Laws

Tax Laws account for five examination questions.

Understanding business tax concepts and maintaining accurate financial records are important responsibilities for construction contractors.

Review the tax fundamentals addressed in the NASCLA guide and their relationship to construction business administration.

Labor Laws

Labor Laws also account for five examination questions.

Study employment responsibilities, payroll practices, and administrative principles associated with managing construction employees.

Understanding labor law concepts supports informed workplace management.

Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors monitor company income, expenses, project costs, and available resources.

Understanding accounting and cash flow principles supports informed decisions and organized business operations.

Business Organization and Financial Management account for seven examination questions.

Risk Management

Construction businesses encounter financial, operational, and contractual risks.

Risk management involves recognizing potential exposures and understanding insurance and business protection concepts.

Environmental and Safety Responsibilities

Construction safety involves identifying workplace hazards and understanding important jobsite responsibilities.

Environmental considerations may involve construction materials, waste, and site conditions.

Understanding these subjects supports responsible construction management.
