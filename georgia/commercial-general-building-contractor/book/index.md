## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is administered by **PSI Services** and is accepted by the Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors for the applicable commercial general contractor trade examination requirement.

The examination covers construction methods, building systems, project management, safety, and procurement responsibilities.

- **Examination:** NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
- **Georgia Licensing Authority:** Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors.
- **Testing Provider:** PSI Services.
- **Examination Format:** Computer-based, multiple-choice.
- **Number of Scored Questions:** 115.
- **Time Allowed:** 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes.
- **Passing Score:** 81 correct answers.
- **Test Type:** Open book.
- **Primary Subjects:** General construction requirements, sitework, concrete, masonry, metals, wood, building finishes, mechanical and electrical systems, and contracting procedures.

The NASCLA examination focuses on the trade and technical portion of commercial general contractor qualification. Georgia also requires the applicable **Georgia Business and Law examination** for its General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier licensing pathways.

## Open Book Test

The **NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an open book test**. Candidates may consult the publications specifically approved in the current PSI candidate information bulletin.

Approved references include construction codes, safety standards, technical manuals, and textbooks covering commercial construction and project management.

Candidates must provide their own approved publications. Reference books are not supplied at the examination center.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed with permanent tabs before the examination. Handwritten notes, unauthorized inserted materials, and loose papers are prohibited.

Temporary indexing tabs, including removable adhesive notes, are not permitted. Writing in references during the examination is prohibited.

A silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator is permitted under the published examination rules.

Because the examination covers numerous construction disciplines, familiarity with the organization of approved references is especially valuable. Practice identifying which publication addresses a subject before searching its index or table of contents.

## Georgia State Requirements

The **Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors** regulates commercial general contractor licensing.

Georgia recognizes two commercial general contractor categories that use the NASCLA trade examination:

- **General Contractor:** Commercial general contracting without an individual project-value restriction.
- **General Contractor Limited Tier:** Commercial general contracting subject to the state's applicable individual contract-value limitation.

Georgia's General Contractor classification requires a minimum net worth of $150,000, while the General Contractor Limited Tier classification requires a minimum net worth of $25,000.

The Board also requires general liability insurance of at least $500,000 per occurrence for these commercial general contractor categories, with workers' compensation insurance where required by Georgia law.

Commercial general contractor applicants must demonstrate qualifying education or construction experience. Georgia recognizes pathways involving construction-related degrees, combinations of education and practical experience, and qualifying construction industry experience.

Applicants must also satisfy applicable personal, business, and licensing qualifications.

The NASCLA examination is a trade examination accepted by multiple participating licensing jurisdictions. Passing it does not automatically create a national contractor license or authorize construction work in every participating state.

## Commercial Construction Fundamentals

Commercial general contractors must understand how building components and construction activities work together.

Study construction sequences, material selection, building systems, construction drawings, inspections, and project coordination. Review the responsibilities involved in organizing multiple trades around a common construction schedule.

## Site Construction and Excavation

Site development prepares a property for building construction.

Study clearing, grading, excavation, drainage, underground utilities, and site stabilization. Review soil conditions, excavation safety, backfill, and construction equipment considerations.

Practice interpreting construction scenarios involving foundations, site preparation, and utility coordination.

## Concrete and Reinforcement

Concrete is used in foundations, slabs, columns, walls, and other structural components.

Study concrete materials, formwork, reinforcing steel, placement, consolidation, curing, and quality control.

Review construction practices that influence concrete strength, durability, and structural performance.

## Masonry Construction

Masonry systems involve brick, concrete masonry units, stone, mortar, and reinforcement.

Study masonry materials, installation methods, joints, wall construction, and structural considerations.

Review common masonry defects and the principles of proper construction workmanship.

## Structural Steel and Metals

Commercial construction frequently incorporates structural steel and other metal building components.

Study steel framing, connections, erection procedures, decking, and structural coordination.

Review handling, installation, and construction safety considerations associated with metal structures.

## Wood Framing and Carpentry

Wood construction remains relevant in commercial buildings and associated structural assemblies.

Study framing systems, trusses, sheathing, connectors, and material properties.

Review practical installation methods and the importance of proper bracing and structural support.

## Roofing and Moisture Protection

Roofing and waterproofing systems protect buildings against environmental exposure.

Study roofing materials, flashing, insulation, drainage, and weatherproofing assemblies.

Review how moisture protection relates to building performance and construction detailing.

## Doors, Windows, Glazing, and Finishes

Building openings and finishes require appropriate material selection and installation.

Study doors, windows, glazing, drywall, interior finishes, and related construction details.

Review accessibility principles, weather protection, and coordination with surrounding building systems.

## Mechanical, Plumbing, and Electrical Coordination

Commercial construction projects involve several interacting building systems.

Study basic HVAC, plumbing, electrical distribution, and equipment coordination principles.

Review how construction managers coordinate specialty contractors, penetrations, equipment access, and installation schedules.

## Construction Safety and OSHA

Construction safety is fundamental to responsible commercial project management.

Study fall protection, excavation hazards, scaffolding, ladders, personal protective equipment, and material handling.

Develop familiarity with the OSHA construction standards and their application to common jobsite activities.

## Procurement, Contracts, and Project Management

Procurement and contracting requirements form the largest NASCLA examination subject area.

Study construction contracts, subcontractor coordination, purchasing, scheduling, project documentation, change orders, and project administration.

Review estimating concepts, contract responsibilities, and the importance of clear construction records.
