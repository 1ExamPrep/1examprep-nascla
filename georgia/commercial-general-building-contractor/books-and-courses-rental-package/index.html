Exam Details

The trade examination used for Georgia General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier applicants is the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Open Book Test

The Georgia NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved references during the examination, subject to testing-center reference rules.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and efficient navigation. Having access to a reference does not eliminate the need to understand the subject. Searching through unfamiliar books for every question can consume valuable examination time. A stronger strategy is to study the concepts in advance, become familiar with the organization of each major reference, and use the books to confirm specific code provisions, tables, standards, procedures, or technical details when necessary.

The rental books in this package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Candidates should still spend time working directly with the books before the examination so the location and structure of important information becomes familiar.

Test Information and Study Materials

A large reference set is most valuable when you know how to use it efficiently. The NASCLA commercial building examination covers numerous construction disciplines, so your preparation should combine technical knowledge with repeated practice locating information in the books.

Start by developing familiarity with each book's table of contents, index, chapter structure, terminology, and major subject areas. During practice, identify which references are most useful for specific types of questions. This builds the association between a construction topic and the book most likely to contain the answer.

Timed practice is equally important. With 115 questions and 330 minutes available, spending too much time searching for one detail can affect your ability to complete the examination. Practice answering questions you know first, using references for confirmation when needed, and maintaining a steady pace throughout the session.
