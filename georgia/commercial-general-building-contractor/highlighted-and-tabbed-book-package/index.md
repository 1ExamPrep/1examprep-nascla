## Exam Details

This complete reference package supports preparation for two separate examinations associated with Georgia commercial general contractor licensing: the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Georgia Business and Law examination.

The NASCLA examination addresses the technical knowledge used to plan, manage, supervise, and complete commercial building projects. Its broad subject coverage reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general contractor coordinating multiple construction trades and project phases.

The listed references address construction jobsite management, project management, estimating, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast concrete, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, stormwater controls, green building, gypsum construction, exterior insulation systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

The Georgia Business and Law examination focuses on the legal, financial, administrative, and operational responsibilities involved in conducting a contracting business in Georgia. Study areas include contractor licensing, business organization, financial management, contracts, employment responsibilities, insurance, bonding, safety, liens, project administration, and other state-specific business requirements.

Georgia uses PSI to administer its contractor examination program. Eligibility to take the Georgia licensing examinations is determined through the State Licensing Board for Residential and Commercial General Contractors. Candidates must follow the Board’s application and examination-approval procedures before scheduling.

General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier candidates must pass both the Georgia Business and Law examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors within the eligibility period provided after approval. Passing the examinations does not independently issue a contractor license. Applicants must complete the licensing application and satisfy all other Georgia requirements.

Candidates who have previously passed the NASCLA examination may transmit their results to Georgia through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The transcript is paired with the applicant’s Georgia licensing materials, but it does not replace the state Business and Law examination or the remaining application requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing center. The examination site does not provide the complete book library.

The open-book format allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for detailed study. The reference set contains thousands of pages across codes, textbooks, manuals, standards, and technical guides. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the books may spend too much time searching for information during the examination.

A reliable open-book strategy starts with recognizing the subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, or technical tables to locate the relevant material.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question about concrete placement or field quality may involve The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. A reinforcing-bar question may require Placing Reinforcing Bars. Steel construction questions may involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders.

Accessibility questions may rely on ICC A117.1, while broader commercial code questions may require the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and jobsite safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

The highlighting and permanent tabs in this package support faster reference navigation. Candidates should still practice with the books before test day so they understand what each tab represents and how the highlighted material fits within the surrounding chapter or code section.

All references brought into the testing room must comply with the current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin before test day and avoid adding loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## State Requirements

The Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and Commercial General Contractors regulates commercial general contractor licensing. A person or business offering or performing covered general contractor services for compensation must hold the appropriate state license unless the work falls within a recognized exemption.

Georgia offers General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier licenses. The unlimited General Contractor license and the Limited Tier license have different scopes and financial requirements. Applicants should choose the license category that matches the size and nature of the projects they intend to perform.

A license may be issued to an individual or connected with a business through a qualifying agent. The application type should reflect how the applicant intends to conduct business. Qualifying-agent applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with representing and qualifying the business entity.

Georgia candidates applying by examination must pass the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Georgia Business and Law examination. The Business and Law requirement also applies in situations where an applicant uses a previously earned NASCLA result.

Applicants must demonstrate the experience required for the selected license. The documentation should accurately describe the applicant’s responsibilities, project involvement, supervisory duties, and commercial construction background.

Financial responsibility is part of the licensing process. The required financial information depends on the license type and application category. Applicants should submit current, complete documents that meet the Board’s instructions.

Insurance and business documentation may also be required. Applicants should review the current checklist for their exact application type and provide every requested item. Missing forms, incomplete affidavits, unresolved qualifying-agent information, or insufficient financial materials can delay the licensing review.

Georgia contractor licenses must be renewed according to the state’s renewal schedule. Commercial general contractor licenses are renewed by June 30 of even-numbered years. License holders are responsible for maintaining the required status and completing renewal before the applicable deadline.
