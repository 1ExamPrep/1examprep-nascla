Exam Details

Georgia General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier applicants applying by examination are required to pass the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Georgia Business & Law Examination.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions. Candidates must answer at least 81 questions correctly, or 70%, to pass. The examination provides 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, for completion.

The NASCLA examination evaluates commercial construction knowledge involving planning and estimating, financial and project management, safety, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood foundations and framing, thermal and moisture protection, doors and glazing, finishes, specialty equipment, and other commercial building subjects.

The separate Georgia Business & Law Examination contains 50 questions. Candidates must answer at least 35 questions correctly for a passing score of 70%. The examination provides 120 minutes for completion.

The Georgia Business & Law content outline includes:

  • Licensing Requirements: 5 questions
  • Estimating and Bidding: 7 questions
  • Business Organization and Financial Management: 7 questions
  • Tax Laws: 5 questions
  • Labor Laws: 5 questions
  • Project Management and Lien Law: 6 questions
  • Contracts: 6 questions
  • Risk Management: 4 questions
  • Environmental and Safety: 5 questions

Preparing for both exams requires a combination of construction knowledge, state-specific business and law review, and efficient use of reference materials. Candidates should practice moving between practical construction subjects and technical regulations while maintaining a steady testing pace.

Open Book Test

Both the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination and the Georgia Business & Law Examination are open book tests.

The Georgia Business & Law examination permits the Georgia NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors, 3rd Edition. The NASCLA commercial examination uses its designated commercial construction reference library.

Permitted reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing. References may also use permanent tabs. Temporary removable tabs such as Post-It notes are not permitted. References must otherwise comply with PSI's examination-center requirements, and candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted books.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity especially important. Candidates should know which book covers a subject before beginning a search. Licensing Steps

Georgia Commercial General Contractor licensing is administered by the Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing process in addition to passing the required examinations.

  1. Select the appropriate license type. Georgia offers General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier licenses for individuals and qualifying agents associated with business entities.
  2. Create an account and apply through Georgia's licensing system. Applicants select the appropriate license type and submit the required application information and documentation.
  3. Document education and experience. Applicants must demonstrate that they satisfy the education and practical experience requirements applicable to commercial general contractor licensure.
  4. Provide project and experience documentation. The application process requires documentation supporting the applicant's commercial construction qualifications.
  5. Receive examination approval. Applicants proceeding by examination must receive approval before taking the required Georgia contractor examinations.
  6. Pass the NASCLA examination. General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier applicants must satisfy the NASCLA commercial trade examination requirement unless another approved licensing pathway applies.
  7. Pass the Georgia Business & Law Examination. Georgia requires this separate state examination for Residential and General Contractor licensing by examination.
  8. Provide financial and insurance documentation. Applicants must meet applicable net worth, general liability insurance, and workers' compensation requirements.
  9. Complete Board review. The Georgia State Licensing Board reviews the application and supporting documentation before approving licensure.

State Requirements

Georgia requires Commercial General Contractor applicants to satisfy experience, competency, financial responsibility, examination, and insurance requirements established by the General Contractor Division.

An applicant for a Georgia General Contractor license must be at least 21 years old and meet the Board's standards for competency, ability, integrity, character, and financial responsibility.

Georgia provides multiple qualifying combinations of education and construction experience. One pathway includes a bachelor's degree from an accredited four-year college or university in engineering, architecture, construction management, building construction, or another field accepted by the Division, together with at least one year of qualifying general contractor experience. Another pathway allows an acceptable combination of college-level academic credits and proven practical experience totaling at least four years.

Financial requirements depend on the license category. Georgia currently identifies a minimum net worth of $150,000 for a Commercial General Contractor and $25,000 for a Commercial General Contractor Limited Tier.

General liability insurance is also required. Georgia regulations require proof of general liability insurance of at least $500,000 for General Contractor licensure. Workers' compensation insurance is required when applicable under Georgia law.

Applicants operating through a company rather than in their personal name generally apply as a qualifying agent associated with the business entity. Georgia's application process requires qualifying-agent documentation and proof that the business is appropriately registered when applicable.
