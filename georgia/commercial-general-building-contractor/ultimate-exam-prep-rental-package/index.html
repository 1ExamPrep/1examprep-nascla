Exam Details

The Georgia commercial general contractor licensing path uses the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the trade examination requirement associated with General Contractor and General Contractor Limited Tier applicants. Georgia candidates must also prepare for the separate Georgia Business & Law examination.

  • Trade Examination: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Exam Format: Open book
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • Additional Georgia Examination: Georgia Business & Law

The NASCLA examination is broad because commercial general contractors work across many construction disciplines. Preparation can involve building code requirements, safety, project management, structural concrete, masonry, framing, steel systems, roofing, accessibility, environmental responsibilities, mechanical and electrical systems, and other areas of commercial construction.

With 115 questions and 330 minutes available, candidates also need to prepare for the pace of the examination. Strong preparation combines knowledge with efficient decision-making and the ability to determine quickly when a reference lookup is necessary.

Open Book Test

The Georgia NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor trade examination is an open-book test. Candidates can use approved references during the examination when those materials comply with the testing provider's reference-handling requirements.

An open-book exam still requires substantial preparation. Having the answer somewhere inside a reference book is only useful when you can identify the correct reference and locate the information efficiently. Candidates who attempt to search through every book for every question can lose valuable time.

A stronger approach is to understand the major concepts first and use your reference materials to confirm specific requirements, technical details, tables, standards, exceptions, or code provisions. Repeated practice with the same books helps you build a mental map of where information is located.

The books in this Ultimate Exam Prep Rental Package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. That preparation gives you an organized starting point, but regular use of the books during your study period remains important. Practice moving between the table of contents, index, tabs, chapters, and major technical sections so the books become familiar tools rather than unfamiliar resources on exam day.

References used during testing must comply with applicable exam-center rules. Exam references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed but must otherwise remain unmarked. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted, and tabs used in examination references must be permanent rather than temporary removable tabs.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective NASCLA preparation requires a balance between construction knowledge and reference-navigation skills. The large number of books associated with the commercial general building examination can feel overwhelming if candidates approach every reference equally from the beginning.

Use your course and reference library together. Study a construction topic through the course, then locate the corresponding information inside the relevant reference. This reinforces the subject while helping you remember which book contains the information.

Practice with the books frequently rather than saving reference navigation for the final days before testing. Learn how each index is organized, how major chapters are structured, and where commonly used tables or technical requirements appear. Repetition can make movement through a large reference library more efficient.

Timed practice is another important part of preparation. The NASCLA examination contains 115 questions and provides 330 minutes. Develop a rhythm that allows you to answer questions you understand, identify questions requiring a lookup, and avoid becoming trapped in a long search for a single answer.

Use highlighting and tabs as navigation aids rather than substitutes for studying. The rental books arrive highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation, giving you a structured starting point. Your goal during the preparation period should be to become comfortable enough with the books that you recognize where to begin looking when a technical question requires confirmation.
