# Georgia NASCLA Exam and General Contractor License Guide

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 13 min read · 9 official sources cited

If you want to run commercial building projects in Georgia, the state expects you to hold a commercial general contractor license, and the testing route to that license runs through two exams: the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and the Georgia Business and Law exam. Both are open book, both are given by PSI, and both have to be passed within the window the board gives you after it approves your application.

## Does Georgia accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. Georgia goes further than most states: for its commercial general contractor licenses, the NASCLA commercial exam is not an alternative to a state trade exam, it is the trade exam. The board's application for a General Contractor or General Contractor Limited Tier license by examination requires you to pass both the Georgia Business and Law exam and the NASCLA exam. Georgia's licensing board also appears on [NASCLA's list of participating agencies](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies) that accept the commercial exam.

If you already passed the NASCLA commercial exam for another state, you do not have to sit for it again. The application tells you to buy your NASCLA transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database at [ned.nascla.org](https://ned.nascla.org) and submit it to the board. You still have to take and pass the Georgia Business and Law exam, and you still have to meet every other Georgia requirement, including experience, net worth and insurance.

Passing NASCLA by itself never produces a Georgia license. Think of it as one required piece of a larger file the board reviews before it issues anything.

## Who issues the Georgia general contractor license?

Commercial general contractor licenses are issued by the [State Licensing Board for Residential and Commercial General Contractors](https://sos.ga.gov/state-licensing-board-residential-and-general-contractors), which sits inside the Georgia Secretary of State's licensing division and meets in Macon. The board has two divisions: a General Contractors Division and a Residential Contractors Division. The General Contractors Division handles the unlimited General Contractor license and the General Contractor Limited Tier license. The Residential Division handles Residential Basic and Residential Light Commercial.

Applications go through the Secretary of State's online GOALS portal. The board says it no longer accepts paper applications and will send them back. GOALS is also where you check status and renew later.

Plumbing, conditioned air, electrical, low voltage and utility contractors are licensed separately, under the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board and its trade divisions. Those trades have their own exams and their own Georgia edition of the NASCLA business guide, which we cover below. If you are in one of those trades, check your division's page for the exact exams you need.

## Georgia general contractor license types and the exams each needs

Georgia's commercial license comes in two tiers, and both take the same two exams. The difference is in project size and financial strength.

Residential licenses use different trade exams. PSI's Georgia bulletin lists a Residential Basic exam and a Residential Light Commercial exam alongside Business and Law, so the NASCLA commercial exam is not part of those paths.

- General Contractor (unlimited): no per-project cap. Requires the NASCLA commercial exam and the Georgia Business and Law exam, an affirmed minimum net worth of $150,000, and general liability coverage of at least $500,000 per occurrence.
- General Contractor Limited Tier: limited to contracts of no more than $1,000,000 each, and change orders cannot push a contract past that limit. Requires the same two exams, a minimum net worth of $25,000, and the same $500,000 per occurrence general liability coverage. To move up later, you apply for the unlimited license and meet its financial rules.
- Individual or qualifying agent: either tier can be held by you personally or by a company that you qualify as its qualifying agent. A qualifying agent application also needs a certificate from the Corporations Division showing the business is authorized to operate in Georgia, and the company name must match that record exactly.

## How to get a Georgia general contractor license, step by step

Here is the order most applicants follow. Your approval letter and the current application checklist control the details, so keep both handy.

1. Pick your tier (unlimited or Limited Tier) and decide whether you are applying as an individual or as a qualifying agent for a company.
2. Confirm you meet the age and experience rules. You must be at least 21, and you need one of the three education and experience options described further down this page.
3. Gather your documents: the notarized affidavit of eligibility signed by a licensed commercial general contractor, a notarized project reference affidavit from a registered architect, professional engineer or building official, your financial affirmations, and a secure and verifiable ID document.
4. Apply through GOALS and pay the application fee. Answer any deficiency notice quickly; the packet says an application can be withdrawn if problems are not fixed within 60 days.
5. Receive your exam approval letter. It opens a one-year eligibility period for testing.
6. Register and schedule with PSI for the Georgia Business and Law exam and the NASCLA commercial exam. If you passed NASCLA before, send your transcript through ned.nascla.org instead of retesting.
7. Pass both exams within the eligibility year.
8. Provide your certificate of insurance showing at least $500,000 per occurrence in general liability, plus workers' compensation if you have three or more employees, with the board listed as certificate holder.
9. Wait for the board to finish its review and issue the license. Do not offer or perform commercial general contracting work until you hold it.

## The Georgia NASCLA exam: format, PSI and scheduling

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions, a 330 minute time limit, and a passing mark of 70 percent. PSI's Georgia bulletin puts that at 81 correct. The exam may also contain unscored pretest questions. It is open book, but the test center does not supply the library. You bring your own approved references.

PSI's Georgia bulletin says reference books may be highlighted, underlined or indexed with permanent tabs. You may not write notes in them or slip in loose papers, so removable sticky notes and handwritten inserts are out. Our pre-printed tabs and highlighted sets are made to stay inside those limits, but always read the current bulletin before test day.

You can register with PSI online at any hour or by phone. The bulletin allows scheduling as late as 7:00 p.m. Eastern the day before an exam if seats are open, and it asks you to cancel at least two days ahead to keep your fee. Arrive 30 minutes early with valid photo ID.

Retakes are limited. The one-year eligibility window applies to both exams, and if you pass one and fail the other you only repeat the one you failed. The PSI bulletin and the board's FAQ describe the number of allowed attempts a little differently, so check your approval letter and ask the board if you are close to the limit. Running out of attempts or time means reapplying.

## The Georgia Business and Law exam

The Georgia Business and Law exam is the state-specific half of the license. According to PSI's Georgia bulletin it has 50 questions, a 120 minute time limit, and a 70 percent passing score, which works out to 35 correct. Like NASCLA, it is open book.

The one approved reference is the Georgia-NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Georgia State Licensing Board edition, 3rd Edition. The same rules apply: highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs are allowed, but writing and loose papers are not. Make sure you buy the 3rd Edition for the residential and general contractor board. A different Georgia edition exists for the Construction Industry Licensing Board, and it is the wrong book for this exam.

Expect questions on licensing law, business organization, contracts, estimating, insurance and bonding, employment and tax obligations, safety, lien rights and financial management. Fifty questions in two hours gives you a bit over two minutes each, which is plenty if you know where things live in the book and very tight if you do not.

Trade contractors under the Construction Industry Licensing Board use the Georgia NASCLA guide, Construction Industry Licensing Board 5th Edition. Exam formats there vary by trade, so read the candidate bulletin for your division before you buy.

## Georgia general contractor license experience requirements

Georgia gives you three ways to qualify on education and experience, listed below. The Limited Tier rule and the board's application packet describe them the same way.

Whichever option you use, the experience must be commercial. The packet says residential or handyman work alone does not count. It also asks whether you supervised a commercial project in the last five years, with primary responsibility for getting it done, and whether you worked with a licensed architect or engineer of record on that job. Describe your role on each project honestly and in detail, since the board checks it against your affidavits.

- A four-year degree in engineering, architecture, construction management, building construction or an approved related field, plus at least one year of experience working as or for a licensed commercial general contractor.
- A mix of college credits and practical experience that adds up to at least four years.
- At least four years of construction-related experience, with at least two years as or for a licensed commercial general contractor and at least one year in administration, marketing, accounting, estimating, drafting, engineering, supervision or project management.

## Net worth, tax and insurance requirements

On the financial side you affirm the minimum net worth for your tier ($150,000 unlimited, $25,000 Limited Tier) and that state and federal taxes, payroll withholding and unemployment taxes are paid for the past three years. A bankruptcy in the last ten years needs a letter and supporting schedules. Audited statements are no longer required up front, but the board may ask for them.

For insurance, both tiers need general liability of at least $500,000 per occurrence. Workers' compensation is required if you have three or more employees. The certificate of insurance is due before the license issues, not with the application.

## Reciprocity: Georgia and other states

The board's FAQ lists reciprocal agreements for the unlimited Commercial General Contractor license with Louisiana and Mississippi. To use one, your license must have come from passing that state's exams, you must live in that state or have your business chartered there, and the license must have been active for at least three years with no discipline for law violations.

The NASCLA exam is a separate kind of portability. Once you pass it, your score sits in the NASCLA National Examination Database, and other agencies that accept the commercial exam can receive your transcript. Every state still applies its own rules, often including its own business and law exam, so a Georgia license plus a NASCLA pass gives you a head start elsewhere rather than an automatic license.

## How to study for two open book exams

Keep the two exams separate in your plan. Business and Law draws on one book, so you can learn its chapter order well. NASCLA draws on a stack of codes, manuals and textbooks, so the skill you need is picking the right book fast.

For NASCLA, group the library by subject: project management and jobsite management, safety under 29 CFR 1926, the building code and accessibility, concrete and reinforcing, steel, masonry, carpentry and trusses, roofing, excavation, and mechanical and electrical systems. Take timed practice questions and, for each one, name the subject, choose the book, then go to the tab or index. Do this until it is automatic.

With 330 minutes for 115 questions you have under three minutes each. Answer what you know, mark the slow ones, and come back. Practice with the exact books you will carry so the tabs feel familiar on test day.

## After you pass: license issue and renewal

Once your scores and insurance certificate are in, the board finishes its review and issues the license. You must notify the board in writing within 30 days if information it relies on changes, according to the Limited Tier rule.

Commercial general contractor licenses renew every two years. Under board rule 553-12-.05 the renewal fee is due by June 30 of even-numbered years, a late penalty applies from July through December 31, and failing to renew by December 31 has the same effect as revocation. Renewals go through GOALS.

The board's FAQ says commercial general contractors have no continuing education requirement. Residential Basic and Residential Light Commercial licensees do, so this changes if you also hold a residential license.

## Georgia NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Is the NASCLA exam required for a Georgia general contractor license?

Yes. If you apply by examination for the unlimited General Contractor or the Limited Tier license, you must pass the NASCLA commercial exam and the Georgia Business and Law exam. If you passed NASCLA before, you can submit your transcript through ned.nascla.org instead of retesting.

### Do I still need the Georgia Business and Law exam if I passed NASCLA in another state?

Yes. Georgia requires its own Business and Law exam even when you transfer a NASCLA score. Use the Georgia-NASCLA guide, Georgia State Licensing Board 3rd Edition, to prepare.

### Are the Georgia contractor exams open book?

Both the NASCLA commercial exam and the Georgia Business and Law exam are open book. You bring your own approved references, which may be highlighted, underlined or permanently tabbed, but not written in or stuffed with loose papers.

### What is the difference between the Georgia General Contractor and Limited Tier licenses?

The Limited Tier license caps each contract at $1,000,000 and needs a $25,000 minimum net worth. The unlimited license has no cap and needs $150,000. Both need $500,000 per occurrence in general liability insurance and the same two exams.

### How long do I have to pass the Georgia exams?

Your exam approval letter is valid for one year, and you must pass both exams in that time. Attempts within that year are limited, so check your letter and the PSI bulletin. If time runs out you will need to reapply.

### How much does it cost to get a Georgia general contractor license?

The published figures are a $200 application fee plus a $10 processing fee, $60 for the Business and Law exam and $106 for the NASCLA exam through PSI. Add the cost of reference books and insurance, and confirm current fees with the board.

### Does Georgia have reciprocity for general contractors?

The board's FAQ lists reciprocity for unlimited commercial general contractors with Louisiana and Mississippi. You need a license earned by exam in that state, residency or a business chartered there, and at least three years of active licensure without discipline.

### Do Georgia commercial general contractors need continuing education?

According to the board's FAQ, commercial general contractors are not required to complete continuing education. Licenses renew every two years by June 30 of even-numbered years.

### Which Georgia Business and Law book do I need?

For general and residential contractor licenses, use the Georgia State Licensing Board 3rd Edition. Plumbing, conditioned air, electrical, low voltage and utility contractors under the Construction Industry Licensing Board use the separate 5th Edition.
