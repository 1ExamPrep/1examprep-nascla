State Requirements

Hawaii regulates construction contracting through the Hawaii Contractors License Board.

Contractor licensing classifications include:

  • Class A: General Engineering Contractor
  • Class B: General Building Contractor
  • Class C: Specialty Contractor classifications

Each classification involves qualifications appropriate to the construction activities performed.

Hawaii contractor licensing is governed by Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 444 and associated administrative regulations.

Business responsibilities relevant to licensed contractors include understanding licensing requirements, preparing agreements, managing construction finances, supervising employees, and maintaining appropriate business records.

The Hawaii NASCLA guide addresses important management subjects, including business organization, insurance, estimating, project scheduling, workplace safety, taxes, and construction liens.

Test Information and Study Materials

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first section of the NASCLA guide introduces the decisions involved in establishing and organizing a contracting business.

Business Planning

Business planning helps contractors identify company objectives, evaluate resources, understand operating expenses, and organize professional services. A structured plan supports informed decisions about company operations.

Choosing a Business Structure

Business structure affects ownership, organization, and administrative responsibilities. Understanding business structures helps contractors recognize important considerations when establishing a company.

Contractor Licensing

Licensing establishes qualifications for performing regulated construction work. Contractors must understand the classification associated with their activities and the importance of maintaining appropriate authorization.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction businesses encounter financial, operational, and contractual risks. Understanding insurance and risk management concepts helps contractors recognize potential business exposures.

Business Management

Effective business management involves planning, communication, accurate records, and responsible decision-making. These skills help contractors organize daily company activities.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section focuses on the responsibilities involved in managing construction projects.

Construction Estimating

Estimating requires evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other project expenses. Understanding estimating principles supports accurate budgets and organized project proposals.

Estimating and Bidding

Estimating and Bidding accounts for ten examination questions. Candidates benefit from understanding how project costs, work requirements, and construction proposals relate to business decisions.

Contract Management

Contract Management is the largest examination category, with twelve questions. Study agreements, project obligations, documentation, and the responsibilities established between contractors and customers.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates employees, subcontractors, equipment, material deliveries, and construction activities. Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize projects and monitor progress.

Project Management

Project Management accounts for nine examination questions. It involves planning, budgeting, scheduling, supervision, and coordination of project resources.

Construction Safety

Construction safety involves workplace hazard awareness, employee responsibilities, and organized jobsite practices. The examination includes Safety and OSHA as a separate subject area.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction work may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions. Understanding these responsibilities supports informed project management.

Employee and Subcontractor Relationships

Construction projects require coordination among employees, subcontractors, and supervisors. Clear communication and accurate documentation support effective working relationships.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section addresses financial and administrative responsibilities associated with operating a construction company.

Financial Management

Financial Management accounts for nine examination questions. Contractors need to understand income, expenses, project costs, and available business resources.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides organized records of financial transactions. Accurate documentation supports project cost monitoring and responsible business administration.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow involves the timing of money received and expenses paid. Understanding payments, payroll, materials, and operating expenses helps contractors recognize important financial considerations.

Tax Laws

Tax Laws account for five examination questions. Study business tax concepts and the importance of maintaining organized financial records.

Labor Laws

Labor Law accounts for five examination questions. Review employment responsibilities, payroll administration, workers' compensation, and unemployment compensation concepts.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights. Understanding lien principles helps contractors recognize the importance of agreements, payment administration, and documentation.
