## Does Hawaii accept the NASCLA exam?

For the trade portion, plan on taking Hawaii's own exam. Hawaii does not appear on [NASCLA's list of participating agencies](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies) that accept the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors, and the board's application materials do not offer it as a substitute for the state trade test. If you hold a NASCLA pass from another state, ask the board directly before you assume it counts for anything here.

Where NASCLA does matter in Hawaii is the Business and Law exam. PSI's Hawaii bulletin names the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Hawaii edition, 1st Edition (2022), as the reference for that test. So when people search for a hawaii nascla exam, what they usually need is this Business and Law exam and its NASCLA study guide.

That makes Hawaii different from states like Georgia or Arizona, where the NASCLA commercial exam itself is part of the license. Here, NASCLA supplies the book, and the state supplies both tests.

## Who issues the Hawaii contractor license?

Contractor licenses are issued by the [Hawaii Contractors License Board](https://cca.hawaii.gov/pvl/boards/contractor/), part of the Professional and Vocational Licensing Division of the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs (DCCA). Contractor licensing is governed by Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 444 and the board's administrative rules in Title 16, Chapter 77.

The board licenses three kinds of license holders: an entity (such as a corporation or LLC), a sole proprietor, and a Responsible Managing Employee, or RME. An entity cannot be licensed without an RME, who is the person that meets the experience and exam requirements on the company's behalf.

Licensees manage renewals and records through the MyPVL portal, and insurance certificates are filed through the DCCA insurance portal. The board accepts applications on a monthly cycle tied to its meeting schedule, so check the filing deadline before you mail your packet.

## Hawaii contractor license classifications and exams

Hawaii sorts contractors into three broad groups, defined in [HRS 444-7](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol10_Ch0436-0474/HRS0444/HRS_0444-0007.htm). Every new applicant takes the Business and Law exam plus the trade exam for the classification they want.

Each classification has its own trade exam, and the RME must show experience in that specific classification. If you want more than one classification, you need experience and a trade exam for each.

- Class A, General Engineering Contractor: fixed works that call for specialized engineering skill, such as highways, bridges, sewers, pipelines, airports and large earthwork or paving. PSI lists the A trade exam at 100 questions, 240 minutes, closed book, 75 percent to pass.
- Class B, General Building Contractor: structures that shelter people, animals or property and involve more than two unrelated building trades, including supervising that work. PSI lists the B trade exam at 80 questions, 240 minutes, closed book, 75 percent to pass.
- Class C, Specialty Contractor: work that needs special skill in one trade, such as electrical, plumbing, drywall, painting, flooring, landscaping or roofing. Each C classification has its own trade exam.
- Business and Law: required of all new applicants regardless of class. 80 questions, 180 minutes, closed book, 75 percent to pass.

## How to get a Hawaii contractor license, step by step

This is the general order. The board's current instructions for your application type (entity, sole proprietor or RME) control the details.

1. Choose your classification (A, B or a specific C) and decide how you will be licensed: as a sole proprietor, or as an entity with an RME.
2. If you are an entity, register with the Business Registration Division first. The entity packet asks for a filed-stamped registration or a certificate of good standing.
3. Document your experience: at least four years of supervisory experience in the classification within the ten years before you apply, supported by at least three notarized experience certificates and a project list for each classification.
4. Pull a full credit report for each officer, partner, manager or member, no more than six months old and covering at least five years. Scores or summaries alone are not accepted.
5. Entities add a CPA-prepared financial statement and a current Hawaii tax clearance.
6. File the application with the $50 non-refundable application fee by the board's monthly deadline.
7. After the board approves you, it sends an approval letter listing your exams and your deadline. Wait up to 24 hours, then register with PSI online or by phone.
8. Pass the Business and Law exam and your trade exam within six months of approval.
9. Provide proof of liability and workers' compensation insurance and pay the license and recovery fund fees the board assesses, then receive your license.

## The Hawaii Business and Law exam

PSI's Hawaii bulletin lists the Business and Law exam at 80 questions, 180 minutes and a 75 percent passing score. It is closed book. That works out to about two and a quarter minutes per question, which is comfortable if you know the material and stressful if you are guessing.

The reference is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Hawaii edition, 1st Edition (2022), ISBN 978-1-948558-34-1. NASCLA describes the book in three parts: starting and planning a business, running construction operations, and business administration such as finance, taxes and liens.

The content outline covers business organization, contract management, estimating and bidding, financial management, labor law, licensing, lien law, project management, risk management, safety and OSHA, and tax law. PSI notes outline changes effective March 30, 2026: Financial Management rises to 9 items, Licensing rises to 8 items, and Public Works Law is dropped. If you studied from an older outline, adjust your plan.

Because the exam is closed book, highlighting and tabs help you study rather than search on test day. Your goal is to recall the rule, not find it.

## Hawaii trade exams, PSI scheduling and retakes

Hawaii's contractor exams have been given by PSI since January 2023, according to the [board's exam page](https://cca.hawaii.gov/pvl/boards/contractor/application-deadline-examination-dates/). Some older board documents still mention Prometric, so go by your approval letter and the current PSI bulletin.

Trade exams are closed book too. The A and B exams each allow 240 minutes. PSI's published exam fees are $85 for the A and B exams and $80 for other classifications, non-refundable and good for one year from payment. Check the bulletin for the Business and Law fee and any changes.

Your eligibility lasts six months from approval. Within that window PSI allows unlimited attempts, though you cannot book a new exam the same day you test, and a retest may be possible within two business days depending on seats. If you do not pass in six months, you file a new application. Register online or call PSI at 833-333-4754.

## Experience, insurance, bond and financial requirements

Experience: under [HAR 16-77-18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-16-77-18), individual applicants and RMEs need at least four years of supervisory experience within the ten years before filing, as a foreman, supervising employee or contractor in the classification sought. The board's RME instructions say the experience must involve on-site supervision of workers, and time spent on design, ordering, scheduling or waiting on deliveries does not count. Some technical training can count, but never for more than one year.

Insurance: the entity application requires general liability with minimum limits of $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence for bodily injury, and $50,000 per occurrence for property damage, plus workers' compensation. Coverage must be continuous. If it lapses for more than 60 days, you reapply as a new applicant.

Financial: entities file a financial statement no more than one year old, compiled, reviewed or audited by a licensed CPA or PA. Every officer, partner, manager or member files a full credit report. The board also looks for a reputation for honesty, financial integrity and fair dealing.

Bond: under [HRS 444-16.5](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol10_Ch0436-0474/HRS0444/HRS_0444-0016_0005.htm), the board may require a bond of at least $5,000 after looking at your financial condition and experience. It is discretionary, not automatic.

## Hawaii contractor license costs and fees

These are the published figures from board and PSI documents. Fees change, so confirm with the board's current fee schedule.

Remember to budget for your study materials and for the CPA work behind an entity financial statement, which can cost more than the state fees.

- Application fee: $50, non-refundable.
- License fee: $260, assessed after board approval.
- Contractors Recovery Fund: listed as $74 or $148 on the board's fee schedule.
- Trade exam fees at PSI: $85 for Class A or B, $80 for other classifications.
- Biennial renewal for the period starting October 1, 2026: $338 for an active entity or sole proprietor, $208 for an active RME, $160 for any inactive license.

## How to study for a closed book exam

Studying for a closed book test is mostly about repetition and recall. Plan several passes through the guide rather than one long read.

First pass: business organization, licensing, insurance and risk management. Second pass: contracts, estimating and bidding, scheduling and project management. Third pass: financial management, labor law, taxes and liens. Fourth pass: safety and OSHA plus anything you missed. Weight your time toward the categories the March 2026 outline expanded, Financial Management and Licensing.

After each chapter, close the book and quiz yourself. Use flash cards or write your own questions, and go back to the highlighted text only for the ones you miss. In the last week, try explaining each outline topic out loud without notes. If you can teach it, you can answer it.

Time yourself on a few 80 question sets so the 180 minute clock feels normal.

## After you pass: renewal and keeping your license active

Hawaii contractor licenses renew every two years by September 30 of even-numbered years. For the October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2028 period, the board accepted renewals from August 10 to September 30, 2026, and offered late restoration within 60 days of expiration at a higher fee.

Active entity and sole proprietor licensees renew with a current tax clearance from the Department of Taxation dated within six months, in a name that matches the license. Keep your liability and workers' compensation certificates current with the board, because a lapse can forfeit both the entity and the RME license.

## Hawaii NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Is the Hawaii Business and Law exam open book?

No. The Hawaii Business and Law exam is closed book, and so are the trade exams. You study from the NASCLA Hawaii 1st Edition guide, but you cannot bring it into the test room.

### Can I use a NASCLA exam score to get a Hawaii contractor license?

Hawaii is not on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the commercial exam, and the board's materials do not offer it in place of the state trade exam. Plan to take Hawaii's own trade exam, and ask the board if you have a specific situation.

### How many questions are on the Hawaii Business and Law exam?

PSI lists 80 questions with a 180 minute time limit and a 75 percent passing score. The content outline changed on March 30, 2026, adding weight to financial management and licensing.

### Which book do I need for the Hawaii Business and Law exam?

The NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Hawaii edition, 1st Edition (2022). We sell it plain, with tabs, highlighted and tabbed, or highlighted and tabbed with flash cards.

### How much experience do I need for a Hawaii contractor license?

You need at least four years of supervisory experience in the classification within the ten years before you apply. The board asks for at least three notarized experience certificates and a project list for each classification.

### How long do I have to pass the Hawaii contractor exams?

Six months from your approval. PSI allows unlimited attempts in that window, but if you have not passed both exams by the deadline you must file a new application.

### Who gives the Hawaii contractor exams?

PSI has given Hawaii contractor exams since January 2023. You register with PSI after the board sends your approval letter.

### What is the difference between a Hawaii Class A, B and C license?

Class A is general engineering, such as roads, bridges and utilities. Class B is general building for structures involving more than two unrelated trades. Class C covers single specialty trades like electrical, plumbing or roofing.

### Does Hawaii require a contractor bond?

Not automatically. State law lets the board require a bond of at least $5,000 based on your finances and experience, so ask the board whether it applies to you.
