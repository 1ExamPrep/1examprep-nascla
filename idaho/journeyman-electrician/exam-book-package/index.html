Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is one of three open-book electrical trade examinations offered through the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The other examinations cover Electrical Contractors/Master Electricians and Residential Electrical Contractors.

NASCLA developed the Journeyman Electrician examination through a national job analysis involving electrical subject matter experts. The examination is structured around knowledge and skills used by journeyman electricians performing installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work.

Major examination content areas include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

The examination places significant emphasis on National Electrical Code knowledge and practical electrical application. Candidates should be prepared to move between general NEC requirements and more specific rules involving conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, feeders, services, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, equipment, and special occupancies.

Idaho electrical examinations are currently offered through PSI. The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses directs candidates to PSI for examination registration and scheduling. Candidates select the applicable exam, complete the registration process, and schedule the examination after their application is accepted through the testing system.

After achieving the required passing result, candidates must still apply for licensure through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Passing the examination does not automatically issue an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies its electrical examination program as consisting of open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should not expect to search every answer from the beginning during the exam. A more effective approach is to develop enough technical understanding to recognize the subject being tested and then use the correct reference to confirm the applicable requirement, formula, table, or safety rule.

Reference navigation should become part of your normal study routine. Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code and practice using the index, Article 100 definitions, chapters, tables, cross-references, and equipment-specific articles. Learn where major subjects are located so that searching becomes more deliberate and efficient.

The safety references should also become familiar. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-jobsite safety preparation, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 addresses general-industry workplace standards. NFPA 70E adds electrical workplace safety practices and hazard-related requirements.

NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References provides practical formulas, tables, conversions, and calculation resources, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications helps reinforce the electrical principles behind code requirements and calculation problems.

When studying, practice identifying the topic before opening a book. Determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and then confirm your solution. Repetition helps turn a collection of books into a working exam-reference system.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an examination requirement and not a license by itself. Idaho Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered through the Idaho Electrical Board and the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.

  1. Review Idaho Journeyman Electrician qualification requirements. Determine which education and experience pathway applies to your background.
  2. Complete the required electrical training and experience. Idaho requires qualifying electrical installation experience before a Journeyman Electrician license can be issued.
  3. Register for the applicable examination. Idaho electrical examinations are currently offered through PSI.
  4. Prepare with the reference materials. Use the 2023 NEC, OSHA standards, NFPA references, theory materials, and field references to prepare across the full examination blueprint.

  6. Apply for Idaho licensure. After passing, complete the Journeyman Electrician license application through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.
  7. Provide supporting documentation. Submit the education, work experience, examination results, license verification, or other documentation required for the pathway being used.
  8. Maintain the license. After licensure, comply with Idaho renewal and continuing-education requirements.

State Requirements

Journeyman Electrician licensing in Idaho is regulated by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. The Board regulates electrical contractors, master electricians, journeyman electricians, apprentices, and limited electrical classifications.

Idaho provides more than one qualification pathway for Journeyman Electrician applicants. One pathway involves completion of an approved four-year sequence of electrical instruction together with at least 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical work experience.

Idaho also recognizes an alternative experience-based route requiring substantially more qualifying field experience when the applicant has not completed the approved instructional pathway. Applicants must document their experience according to the Division's requirements.

Not every type of electrical-related work qualifies toward journeyman experience. Idaho distinguishes between qualifying electrical installation experience and work in categories that do not satisfy the Journeyman Electrician experience requirement. Candidates should organize employment verification carefully when preparing a license application.

Initial applicants must pass the applicable examination before applying for licensure. Idaho's current examination process is administered through PSI, and the Division directs candidates to the testing provider for examination selection, registration, scheduling, and candidate bulletins.

After passing, applicants must complete the Idaho licensing application process. Examination success is one component of licensure, while the Division separately evaluates the applicant's qualifications, experience, education, documentation, and other licensing requirements.

Idaho also requires licensed journeyman electricians to comply with continuing-education and renewal requirements. Maintaining familiarity with current electrical code requirements remains important after the initial licensing examination.

    A federal construction-industry safety reference covering occupational safety and health requirements applicable to construction jobsites and electrical work performed in construction environments.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910
    A federal general-industry safety reference addressing workplace safety requirements, including provisions related to electrical hazards and electrical work environments.
  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace safety standard focused on practices and procedures intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards and support safer work practices.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023
    A compact electrical field reference containing frequently used formulas, tables, conversions, calculations, and practical electrical information.
  • NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2022
    A fire alarm and signaling systems reference covering detection, initiating devices, notification, signaling functions, and related system requirements.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference focused on strengthening electrical theory knowledge and connecting electrical principles with practical National Electrical Code applications and calculations.

Practice locating requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, electrical equipment, and special occupancies. Electrical theory should be studied alongside code navigation. NFPA 70E adds electrical workplace safety practices that help candidates prepare for electrical hazard-related questions.

NFPA 72 should be incorporated into study for fire alarm and signaling-system topics. Specialty subjects may represent a smaller portion of an examination than general wiring requirements, but unfamiliar reference sections can consume valuable testing time if candidates have not practiced navigating them in advance.

Testing and troubleshooting should also be included in your preparation routine. Review voltage, current, resistance, continuity, equipment behavior, circuit conditions, and the logical use of test instruments. Journeyman electricians are expected to do more than install wiring; they must also diagnose and correct electrical problems.

Plans and specifications are another important part of journeyman-level electrical work. Practice recognizing electrical symbols, circuit information, equipment locations, layouts, and installation requirements. The ability to translate plans and specifications into field work is a practical skill that also supports examination preparation.
