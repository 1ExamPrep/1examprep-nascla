## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is one of three open-book electrical trade examinations offered through the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The other examinations cover Electrical Contractors/Master Electricians and Residential Electrical Contractors.

NASCLA developed the Journeyman Electrician examination through a national job analysis involving electrical subject matter experts. The examination is structured around knowledge and skills used by journeyman electricians performing installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work.

Major examination content areas include:

- Interpreting Plans and Specifications
- Basic Electrical Safety
- Electrical Theory and Principles
- Testing and Troubleshooting
- General Code Requirements
- Wiring and Protection
- Wiring Methods and Materials
- Equipment for General Use
- Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
- Special Conditions and Communication Systems

The examination places significant emphasis on National Electrical Code knowledge and practical electrical application. Candidates should be prepared to move between general NEC requirements and more specific rules involving conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, feeders, services, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, equipment, and special occupancies.

Idaho electrical examinations are currently offered through **PSI**. The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses directs candidates to PSI for examination registration and scheduling. Candidates select the applicable exam, complete the registration process, and schedule the examination after their application is accepted through the testing system.

After achieving the required passing result, candidates must still apply for licensure through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Passing the examination does not automatically issue an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**. NASCLA identifies its electrical examination program as consisting of open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should not expect to search every answer from the beginning during the exam. A more effective approach is to develop enough technical understanding to recognize the subject being tested and then use the correct reference to confirm the applicable requirement, formula, table, or safety rule.

Ugly's Electrical References provides practical formulas, tables, conversions, and calculation resources, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications helps reinforce the electrical principles behind code requirements and calculation problems.

When studying, practice identifying the topic before opening a book. Determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and then confirm your solution. Repetition helps turn a collection of books into a working exam-reference system.

## State Requirements

Journeyman Electrician licensing in Idaho is regulated by the **Idaho Electrical Board** through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. The Board regulates electrical contractors, master electricians, journeyman electricians, apprentices, and limited electrical classifications.

Idaho provides more than one qualification pathway for Journeyman Electrician applicants. One pathway involves completion of an approved four-year sequence of electrical instruction together with at least **8,000 hours of qualifying electrical work experience**.

Idaho also recognizes an alternative experience-based route requiring substantially more qualifying field experience when the applicant has not completed the approved instructional pathway. Applicants must document their experience according to the Division's requirements.

Not every type of electrical-related work qualifies toward journeyman experience. Idaho distinguishes between qualifying electrical installation experience and work in categories that do not satisfy the Journeyman Electrician experience requirement. Candidates should organize employment verification carefully when preparing a license application.

Initial applicants must pass the applicable examination before applying for licensure. Idaho's current examination process is administered through PSI, and the Division directs candidates to the testing provider for examination selection, registration, scheduling, and candidate bulletins.

After passing, applicants must complete the Idaho licensing application process. Examination success is one component of licensure, while the Division separately evaluates the applicant's qualifications, experience, education, documentation, and other licensing requirements.

Idaho also requires licensed journeyman electricians to comply with continuing-education and renewal requirements. Maintaining familiarity with current electrical code requirements remains important after the initial licensing examination.
