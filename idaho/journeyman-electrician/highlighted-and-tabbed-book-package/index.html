Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is designed to measure the knowledge and skills needed to properly perform the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes. NASCLA developed the examination using a national occupational analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts.

The NASCLA electrical examination program includes separate examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors. Idaho currently accepts both the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains 100 scored questions and requires candidates to answer at least 75 questions correctly to pass. Candidates are allowed 300 minutes, or 5 hours, to complete the examination.

Major areas of examination preparation include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

These subjects require candidates to combine code navigation with practical electrical understanding. National Electrical Code questions may involve services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, special occupancies, and communication systems.

The examination also measures knowledge that is not limited to direct NEC lookup. Electrical theory, troubleshooting, safety, calculations, plans, and practical installation concepts should all be included in a complete preparation schedule.

NASCLA examination applications are completed through the NASCLA National Examination Database. Once an application is approved, candidates receive information for scheduling the examination through PSI. NASCLA currently provides an approved candidate with up to three opportunities to take and pass the examination during the one-year eligibility period.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their authorized references to the testing center.

Reference preparation is especially important for an open-book electrical examination. The purpose of highlighting and permanent tabs is to make frequently used sections easier to locate while studying and working through exam-style questions. Organized references can help candidates move more deliberately between books instead of searching from the beginning each time a question requires a lookup.

Current examination rules permit qualifying reference materials to be prepared in advance with highlighting, underlining, and permanent indexing. Temporary markers such as Post-it notes are prohibited. Candidates should also follow examination rules regarding handwritten notes, loose papers, attached materials, and any other prohibited additions to references.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is one of the most important references in this package. Candidates should practice using its index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, articles, tables, and cross-references. Familiarity with the structure of the NEC can make it easier to determine whether a question belongs in a general chapter, a wiring-method article, an equipment article, or a special-occupancy section.

The OSHA and NFPA safety references should also become familiar before examination day. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 addresses construction-industry safety, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 addresses general-industry workplace requirements. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications support formula, calculation, and theory review. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Working with all of these references during practice helps candidates develop a repeatable method for deciding which book to use for a particular question.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license. Licensing is administered by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.

  1. Review Idaho's Journeyman Electrician qualification pathways. Determine whether you qualify through approved apprenticeship schooling and supervised work experience, a work-experience pathway, or another recognized licensing route.
  2. Complete the applicable experience and training requirements. Idaho requires qualifying supervised electrical installation experience before a Journeyman Electrician license can be issued.
  3. Prepare for the examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, OSHA requirements, NFPA safety standards, calculations, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  4. Apply for and take the NASCLA examination. Complete the applicable NASCLA examination process and schedule through PSI after approval.
  5. Pass the Journeyman Electrician examination. Initial Idaho applicants must pass the applicable examination before applying for the Journeyman Electrician license.
  6. Apply for Idaho licensure. Submit the Journeyman Electrician license application to the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.
  7. Provide supporting documentation. Submit work verification, apprenticeship completion records, license verification, test results, or other documentation required for the pathway being used.
  8. Maintain the license. After issuance, comply with Idaho's current renewal and continuing-education requirements.

State Requirements

Idaho Journeyman Electrician licensing is regulated by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Applicants must satisfy both examination and experience requirements before the Journeyman Electrician license is issued.

Idaho provides an apprenticeship-school pathway that combines completion of a Board-approved electrical apprenticeship program with 8,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work. Work verification is required as part of the licensing documentation.

Idaho also provides a work-experience pathway for applicants who do not qualify through the approved apprenticeship-school route. That pathway requires documentation of 16,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work.

Certain types of work do not count toward the Journeyman Electrician experience requirement. Idaho specifically excludes work experience in appliance repair, motor winding, and communications from the qualifying experience used to obtain a Journeyman Electrician license.

Idaho's application materials also provide reciprocal licensing pathways for qualifying electricians licensed in participating jurisdictions. Reciprocity is separate from taking the NASCLA examination and has its own license-verification and eligibility requirements.

Initial applicants must pass the applicable NASCLA examination before applying for licensure. Idaho's Journeyman Electrician application specifically asks whether the applicant has taken and passed the NASCLA examination through PSI and whether the examination was taken through Idaho. Applicants who tested outside Idaho are instructed to provide a copy of their test scores with the application.

Electrical examinations are currently offered through PSI. Idaho's Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses directs candidates to PSI for exam registration and scheduling.

Idaho's electrical licensing rules also require continuing education for journeyman and master electricians. Continuing education helps licensed electricians remain familiar with electrical-code changes and other technical developments that affect professional practice.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Idaho NASCLA Journeyman Electrician preparation plan should combine reference navigation with technical understanding. Highlighted and tabbed books can support that process by helping candidates repeatedly return to important sections as they work practice questions and review difficult topics.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Study branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, equipment, motors, and special installations. Use the highlighted portions and permanent tabs as navigation aids while still learning how the NEC itself is structured.

Electrical theory should receive regular study time. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current relationships, transformers, motors, loads, and common electrical calculations. Theory questions require understanding rather than simple code lookup.

Safety preparation should include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Learn the role of each reference so you can recognize whether a question involves construction safety, general-industry requirements, or electrical safe-work practices.

Testing and troubleshooting are also important journeyman skills. Review how voltage, current, resistance, continuity, circuit conditions, equipment behavior, and test instruments are used to locate electrical problems. A systematic troubleshooting approach can help with questions that require practical reasoning.

NFPA 72 should be incorporated into preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Specialty subjects can require familiarity with reference sections that electricians may not navigate as frequently as the NEC, making advance preparation useful.

When working practice questions, identify the topic before opening a book. Determine the most appropriate reference, use the permanent tabs to reach the relevant area, confirm the rule or formula, and then solve the problem. This routine helps combine electrical knowledge with open-book exam efficiency.
