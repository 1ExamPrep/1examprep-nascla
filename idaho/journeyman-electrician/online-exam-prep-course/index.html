Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized electrical trade examination developed through a national occupational analysis. Subject matter experts from multiple jurisdictions contributed to the job analysis and examination content so that the test reflects knowledge and tasks associated with journeyman electrical work.

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains 100 scored questions distributed across several content domains. The published examination blueprint includes:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications: 8 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 6 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions

The largest portions of the examination are Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements. Candidates should devote substantial study time to National Electrical Code navigation while also preparing for electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, equipment, special systems, and plans and specifications.

Idaho's Electrical Board accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Initial Idaho Journeyman Electrician applicants must pass the applicable NASCLA examination through PSI before applying for licensure. If the NASCLA examination was taken outside Idaho, the applicant must include a copy of the passing test scores with the Idaho license application.

Idaho electrical examinations are currently offered through PSI. Candidates select the applicable exam through the testing provider's Idaho examination system, complete the examination registration process, and schedule their testing appointment after the registration is accepted.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program consists of open-book trade examinations using authorized reference materials under the applicable testing rules.

Open-book testing requires more than bringing reference books into the testing center. Candidates need to understand how each book is organized, recognize which reference is appropriate for a question, find the relevant section efficiently, and correctly apply the information. The examination covers multiple technical categories, making reference familiarity an important part of preparation.

Practice National Electrical Code navigation regularly. Become comfortable using the NEC index, locating articles, following cross-references, interpreting tables, reviewing definitions, and moving between general rules and equipment-specific requirements. A candidate who understands the structure of the NEC can often locate information more efficiently than someone who relies entirely on memorization.

The same principle applies to the other references. Safety questions may direct you toward OSHA regulations or NFPA 70E. Fire alarm topics may require familiarity with NFPA 72. Electrical calculations and technical concepts may be reinforced through Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications.

During study sessions, avoid immediately searching for every answer. First determine what the question is testing. Identify the likely reference and subject area, then locate the supporting information. Repeating this process helps build the reference-navigation habits needed for an open-book electrical examination.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an examination requirement and does not automatically issue an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates must complete the licensing process administered by the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.

  1. Meet the Idaho education and experience pathway. Determine whether you qualify through the approved apprenticeship and work-experience route or the alternative experience route recognized by Idaho.
  2. Complete the required qualifying experience. Idaho requires journeyman applicants to document the electrical installation experience associated with the licensing pathway being used.
  3. Register for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Idaho electrical licensing examinations are currently offered through PSI.
  4. Prepare for the exam. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, and other NASCLA journeyman content domains.
  5. Take and pass the examination. Initial applicants must pass the applicable NASCLA examination before applying for Idaho Journeyman Electrician licensure.
  6. Submit the Idaho Journeyman Electrician license application. Complete the application through the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses and provide the required supporting documentation.
  7. Provide examination documentation when required. Applicants who passed the NASCLA examination outside Idaho must include a copy of their test scores with the application.
  8. Maintain the license. After licensure, follow Idaho's renewal and continuing-education requirements for journeyman electricians.

State Requirements

Journeyman Electrician licensing in Idaho is administered by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Initial applicants must meet the state's education or experience requirements and pass the applicable examination before applying for licensure.

Idaho provides two primary qualification pathways for journeyman licensure. One pathway requires completion of a four-year sequence of electrical instruction approved by the Idaho Division of Career Technical Education together with at least 8,000 hours of qualifying electrical work experience. The alternative pathway requires proof of 16,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience.

The Idaho Journeyman Electrician application also requires applicants who are currently licensed as Journeyman Electricians in another jurisdiction to provide license verification from the licensing state. The verification must establish the applicable education and experience background. A copy of the license itself is not accepted as a replacement for official verification.

Idaho does not count every type of electrical-related work toward journeyman qualification. Work experience involving appliance repair, motor winding, or communications does not count toward the experience requirements for Journeyman Electrician licensure. Idaho also limits the amount of experience obtained while practicing as a limited electrical installer or trainee that may be applied toward journeyman requirements.

Active apprentice registration is important for electrical experience earned in Idaho. The state's work verification process requires qualifying experience to be documented, and Idaho experience must have been obtained while holding the applicable active registration when registration was required.

After an applicant satisfies the experience and examination requirements, the individual completes the Idaho Journeyman Electrician license application. Passing the NASCLA examination does not substitute for the state's licensing application or supporting documentation.

Idaho also requires continuing education for renewal of Journeyman Electrician licenses. Journeymen and Master Electricians must complete required electrical code and related continuing-education hours during the applicable three-year licensing cycle.

Test Information and Study Materials

An effective Idaho NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine code navigation with practical electrical knowledge. Because the exam includes 100 scored questions distributed across ten content domains, preparation should cover both high-volume NEC subjects and smaller technical categories.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Wiring and Protection accounts for 18 questions, Wiring Methods and Materials accounts for 16 questions, and General Code Requirements accounts for 15 questions. Together, these categories represent a substantial portion of the exam blueprint. Practice locating conductor requirements, overcurrent protection rules, grounding and bonding provisions, raceway requirements, cable articles, boxes, fittings, services, feeders, and branch-circuit rules.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 10 questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, series and parallel circuits, alternating current concepts, loads, transformers, motors, conductor calculations, and related electrical principles. Understanding why a calculation works can make it easier to solve unfamiliar problems.

Testing and Troubleshooting accounts for 7 questions. Develop a logical approach to diagnosing electrical conditions. Review how voltage, resistance, current, continuity, and equipment behavior can be used to identify circuit problems and improper operation.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA and NFPA 70E. The examination includes Basic Electrical Safety as a dedicated content area, and safe electrical work is also connected to many practical installation and troubleshooting scenarios.

Interpreting Plans and Specifications accounts for 8 questions. Practice reading electrical plans, identifying equipment and circuit information, recognizing symbols, and translating design information into installation requirements. Journeyman electricians frequently work from plans and specifications, making this an important practical skill.

Special Occupancies and Equipment accounts for 8 questions. Become familiar with NEC requirements that apply outside ordinary installations. These topics can be difficult when candidates rarely encounter the corresponding articles during daily work, so targeted navigation practice is useful.

Special Conditions and Communication Systems represent a smaller portion of the blueprint but should not be ignored. NFPA 72 and applicable NEC communication-system provisions can help reinforce preparation involving fire alarms and signaling systems.

Use your reference books throughout your study sessions. Practice identifying the subject before opening a book, selecting the reference most likely to contain the answer, and locating the correct material efficiently. Open-book exam preparation works best when reference navigation becomes part of the learning process rather than something saved for the final days before testing.
