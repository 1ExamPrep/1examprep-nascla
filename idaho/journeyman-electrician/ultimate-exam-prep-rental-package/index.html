Exam Details

The Idaho Electrical Board accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians as part of its participation in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA's electrical examination program provides standardized trade examinations that participating licensing agencies may use as an alternative to their own trade examinations.

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is intended to evaluate technical knowledge associated with journeyman-level electrical work. Preparation should cover a broad range of subjects involving electrical installations, code compliance, calculations, workplace safety, troubleshooting, equipment, wiring systems, and electrical theory.

Major study areas include:

  • Interpreting plans and specifications
  • Basic electrical safety
  • Electrical theory and principles
  • Testing and troubleshooting
  • General electrical code requirements
  • Wiring and protection
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Equipment for general use
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Special conditions and communication systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also understand common electrical formulas, calculations, testing procedures, and troubleshooting methods.

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. An approved NASCLA application is currently valid for one year. Candidates must complete the applicable NASCLA examination process and separately satisfy Idaho's licensing requirements.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not create a national electrician license. The Idaho Electrical Board remains responsible for determining whether an applicant has met the qualifications required for an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Authorized reference materials may be used in accordance with the examination rules that apply to the candidate's testing appointment.

Open-book testing requires more than simply bringing reference books into the examination room. Candidates should know how the references are organized, which book is likely to contain a particular answer, and how to move efficiently between indexes, articles, tables, formulas, definitions, and technical standards.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue an Idaho Journeyman Electrician license. Applicants must complete the licensing process administered by the Idaho Electrical Board and Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.

  1. Choose the applicable Idaho qualification pathway. Determine whether you qualify through the approved apprenticeship pathway or the electrical work-experience pathway.
  2. Document the required training and experience. Idaho requires evidence supporting the experience and education used for journeyman eligibility.
  3. Submit the required application materials. Complete the Idaho electrical journeyman application and provide the required supporting documentation.
  4. Complete the applicable examination requirement. Idaho accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians as an examination option.
  5. Prepare for the examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, calculations, OSHA requirements, NFPA safety standards, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, and fire alarm systems.
  6. Provide required examination documentation. Follow the applicable NASCLA and Idaho procedures for reporting the examination result.
  7. Complete Idaho's licensing requirements. The Idaho Electrical Board reviews whether the applicant satisfies the applicable experience, training, examination, and documentation requirements.
  8. Receive the journeyman license. A license is issued only after the applicable Idaho requirements have been satisfied.

State Requirements

Idaho provides two primary qualification pathways for a Journeyman Electrician applicant. The first combines an approved four-year sequence of electrical instruction with supervised electrical installation experience. The second is based on a higher amount of qualifying electrical work experience.

Under the apprenticeship pathway, an applicant must successfully complete an approved four-year electrical apprenticeship program and document 8,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work.

Idaho allows an applicant who has completed the approved four-year sequence of instruction to sit for the journeyman examination before all 8,000 work hours are completed. However, the Journeyman Electrician license is not issued until the applicant provides documentation showing completion of the required 8,000 hours of supervised electrical installation work.

The alternative work-experience pathway requires 16,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience. Applicants using this pathway must provide work verification supporting the required experience.

Idaho specifically excludes certain types of work from the journeyman experience requirement. Experience involving appliance repair, motor winding, or communications does not count toward the experience required to take the journeyman examination or obtain a Journeyman Electrician license.

Idaho also limits how much experience obtained while working as a limited electrical installer or trainee can be applied toward journeyman licensure. No more than 2,000 hours of qualifying limited electrical installer or trainee experience may be applied toward the journeyman experience requirement.

Applicants should maintain complete records of apprenticeship registration, supervised electrical installation work, training, and employer verification. Accurate documentation is an important part of demonstrating eligibility to the Idaho Electrical Board.

The Idaho Journeyman Electrician license is an individual electrical license. Holding or passing the NASCLA examination does not eliminate the requirement to satisfy Idaho's licensing standards. Applicants must meet the Board's applicable experience, education, examination, application, and documentation requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Idaho NASCLA Journeyman Electrician preparation plan should combine technical study with repeated reference-book practice. With 1 year of course access, candidates have an extended period to work through electrical subjects, improve weak areas, practice calculations, and become comfortable navigating the highlighted and tabbed books.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Study services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, and special installations.

Rather than attempting to memorize every NEC section number, practice identifying the subject of a problem and then using the index, article structure, definitions, tables, and cross-references to locate the applicable requirement. This approach supports the skills needed for an open-book exam.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside code navigation. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, loads, and common calculations. Understanding the underlying principle can make it easier to recognize the formula or code requirement needed to solve a problem.

Safety preparation should incorporate OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand the role of each reference and practice locating material involving construction safety, general workplace requirements, and electrical safe-work practices.

Testing and troubleshooting should also receive regular attention. Review voltage, current, resistance, continuity, test instruments, circuit operation, equipment behavior, and systematic fault diagnosis. Journeyman-level preparation involves knowing how systems operate as well as understanding applicable code requirements.

NFPA 72 provides focused support for fire alarm and signaling-system study. Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications can be used for calculations, formulas, theory concepts, and practical electrical problem solving.

Use the course and reference library together. Review a technical subject, work through practice-oriented questions, identify the correct book, navigate to the applicable section, and confirm the rule or concept. Consistent repetition helps develop both technical understanding and reference-navigation efficiency.
