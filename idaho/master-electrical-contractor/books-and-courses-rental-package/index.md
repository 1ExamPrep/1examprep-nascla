## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Idaho is administered through the Idaho Electrical Board within the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Idaho regulates Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, apprentices, specialty contractors, and specialty electricians.

An individual applicant for an Idaho Electrical Contractor license must satisfy one of two qualification pathways. The applicant may have completed 2,000 hours of work while holding an Idaho Electrical Journeyman license, or the applicant may hold an active Idaho Master Electrician license.

A business entity applying for an Electrical Contractor license must designate an individual to represent the entity for examination purposes. The designated person must be a supervisory employee and may not represent another contractor-license applicant at the same time.

The entity must also employ at least one active Idaho licensed Electrical Journeyman or Idaho Master Electrician. When the supervising electrician is a Journeyman, the Journeyman must have completed 2,000 hours of qualifying work.

Idaho's electrical rules connect the contractor's allowable scope of work to the license held by the supervising electrician. This makes the supervising electrician an important part of the contractor's ongoing licensing structure rather than simply an individual named for application purposes.

The supervising Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, residential electrician, or limited electrical installer must be available during working hours to carry out supervisory responsibilities. The supervising electrician is responsible for supervising the electrical installations performed by the contractor within the scope of that person's license.

After passing the applicable contractor examination, applicants must provide proof of insurance before the license is issued. Idaho requires electrical contractor applicants to provide proof of $300,000 in liability insurance and workers' compensation insurance unless the applicant qualifies for an exemption from workers' compensation requirements.

Idaho participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts both the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. The NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted testing component, but Idaho retains responsibility for approving and issuing the applicable state license.

Candidates should distinguish between an Idaho Master Electrician license and an Idaho Electrical Contractor license. A Master Electrician is an individual credential, while an Electrical Contractor license authorizes the individual or business entity to operate as an electrical contractor under Idaho's licensing rules.
