Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized electrical trade examinations for participating licensing agencies.

The examination is developed around the work performed by experienced electrical professionals and the knowledge needed to complete that work safely and competently. Candidates should prepare for both technical questions and questions requiring application of electrical codes, standards, theory, and professional responsibilities.

Major subjects associated with the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, Code requirements, grounding and bonding, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment for general use, specialized electrical systems, tools and testing equipment, and energy-related requirements.

Because the examination addresses several different areas of electrical work, candidates should avoid focusing exclusively on one reference. The National Electrical Code is central to preparation, but theory, safety, project-management, contractor-management, and energy-efficiency references also support the broader examination scope.

Idaho currently offers electrical examinations through PSI. Candidates can select the applicable examination through the Idaho examination system and access the corresponding candidate bulletin for current exam-specific information and reference requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program uses open-book electrical trade examinations, including the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Idaho also instructs electrical examination candidates to bring their own permitted reference materials to the examination center.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for study. The reference materials contain thousands of pages, and searching unfamiliar books can consume valuable examination time. Candidates should build enough familiarity with the collection to know where important subjects are located before the test begins.

National Electrical Code navigation should be practiced regularly. Candidates should learn the NEC index, definitions, chapter organization, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and specialized electrical installations.

Supporting references should also become familiar tools. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. Electrical textbooks and field references support theory and calculation practice. Project-management and contractor references cover planning, coordination, business responsibilities, risk, and professional project execution.

Idaho's current electrical examination guidance permits pre-exam highlighting, underlining, and permanent indexing of permitted reference materials. Writing during the examination is not allowed, and additional papers and temporary markers such as Post-it notes are prohibited. Candidates should always prepare their books according to the current candidate bulletin for the examination they are scheduled to take.

A useful preparation exercise is to work through questions while timing each reference search. Identify the subject first, select the book most likely to contain the information, locate the controlling provision, and apply it to the problem. Repeated practice can improve both technical understanding and navigation speed.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Idaho is administered by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Idaho separately regulates Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and specialty electrical classifications.

The Idaho Electrical Board is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. Idaho accepts both the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the Journeyman Electrician examination.

An applicant pursuing an Idaho Master Electrician license through the experience pathway must have held an Electrical Journeyman license for at least four years. Idaho also provides a reciprocity pathway for qualifying applicants who hold an active master electrician license from another jurisdiction and can provide the required license verification.

The reciprocity verification must document licensure by examination, show that the journeyman license was held for at least four years, confirm that the master license is active, and provide the original license issuance date.

Electrical contractor licensing has separate requirements. Idaho requires an electrical contractor applicant to satisfy the contractor application and examination process and maintain the appropriate supervising electrician relationship. The permitted scope of the contractor's electrical work is tied to the scope of the licensed individual responsible for supervision.

Idaho requires electrical work performed by a contractor to be supervised by an appropriately licensed electrical professional. Depending on the contractor and classification, this can involve a master electrician, journeyman electrician, residential electrician, or limited electrical installer within the applicable scope of practice.

An individual contractor may serve as the contractor's own supervising electrician when the individual holds the appropriate active Idaho electrical license for the work being performed. Business entities must identify the supervising electrical professional and comply with the contractor requirements established by the Idaho Electrical Board.

Idaho also requires an Electrical Contractor license before a person or business acts or attempts to act as an electrical contractor within the regulated scope of work. Electrical contracting includes qualifying electrical installation, maintenance, and repair activities.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not automatically issue an Idaho electrical license. The NASCLA examination provides an accepted trade-examination credential, while the Idaho Electrical Board remains responsible for licensing qualifications, applications, supervision requirements, and issuance of Idaho credentials.

  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th
    A contractor-focused reference covering business operations, contracts, project management, planning, risk, financial concepts, and professional construction responsibilities.
  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace-safety standard addressing practices intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while performing work on or around electrical equipment and systems.
  • Ugly's Electrical References
    A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, conversions, tables, and technical information useful for electrical problem solving and exam preparation.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference connecting electrical theory, calculations, and fundamental electrical principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The strongest way to use an electrical exam book package is to study from the references throughout the preparation process rather than opening them for the first time at the testing center. Candidates should learn the purpose of each reference and the way information is organized within it.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Practice finding the exact controlling provision rather than relying only on memorized article numbers.

Electrical theory should be studied through problem solving. Use the electrical theory references to reinforce voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other fundamental concepts. For calculation questions, identify the information given, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and determine whether a Code requirement affects the final result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn the role of each reference so you can recognize whether a question involves general construction safety or electrical workplace safety. Familiarity with their organization can make exam searches faster and more deliberate.

Project-management preparation should cover planning, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, documentation, and other responsibilities associated with professional contracting. The NASCLA contractor reference and project-management material help candidates prepare for subjects beyond technical installation alone.

Energy-efficiency concepts should also remain part of the study schedule. Become familiar with the structure and terminology of the ASHRAE/IES standard so that related requirements can be located efficiently when needed.

Timed reference-navigation exercises are especially useful for an open-book test. Select a question, determine which book applies, locate the supporting information, complete any necessary calculation, and track the time required. Topics that consistently require long searches can then receive additional study attention.
