NASCLA offers standardized electrical examinations for Electrical Contractors, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors. Participating jurisdictions may accept the applicable examination as part of their own licensing process.

The Electrical Contractor examination is designed for advanced electrical professionals and covers a broad range of trade knowledge. Preparation should include electrical theory, Code application, personal and jobsite safety, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, electrical equipment, project-management concepts, electrical calculations, special systems, and energy-related material.

Because the examination is built around multiple technical references, candidates should become comfortable recognizing which source is most likely to contain the information needed. ## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Idaho is administered by the Idaho Electrical Board through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Idaho separately licenses Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, Electrical Contractors, limited electrical contractors, and other electrical classifications.

A Master Electrician license is an individual credential. Idaho's licensing rules establish that an applicant for a Master Electrician license must have at least four years of experience as a licensed journeyman electrician before qualifying for the master-level examination pathway.

Idaho's current Master Electrician application recognizes the NASCLA examination. The application asks whether the applicant has taken and passed the applicable NASCLA examination through PSI and requires a passing examination result before the license application is completed. Applicants who tested outside Idaho must provide a copy of their test scores.

An Idaho Electrical Contractor license is separate from the Master Electrician credential. Contractor applicants must complete the contractor licensing requirements established by the Idaho Electrical Board and must operate with an appropriately licensed supervising electrician.

Idaho's current electrical rules state that a contractor's allowable scope of work is the same as the scope of its licensed employee. The supervising electrician must be available during working hours and is responsible for supervising electrical installations performed by the contractor.

The supervising electrician may be a Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, Residential Electrician, or Limited Electrical Installer when the contractor's work falls within that individual's authorized scope. This licensing structure makes it important to distinguish the business contractor license from the individual electrical license used to supervise the work.

An individual electrical contractor may serve as his or her own supervising electrician when the individual holds the appropriate active electrical credential for the work being performed. For businesses, the supervising electrician provides the licensed technical supervision required for electrical installations.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not automatically issue an Idaho license. The examination can satisfy an applicable testing requirement, but the applicant must still submit the required application and meet the experience, supervision, and licensing requirements associated with the requested credential.

Project-management and contractor-business material should remain part of preparation. Electrical contractors operate within projects that require planning, communication, coordination, documentation, scheduling, risk management, and professional organization. Energy-efficiency references add another technical area candidates should learn to navigate.

Timed reference exercises can help candidates connect technical knowledge with open-book preparation. Select a practice question, identify the subject, choose the most likely reference, locate the supporting information, and measure the time required.
