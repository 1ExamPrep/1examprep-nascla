Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes a standardized examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The Idaho Electrical Board is a participating agency that accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

The NASCLA electrical examination is built around a national job analysis representing knowledge and responsibilities associated with experienced electrical work. Preparation involves more than National Electrical Code memorization. Candidates should develop familiarity with electrical theory, safety, project design and management, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment, special electrical systems, energy-related requirements, and other trade subjects reflected in the examination program.

Idaho's Master Electrician application specifically recognizes the applicable NASCLA examination. Initial applicants must pass the required examination before applying for licensure. When a qualifying NASCLA examination has been taken outside Idaho, the Idaho application process requires the passing examination result to be provided with the application.

Idaho also licenses Electrical Contractors. The contractor licensing process has requirements that are separate from the Master Electrician license, including contractor qualification, examination, insurance, and business requirements. Idaho currently uses PSI for its licensing examinations, and the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses provides the Contractor’s Business and Law Reference Manual as an official examination reference.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use authorized references under the testing program's examination rules.

Idaho's contractor examination process also allows candidates to bring authorized reference materials. The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses states that the Contractor Reference Manual must be printed, hole-punched, and placed in a three-ring loose-leaf binder before the examination. Pre-exam highlighting, underlining, and permanent indexing are permitted. Temporary markers such as Post-it notes and additional loose papers are not permitted.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates need to identify the subject being tested, determine which reference is most relevant, locate the applicable section, and use the information efficiently. A large electrical reference library can be difficult to navigate without repeated practice.

Licensing Steps

Idaho distinguishes between Master Electrician and Electrical Contractor licensing. Candidates pursuing a professional electrical contracting business should understand the requirements that apply to both the technical licensing pathway and the contractor business entity.

  1. Determine the Idaho electrical license pathway that applies to you. Identify whether you are pursuing Master Electrician licensure, Electrical Contractor licensure, or the combination of credentials and business licensing needed for your intended operations.
  2. Prepare for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Study electrical theory, the 2023 NEC, safety, wiring systems, equipment, project-management concepts, and the other subjects covered by the examination program.
  3. Take and pass the applicable examination. Idaho requires initial applicants to complete the required examination before submitting the applicable license application.
  4. Complete the Idaho licensing application. Submit the required application and supporting information to the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses.
  5. Satisfy Electrical Contractor qualification requirements. Idaho applies separate qualification requirements to individuals and business entities seeking an Electrical Contractor license.
  6. Complete applicable Idaho contractor testing. Idaho licensing examinations are currently offered through PSI. The DOPL Contractor’s Business and Law Reference Manual is part of the contractor exam preparation materials.
  7. Provide required insurance documentation. Electrical Contractor applicants must satisfy Idaho's applicable liability and workers' compensation insurance requirements before licensure.

  9. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included to support business banking, tax administration, employee hiring, and other company operations.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Idaho is administered through the Idaho Electrical Board and the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. Idaho regulates Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and several specialty electrical classifications.

For an individual seeking an Idaho Electrical Contractor license, the current DOPL application provides two qualification pathways. The individual must have completed 2,000 hours of work while holding an Electrical Journeyman license or hold an active Idaho Master Electrician license.

A business entity may also apply for an Electrical Contractor license. The entity must designate a supervisory employee to represent the business, and the business must employ at least one active Idaho licensed Electrical Journeyman or Idaho Master Electrician. When the supervising electrician holds a Journeyman license, the applicable Idaho work-experience requirement must also be satisfied.

Electrical Contractor applicants must provide proof of insurance after passing the applicable examination. Idaho requires $300,000 in liability insurance and workers' compensation insurance unless the applicant qualifies for an exemption from workers' compensation coverage.

The Idaho Electrical Board lists separate license categories and terms for Contractors and Master Electricians. This distinction is important when building an electrical contracting company: a Master Electrician credential and an Electrical Contractor business license perform different roles within the state's regulatory system.

Idaho requires electrical work that falls within the state's regulated scope to be performed under the appropriate license. Electrical permits are also required for regulated electrical work, with permitting jurisdiction depending on the location and whether the state or an authorized local jurisdiction performs the inspection.
