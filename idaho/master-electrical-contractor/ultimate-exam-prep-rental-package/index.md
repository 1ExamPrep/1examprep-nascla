## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Idaho is administered through the Idaho Electrical Board within the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses. The Board regulates individual electrical licenses and contractor-related requirements within the state.

Idaho participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. The state also accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination.

For Master Electrician applicants, Idaho's application materials require the applicable NASCLA examination to be passed before the license application is submitted. Applicants who took the examination outside Idaho must include a copy of their examination scores.

The NASCLA examination is not a national license. It is an examination credential that can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement in participating jurisdictions. Idaho applicants must still complete the state licensing process and satisfy all additional requirements that apply.

Idaho also regulates contractor businesses separately from individual electrician credentials. Contractor registration requirements can include business ownership information, workers' compensation documentation or an applicable exemption statement, and general liability insurance.

Idaho contractor registration materials require general liability insurance of at least $300,000 single limit, including products and continued operations coverage. Workers' compensation insurance documentation is also required when applicable.

Contractor applicants must provide identifying and ownership information for the business and specify the type of construction activity undertaken. This business registration structure is distinct from the individual electrical credential held by a Master Electrician or Journeyman Electrician.

Candidates preparing for electrical contracting should therefore distinguish between the individual technical credential and the requirements applying to the contracting business. An examination result may satisfy one component, while business registration, insurance, supervision, and other licensing requirements remain separate.

The Idaho Electrical Board's rules and statutes govern electrical contractors and journeymen. Candidates should plan their licensing process around the credential and business structure they intend to use rather than treating the NASCLA examination as the final licensing step.
