Does Indiana Accept the NASCLA Electrical Exam?

Not statewide. Indiana leaves electrician licensing to local jurisdictions, and the IAEI's Indiana page points electricians to their city or county for licensing. Indiana also does not appear on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the commercial general building contractor exam.

Vanderburgh County is the Indiana jurisdiction that uses NASCLA's electrical program. NASCLA's Vanderburgh County page lists two accepted exams: Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician and Journeyman Electrician. The county gives these exams locally through Prov.

There is one condition that surprises people. Vanderburgh County does not currently accept NASCLA transcripts. If you passed a NASCLA electrical exam in Idaho, Missouri or another participating state, you cannot simply send that score to Evansville. You have to register with the local board and test in Evansville. And passing does not issue a license by itself; the local board still reviews your application, recommendations and experience.

Who Issues Electrical Licenses in Evansville and Vanderburgh County

Licensing is handled by the Evansville-Vanderburgh County Building Commission, Civic Center Complex, Room 310, 1 NW Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd., Evansville, IN 47708. The Commission issues contractor and tradesman licenses, apprentice tradesman licenses and master licenses in several trades, including electrical. Its license clerk can be reached at (812) 436-7880, Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

The Commission takes your application and sends it to the appropriate trade board for approval. After you test, the Licensing and Disciplinary Board reviews scores at its monthly meeting, which the Commission lists as the fourth Thursday of the month, before final approval.

Because this is a local license, it covers work under the Evansville and Vanderburgh County rules. It is not an Indiana state license, and other Indiana cities and counties may have different requirements or may not recognize it. Ask the jurisdiction where the job is before you assume your license carries over.

Vanderburgh County Electrical License Types and Which Exam Each Needs

The Prov Candidate Information Bulletin for Vanderburgh County lists exactly two exams. Here is how they line up with local credentials.

  • Master Electrician / Unlimited Electrician (electrical contractor level): NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam, 110 questions, 4.5 hours, 75 percent to pass.
  • Journeyman Electrician: NASCLA Journey-level Electrician exam, 110 questions, 5 hours, 70 percent to pass.
  • Apprentice Tradesman: a separate local application. No NASCLA exam is listed for it in the bulletin.
  • Other trades such as mechanical, sheet metal and refrigeration are licensed by the same Commission but are outside the NASCLA electrical program and our Indiana products.

How to Get a Vanderburgh County Electrical License, Step by Step

The process runs through the local board first, then Prov, then back to the board. Here is the sequence the Commission and the bulletin describe.

  1. Fill out the Building Commission license application in ink or typed, and state which license you want to test for.
  2. Get three notarized letters of recommendation from reputable business or professional people who are not related to you by blood or marriage. For electrical applicants, the letters must be on letterhead and show at least four years of experience.
  3. Complete the middle section of the Criminal Record History form and have it notarized.
  4. Submit the application and letters together. The Commission forwards them to the proper board.
  5. Once the board approves you, use the Known Candidate ID on your registration letter to schedule with Prov online or at 866-720-7768, and pay the exam fee.
  6. Test at the Ivy Tech Certification Center in Evansville and get your score at the site. Prov reports it to the county automatically.
  7. Pay the license fee by cash or check to the Building Commission and provide proof of required insurance, a surety bond and a copy of your passing score to receive a provisional license, pending the board's monthly score review.

The Indiana NASCLA Electrical Exam in Evansville: Format and Rules

Both exams are computer-based and open book. The only Indiana test site in the bulletin is the Ivy Tech Certification Center, 3401 First Avenue, Evansville, IN 47710. Bring a current government-issued photo ID; you will also sign a log and be photographed.

The Electrical Contractor exam has 110 questions, including 10 experimental items, with 4.5 hours allowed. Its outline: Project Design and Management 8, Safety 9, Electrical Theory and Principles 11, General Code Requirements 17, Wiring and Protection 17, Wiring Methods and Materials 16, General Equipment Use 13, Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions 8, and Communication Systems 1.

The Journey-level exam also has 110 questions (100 scored, 10 experimental) with 5 hours allowed. Wiring and Protection is the largest block at 18, followed by cabinets, boxes, fittings and conduit at 16 and General Code Requirements at 15. The rest covers plans and specs, safety, theory, testing and troubleshooting, equipment and special systems.

You can highlight, underline in pen and add permanent tabs. Handwritten notes, movable tabs, sticky notes and added photocopies are not allowed, and you cannot mark your books during the test.

If you fail, you go back to the board for a new eligibility. Each eligibility covers one attempt, and the bulletin says the board offers the exam up to three times a year. To avoid losing your fee, cancel or reschedule at least three business days ahead.

Required References and the Business and Law Content

Vanderburgh County does not give a separate state business and law exam for these licenses. Business and project management content is built into the master-level exam, and the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th Edition, is on its reference list.

Master exam references: NFPA 70 (NEC, 2020 or 2023), Ugly's Electrical References 2023, Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications (2022), NFPA 70E 2024, the NASCLA Contractors Guide 14th Edition, 29 CFR 1926 and 1910 (2024), ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 and the PMBOK Guide.

Journey-level references: NFPA 70 (2020 or 2023), OSHA 29 CFR 1926 and 1910, NFPA 70E 2024, Ugly's 2023, NFPA 72 (2022) and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications (2022). Check the current bulletin before you buy, because editions get updated.

Experience, Insurance and Bond Requirements for an Evansville Electrician License

Experience: the Building Commission requires electrical applicants to show at least four years of experience through their recommendation letters. The letters must be personal recommendations from individuals, not company letters, and board members may ask that they state plainly that the writer recommends you.

Character: your three letters should come from reputable business or professional people outside your family. You also complete a notarized criminal record history form.

Insurance and bond: after you pass, you must show proof of required insurance and a surety bond to receive your provisional license. The Commission's pages do not publish the coverage or bond amounts, so call the license clerk to get the current figures before you shop for a policy.

Vanderburgh County Electrical License Costs

The published exam fee in the current Prov bulletin is $125 per attempt, the same for first tries and retakes, paid when you schedule. Prov also lists practice exams at $25 each and a hand score request at $25.

The Building Commission does not post its license fee amounts online. License fees are paid by cash or check to the Building Commission; cards are not accepted. Insurance and bond premiums are on top of that. Call (812) 436-7880 for the current fee schedule.

Reciprocity and Working Elsewhere in Indiana

Vanderburgh County does not take NASCLA transcripts, so an out-of-county score will not replace the Evansville exam. Going the other way, a score earned in Evansville is a local result, and whether another agency recognizes it is that agency's call.

Within Indiana, every city or county that licenses electricians sets its own rules. If you work across county lines, check each jurisdiction's requirements. NASCLA's electrical agency list shows the state boards that use the program, and each one sets its own conditions on scores from elsewhere.

After You Pass the Vanderburgh County Exam

Your score goes to the county automatically, and you get a copy at the test site. Take your passing score, license fee, insurance proof and surety bond to the Building Commission for a provisional license. Final approval follows the Licensing and Disciplinary Board's score review.

The Commission's licensing pages do not spell out renewal periods or continuing education for electrical licenses, so ask the license clerk what applies when your license is issued. If you want a hand score review, Prov requires a signed request within 90 days of your exam.

Indiana NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does Indiana have a statewide NASCLA electrical exam?

No. Indiana does not license electricians statewide. Vanderburgh County is the Indiana jurisdiction that uses NASCLA's electrical exams, and our Indiana products are built for those county exams.

Which NASCLA exams does Vanderburgh County accept?

NASCLA lists two: Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician and Journeyman Electrician. Prov gives both in Evansville.

Can I transfer a NASCLA electrical score from another state to Evansville?

No. Vanderburgh County does not currently accept NASCLA transcripts. You must register with the local board and test in Evansville.

How many questions are on the Vanderburgh County master electrician exam?

It has 110 questions, including 10 experimental items, and you get 4.5 hours. The passing score is 75 percent.

What score do I need on the journeyman exam?

The Journey-level exam requires 70 percent. It has 110 questions (100 scored) and a 5-hour time limit.

How much does the exam cost?

The current Prov bulletin lists $125 per attempt, paid when you schedule. Building Commission license fees are separate and not posted online, so call the license clerk.

How much experience do I need for a Vanderburgh County electrical license?

The Building Commission requires electrical applicants to show at least four years of experience, documented in three notarized recommendation letters on letterhead.

What happens if I fail?

You return to the board for a new eligibility before retesting. Each eligibility covers one attempt, and the exam is offered up to three times a year.

Can I tab and highlight my books?

Yes. Highlighting, underlining in pen and permanent tabs are allowed. Handwritten notes, movable tabs, sticky notes and added photocopies are not.
