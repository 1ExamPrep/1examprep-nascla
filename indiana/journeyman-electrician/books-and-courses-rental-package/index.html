Exam Details

The current examination contains 110 questions. Of those questions, 100 are scored and 10 are experimental items. Candidates are allowed 5 hours to complete the examination, and the current passing standard is 70%.

The examination content is distributed across the following subject areas:

Candidates should develop strong National Electrical Code navigation skills while also reviewing safety, theory, troubleshooting, plans, and specialty electrical systems.

Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use authorized reference materials that comply with the current Prov testing rules.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation a major part of preparation. Candidates should be able to identify the subject of a question, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the applicable code provision, table, formula, or technical concept, and then apply that information efficiently.

All rental books in this package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting helps draw attention to important material, while permanent tabs create recognizable navigation points throughout each reference. Candidates should use these features during regular study so the organization of the books becomes familiar before exam day.

Prov currently allows approved references to contain highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not permitted. Moveable tabs, including Post-it notes, are prohibited. Added photocopies, photocopied reference books, and test-preparation study guides are also not permitted as exam-room references.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should become one of the most familiar books in the package. Practice using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, chapter structure, individual articles, tables, and cross-references. Learn how general requirements connect with specific rules for wiring methods, conductors, equipment, occupancies, and special installations.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-industry safety review, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 covers general-industry workplace requirements. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications support formulas, calculations, theory, and practical problem solving. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Vanderburgh County electrical license. Candidates must complete the local licensing process before performing work that requires the credential.

  1. Review the local journeyman requirements. Confirm that your electrical experience satisfies the Vanderburgh County standard for the journeyman license.
  2. Complete the local application. Submit the applicable application to the Building Commission or local licensing authority.
  3. Provide recommendation letters. Vanderburgh County requires three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters as part of the application process.
  4. Document electrical experience. Provide the required experience verification showing qualifying electrical field work.
  5. Receive approval to test. The local board reviews the application and determines eligibility before the candidate schedules the examination.
  6. Schedule through Prov. After eligibility is granted, schedule the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination at the approved Evansville testing location.
  7. Prepare with the rental course and books. Study the 2023 NEC, safety standards, theory, calculations, troubleshooting, equipment, wiring methods, and specialty systems.
  8. Take the examination in Evansville. Vanderburgh County currently requires local testing and does not accept outside NASCLA transcripts for this pathway.
  9. Complete the local licensing process. After passing, submit any remaining documentation required for issuance of the credential.

State Requirements

Indiana does not use one statewide journeyman electrician license that replaces all local electrical licensing requirements. In Vanderburgh County and Evansville, electrical licensing is administered locally through the applicable Building Commission and licensing boards.

Electrical applicants must document at least four years of experience or 6,200 hours of field experience in the trade. Experience verification must meet the local documentation requirements associated with the application.

The application process also requires three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters. These recommendation letters are part of the local licensing application and should be completed according to the Building Commission's instructions.

After the application and supporting documentation are submitted, the appropriate board reviews the candidate's qualifications before granting approval to test. Candidates should not schedule the examination until the required eligibility is issued.

Vanderburgh County currently requires candidates to take the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination in Evansville. NASCLA transcripts from tests completed in other jurisdictions are not accepted for this local pathway.

The current retesting process requires a candidate who does not pass to return to the Board for a new eligibility before another attempt. Each eligibility covers one examination attempt, and candidates may participate in up to three examination administrations per year, provided a new eligibility is obtained for each attempt.

Passing the examination does not itself create a statewide Indiana electrical license. The local authority remains responsible for the journeyman credential and determines whether all experience, application, documentation, and examination requirements have been satisfied.

Candidates should also distinguish a journeyman license from other local electrical credentials. Vanderburgh County maintains separate licensing categories with different qualification requirements and scopes of authority.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine technical learning with repeated use of the reference library. The online course provides 6 months of course access, giving candidates a structured period to work through exam topics and practice navigating the highlighted and tabbed books.

Begin with Wiring and Protection, which represents 18 scored questions. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor protection, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and related National Electrical Code requirements.

The category involving cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit represents 16 scored questions. Practice locating requirements for raceways, boxes, conductor fill, fittings, conduit systems, and installation conditions.

General Code Requirements represents 15 scored questions. Candidates should become comfortable with NEC definitions, general installation rules, equipment requirements, working spaces, and provisions that apply across multiple electrical systems.

Electrical Theory and Principles and Equipment for General Use each represent 10 scored questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, transformers, motors, loads, calculations, and equipment-specific code requirements.

Basic Electrical Safety represents 8 scored questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E support review of construction safety, general workplace safety, and electrical safe-work practices.

Testing and Troubleshooting represents 7 scored questions. Review voltage, resistance, current, continuity, test instruments, circuit behavior, and logical fault diagnosis. These questions may require practical electrical reasoning instead of direct code lookup.

Interpreting Plans and Specifications represents 6 scored questions. Practice reading symbols, equipment locations, circuit information, and installation details shown on electrical drawings and project documents.

Special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm systems should also be included in preparation. NFPA 72 supports targeted study of fire alarm and signaling-system topics.

Use the course and books together. Work through a technical subject, answer practice-oriented questions, identify the correct reference, navigate to the applicable section, and use the book to confirm the underlying rule or concept.
