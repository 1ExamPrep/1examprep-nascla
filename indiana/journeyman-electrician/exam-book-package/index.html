Exam Details

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination currently contains 110 questions. Of those questions, 100 are scored examination questions and 10 are experimental items. Candidates are allowed 5 hours to complete the examination.

The journey-level examination is graded against a 70% cut score. Candidates who achieve a score of 70% or higher receive a passing grade.

The current examination content is divided among the following subject areas:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Selects and Installs Cabinets, Boxes, Fittings, Conduit: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

Wiring and Protection represents the largest scored subject area, followed closely by cabinets, boxes, fittings and conduit, General Code Requirements, Electrical Theory and Principles, and Equipment for General Use. Candidates should therefore prepare for both detailed National Electrical Code navigation and broader electrical trade knowledge.

The exam also includes safety, troubleshooting, plans and specifications, special occupancies, special equipment, and communication systems. A strong preparation strategy should address the entire published content outline rather than focusing only on the highest-volume topics.

Testing for Vanderburgh County is conducted in Evansville. NASCLA specifically identifies Vanderburgh County as a participating agency that requires candidates to take the examination in Evansville instead of submitting a transcript from another jurisdiction.

Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use authorized reference materials that meet the testing provider's current requirements.

Prov permits approved references to contain highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs. These methods can help candidates organize important sections and locate frequently used material more efficiently during the examination.

Handwritten notes are not permitted in the reference books. Moveable tabs are not allowed, including Post-it notes. Candidates may not add photocopied documents to a reference book, and photocopied versions of reference books are not permitted. Test-preparation study guides and practice examinations are also prohibited as exam-room references.

Candidates may not highlight, underline, write in, or otherwise mark reference books during the examination itself. Reference preparation must be completed before entering the testing room.

Because the examination is open book, preparation should include regular practice with the references. Start by identifying what a practice question is testing, decide which book is likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and then apply the information. This approach develops both electrical understanding and exam-reference navigation.

The 2023 National Electrical Code deserves substantial attention. Candidates should become comfortable with the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapter structure, articles, tables, and cross-references. Knowing how the Code is organized can reduce unnecessary searching during a timed examination.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Vanderburgh County electrical license. Candidates must complete the local licensing process administered through the Building Commission.

  1. Complete the local license application. Clearly identify the electrical license for which you are applying.
  2. Obtain the required recommendation letters. Vanderburgh County requires three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters as part of the application process.
  3. Document qualifying electrical experience. Electrical applicants must provide proof of the required field experience. Experience verification letters must meet the local documentation requirements.
  4. Submit the application packet to the Building Commission. The application and required supporting documents are submitted for review.
  5. Receive approval to test. The appropriate board reviews the application and determines whether the candidate is eligible to sit for the examination.
  6. Schedule the NASCLA examination through Prov. After receiving eligibility, schedule the examination at the approved Evansville testing location.
  7. Prepare the authorized reference books. Highlight, underline, and permanently tab the books within the current testing rules before exam day.
  8. Take and pass the examination. Journey-level candidates must earn the required 70% passing score.
  9. Complete the local licensing process. After passing, return to the Building Commission and provide the documentation required for issuance of the applicable license.

State Requirements

Indiana does not use one single statewide journeyman electrician license for every local jurisdiction. Electrical licensing requirements can be established and administered locally. In Vanderburgh County and Evansville, the Building Commission manages the local application and examination-approval process for electrical licensing.

Electrical applicants must demonstrate at least four years of experience or 6,200 hours of field experience in the trade for which they are applying. Experience verification for electrical applicants must be provided on letterhead and notarized according to the local application instructions.

The application package also requires three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters. These are personal recommendations rather than company recommendation letters.

Once an application is submitted, it is presented to the appropriate licensing board for approval to test. Candidates should not schedule the examination until they have received the required eligibility approval.

Vanderburgh County is unusual among NASCLA jurisdictions because it does not currently accept NASCLA examination transcripts from tests completed elsewhere. A candidate seeking the Vanderburgh County credential must take the applicable NASCLA examination in Evansville.

After passing the examination, candidates must return to the Building Commission to complete the license-issuance process. The local licensing authority requires the applicable licensing documentation before the credential is issued.

The current Prov retesting policy also requires candidates who do not pass to return to the Board for a new eligibility before testing again. Each eligibility is valid for one examination attempt. The Board permits candidates to participate in up to three examination administrations per year, provided a new eligibility is obtained for each attempt.

Candidates should distinguish between passing the NASCLA examination and receiving the local license. The examination establishes the trade-testing result, while Vanderburgh County remains responsible for reviewing qualifications and issuing the applicable credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

The current examination outline makes it possible to organize preparation around the areas carrying the greatest number of scored questions. Begin with Wiring and Protection, which contains 18 scored questions. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and conductor-related requirements.

The next major category involves selecting and installing cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit, with 16 scored questions. Practice locating NEC rules for raceways, boxes, fittings, conduit systems, conductor fill, installation conditions, and other wiring-method requirements.

General Code Requirements accounts for 15 scored questions. Develop strong familiarity with broadly applicable NEC provisions, including definitions, installation rules, working spaces, equipment requirements, and other subjects that affect multiple electrical systems.

Electrical Theory and Principles and Equipment for General Use each account for 10 scored questions. Electrical theory preparation should include voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, and common electrical calculations.

The OSHA references and NFPA 70E support the eight questions assigned to Basic Electrical Safety. Review construction-site requirements, general-industry workplace rules, and electrical hazard-control practices. Learn which reference applies to a particular type of safety question so you do not waste time searching the wrong book.

Testing and Troubleshooting contains seven scored questions. Prepare by reviewing voltage, resistance, current, continuity, circuit behavior, equipment operation, and the logical use of electrical test instruments. Troubleshooting problems often require applying electrical theory rather than simply locating a code section.

Interpreting Plans and Specifications represents six scored questions. Practice identifying symbols, equipment, circuits, installation information, and other details from electrical drawings and project documents.

Special occupancies, special equipment, fire alarms, special conditions, and communication systems should also be included in your preparation schedule. NFPA 72 is particularly useful for fire alarm and signaling-system topics.

Use the books continuously while studying. Instead of answering a practice question from memory and checking the book afterward, practice the same process you will use on an open-book test: identify the subject, select the appropriate reference, locate the relevant information, and apply it to the problem.
