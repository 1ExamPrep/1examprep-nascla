## Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**. Candidates may use authorized reference materials that meet the testing provider's current requirements.

Prov permits approved references to contain **highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs**. These methods can help candidates organize important sections and locate frequently used material more efficiently during the examination.

Handwritten notes are not permitted in the reference books. Moveable tabs are not allowed, including Post-it notes. Candidates may not add photocopied documents to a reference book, and photocopied versions of reference books are not permitted. Test-preparation study guides and practice examinations are also prohibited as exam-room references.

Candidates may not highlight, underline, write in, or otherwise mark reference books during the examination itself. Reference preparation must be completed before entering the testing room.

Because the examination is open book, preparation should include regular practice with the references. Start by identifying what a practice question is testing, decide which book is likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and then apply the information. This approach develops both electrical understanding and exam-reference navigation.

The **2023 National Electrical Code** deserves substantial attention. Candidates should become comfortable with the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapter structure, articles, tables, and cross-references. Knowing how the Code is organized can reduce unnecessary searching during a timed examination.

## State Requirements

Indiana does not use one single statewide journeyman electrician license for every local jurisdiction. Electrical licensing requirements can be established and administered locally. In Vanderburgh County and Evansville, the Building Commission manages the local application and examination-approval process for electrical licensing.

Electrical applicants must demonstrate at least **four years of experience or 6,200 hours of field experience** in the trade for which they are applying. Experience verification for electrical applicants must be provided on letterhead and notarized according to the local application instructions.

The application package also requires **three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters**. These are personal recommendations rather than company recommendation letters.

Once an application is submitted, it is presented to the appropriate licensing board for approval to test. Candidates should not schedule the examination until they have received the required eligibility approval.

Vanderburgh County is unusual among NASCLA jurisdictions because it does not currently accept NASCLA examination transcripts from tests completed elsewhere. A candidate seeking the Vanderburgh County credential must take the applicable NASCLA examination in Evansville.

After passing the examination, candidates must return to the Building Commission to complete the license-issuance process. The local licensing authority requires the applicable licensing documentation before the credential is issued.

The current Prov retesting policy also requires candidates who do not pass to return to the Board for a new eligibility before testing again. Each eligibility is valid for one examination attempt. The Board permits candidates to participate in up to **three examination administrations per year**, provided a new eligibility is obtained for each attempt.

Candidates should distinguish between passing the NASCLA examination and receiving the local license. The examination establishes the trade-testing result, while Vanderburgh County remains responsible for reviewing qualifications and issuing the applicable credential.
