State Requirements

Vanderburgh County's current local ordinance provides that a person cannot be issued a journeyman license unless the applicant establishes at least four years of practical experience in the applicable trade.

The local ordinance also requires journeyman electrical applicants to complete an examination process. For current NASCLA electrical testing, Vanderburgh County has contracted with Prov to administer the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination.

Candidates should distinguish between a journeyman electrician license and a master or contractor license. Vanderburgh County maintains separate licensing requirements for different classifications, and the scope of authority associated with each credential is different.

Vanderburgh County also differs from most NASCLA jurisdictions because it does not currently accept NASCLA transcripts for this examination pathway. NASCLA specifically states that candidates must take the examination in Evansville, Indiana. A passing score earned through another NASCLA jurisdiction does not replace the local testing requirement for Vanderburgh County.

The NASCLA examination itself is not a national electrical license. NASCLA provides an accredited examination accepted or administered by participating agencies, while the local agency remains responsible for reviewing qualifications and issuing the actual credential.

Applicants should complete all local application and licensing requirements before performing work that requires a journeyman license. Local rules govern the license itself, while Prov administers the examination under contract with Vanderburgh County.

Testing and troubleshooting are also important journeyman skills. Review voltage, current, resistance, continuity, circuit conditions, equipment behavior, and the logical use of electrical test instruments. A systematic troubleshooting process is especially useful for questions that require technical reasoning rather than a simple code lookup.

Plans and specifications should be incorporated into preparation. Practice recognizing electrical symbols, equipment locations, circuit information, and installation requirements shown in project documents and electrical drawings.

NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the standard and the location of information involving initiating devices, detection, notification, and system functions.
