Exam Details

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination is administered by Prov. Candidates must first register with the licensing authority. After registration and eligibility are established, the candidate receives information used to schedule the examination through Prov.

The current examination contains 110 questions and allows 5 hours for completion. The 110 questions include 100 scored questions and 10 experimental items. Experimental items are included for examination-development purposes.

The current examination content is distributed across these areas:

  • Interpreting Plans & Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Selects and Installs Cabinets, Boxes, Fittings, Conduit: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions
  • Experimental Items: 10 questions

The current Vanderburgh County candidate bulletin states that Journey-level examinations are graded against a 70% passing score. Candidates receive their score result at the testing center after completing the examination, and Vanderburgh County is notified of the results automatically.

The current examination fee is $125 per attempt. The same testing fee applies to the first examination and retests. Candidates take the examination by computer at the designated testing facility in Evansville.

Wiring and Protection, cabinets and conduit, and General Code Requirements account for a substantial part of the examination. Candidates should build strong NEC navigation skills while also maintaining regular study of theory, safety, troubleshooting, equipment, plans, and specialty electrical systems.

Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use the authorized references identified for the examination, subject to Prov's testing-center rules.

Authorized reference materials are inspected by the test administrator before candidates are permitted into the testing room. Prov allows candidates to prepare approved references by highlighting, underlining in pen, and attaching permanent tabs.

Handwritten notes are not permitted in the reference books. Moveable tabs and Post-it notes are not allowed. Candidates also may not add photocopied documents to a reference or use photocopied versions of reference books. Test-preparation study guides and practice examinations are not approved exam-room references.

Open-book testing still requires strong preparation. With 110 questions and several technical references, searching randomly for every answer can consume valuable time. Candidates should develop familiarity with the organization of the NEC and the other permitted books before exam day.

Practice identifying the subject of a question before opening a reference. Decide which book should contain the answer, use the index or permanent tabs to find the appropriate area, and then confirm the applicable rule or technical information. Repeating this process helps develop an efficient reference-navigation routine.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination is part of the Vanderburgh County licensing process, but passing the examination does not automatically issue a license. Candidates must follow the registration and licensing procedures established by Vanderburgh County.

  1. Register with the licensing authority. Vanderburgh County requires candidates to register with the Board before scheduling the licensing examination.
  2. Receive examination eligibility. After registration, the candidate receives the information needed for Prov to locate the approved eligibility record.
  3. Schedule the exam through Prov. Approved candidates may schedule the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination with Prov.
  4. Prepare using the authorized references. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, and other tested topics.
  5. Take the examination in Evansville. Vanderburgh County candidates must test at the designated Evansville, Indiana testing facility.
  6. Earn the required passing score. Journey-level examinations are graded against a 70% passing score.
  7. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Follow the licensing authority's requirements after examination completion before performing work that requires the applicable credential.

If a candidate does not pass, the current Vanderburgh County testing rules require the individual to return to the Board and request new eligibility before retesting. Each eligibility is valid for one examination attempt. The Board allows examination participation up to three times per year, provided new eligibility is obtained for each attempt.

Reference Books

The current Vanderburgh County NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination identifies the following authorized references:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code 2023
    The primary electrical code reference covering branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, and communication systems.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    A construction-industry safety reference addressing federal occupational safety and health requirements applicable to jobsites and electrical work performed in construction environments.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910
    A federal general-industry safety reference containing workplace requirements, including provisions relevant to electrical hazards and electrical work environments.
  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace safety standard focused on practices and procedures intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023
    A compact electrical reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, tables, conversions, and practical electrical field information.
  • NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2022
    A fire alarm and signaling systems reference covering detection, initiating devices, notification, signaling functions, and related system requirements.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference focused on strengthening electrical theory knowledge and connecting fundamental electrical principles with National Electrical Code applications and calculations.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should reflect the examination blueprint. Wiring and Protection contains 18 questions, making it the largest scored subject area. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor protection, grounding and bonding, and overcurrent protection while practicing how to locate the applicable NEC provisions.

The section covering cabinets, boxes, fittings, and conduit contains 16 questions. Develop familiarity with raceways, boxes, fittings, conductor installation, and practical wiring methods. Learn how general rules interact with requirements for specific wiring systems.

General Code Requirements contains 15 questions. Practice finding definitions, general installation rules, working-space requirements, equipment requirements, and other code provisions that apply across multiple systems.

Electrical Theory and Principles contains 10 questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, loads, conductors, transformers, motors, and common calculations. Understanding the relationship among electrical quantities can help with questions that require more than direct code lookup.

Testing and Troubleshooting contains 7 questions. Practice interpreting voltage, resistance, current, continuity, and equipment behavior. A systematic troubleshooting approach can help you work through unfamiliar scenarios more efficiently.

Basic Electrical Safety accounts for 8 questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E to study construction safety, workplace safety, and electrical hazard controls.

Plans and specifications, special occupancies, equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm topics should also be included in the study schedule. These areas may contain fewer questions individually, but unfamiliar references can consume exam time if you have not practiced navigating them.
