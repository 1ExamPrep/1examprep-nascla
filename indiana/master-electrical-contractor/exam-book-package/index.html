Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized electrical trade examinations accepted or administered by participating regulatory agencies.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Pretest Questions: 10 non-scored items may be included
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Vanderburgh County Scheduling Provider: Prov
  • Local Testing Requirement: Candidates using this pathway for Vanderburgh County must take the exam in Evansville, Indiana.

The current NASCLA examination specifications are designed around practical electrical competency. Questions are multiple choice and are developed from an occupational analysis representing work performed by electrical professionals. Candidates should expect application-oriented questions rather than an examination based entirely on memorizing definitions or Code article numbers.

The exam addresses technical subjects such as electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and equipment, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-efficiency or management concepts.

With a 270-minute testing period, candidates need to balance direct knowledge, calculations, and reference searches. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials subject to the rules of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and the examination administrator.

Open book does not mean that every question can be efficiently researched from scratch. State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing for this product's target jurisdiction is handled locally through the Evansville-Vanderburgh County Building Commission. Indiana does not use a single statewide electrical contractor license for this local pathway, making the requirements of the applicable local licensing authority especially important.

The Building Commission handles contractor licensing as part of its responsibility for construction safety, permits, inspections, property maintenance, and related building regulation within its jurisdiction. Contractors and tradesmen seeking applicable local licenses must satisfy the licensing requirements established by the Commission.

Current Building Commission licensing information states that contractors and tradesmen must pass a written examination and provide verification of experience. Applicants must also provide proof of the required insurance, bond, and workers' compensation insurance as applicable to the license.

For electrical-license applicants, the Building Commission's current licensing process requires proof of at least four years of experience or 6,200 hours of experience in the electrical field. Supporting electrical experience documentation must meet the Commission's application requirements.

The local licensing process also calls for three signed and notarized personal recommendation letters as part of the application package. Electrical applicants must submit the required experience documentation with their application materials for review by the appropriate local board.

    A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, conversions, tables, and technical information useful for electrical problem solving and exam preparation.

    A study reference connecting fundamental electrical theory, calculations, and electrical principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to use an exam book package is to make the references part of your preparation from the beginning. Candidates should learn what each book covers and how its information is organized instead of waiting until examination day to begin navigating the materials.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice finding requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment. Work with definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-referenced sections so you become comfortable following a Code question through multiple provisions when necessary.

Electrical theory should be reviewed through active calculations. Use the theory references to reinforce voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other fundamentals. For each calculation, identify the known values, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, calculate carefully, and then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the final selection.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Understanding the purpose of each reference makes it easier to determine where to search when a question involves construction safety, electrical hazards, or workplace electrical procedures.

Project-management material should also receive regular attention. Electrical contractors operate within projects that involve planning, estimating, communication, coordination, documentation, risk, and schedule considerations. These subjects complement technical electrical knowledge and should remain part of a balanced preparation plan.

Timed reference-navigation exercises can help candidates develop exam-ready habits. Select a practice question, identify the subject, choose the appropriate reference, locate the supporting material, and measure the time required. Topics that repeatedly require long searches can then receive additional study attention.
