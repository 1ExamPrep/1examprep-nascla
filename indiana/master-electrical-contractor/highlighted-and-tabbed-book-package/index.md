## Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use authorized references during the examination, subject to the reference-material rules established by Prov for Vanderburgh County.

Current examination rules permit highlighting, underlining in pen, and attaching permanent tabs. Handwritten notes are not permitted in the reference books. Moveable tabs and Post-it notes are not allowed, and candidates cannot add photocopied documents to their references. Photocopied versions of reference books are also prohibited.

These rules make a properly prepared highlighted and tabbed reference set valuable for study. Permanent tabs provide a practical way to identify major sections before exam day, while highlighting can help important text stand out when reviewing technical provisions. Candidates should become comfortable with the location and purpose of each tab rather than relying on the tabs alone.

The National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Work with the index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice locating requirements for services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

Safety references also deserve focused review. OSHA construction-safety requirements and NFPA 70E workplace electrical-safety requirements address different aspects of professional electrical work. Candidates should understand which reference is most likely to contain the answer to a particular safety question.

Electrical theory should be practiced through calculations and applied problems. Study voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other fundamental concepts. Develop a consistent approach for identifying known values, selecting the appropriate formula, completing the calculation, and checking whether a Code requirement affects the result.

During the examination, candidates may not highlight, underline, mark, or write information into their references. All permitted highlighting and permanent tabbing should therefore be completed before entering the testing room.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Highlighted and tabbed references work best when candidates actively use them throughout preparation. Do not wait until exam day to learn where the tabs lead. Practice moving between the NEC, safety references, theory resources, project-management material, and other books while answering realistic electrical questions.

Begin with the subjects carrying the largest portions of the examination. General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each account for 17 questions in the current outline, while Wiring Methods and Materials accounts for 16. General Equipment Use accounts for another 13 questions. These areas make National Electrical Code navigation an important part of a balanced study plan.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 11 questions. Use theory references for active problem solving instead of passive reading. Practice equations, circuit relationships, power calculations, motors, transformers, and other common electrical concepts until you can identify the correct approach quickly.

Safety accounts for 9 questions, while Project Design and Management accounts for 8. Review OSHA and NFPA 70E material and become familiar with project planning and management concepts. Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions account for 8 questions, so candidates should also practice navigating the specialty portions of the NEC.

Use timed lookup drills to measure progress. Select a practice question, identify the subject, choose the correct reference, move to the applicable permanent tab, locate the relevant information, and verify the answer. This method builds technical knowledge while improving the reference-navigation skills that matter on an open-book test.
