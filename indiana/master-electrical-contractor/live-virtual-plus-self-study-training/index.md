## Open Book Test

The Vanderburgh County NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is open book. Candidates may use authorized references in accordance with the current Prov Candidate Information Bulletin and test-center requirements.

Prov specifically encourages candidates to become familiar with the examination subject areas and authorized reference materials before testing. Candidates may prepare approved references by highlighting text and placing permanent tabs on important pages. Current reference-material rules allow highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs.

Handwritten notes are not permitted in the reference books. Moveable tabs, Post-it notes, and additional photocopied materials are not allowed. Candidates may not write, highlight, or otherwise add material to their books during the examination.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive substantial navigation practice. Candidates should become familiar with the NEC index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Important preparation areas include general Code requirements, branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor requirements, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, and related calculations. Build a consistent approach: determine what the question provides, identify what must be solved, select the correct formula or reference, complete the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the result.

Safety preparation should include both construction-safety material and electrical workplace-safety requirements. Candidates should learn which reference is likely to contain a particular safety topic rather than searching every safety resource for each question.

Live instruction can help candidates understand how these subject areas connect, while self-study provides the repetition necessary to strengthen navigation skills. Timed practice with the actual references can help candidates become more comfortable deciding which book to use and where to begin the search.
