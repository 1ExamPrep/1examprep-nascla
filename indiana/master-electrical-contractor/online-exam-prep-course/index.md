## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials according to current testing rules. Effective open-book preparation should focus on learning where information is located and how to retrieve it efficiently.

The National Electrical Code is particularly important. Candidates should become comfortable with the NEC structure, index, Article 100 definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice locating rules involving branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, and special installations.

The supporting references should also become familiar tools. Safety questions may involve OSHA construction requirements or NFPA 70E. Electrical theory questions may require principles and calculations found in electrical textbooks and field references. Project-management questions can involve planning, organization, risk, coordination, and other contractor responsibilities.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed under NASCLA examination rules. References must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Temporary tabs such as removable notes are not permitted. Candidates should prepare their books according to the current examination rules before arriving at the testing location.

A useful study exercise is to time individual reference searches. Read a practice question, identify the electrical subject, decide which book contains the answer, find the applicable provision, and confirm the result. Repeating this process can improve navigation speed and make open-book testing more manageable.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should combine Code study with theory, calculations, safety, project-management review, and repeated reference navigation. Candidates should avoid treating an open-book examination as a test where every answer can simply be searched without prior knowledge.

Start with the heavily represented Code subjects. General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Equipment for General Use make up a substantial percentage of the scored examination. Review services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements.

Electrical Theory should be studied through active calculation practice. Develop a routine for identifying known values, determining what must be solved, selecting the appropriate formula, maintaining consistent units, completing the calculation, and determining whether a Code requirement changes the final answer.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E workplace electrical safety. Learn which reference applies to different types of safety questions so you can avoid wasting time searching in the wrong book.

Project Design and Management should also remain part of the study schedule. Electrical contractors operate within construction projects that require planning, coordination, communication, scheduling, risk awareness, documentation, and management responsibilities. These professional subjects are included in the NASCLA examination blueprint.

Timed practice is particularly useful. Work through sets of questions using the references and monitor the time required for calculations and lookups. If a particular topic repeatedly takes too long to find, spend additional study time learning how that section of the reference is organized.
