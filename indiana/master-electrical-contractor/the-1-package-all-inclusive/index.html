Exam Details

Vanderburgh County, Indiana has contracted with Prov, Inc. to administer the NASCLA Electrical Contractor (Master Electrician/Unlimited Electrician) examination. Candidates must first register with the licensing authority before they can schedule the exam through Prov.

The current examination contains 110 questions and allows 4.5 hours for completion. The exam is designed to measure the knowledge expected of individuals who conduct business involving the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

The examination covers a broad range of electrical contractor subjects. Current content areas include Project Design and Management, Safety, Electrical Theory and Principles, General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, General Equipment Use, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment and Special Conditions, Communication Systems, and experimental items.

The current exam distribution includes 8 questions in Project Design and Management, 9 in Safety, 11 in Electrical Theory and Principles, 17 in General Code Requirements, 17 in Wiring and Protection, 16 in Wiring Methods and Materials, 13 in General Equipment Use, 8 in Special Occupancies, Special Equipment and Special Conditions, 1 in Communication Systems, and 10 experimental questions.

The current Vanderburgh County candidate bulletin lists the examination fee at $125 per exam, including retests. Candidates take the examination by computer at the designated testing facility in Evansville, Indiana.

Vanderburgh County candidates are required to take the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination through the county testing process. NASCLA identifies Vanderburgh County as an agency that does not currently accept transcripts, so candidates must take the examination in Evansville.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in Indiana can involve local jurisdiction requirements. For this package, the relevant licensing jurisdiction is Vanderburgh County. Candidates pursuing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician pathway must follow Vanderburgh County's registration, testing, and licensing procedures.

NASCLA lists Vanderburgh County as a participating Electrical Examination Program agency that accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Vanderburgh County also accepts the NASCLA Journey-level Electrician examination.

Unlike some NASCLA participating agencies, Vanderburgh County does not currently accept NASCLA transcripts. Candidates seeking this licensing pathway must take the examination in Evansville, Indiana rather than relying on a previously transmitted NASCLA examination transcript.

Vanderburgh County has contracted with Prov to administer the licensing examinations. Candidates must register with the licensing authority before scheduling the exam, and the candidate's testing information is then made available to Prov for scheduling.

The NASCLA examination should not be confused with a national electrical license. NASCLA provides an accredited trade examination used by participating agencies, but the licensing authority retains control over its own application process, eligibility requirements, approvals, and contractor licensing decisions.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Vanderburgh County NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor preparation plan should combine technical study with repeated use of the authorized references. The examination includes several major electrical knowledge areas, so candidates should avoid relying on NEC review alone.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a central part of your study routine. Practice locating articles, working with the index, reading tables, following cross-references, and determining where common code subjects are organized. Familiarity with the structure of the NEC can reduce unnecessary searching during an open-book exam.

Electrical theory also deserves focused study. Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to strengthen your understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, conductors, loads, and calculations.

Safety is a dedicated exam content area. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction jobsite safety preparation, while NFPA 70E supports electrical workplace safety review. Candidates should understand the distinction between general jobsite safety requirements and electrical hazard-related practices.

Project Design and Management is also specifically tested. Contractors need to understand more than field installation techniques. Planning, coordination, project organization, and management responsibilities are part of the broader knowledge expected of an electrical contractor.

The current exam includes 110 questions in 4.5 hours, so pacing should be part of your preparation. Some questions may be answered from knowledge, while others may require reference searches or calculations. Practicing with your highlighted and tabbed books can help you develop a more consistent method for deciding when to search and where to look.

Use the reference set throughout your course preparation. When answering practice questions, identify the subject first and choose the likely book before searching. This creates a more deliberate approach to open-book testing and helps reinforce familiarity with the entire reference library.
