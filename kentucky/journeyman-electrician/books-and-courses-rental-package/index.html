Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA developed the examination using a national job analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts to represent the technical knowledge and practical skills used by journeyman electricians.

Kentucky is listed by NASCLA as an agency that accepts both the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the Journeyman Electrician examination. Kentucky began accepting the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination as an examination option in 2025.

The examination is designed around broad areas of journeyman electrical competency. Candidates should prepare for subjects including:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also devote time to electrical theory, troubleshooting, calculations, workplace safety, and interpretation of electrical plans.

The NASCLA examination is a trade examination rather than a license. Passing it does not independently authorize electrical work in Kentucky. Candidates must still apply for Kentucky licensure and satisfy the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction's qualification requirements.

NASCLA examination applications are handled through its National Examination Database. Candidates who pass an accredited examination can use the database to transmit their examination result electronically to participating regulatory agencies that accept the examination.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is one component of becoming licensed as an electrician in Kentucky. Licensing is administered through the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction.

  1. Review Kentucky electrician qualifications. Determine how your electrical work experience, approved training, education, teaching experience, or qualifying military experience applies to the state's licensing requirements.
  2. Gather experience documentation. Kentucky requires verifiable electrical trade experience and supporting records appropriate to the qualification pathway being used.
  3. Select the NASCLA examination pathway. Kentucky accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination as an examination option for electrician applicants.
  4. Prepare for the trade examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, OSHA requirements, NFPA safety standards, calculations, troubleshooting, equipment, wiring methods, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the current NASCLA application, scheduling, identification, and testing requirements.
  6. Submit the examination result to Kentucky. Complete the applicable NASCLA transcript process and provide the examination documentation required for the Kentucky licensing application.
  7. Complete the Kentucky electrician application. Submit the required application and qualifying experience documentation to the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction.
  8. Receive the applicable electrician license. Kentucky issues the license after the applicant satisfies the statutory and regulatory requirements.
  9. Maintain the license. Follow Kentucky's current renewal and continuing-education requirements after licensure.

State Requirements

Electrician licensing in Kentucky is administered by the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Kentucky maintains separate licensing categories for electricians, master electricians, and electrical contractors.

Under Kentucky law, an applicant for an electrician license must generally complete 8,000 hours of verifiable experience in the electrical trade. The qualifying experience must meet the requirements established by Kentucky law and the Department.

Kentucky permits certain education and training to substitute for a portion of the experience requirement. Qualifying electrical training, education, teaching experience, and applicable supervised military occupational specialty experience can be applied according to the specific provisions established under Kentucky law.

Completion of qualifying electrical training can substitute for part of the required field experience. Kentucky law also recognizes qualifying associate degree or diploma programs in electrical technology or related fields from the Kentucky Community and Technical College System or another accredited college or university.

Applicable supervised military occupational specialty experience can also be used when it meets Kentucky's requirements. Documentation requirements apply to candidates using military experience as part of the licensing pathway.

Applicants should maintain accurate records showing employers, dates of employment, electrical duties, training programs, educational credentials, and other information needed to demonstrate qualifying experience. Kentucky may require verification documents before determining that an applicant meets the licensing standard.

Kentucky law requires an electrician applicant to achieve a passing score on the examination required or accepted by the Department. The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is now one accepted option.

Kentucky electrician licenses are distinct from master electrician and electrical contractor licenses. An individual seeking to perform work or operate a contracting business that requires another license classification must satisfy the separate requirements for that credential.

Kentucky electrician licensing rules also provide for license renewal. Current Kentucky law provides for electrician licenses to renew on a two-year cycle.

The examination pathway and the license application should be treated as connected but separate parts of the process. The examination demonstrates trade knowledge, while Kentucky evaluates the applicant's experience and other qualifications before issuing the license.
