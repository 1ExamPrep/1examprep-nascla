Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is designed to evaluate the knowledge and skills needed to properly perform electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work in accordance with applicable electrical codes and industry standards.

NASCLA developed the examination using a national occupational analysis with input from electrical subject matter experts. The examination covers a broad range of journeyman-level subjects rather than focusing exclusively on memorized NEC rules.

Major areas of preparation include:

  • Interpreting electrical plans and specifications
  • Basic electrical safety
  • Electrical theory and principles
  • Testing and troubleshooting
  • General electrical code requirements
  • Wiring and protection
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Equipment for general use
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Special conditions and communication systems

Kentucky officially began offering applicants the option of using the NASCLA examination for electrician and master electrician licensure in 2025. As of January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical examinations are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code, making the 2023 NEC especially important for current electrical licensing preparation.

Applicants using the NASCLA examination for Kentucky licensure are required to provide Kentucky with a copy of their passing score sheet and submit an electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Authorized reference materials may be used under the examination rules established for the NASCLA testing program.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should be able to recognize what a question is testing, determine which reference is most likely to contain the relevant information, locate the applicable rule or formula, and apply that information efficiently.

Safety references should also be incorporated into regular study. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 addresses construction-industry safety requirements, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 covers general-industry workplace standards. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

Use the remaining references for theory, calculations, field information, and specialty systems. When practicing, identify the subject first and then select the most appropriate reference. Repeating this process can help turn a collection of books into an organized working reference system.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Kentucky electrician license. Kentucky licensing is administered through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, and applicants must satisfy the state's qualification requirements.

  1. Review Kentucky electrician qualification requirements. Determine how your electrical work experience, apprenticeship training, education, teaching experience, or qualifying military experience applies to the state's requirements.
  2. Document your qualifying experience. Kentucky requires verifiable electrical experience and supporting documentation appropriate to the qualification pathway being used.
  3. Choose the NASCLA examination pathway. Kentucky accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination as an option for electrician applicants.
  4. Prepare with the electrical references. Build familiarity with the 2023 NEC, safety standards, electrical theory, calculations, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Follow NASCLA's application, scheduling, and examination procedures.
  6. Submit your examination documentation to Kentucky. Applicants using the NASCLA pathway must submit a copy of the passing score sheet and an electronic NASCLA transcript through the National Examination Database.
  7. Complete the Kentucky electrician application. Provide the required experience documentation and other materials requested by the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction.
  8. Maintain your license. After licensure, comply with Kentucky's applicable renewal and continuing-education requirements.

State Requirements

Kentucky regulates electricians, master electricians, and electrical contractors through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. An electrician license is separate from a master electrician license and an electrical contractor license.

Under Kentucky law, an applicant for an electrician license generally must document 8,000 hours of verifiable experience in the electrical trade. Qualifying education or training may reduce the amount of field experience required under specific pathways established by Kentucky law.

For example, completion of an approved apprenticeship training program consisting of at least 576 classroom hours may substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable electrical trade experience. A qualifying associate degree or diploma in electrical technology may also substitute for 4,000 hours. Kentucky also recognizes qualifying teaching experience and applicable supervised military occupational specialty experience under its licensing laws.

Experience used for electrical licensure must involve electrical work within the scope of the National Electrical Code, except where Kentucky's military-experience provisions apply. The Department may request additional proof when necessary to evaluate whether the submitted experience meets licensing requirements.

Kentucky requires electrician applicants to pass an examination administered by an approved examination provider or another examination recognized as equivalent by the Department. The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is now an accepted option for Kentucky electrician applicants.

Under Kentucky's electrical licensing regulations, a qualifying passing examination score is valid for three years. Applicants should complete the licensing process while the examination result remains valid.

Kentucky adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code for mandatory enforcement beginning January 1, 2025. Kentucky initially allowed candidates to choose between examination versions based on the 2017 and 2023 NEC during a transition period, but beginning January 1, 2026, electrical examinations moved fully to the 2023 NEC.

Passing NASCLA should not be confused with receiving a national electrical license. NASCLA provides an accredited examination that participating agencies such as Kentucky can accept. Kentucky remains responsible for evaluating applicant qualifications and issuing the actual electrician license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Kentucky NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine National Electrical Code navigation with electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, calculations, and practical trade knowledge. The reference package gives candidates resources for building that broader preparation strategy.

Begin with the 2023 NEC. Practice locating requirements for services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, electrical equipment, and special installations. Avoid trying to memorize every code section. Instead, learn how the NEC is organized and develop a repeatable method for finding information.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside code requirements. Use Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to strengthen your understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, transformers, motors, loads, and related calculations.

Ugly's Electrical References can support calculation practice and review of commonly used electrical formulas and tables. Practice deciding which formula applies rather than memorizing isolated answers. The goal is to understand the relationships among electrical quantities so you can solve unfamiliar problems.

Safety preparation should include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Learn to distinguish between construction safety requirements, general workplace standards, and electrical-specific safe-work practices.

NFPA 72 can support review of fire alarm and signaling systems. Specialty subjects may appear less frequently in everyday electrical work, making advance familiarity with the applicable reference especially useful.

Testing and troubleshooting are also important journeyman skills. Review how voltage, current, resistance, continuity, circuit conditions, and test instruments are used to identify electrical problems. A logical troubleshooting process can help with practical questions that cannot be answered through a simple code lookup.

Use your books while working practice questions. First identify the topic, then choose the reference most likely to contain the answer. Find the relevant section and use it to support your solution. This process reinforces electrical knowledge while developing the navigation habits useful for an open-book exam.
