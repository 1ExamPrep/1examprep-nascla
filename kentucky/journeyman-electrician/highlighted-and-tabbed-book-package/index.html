Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized electrical trade examination developed around the knowledge and skills used by journeyman electricians. The examination program was developed using a national occupational analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts representing the work performed across multiple jurisdictions.

Kentucky officially provides applicants with the option to use the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination for electrician licensure. Kentucky's Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction announced the NASCLA examination option in 2025, allowing candidates to choose the accepted NASCLA pathway instead of relying exclusively on Kentucky's state-specific electrical examination route.

Current Kentucky electrical examinations are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code. Kentucky began mandatory enforcement of the 2023 NEC on January 1, 2025, and after a transition period, electrical examinations moved fully to the 2023 NEC beginning January 1, 2026.

  • Testing and Troubleshooting

These areas require candidates to combine National Electrical Code knowledge with electrical theory and practical trade understanding. Preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, motors, equipment, specialty installations, safety procedures, and troubleshooting principles.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is one component of becoming licensed as an electrician in Kentucky. The Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction evaluates each applicant's qualifications before issuing the license.

  1. Review Kentucky electrician qualification requirements. Determine how your electrical work experience, apprenticeship training, education, teaching experience, or qualifying military experience applies to the licensing requirements.
  2. Document your electrical experience. Kentucky requires verifiable experience and supporting documentation that establishes the applicant's qualifications.
  3. Choose the NASCLA examination pathway. Kentucky accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination as an approved examination option.
  4. Prepare with the required references. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, workplace safety, troubleshooting, calculations, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the examination program's current rules for application, scheduling, identification, reference materials, and testing procedures.
  6. Submit your NASCLA results. Provide Kentucky with a copy of the passing score sheet and submit the electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  7. Complete the Kentucky electrician application. Submit the required application, experience verification, supporting records, and other required documentation.
  8. Maintain the license after issuance. Follow Kentucky's renewal and continuing-education requirements applicable to licensed electricians.

State Requirements

Electrician licensing in Kentucky is administered by the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Kentucky regulates electricians, master electricians, and electrical contractors as separate licensing categories.

An applicant for a Kentucky electrician license generally must provide proof of 8,000 hours of verifiable experience in the electrical trade. Kentucky recognizes specific education, apprenticeship, teaching, and military pathways that may substitute for portions of the required practical experience.

An applicant may use qualifying apprenticeship training toward the experience requirement. Kentucky recognizes approved electrical training programs and requires documentation showing participation and completion of the applicable instructional hours.

Qualifying electrical education can also reduce the practical-experience requirement. Kentucky's current application guidance recognizes qualifying electrical technology education from the Kentucky Community and Technical College System or another accredited college or university.

Kentucky also recognizes qualifying teaching experience in electrical technology or a related field as a substitute for a portion of the required work experience. Applicable supervised military occupational specialty experience may also be used according to Kentucky law.

Experience submitted for an electrician license must be verifiable. Kentucky may require employer documentation or other approved records demonstrating the applicant's electrical work. The Department may request additional proof when submitted experience appears incomplete or insufficient.

Kentucky currently allows candidates to pass the electrician examination through an approved provider, including the accepted NASCLA option. Candidates using NASCLA must provide the required examination documentation to the Department when completing the licensing process.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is currently Kentucky's adopted NEC edition. Kentucky began mandatory enforcement of the 2023 NEC on January 1, 2025, and all Kentucky electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC beginning January 1, 2026.

Kentucky electrician licenses are separate from electrical contractor and master electrician licenses. Candidates planning to operate an electrical contracting business should review the additional licensing requirements associated with the appropriate contractor and master electrician credentials.

Testing and troubleshooting should also be included in a study schedule. Review voltage, current, resistance, continuity, circuit operation, and electrical test equipment. Develop a logical process for diagnosing faults rather than relying exclusively on code lookup.
