## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized trade examination developed from a national occupational analysis of journeyman electrical work. The examination is intended to measure competency in knowledge and skills used to install, maintain, troubleshoot, and repair electrical systems in accordance with applicable codes and standards.

The examination contains **100 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum score of **70** to pass. The published NASCLA examination information allows **270 minutes**, or 4.5 hours, for the scored examination. NASCLA may also include non-scored experimental questions used for future examination development.

The examination content is divided among the following subject areas:

- **Interpreting Plans and Specifications:** 6 scored questions
- **Basic Electrical Safety:** 8 scored questions
- **Electrical Theory and Principles:** 10 scored questions
- **Testing and Troubleshooting:** 7 scored questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 15 scored questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 18 scored questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 scored questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 10 scored questions
- **Special Occupancies and Special Equipment:** 8 scored questions
- **Special Conditions and Communication Systems:** 2 scored questions

Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements make up a significant portion of the scored examination. Candidates should therefore devote substantial study time to NEC navigation while continuing to prepare for electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, plans, equipment, and specialty systems.

NASCLA examination applications are processed through the NASCLA National Examination Database. After approval, candidates receive information for scheduling through PSI. An approved NASCLA examination application is valid for one year, and NASCLA currently allows candidates up to three examination attempts during that eligibility period.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**. Authorized reference materials may be used during the examination under the applicable NASCLA and testing-center rules.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates must recognize the subject being tested, determine which reference contains the needed information, find the correct section, interpret the requirement, and apply it to the question within the available testing time.

## State Requirements

Kentucky regulates electricians, master electricians, and electrical contractors through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. The licenses are separate. A Kentucky electrician license represents the journeyman-level credential, while Master Electrician and Electrical Contractor licenses have their own qualification requirements.

Under current Kentucky law, an applicant for an electrician license generally must have completed **8,000 hours of verifiable experience in the electrical trade** since the applicant's sixteenth birthday. Kentucky allows certain education or training to substitute for part of that experience requirement.

Completion of an acceptable electrical training course may substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable experience. Kentucky also recognizes specified electrical training, teaching, and qualifying associate degree or diploma programs as experience substitutions under the state's licensing statute.

Electrical experience submitted for licensure must be verifiable. Kentucky regulations identify several acceptable forms of documentation, including certain tax records, qualifying employment affidavits, approved apprenticeship records, and applicable military experience documentation.

Kentucky requires applicants for electrician, master electrician, and electrical contractor licenses to pass an examination administered by an approved examination provider. A qualifying passing examination score is generally valid for **three years** under Kentucky's electrical licensing regulations.

Kentucky became a participating agency for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in 2025. NASCLA lists the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction as accepting both the Journeyman Electrician and Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examinations.

Passing NASCLA is not a national license. The examination can satisfy an accepted trade examination requirement for participating jurisdictions, but Kentucky remains responsible for evaluating experience, processing applications, and issuing the actual electrician license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

An effective Kentucky NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should balance code navigation with electrical theory and practical trade knowledge. Because the examination contains 100 scored questions spread across ten content areas, candidates should avoid concentrating exclusively on a single subject.

Start with the **2023 NEC**. Wiring and Protection contains 18 scored questions, Wiring Methods and Materials contains 16, and General Code Requirements contains 15. Together, these categories make NEC familiarity one of the most important parts of preparation.

Practice locating requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, and general electrical equipment. Pay attention to how general code requirements interact with more specific equipment and occupancy provisions.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 10 scored questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, conductor calculations, and basic electrical formulas. Understanding the underlying principles can make calculations easier to solve when a question is presented in an unfamiliar format.

Testing and Troubleshooting should also receive focused study. Journeyman electricians need to understand how electrical systems operate and how measurements can be used to identify faults. Review voltage, current, resistance, continuity, circuit conditions, test instruments, and logical troubleshooting procedures.

Safety preparation should include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Study both general workplace responsibilities and hazards specifically associated with electrical work. Knowing the difference between construction safety requirements, general industry requirements, and electrical safety practices can help when answering scenario-based questions.

Interpreting Plans and Specifications is another tested area. Practice identifying electrical symbols, equipment, circuit information, locations, layouts, and installation requirements from plans. Journeyman electricians regularly translate plans and specifications into field installations, making this a practical skill as well as an examination subject.

Special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm systems should not be overlooked. These categories may contain fewer questions than wiring and protection, but unfamiliar code sections can consume valuable examination time. Practicing navigation in advance helps reduce that problem.
