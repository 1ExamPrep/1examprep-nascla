## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book trade examination. Authorized references may be used according to current NASCLA and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for technical preparation. Electrical references contain extensive information, and searching every question from the beginning can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should build enough subject knowledge to identify what a question is asking before opening a reference.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a major part of preparation. Kentucky adopted the 2023 NEC and, beginning January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical licensing examinations are based on the 2023 edition. Candidates preparing for current Kentucky electrical licensing should therefore become comfortable with the organization and terminology of the 2023 NEC.

Practice using the NEC index, definitions, article structure, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Important study areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and specialty electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be practiced actively. Work through voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related calculations. A consistent method can help: identify the known information, determine what must be solved, choose the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the result.

Safety preparation should include OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning how each reference is organized can make open-book preparation more efficient.

Live virtual instruction can help demonstrate how these subjects connect, while self-study gives candidates the opportunity to revisit difficult material, repeat calculations, practice Code research, and improve familiarity with supporting references.

## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Kentucky is administered by the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Kentucky issues separate licenses for electrical contractors, master electricians, and electricians.

Kentucky accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. The state's participation in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program gives applicants an alternative examination pathway while Kentucky continues to determine whether the applicant meets the state's licensing requirements.

For a Kentucky master electrician license, the applicant must submit the applicable state application and proof of qualifying electrical experience. Kentucky law provides multiple experience pathways. One pathway allows an applicant to qualify with eight years of verifiable experience in the electrical trade. Another allows six years of experience together with completion of an acceptable electrical training course. Kentucky also recognizes certain education-based substitutions when the applicant meets the statutory conditions.

Kentucky's current application guidance describes the eight-year experience requirement as 16,640 hours, or 96 months, of full-time electrical work. Applicants can use approved training or education pathways where applicable to satisfy portions of the experience requirement.

The Electrical Contractor license is separate from the Master Electrician license. A Kentucky applicant seeking an Electrical Contractor license must provide the name and license number of the master electrician affiliated with the contractor applicant. The contractor must also provide proof of insurance required by Kentucky law.

Current Kentucky regulations establish biennial licensing for electrical contractors and individual electrical licensees. The application process is administered through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction and requires the applicant to submit the forms and supporting documentation applicable to the credential being requested.

Kentucky adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code for mandatory enforcement beginning January 1, 2025. During 2025, applicants were allowed to select examinations based on either the 2017 or 2023 NEC. Beginning January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical licensing examinations moved entirely to the 2023 NEC.

Applicants using the NASCLA examination for Kentucky licensure must submit a copy of their passing score information and an electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database. NASCLA examination results do not automatically create a Kentucky license. Candidates must still satisfy Kentucky's application, experience, affiliation, insurance, and other licensing requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine technical instruction with repeated independent practice. Live virtual classes help candidates organize a broad examination scope, while self-study allows additional time for calculations, Code review, reference navigation, and reinforcement of difficult concepts.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice using the index and article structure to locate requirements rather than relying only on memorized section numbers. Follow cross-references, work with tables, review definitions, and become familiar with the organization of commonly tested electrical subjects.

Electrical calculations should also be practiced consistently. Work through circuit theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, conductor requirements, and other electrical problems. Focus on the reasoning and method behind each calculation rather than memorizing isolated answers.

Safety preparation should involve direct use of OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn the purpose of each reference and practice finding relevant material so you can distinguish between general construction-safety requirements and workplace electrical-safety requirements.

Project-management and contractor-business references should remain part of the study schedule. Electrical contractors operate within projects that require planning, coordination, communication, documentation, risk management, and professional organization. Energy-efficiency material should also be reviewed so candidates become familiar with the organization of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Timed reference exercises can help connect technical knowledge with open-book testing skills. Select a practice question, identify the subject, determine which reference is appropriate, locate the supporting information, and track how long the process takes. Repeated practice can reveal which books and technical areas need additional attention.
