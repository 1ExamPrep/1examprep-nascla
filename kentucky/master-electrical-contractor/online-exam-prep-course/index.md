## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The examination evaluates knowledge associated with conducting electrical work involving installation, maintenance, service, and repair in accordance with applicable electrical standards and codes.

- **Examination:** NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
- **Exam Classification:** Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
- **Scored Questions:** 100
- **Pretest Questions:** 10 non-scored questions may also be included
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70
- **Time Allowed:** 270 minutes
- **Exam Format:** Open book
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The examination is designed to evaluate both technical knowledge and the ability to apply electrical requirements to realistic situations. Candidates should prepare for application-based questions rather than relying only on memorized definitions or Code article numbers.

The 270-minute testing period provides four hours and thirty minutes for the examination. Candidates need to manage that time across direct knowledge questions, reference searches, and electrical calculations. Developing a consistent pace during practice can help prevent a small number of difficult questions from consuming too much examination time.

The examination may include non-scored pretest questions used for future examination development. These items are mixed into the test and are not identified to candidates. They do not affect the final score, so candidates should approach every question as though it counts.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are permitted to use approved reference materials in accordance with current NASCLA and PSI examination-center rules.

Open-book testing places a premium on navigation. The National Electrical Code alone contains a large volume of technical material, and the complete examination reference collection covers electrical theory, occupational safety, workplace electrical safety, project management, energy standards, business and project-management concepts, formulas, and other electrical topics.

Effective preparation should therefore include more than reading. Practice finding conductor requirements, overcurrent protection rules, grounding and bonding provisions, wiring-method requirements, equipment rules, calculations, safety information, definitions, tables, and special-condition requirements directly in the references.

When studying, start by identifying the main topic in each practice question. Decide which reference is most likely to contain the information, locate the relevant section, and confirm how the requirement applies. Over time, this process can improve both recognition and lookup speed.

Book familiarity is especially valuable when several references cover related concepts. A candidate should be able to distinguish when a question is asking for an NEC requirement, an electrical-theory principle, a workplace-safety rule, a project-management concept, or information contained in another approved reference.

## State Requirements

Kentucky requires master electrician applicants to demonstrate substantial electrical-trade experience. A master electrician applicant is required to document 16,000 hours of verifiable experience in the electrical trade since age sixteen, subject to the substitutions and documentation methods recognized under Kentucky law and administrative regulations.

Approved education and training can substitute for a portion of the required experience. Completion of an approved apprenticeship-training program consisting of at least 288 classroom hours can substitute for 4,000 hours of verifiable electrical experience. Qualifying teaching experience in electrical technology or a related field may also substitute for 4,000 hours.

An associate degree or diploma in electrical technology from the Kentucky Community and Technical College System or another accredited college or university may substitute for 6,000 hours of verifiable electrical-trade experience when accepted under Kentucky's licensing requirements.

Applicants must also pass an approved master electrician examination. Kentucky currently recognizes the NASCLA examination as one of the available examination options for master electrician applicants. Candidates using the NASCLA route must submit a copy of their passing results and the required electronic NASCLA transcript as part of the Kentucky licensing process.

An electrical contractor license and a master electrician license are related but distinct Kentucky credentials. To apply for a Kentucky electrical contractor license, the contractor must have a Kentucky master electrician license or employ a Kentucky licensed master electrician. Contractors should make sure they are following the application requirements for the specific credential they intend to obtain.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Kentucky license. The NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, while Kentucky continues to evaluate experience, documentation, applications, and other licensing requirements independently.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine technical review with regular problem solving. Electrical theory needs to be understood well enough to recognize which formulas and relationships apply, while Code questions require both comprehension and efficient navigation. Candidates should avoid relying exclusively on one type of preparation.

Begin with core NEC subjects that appear repeatedly in professional electrical work. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Work through practice questions that require you to find and apply exact Code provisions rather than simply identifying an article number.

Electrical calculations should be practiced repeatedly. Build a routine for identifying known values, selecting the appropriate formula, keeping units consistent, performing the calculation accurately, and determining whether an NEC requirement changes the final selection or result.

Safety is another essential part of preparation. OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E address different aspects of workplace protection and electrical safety. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of both references and understand the types of situations in which each resource may be relevant.

Project management and contractor-focused material also deserve dedicated study time. Electrical contractors often work within larger construction projects requiring planning, coordination, scheduling, estimating, risk management, communication, and effective administration. Review these areas alongside technical electrical subjects instead of leaving them until the end of your preparation.

As exam day approaches, complete timed practice sessions. Use the same references you intend to study from, practice looking up unfamiliar material, and monitor how long calculations and reference searches take. Timed preparation can reveal whether you are spending too much time on individual questions and help you develop a better pacing strategy.
