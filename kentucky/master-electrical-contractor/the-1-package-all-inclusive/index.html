Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes an examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Kentucky is a participating jurisdiction through the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction.

Kentucky began offering applicants the option to use the NASCLA examination for Master Electrician licensure in 2025. Candidates who use the NASCLA examination must submit both a copy of the passing score sheet and an electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database as part of the Kentucky licensing process.

The NASCLA electrical examination is based on a national job analysis developed around the work performed by experienced electrical professionals. Preparation includes electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, project design and management, safety, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special electrical systems, and related subjects.

Kentucky also separately licenses electrical contractors. An electrical contractor license allows a business to conduct electrical contracting operations, but the contractor must be associated with a Kentucky licensed Master Electrician. For a business entity applying for an electrical contractor license, Kentucky requires a designated individual to take the applicable contractor examination on behalf of the business.

Kentucky's electrical contractor examination focuses on Business, Law and Project Management. The current Kentucky Electrical Licensing Candidate Information Bulletin lists 40 questions with 1.5 hours allowed for completion. Tested areas include business organization, contract management, estimating and bidding, financial management, labor laws, licensing, lien laws, project management, risk management, OSHA safety, and tax laws.

Licensing Steps

Kentucky electrical licensing includes separate licenses for Master Electricians and Electrical Contractors. Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination can satisfy the approved examination component for a Kentucky Master Electrician applicant, but additional licensing requirements still apply.

  1. Prepare for the NASCLA Master Electrician examination. Study electrical theory, NEC requirements, wiring methods, safety, project-management topics, equipment, and other subjects covered by the examination.
  2. Take and pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Complete the NASCLA examination application and testing process.
  3. Send the required NASCLA results to Kentucky. Applicants using NASCLA for Kentucky Master Electrician licensure must provide a copy of the passing score sheet and submit an electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  4. Document qualifying electrical experience. Kentucky requires Master Electrician applicants to satisfy the state's electrical experience and training requirements.
  5. Submit the Master Electrician license application. Complete the required application through the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction and provide the required supporting documentation.
  6. Complete the Electrical Contractor licensing requirements. A business seeking an electrical contractor license must satisfy Kentucky's contractor examination, insurance, application, and affiliation requirements.
  7. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or corporation.
  8. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included for business banking, taxes, hiring employees, and other business operations.
  9. Address contractor compliance requirements. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to help organize the responsibilities involved in operating an electrical contracting business in Kentucky.

State Requirements

Kentucky electrical licensing is administered by the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. The state regulates electrical contractors, Master Electricians, and electricians under Kentucky electrical licensing laws and administrative regulations.

For a Kentucky Master Electrician license, applicants must satisfy the state's experience requirements in addition to passing an approved examination. Kentucky's application checklist identifies the NASCLA examination as one of the approved Master Electrician examination options.

Kentucky requires significant electrical trade experience for Master Electrician applicants. The state recognizes qualifying electrical work performed within the scope of the National Electrical Code and requires applicants to submit documentation supporting their experience. Approved electrical training programs may also be used as part of the qualification pathway when they meet Kentucky requirements.

A Kentucky electrical contractor license is separate from the Master Electrician license. A business may hold an Electrical Contractor license without the business owner personally holding the Master Electrician license, but the contractor must be associated with a Kentucky licensed Master Electrician.

Kentucky also requires electrical contractor applicants to provide proof of required insurance. State law requires compliance with workers' compensation and unemployment insurance requirements when applicable and requires general liability insurance of at least $500,000 for an electrical contractor license.

Examination results used for Kentucky electrical licensing have a defined validity period under Kentucky regulations. Applicants should complete the licensing application process while their qualifying examination result remains valid and submit all required supporting documentation to the Department.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Kentucky NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor study plan should combine electrical theory, code navigation, safety, project management, and business-law preparation. Because this licensing pathway involves both technical and business responsibilities, candidates benefit from studying each area separately while also understanding how they work together in professional electrical contracting.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a central part of the electrical trade study schedule. Practice using the index, locating major articles, reviewing tables, following cross-references, and recognizing where different types of electrical requirements are organized.

Electrical theory also deserves dedicated study time. Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications can support review of circuits, current, voltage, resistance, power, conductors, loads, and calculations. Understanding the underlying electrical principles can make it easier to apply code requirements to unfamiliar problems.

Safety preparation should include construction jobsite safety and electrical workplace safety. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E provide resources for developing familiarity with those subjects.

Kentucky electrical contractors must also understand business responsibilities. The state contractor examination covers business organization, contracts, estimating and bidding, financial management, labor laws, licensing, lien laws, project management, risk management, safety, and taxes. Make time for business-law preparation rather than treating it as an afterthought to the technical exam.

Use your highlighted and tabbed references during practice sessions. When you encounter a study question, first identify the topic and decide which book is most likely to contain the answer. Then navigate to the relevant section. Repeating this process helps build practical open-book testing habits.
