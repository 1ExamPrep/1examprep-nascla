Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and is recognized by participating licensing agencies across multiple jurisdictions.

The Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction is a participating agency and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Kentucky also accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination for the applicable individual licensing pathway.

Kentucky began allowing Master Electrician and Electrician applicants to use NASCLA examinations as an examination option in 2025. Candidates using a NASCLA examination for Kentucky licensure must submit their passing score information and make their electronic NASCLA transcript available through the NASCLA National Examination Database as part of the licensing process.

Kentucky adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code for statewide enforcement beginning January 1, 2025. Kentucky allowed a transition period for electrical licensing examinations during 2025, and beginning January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical examinations are based on the 2023 NEC.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination covers a broad range of professional electrical knowledge. Candidates should prepare for electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special electrical installations, and other technical subjects associated with Master Electrician and Electrical Contractor work.

The breadth of the examination makes a multi-reference study strategy valuable. The National Electrical Code is central to preparation, but supporting references address construction safety, workplace electrical safety, electrical theory, calculations, project management, energy standards, and professional contractor responsibilities.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Approved reference materials may be used during testing according to the current NASCLA and examination-provider requirements.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who understand both the electrical material and the organization of the references. Having a book available does not eliminate the need to recognize what a question is asking. Candidates should first identify the subject, determine which reference is most appropriate, and then locate the applicable information efficiently.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using definitions, the index, articles, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Study should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special electrical systems.

Electrical theory should also be studied through active problem solving. Candidates should practice voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical circuits, motors, transformers, and related calculations. A consistent calculation method can help candidates identify the known values, determine the requested value, select the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and determine whether a Code provision changes the result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction workplace safety, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning which reference applies to a particular safety issue can make open-book research more efficient.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Kentucky is administered through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Kentucky regulates Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Electricians as separate licenses, so candidates should determine which credential or combination of credentials is needed for their professional plans.

The current Kentucky application checklist for a Master Electrician license requires the applicant to pass an accepted Master Electrician examination through an approved pathway that includes NASCLA. The applicant must also submit proof of qualifying electrical experience and the required licensing documentation.

Kentucky's current Master Electrician application materials identify a qualifying experience requirement totaling 16,000 hours in the electrical trade, with approved apprenticeship training and other qualifying documentation recognized under the applicable licensing rules. Candidates should maintain clear records of electrical employment and training so the required experience can be documented during the application process.

Kentucky permits the NASCLA Master Electrician examination as one of the accepted examination options. Applicants using the NASCLA pathway are required to provide a copy of the passing examination results and an electronic transcript through the NASCLA National Examination Database.

The state's current adopted electrical code is the 2023 National Electrical Code. Mandatory statewide enforcement of the 2023 NEC began January 1, 2025. As of January 1, 2026, Kentucky electrical licensing examinations are based on the 2023 NEC, making familiarity with the 2023 edition especially important for current preparation.

A Kentucky Electrical Contractor license is separate from the Master Electrician credential. To apply for an Electrical Contractor license, the contractor must have a Kentucky Master Electrician license or employ a Kentucky licensed Master Electrician. The contractor application identifies the affiliated Master Electrician as part of the licensing process.

Kentucky Electrical Contractor applicants must also submit the required proof of insurance. Business applicants are responsible for satisfying the contractor licensing requirements established by the Department in addition to the individual qualifications held by the affiliated Master Electrician.

Candidates should therefore distinguish between passing the technical examination, receiving an individual Master Electrician license, and obtaining an Electrical Contractor license for a business. These are connected steps, but they are not interchangeable.

The NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted examination requirement, but final licensing authority remains with the Kentucky Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Applicants must complete the application and provide the experience and supporting documentation required for the license they are seeking.
