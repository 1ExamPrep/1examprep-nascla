Licensing Steps

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors administers the contractor licensing process for the state.

Applicants pursue licenses associated with the type and scope of construction work they intend to perform. The process includes application review, qualifying party requirements, and applicable testing.

  1. Identify the Appropriate Classification: Determine the type of contracting work the business intends to perform and select the corresponding licensing classification.
  2. Review Licensing Qualifications: Understand the applicant and qualifying party requirements associated with the requested classification.
  3. Prepare Application Information: Organize the business, ownership, and qualifying party information required for the application.
  4. Submit the Application: Complete the applicable licensing process through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.
  5. Complete Qualifying Party Verification: Follow the Board's online verification process and respond to requests for supporting information.
  6. Receive Examination Approval: Obtain the Board's approval for applicable testing and Business and Law requirements.
  7. Complete Business and Law: Access the LSLBC Contractor Portal and complete the online course and assessment.
  8. Prepare for Trade Examinations: Study the technical subjects associated with the selected classification when examination requirements apply.
  9. Schedule Applicable Trade Testing: Follow PSI registration procedures after receiving examination eligibility information.
  10. Complete Remaining Licensing Requirements: Satisfy the conditions associated with the requested Louisiana contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

The first major section of the Louisiana NASCLA guide focuses on the decisions involved in establishing and organizing a construction contracting business.

Business Planning

Business planning provides a foundation for evaluating company objectives, financial resources, operational responsibilities, and available opportunities.

Contractors benefit from understanding how business planning affects their ability to organize projects, manage expenses, and make informed decisions.

Choosing a Business Structure

Business structure affects company organization, ownership responsibilities, and administrative management.

Understanding different organizational structures helps contractors recognize important considerations when establishing or managing a construction business.

Contractor Licensing

Contractor licensing establishes requirements for performing regulated construction activities.

Louisiana contractors must understand the classifications associated with their intended work and the importance of operating within the scope of appropriate credentials.

Insurance and Risk Management

Construction businesses encounter financial, operational, and contractual risks.

Insurance and risk management concepts help contractors recognize potential exposures and understand important business protection principles.

Business Management and Marketing

Effective business management requires organization, communication, planning, and professional decision-making.

Marketing introduces concepts related to communicating services, developing customer relationships, and managing business opportunities.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second major section addresses important responsibilities associated with running construction projects and coordinating company activities.

Construction Estimating

Construction estimating involves evaluating labor, materials, equipment, subcontractor costs, and other project expenses.

Understanding estimating principles helps contractors recognize the resources required to complete work and prepare organized project proposals.

Estimating and Bidding

Estimating and bidding involve identifying project requirements, evaluating costs, and preparing construction proposals.

Contractors benefit from understanding how project scope, available resources, and financial considerations influence pricing decisions.

Contract Management

Construction contracts establish responsibilities between contractors, customers, and other project participants.

Contract management includes understanding agreement terms, organizing project documentation, communicating expectations, and administering contractual obligations.

Project Scheduling

Scheduling coordinates employees, subcontractors, materials, equipment, and construction activities.

Understanding scheduling principles helps contractors organize work and monitor project progress.

Project Management

Project management combines planning, scheduling, budgeting, supervision, and communication.

These responsibilities are important for coordinating resources, monitoring work, and maintaining organized construction operations.

Construction Safety

Construction safety involves recognizing workplace hazards and understanding important jobsite responsibilities.

Safety awareness is relevant to construction supervisors, employees, subcontractors, and business owners.

Environmental Responsibilities

Construction projects may involve environmental considerations related to materials, waste, and site conditions.

Understanding these concepts supports responsible business administration and project management.

Employee Management

Managing employees involves communication, organization, workplace responsibilities, and professional supervision.

Effective employee management helps contractors coordinate activities and maintain productive working relationships.

Subcontractor Relationships

Subcontractors perform specialized activities on many construction projects.

Strong subcontractor coordination involves clear agreements, organized schedules, communication, and documentation.

Customer Relations

Professional customer relationships depend on clear expectations, consistent communication, accurate records, and organized project administration.

Understanding these concepts helps contractors approach construction services professionally.

Part 3: Business Administration

The third section focuses on the administrative and financial responsibilities involved in operating a construction business.

Financial Management

Financial management helps contractors monitor income, expenses, project costs, and available resources.

Understanding these concepts supports more informed decisions about business operations and construction projects.

Construction Accounting

Accounting provides a framework for documenting financial transactions and monitoring business activities.

Organized records help contractors understand expenses, project costs, and company financial performance.

Cash Flow Management

Cash flow concerns the timing of payments received and expenses paid.

Contractors must recognize how customer payments, payroll, materials, equipment, and other expenses affect available business funds.

Tax Fundamentals

Taxes are an important part of contractor financial administration.

Understanding basic tax concepts and maintaining organized financial records helps construction businesses manage administrative responsibilities.

Construction Lien Laws

Construction liens involve certain payment and property rights.

Understanding lien principles helps contractors recognize the importance of contracts, payment procedures, and project documentation.
