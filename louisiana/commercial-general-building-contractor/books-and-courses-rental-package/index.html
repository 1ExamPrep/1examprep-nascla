Exam Details

This package supports preparation for Louisiana contractor business and law requirements and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Louisiana contractor licensing is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

The NASCLA examination is a standardized commercial general building trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies, including Louisiana. Contractors who pass the examination may use their result in place of an accepted state-specific trade examination when applying through a participating agency and classification.

Louisiana candidates using NASCLA for trade-examination credit must still complete Louisiana’s licensing process and satisfy the applicable business and law requirement. Louisiana currently uses an online Business and Law course for applicants receiving examination credit through NASCLA or reciprocity. Completing NASCLA does not automatically create a Louisiana contractor license.

The NASCLA commercial building examination addresses a wide range of construction topics. The included references cover general requirements, project management, jobsite operations, site construction, concrete, masonry, reinforcing steel, structural steel, precast concrete, post-tensioning, wood construction, roofing, gypsum systems, accessibility, safety, mechanical systems, electrical systems, stormwater controls, procurement, and contracting.

Candidates apply for the NASCLA examination and receive authorization before scheduling. After passing, the result is stored in the NASCLA National Examination Database. The candidate can then send an examination transcript electronically to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors for use with a license application.

The NASCLA transcript addresses only the accepted trade-examination component. Louisiana continues to evaluate the applicant, business entity, qualifying party, requested classification, financial information, insurance, and other licensing requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing permitted references to the testing location and following the current rules for books, tabs, highlighting, markings, bindings, and supplemental materials.

An open-book format does not remove the need for detailed study. The commercial building reference library contains thousands of pages, and candidates cannot depend on reading complete chapters during the examination. They must recognize the construction subject, determine which book is most likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and interpret the material efficiently.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books help create a practical navigation system. Tabs can make major chapters, construction divisions, and code subjects easier to locate. Highlighting can draw attention to selected definitions, requirements, procedures, tables, and technical concepts.

Candidates should use the reference books throughout the six-month course-access period. When completing practice questions, locate the supporting information in the applicable reference and review the surrounding section. This method helps connect technical knowledge with the organization and location of information inside each book.

Current examination rules may restrict loose papers, removable notes, temporary tabs, handwritten additions, or other materials. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and inspect every reference before the examination appointment.

The Louisiana Business and Law component required for applicants using NASCLA is delivered as an online course rather than the NASCLA trade examination. Candidates should complete that requirement through the process established by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Louisiana contractor classification. Determine which classification matches the commercial construction work the business plans to bid, contract for, supervise, and perform.
  2. Determine whether a Louisiana license is required. Review the project type, contract value, and scope of work under Louisiana contractor licensing laws.
  3. Establish the applying business entity. Complete the required business registration and ensure the legal business name is used consistently throughout the licensing application.
  4. Identify the qualifying party. The applicant must designate the individual who will qualify the business for the requested classification.
  5. Submit the Louisiana contractor application. Complete the current application and provide the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, financial, and classification information.
  6. Complete the NASCLA examination application. Apply directly through the NASCLA examination program and receive authorization before scheduling the commercial general building exam.
  7. Prepare with the approved references. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental books with the trade course to study each major construction subject.
  8. Pass the NASCLA examination. Complete the accredited commercial general building trade examination through the designated testing provider.
  9. Send the NASCLA transcript. Use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send the passing result to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.
  10. Complete Louisiana Business and Law. Finish the online Louisiana Business and Law course required for applicants using NASCLA examination credit.
  11. Provide remaining licensing documents. Submit any required financial statement, insurance documentation, qualifying-party information, or supporting records.
  12. Complete final Board review. Respond to requests for corrections, missing documents, or additional information before the license is issued.
  13. Receive the license before contracting. Do not offer, bid, or perform work requiring a Louisiana contractor license until the appropriate license and classification have been approved.

State Requirements

Louisiana contractor licensing is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. The Board licenses commercial contractors, residential contractors, home improvement registrants, and contractors working in regulated classifications.

Louisiana accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as a trade-examination option for applicable commercial building classifications. Passing the NASCLA examination may provide examination credit, but it does not replace the Louisiana license application or the Board’s review of the applicant’s qualifications.

Applicants using NASCLA examination credit must complete the Louisiana Business and Law online course. The course addresses Louisiana contractor licensing, laws, regulations, and business responsibilities. It is separate from the NASCLA commercial building trade examination.

The contractor license is issued to the applying individual or business entity. The applicant must identify the qualifying party connected with the trade classification and provide the business, ownership, financial, and supporting information required by the Board.

Louisiana contractor classifications define the types of work a licensee may perform. Candidates should select a classification that matches their intended construction activities. Additional classifications may be required for work that falls outside the scope of the approved commercial building classification.

Applicants may be required to provide financial information and proof of insurance as part of the licensing process. The Board must receive and approve all required documents before issuing a contractor license or adding a classification.

State licensing does not replace local permits, occupational licenses, tax registrations, inspections, zoning approvals, or other project-specific requirements. Contractors remain responsible for complying with all state and local rules that apply to their business and construction projects.
