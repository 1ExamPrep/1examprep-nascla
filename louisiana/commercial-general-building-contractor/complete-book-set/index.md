## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies, including the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

The examination measures knowledge used to plan, supervise, manage, and complete commercial building projects. It covers a wide range of construction subjects rather than concentrating on a single specialty trade.

Major areas represented by the listed books include project management, jobsite administration, estimating, contracts, safety, concrete, reinforcing steel, post-tensioning, precast construction, wood trusses, steel decking, steel joists, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical systems.

Candidates apply for the NASCLA examination through the applicable NASCLA examination process. After passing, the result is stored in the NASCLA National Examination Database. Candidates may electronically transmit an official transcript to Louisiana or another participating licensing agency.

Passing the NASCLA examination may satisfy the applicable technical trade-examination requirement, but it does not automatically issue a Louisiana contractor license. Applicants must complete the Louisiana license application and meet the requirements established by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

Louisiana qualifying parties using a NASCLA examination result must also complete the Louisiana Business and Law requirement. The current Louisiana Business and Law component is delivered as an online course through the contractor portal and is separate from the NASCLA technical examination.

The examinations and licensing requirements may be completed in the order permitted by the Louisiana Board. All remaining licensing requirements must be satisfied before a license, classification, or qualifying-party designation is issued.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete examination library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved books, but it does not remove the need for detailed preparation. The library contains thousands of pages across textbooks, construction codes, standards, manuals, and technical guides.

A candidate who is unfamiliar with the references may spend too much time searching through unrelated books. A stronger approach is to identify the primary subject of the question, choose the publication most likely to address it, and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, or technical tables to find the information.

For example, structural concrete questions may require ACI 318, while questions about reinforcing-bar placement may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field practices may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel construction questions may involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders. Wood-truss questions may require the BCSI guide, while precast and post-tensioning questions rely on their respective specialty publications.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may rely on the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and construction safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Candidates should use the listed editions during study because code sections, page numbers, terminology, illustrations, and technical requirements may differ between editions. Regular practice with the actual references helps build speed and familiarity before examination day.

All books brought into the testing room must comply with the current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Choose the appropriate Louisiana classification.** The requested classification should correspond with the commercial construction work the company intends to perform.
2. **Determine the license type.** Review whether the company needs a commercial contractor license and which classifications or subclassifications apply to the proposed work.
3. **Identify the qualifying party.** The business must designate the individual responsible for qualifying the company for the requested classification.
4. **Submit the Louisiana license application.** Provide the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, classification, financial, and supporting information.
5. **Receive contractor portal access.** Applicants and qualifying parties receive access to the Louisiana contractor portal after the application is submitted and processed.
6. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the NASCLA application process and receive authorization before scheduling the commercial general building examination.
7. **Prepare with the listed references.** Study the technical subjects and practice locating information throughout the complete book set.
8. **Pass the NASCLA examination.** The result may be transmitted to Louisiana through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
9. **Complete the Louisiana Business and Law requirement.** Qualifying parties using NASCLA credit must complete the Louisiana online Business and Law course through the contractor portal.
10. **Submit remaining licensing documents.** Provide financial information, insurance documentation, qualifying-party records, and other materials required for the requested license.
11. **Complete the Board’s review.** The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors evaluates the application, examination credit, and supporting documentation.
12. **Receive the license before contracting.** The business must obtain the applicable Louisiana license before performing work that requires state contractor licensure.

## State Requirements

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors regulates commercial, residential, mold remediation, and home improvement contractors in the state. Applicants must obtain the appropriate license and classification before performing covered contracting work.

A commercial license applies to qualifying commercial construction projects that fall within Louisiana’s contractor licensing laws. The applicant should select the classification that accurately describes the work the company intends to perform.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. A qualifying party is connected with the license and demonstrates the applicable technical knowledge for the requested classification.

Louisiana accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as a trade-examination option. A candidate who has already passed the NASCLA examination may transmit the result to the Louisiana Board through the National Examination Database.

Using a NASCLA examination result does not replace the Louisiana license application. The company must still provide all required business, ownership, financial, qualifying-party, insurance, and classification information.

Qualifying parties using NASCLA or reciprocity credit must complete Louisiana’s online Business and Law course. The course is accessed through the LSLBC contractor portal after the application process has been initiated.

Applicants should submit complete and accurate documents. Missing financial information, incomplete qualifying-party records, unresolved ownership details, or missing insurance materials may delay the licensing process.

The Louisiana Board makes the final licensing decision. Passing an examination or purchasing this complete book set does not independently authorize the applicant to perform licensed contracting work.
