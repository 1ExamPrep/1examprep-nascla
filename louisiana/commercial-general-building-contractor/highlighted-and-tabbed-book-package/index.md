## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a broad technical trade examination designed for commercial general building contractor candidates. It addresses the knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial construction projects involving multiple building trades.

The technical subjects represented by this book set include project planning, jobsite supervision, estimating, contracts, safety, concrete construction, reinforcing steel, post-tensioning, precast concrete, wood trusses, structural metals, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and building-service systems.

The examination requires candidates to work across multiple books rather than rely on one general textbook. Some topics overlap between publications, making it important to recognize the specific focus of each reference. A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a concrete field-quality question may be found in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

A passing NASCLA result may be submitted to participating contractor licensing agencies for consideration toward an applicable technical trade-examination requirement. Licensing agencies continue to apply their own application, qualifying-party, financial, insurance, classification, and business requirements.

Louisiana applicants using a NASCLA result must still complete the licensing process administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Examination credit does not independently issue a contractor license or authorize a business to perform regulated contracting work.

Louisiana also maintains a Business and Law requirement for qualifying parties using NASCLA examination credit. The Louisiana-focused business reference in this package provides useful preparation for understanding state contractor operations, while applicants must complete the current Business and Law requirement through the process directed by the licensing board.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference books to the examination location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. The examination library contains thousands of pages distributed across textbooks, standards, codes, manuals, and specialty construction guides. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the references may spend too much time searching through unrelated publications.

A practical open-book method begins with subject recognition. After reading a question, the candidate should identify the primary construction topic, choose the publication most likely to address it, and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, or technical tables to locate the information.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Louisiana contractor classification.** The requested classification should match the commercial construction work the business intends to perform.
2. **Identify the qualifying party.** The contractor must designate the individual who will qualify the company for the requested classification.
3. **Complete the Louisiana contractor application.** Submit the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, classification, and contact information.
4. **Provide supporting documentation.** Supply the financial, insurance, business, and qualifying-party documents required for the selected license and classification.
5. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the applicable NASCLA application process and receive authorization before scheduling the technical trade examination.
6. **Study the listed reference books.** Review the technical content and practice locating information through the highlighted and tabbed library.

8. **Complete the Louisiana Business and Law requirement.** Qualifying parties using NASCLA examination credit must complete the state process assigned through the Louisiana contractor portal.
9. **Submit the NASCLA result.** Follow the required transcript or examination-credit procedure so the licensing board can review the result.
10. **Complete the licensing review.** The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors evaluates the application, classification, qualifying-party information, examination credit, and supporting documents.
11. **Receive the license before contracting.** The business must obtain the appropriate Louisiana contractor license before performing work that requires state licensure.

## State Requirements

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors regulates commercial contractors and determines the classifications under which licensed businesses may operate. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the work they intend to bid, supervise, or perform.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. A qualifying party is associated with the company and must satisfy the applicable technical knowledge requirements for the requested classification.

Louisiana accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as an examination-credit option for applicable commercial building work. The NASCLA result addresses technical trade knowledge but does not replace the Louisiana license application or the remaining state requirements.

Applicants may be required to submit ownership information, financial documentation, qualifying-party records, insurance materials, classification details, and other supporting documents. The exact materials depend on the license type, business structure, and classification requested.

Qualifying parties using NASCLA examination credit must also complete Louisiana’s current Business and Law requirement. Access to the applicable process is provided through the contractor portal after the licensing application has been submitted and processed.

Incomplete applications, missing financial information, unresolved ownership details, or missing qualifying-party documents may delay review. Applicants should provide accurate and complete materials and respond promptly to requests from the licensing board.

Passing the examination and purchasing the reference set do not guarantee licensing approval. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors determines whether the applicant has satisfied the requirements for the requested license and classification.
