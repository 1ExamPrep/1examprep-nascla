## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies, including the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains **115 scored questions**. Candidates must answer at least **81 questions correctly** to pass. The examination provides **330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes**, for completion.

The exam covers a broad range of commercial construction knowledge, including:

- Planning and estimating
- Financial and project management
- Construction safety
- Site construction
- Concrete construction
- Masonry
- Metals
- Wood foundations and framing
- Thermal and moisture protection
- Doors, windows, and glazing
- Finishes
- Specialty equipment and conveyance systems

The NASCLA examination is intended to measure commercial general building knowledge across multiple construction disciplines rather than one specialty. Candidates may need to work with building codes, safety regulations, technical standards, construction-management principles, practical trade knowledge, and detailed industry references.

Louisiana also requires the qualifying party for a commercial contractor license to complete the state's Business and Law requirement. The Louisiana Business and Law test is completed through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors portal after the qualifying party receives approval. The current Louisiana Business and Law test contains **50 questions** and requires a score of **70 or higher** to pass.

## Licensing Steps

Commercial contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Applicants must complete the applicable state licensing requirements even when using a NASCLA examination result for an accepted trade classification.

1. **Select the appropriate Louisiana commercial classification.** Applicants must identify the classification or subclassification that matches the commercial work the company intends to perform.
2. **Establish the applicant business.** The contractor license may be issued to an individual or qualifying business entity. Corporations, LLCs, and limited partnerships must maintain active and good-standing status with the Louisiana Secretary of State.
3. **Complete the Louisiana contractor application.** Applicants submit the required business, ownership, classification, and qualifying-party information through the licensing process.
4. **Designate a Qualifying Party.** Commercial applicants must designate a qualifying party to represent the licensed entity and satisfy applicable classification and examination requirements.
5. **Complete the Business and Law requirement.** The qualifying party must complete Louisiana's Business and Law course and test unless that requirement has previously been satisfied.
6. **Satisfy the applicable trade requirement.** A NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result may satisfy an accepted trade examination requirement for applicable Louisiana licensing purposes.
7. **Provide the required financial statement.** Louisiana commercial contractor applicants must submit a current financial statement meeting the Board's requirements.
8. **Complete Board review.** The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors issues the license after all application, document, qualifying-party, examination, and classification requirements are completed.

## State Requirements

Louisiana generally requires a Commercial contractor license for commercial projects valued at **$50,000 or more**, including labor and materials. Commercial subcontractors and specialty contractors performing qualifying work may also be subject to licensing requirements.

Electrical, mechanical, and plumbing commercial projects have a lower licensing threshold. Louisiana requires the applicable commercial license when these projects exceed **$10,000**, including labor and materials. Certain regulated activities, including asbestos, hazardous waste, lead-based paint abatement or removal, and underground storage tank work, are subject to licensing beginning at $1.

Commercial applicants must provide a financial statement showing at least **$10,000 in net worth**. The financial statement must meet the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors' application requirements and be current within the required period.

Every commercial applicant must designate a Qualifying Party. The Qualifying Party represents the contractor for compliance with licensing laws, Business and Law requirements, and applicable classification requirements.

Applicants forming a corporation, LLC, or limited partnership must have an active and good-standing registration with the Louisiana Secretary of State. Business applicants must provide the legal company name, Federal Tax ID or EIN, and applicable entity documentation during the licensing process.
