## State Requirements

Louisiana contractor licensing requires applicants to select a classification that matches the type of work they intend to perform. Commercial general building work may involve broad responsibility for coordinating construction activities, managing subcontractors, supervising projects, and ensuring that work complies with applicable plans, specifications, codes, and safety requirements.

The contractor license is issued to the approved applicant or business entity. The applying business must provide the information requested for its ownership, organization, qualifying party, financial responsibility, and insurance. The exact requirements depend on the requested classification and the current licensing rules.

A qualifying party is generally associated with the business and establishes the examination or technical qualification for the requested classification. The qualifying party does not replace the business entity’s responsibility to meet all remaining application, financial, insurance, and compliance obligations.

The NASCLA commercial general building examination may be used for an accepted trade-examination pathway where permitted. The NASCLA result does not replace Louisiana-specific business and law requirements or the remaining application requirements.

Contractors must also comply with local permit, inspection, business-license, zoning, tax, and registration requirements. A state contractor license does not replace approvals required by cities, parishes, project owners, or other authorities.

Applicants should use current forms and instructions when submitting a license application. Business names, qualifying-party information, examination records, financial documents, and insurance records should be consistent across all application materials.
