Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and is accepted by participating state and local licensing agencies.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractor / Master Electrician / Unlimited Electrician
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The examination covers a broad range of electrical knowledge and professional responsibilities. Current areas include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and special equipment, tools and test equipment, circuit protection, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-related topics.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book examination. Approved references may be used according to current examination and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing makes familiarity with the books especially valuable. A candidate may have access to the correct reference but still spend too much time searching if the organization of that book is unfamiliar. Regular study with the highlighted and tabbed rental references can help candidates learn where major subjects are located and how related information is organized.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and specialty systems.

Electrical theory should be practiced through active problem solving. Work through voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other electrical calculations. A consistent method can help candidates identify the information provided, determine what must be solved, select the proper formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether a Code requirement affects the result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. Learning how these books differ and where major subjects are located can make reference searches more efficient.

Project-management and contractor-business references should also be included in preparation. Electrical contractors work within projects that involve planning, communication, scheduling, coordination, risk, documentation, and professional responsibilities in addition to technical electrical work.

  4. Complete qualifying-party approval. The qualifying party must satisfy the relationship and qualification requirements established by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Electrical is identified as a major classification within Louisiana's commercial contractor licensing system.

Louisiana requires the applicable commercial contractor license when regulated Electrical work exceeds $10,000 in project value. The threshold includes labor and materials. Contractors performing work within this classification must satisfy the licensing requirements established by the Board.

Louisiana contractor applicants must designate a qualifying party. The qualifying party represents the licensed entity for the classification and must satisfy the qualification requirements associated with the contractor's requested scope of work.

Louisiana accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for the Electrical classification. When an applicant requests credit from a NASCLA Electrical examination, the passing transcript must be made available to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors through the NASCLA examination database.

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors does not treat a NASCLA result as a contractor license by itself. The NASCLA examination is an accepted trade-examination credential, while Louisiana retains authority over the contractor application, qualifying-party approval, Business and Law requirements, classifications, and final licensing decision.

Applicants should also distinguish between technical electrical preparation and Louisiana contractor-business requirements. The NASCLA electrical examination focuses on electrical trade knowledge, while Louisiana's licensing process includes separate contractor-business and qualifying-party components.

The contractor business must maintain an eligible qualifying party for the applicable classification. Changes involving the qualifying party can affect the contractor's licensing status, so the qualifying-party relationship is an ongoing component of Louisiana contractor licensing rather than only an examination requirement.

Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination can satisfy an accepted trade-examination component, but applicants must still complete the state application process and meet every licensing requirement associated with the Electrical classification.
