Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates can use approved reference materials in accordance with the rules governing their examination administration.

Open-book testing rewards preparation rather than replacing it. The collection of electrical references contains thousands of pages, and candidates have 270 minutes to complete 100 scored questions. A candidate who searches every question without knowing where to begin can spend too much of the available examination time locating information.

National Electrical Code navigation should become part of every study session. Candidates should practice using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, cross-references, and other organizational tools. Common study areas include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, and electrical equipment.

The supporting references also deserve regular use. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction safety requirements. NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. Electrical textbooks and field references reinforce theory, formulas, and calculations. Project-management resources address planning, coordination, project responsibilities, and management concepts. The energy standard adds another specialized area that candidates should learn to navigate.

A practical method is to work through practice questions with the books open and time each lookup. Identify the subject of the question first, select the reference most likely to contain the information, locate the applicable section, and determine how the requirement applies. Repeating this process develops both subject familiarity and reference-navigation speed.

Candidates should prepare their books according to the current examination rules governing markings, highlighting, indexing, tabs, and additional materials. Using the references consistently during preparation helps make exam-day navigation more natural.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Electrical is one of the state's major commercial contractor classifications.

Louisiana defines the Electrical major classification to include the installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition of electrical wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and related hardware used for supplying electricity to a structure, building, or project.

A Louisiana commercial contractor license is required for electrical, mechanical, or plumbing projects exceeding $10,000 in value, including labor and materials. This threshold is specific to those regulated trade projects and differs from the general commercial project threshold that applies to many other types of contracting work.

Louisiana requires contractor applicants to designate a qualifying party. The qualifying party represents the contractor for compliance with the Louisiana Business and Law requirement and for the examination or credential requirements associated with the classifications listed on the license.

A qualifying party may be a sole proprietor, qualifying full-time employee, stockholder, officer, incorporator, partner, member, or manager when the relationship satisfies Louisiana's requirements. The qualifying party must complete the required verification process and meet the examination or credential requirements associated with the requested contractor classification.

Louisiana requires a qualifying party for each classification carried on the license. The contractor must also maintain an eligible qualifying party while the license remains active. When a qualifying party disassociates from the contractor, Louisiana requires the licensee to report the change and establish another qualifying party within the applicable state timeframe.

The Louisiana Business and Law requirement is separate from the electrical trade examination. The current Louisiana Business and Law examination is completed through the LSLBC Contractor Licensing Portal. It contains 50 questions, and a score of 70 or above is required to pass.

Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Louisiana contractor license. The applicant must complete the Louisiana licensing application, maintain the required qualifying-party relationship, satisfy the Business and Law requirement, and complete the additional conditions established for the commercial contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

The most effective way to use an electrical exam book package is to work with the references throughout your preparation rather than reserving them for test day. Candidates should become comfortable identifying which reference applies to a subject and where important information is organized within that book.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment. Practice using definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-references instead of memorizing isolated article numbers.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Use the theory and calculation references to reinforce voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other electrical principles. For each calculation, identify the known information, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, and verify whether an NEC provision affects the final answer.

Safety preparation should incorporate both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. These references address different areas of construction and electrical workplace safety. Familiarity with both can help candidates recognize which source should be used when answering different types of safety questions.

Project-management preparation should address planning, estimating, coordination, documentation, communication, risk, scheduling, and other professional responsibilities associated with electrical contracting. The project-management and NASCLA contractor references help candidates prepare for subjects that extend beyond technical electrical installation.

Energy-efficiency material should also be included in the study schedule. Become familiar with the organization and terminology of the ASHRAE/IES standard so that relevant information can be found without starting an unfamiliar search during the examination.

Timed practice can help bring all of these skills together. Choose questions from several subject areas, determine which reference applies, locate the information, complete any necessary calculations, and record how long the process takes. Topics that consistently require excessive lookup time can then receive additional study attention.
