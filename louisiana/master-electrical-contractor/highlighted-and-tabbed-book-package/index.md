## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Electrical is identified as a major classification under the state's contractor licensing system.

The Electrical major classification covers the installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition of wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and hardware used for supplying electricity to a structure, building, or project when the work falls within Louisiana's licensing requirements.

Louisiana requires a commercial contractor license for electrical projects exceeding $10,000 in value, including labor and materials. This electrical threshold is different from the general commercial construction threshold that applies to many other types of contracting work.

Louisiana requires contractor applicants to designate a qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual who represents the licensed entity for purposes of complying with licensing law, satisfying the Business and Law requirement, and meeting the requirements associated with the contractor classifications carried on the license.

Qualifying parties can include a sole proprietor, certain owners or officers, partners, members or managers of a limited liability company, or qualifying full-time employees when the relationship satisfies the Board's requirements. Supporting documentation may be required to verify the qualifying party's relationship to the contracting business.

The qualifying party must meet the examination or credential requirements associated with the classification being requested. Louisiana requires the contractor to maintain an eligible qualifying party for Business and Law and for each classification carried on the license.

Louisiana's licensing application specifically allows applicants to request credit from a passing NASCLA examination for Electrical. When this option is selected, the applicant is directed to use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send the transcript to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

The Business and Law requirement is separate from the NASCLA electrical trade examination. Louisiana contractor applicants must complete the applicable Business and Law requirement through the state's licensing process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create a Louisiana electrical contractor license. The applicant must still satisfy the qualifying-party requirements, Business and Law requirements, application requirements, and other licensing conditions established by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

- **NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th**
A contractor-focused reference covering business operations, project management, contracts, planning, risk, financial concepts, and professional construction responsibilities.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
An electrical workplace-safety standard addressing practices intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while working on or around electrical systems and equipment.
- **Ugly's Electrical References**
A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, conversions, tables, and practical technical information useful for electrical problem solving.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
A study reference connecting fundamental electrical theory and calculation principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.
