Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized electrical trade examinations accepted by participating licensing agencies.

Louisiana is a participating jurisdiction in the NASCLA electrical program. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for the Electrical major classification. Applicants requesting credit from a passing NASCLA Electrical examination must have their examination transcript sent to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors through the NASCLA examination database.

The examination is built around practical electrical knowledge and the application of technical information. Preparation should include electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, personal and jobsite safety, grounding and bonding, circuit protection, wiring methods, equipment, specialized electrical systems, project-management concepts, and energy-related material.

Because these subjects appear across several references, candidates should learn to recognize which source is most appropriate for each type of question. A wiring-method problem may require the NEC, while a construction-safety question may require OSHA Part 1926. Workplace electrical-safety questions may lead to NFPA 70E, while theory and calculation questions may require electrical textbooks or field references.

The NASCLA examination is only one component of Louisiana contractor licensing. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors evaluates the applicant, qualifying party, classification, Business and Law requirements, and other licensing documentation before issuing a contractor license.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book trade examination. Approved references may be used according to the current NASCLA and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to understand electrical concepts. Reference books contain extensive technical information, and searching every question from the beginning can consume valuable time. Candidates should build enough technical knowledge to identify the subject first and then select the reference most likely to contain the needed information.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the index, definitions, article structure, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Important areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related calculations. A consistent approach can help: identify what is known, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether a Code requirement changes the final answer.

Safety preparation should include OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. Learning the purpose and layout of each reference can help candidates avoid unnecessary searching during open-book practice.

Live virtual instruction can help explain how these references and technical concepts connect. Self-study preparation allows candidates to revisit topics after class, repeat calculations, practice Code searches, and spend additional time on subjects that need more attention.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Electrical is a major classification under Louisiana's commercial contractor licensing system.

The Electrical classification applies to installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition involving wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and hardware used to supply electricity to a structure, building, or project when the work falls within Louisiana's licensing requirements.

Louisiana requires a commercial contractor license for Electrical work when the project value exceeds $10,000. The value includes labor and materials. The Electrical classification also covers qualifying low-voltage work when the installation involves penetrating walls, ceilings, floors, closed spaces, or the building envelope.

Louisiana contractor applicants must designate a qualifying party. The qualifying party represents the licensed entity for the applicable classification and licensing requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, partner, member, manager, or qualifying employee when the relationship satisfies Louisiana requirements.

Every qualifying party must complete the Louisiana qualifying-party verification process before the Board can complete its review. Once approved, the qualifying party receives the applicable instructions for any trade examination or Business and Law requirement that remains outstanding.

Louisiana accepts passing NASCLA Electrical examination results for the Electrical classification. The NASCLA transcript must be released through the NASCLA examination database so the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors can access the result. The Board does not accept a NASCLA transcript submitted directly by the contracting company.

The Louisiana Business and Law requirement is separate from the NASCLA electrical trade examination. Candidates using NASCLA examination credit must satisfy the applicable Louisiana Business and Law requirement. The current Louisiana process provides the Business and Law test through the contractor licensing portal. It consists of 50 questions and requires a score of 70 or higher.

Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Louisiana contractor license. Applicants must complete the state's licensing process, maintain an eligible qualifying party, satisfy Business and Law requirements, submit the required application materials, and receive approval from the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

Reference Books

    The primary electrical Code reference covering branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, electrical equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    A federal construction-safety reference addressing workplace hazards, worker protection, jobsite practices, electrical safety considerations, and occupational-safety requirements associated with construction work.
  • Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, 6th
    A comprehensive electrical textbook covering electrical theory, circuits, calculations, alternating and direct current, motors, transformers, electrical components, formulas, and practical applications.
  • A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge
    A project-management reference addressing structured approaches to planning, execution, communication, coordination, monitoring, risk, control, and professional project delivery.
  • ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022, Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings - I-P edition
    An energy-efficiency standard covering requirements for applicable buildings and building systems, including electrical and energy-management considerations.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th
    A contractor-focused reference addressing business operations, project management, contracts, planning, risk, financial concepts, and professional construction responsibilities.
  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace-safety standard addressing practices intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while working on or around electrical systems and equipment.
  • Ugly's Electrical References
    A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, conversions, tables, and practical technical information useful for electrical problem solving.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A technical study reference connecting electrical theory and calculation principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should combine instructor-led training with regular independent practice. Live virtual classes provide structure for working through electrical concepts, while self-study allows candidates to reinforce those concepts at their own pace.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code and practice using it as a working reference. Use the index, follow cross-references, locate tables, review definitions, and become familiar with how the NEC organizes related electrical requirements. The goal is to develop navigation habits rather than rely entirely on memorized article numbers.

Electrical theory should be reinforced with calculations. Practice circuit relationships, voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, and other fundamental topics. Consistent problem-solving practice can help candidates identify which method applies and determine when a Code provision must be added to the calculation.

Safety preparation should involve direct work with OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn the purpose of each reference and practice finding safety information without searching through the wrong book. Project-management material and contractor-business concepts should also remain part of the study schedule.

Energy-efficiency preparation should include familiarity with the organization and terminology of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022. Candidates do not need to approach every reference in the same way, but they should understand how each book contributes to the overall examination scope.

Timed reference exercises can help connect technical study with open-book test preparation. Select a practice question, identify the subject, choose the most likely reference, locate the applicable information, and track how long the lookup takes. Repeated practice can reveal which subjects and references require additional attention.
