Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The examination contains 100 scored questions, allows 270 minutes, and requires a minimum passing score of 75%.

The examination content outline includes:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question

General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection are the two largest categories with 17 questions each. Wiring Methods and Materials follows with 16 questions, while Equipment for General Use contributes another 13 questions.

Together, those four areas account for a large portion of the examination, making familiarity with the 2023 National Electrical Code especially important. Candidates should also prepare carefully for theory, safety, project-management, and energy-related questions because these subjects require different references and problem-solving approaches.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates bring permitted reference materials into the examination and must follow the applicable testing rules governing book preparation and use.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who know where information is located. With 100 scored questions and 270 minutes available, the average time is approximately 2 minutes and 42 seconds per question.

Practice should therefore include more than reading. Candidates should repeatedly identify a topic, choose the likely reference, find the relevant section, and confirm the answer within a reasonable amount of time.

For example, questions involving grounding, branch circuits, services, feeders, raceways, conductor ampacity, motors, transformers, or special occupancies may direct candidates to the 2023 NEC. Construction safety questions may require OSHA, while questions involving arc flash, shock protection, or energized electrical work may require NFPA 70E.

Electrical theory questions may be supported by Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, or Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. Project-management questions may require the project-management or NASCLA business reference rather than an electrical code book.

The goal is to develop a consistent search strategy instead of opening several books for every question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the Louisiana Electrical classification. Confirm that the major Electrical classification matches the work your business intends to perform.
  2. Apply through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Complete the applicable commercial contractor licensing application and identify the requested classification.
  3. Identify the qualifying party. Louisiana contractor applications require a qualifying party who is responsible for satisfying the examination requirement for the classification.
  4. Use the NASCLA Electrical examination pathway. Louisiana accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for Electrical examination credit.
  5. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Review the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, wiring methods, safety, project management, equipment, special occupancies, and energy standards.
  6. Pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Candidates must answer at least 75 of the 100 scored questions correctly within the 270-minute testing period.
  7. Send the NASCLA transcript to Louisiana. The qualifier's NASCLA examination transcript must be released through the NASCLA National Examination Database so the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors can view the passing result.
  8. Complete Louisiana Business and Law. The qualifying party must satisfy Louisiana's applicable Business and Law requirement as part of the contractor licensing process.
  9. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. Submit the additional business and licensing information required by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.
  10. Receive final Board approval. Passing NASCLA alone does not issue a Louisiana contractor license. The Board must approve the complete application.

State Requirements

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors regulates commercial contractor licensing and recognizes Electrical as a major classification.

The Electrical classification includes installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition involving wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and hardware for supplying electricity to buildings, structures, and projects.

Louisiana requires the major Electrical contractor classification when the value of applicable electrical work exceeds $10,000.

The Electrical classification also addresses certain low-voltage and communications work. When low-voltage or fiber-optic installation involves mounting equipment or running wiring or cabling through walls, floors, ceilings, closed spaces, or the building envelope, the Electrical classification may be required under Louisiana's classification rules.

Louisiana participates directly in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for Electrical trade examination credit.

Applicants using a NASCLA result must have the qualifier's transcript released to the Louisiana Board through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The Board does not rely on a transcript submitted directly by the contractor company in place of the NASCLA database process.

The NASCLA Electrical examination provides credit for Louisiana's Electrical examination requirement. It does not automatically satisfy unrelated Louisiana trade classifications.

Louisiana also requires qualifying parties to satisfy its Business and Law requirement. The current Louisiana process uses an online Business and Law course with an examination at the conclusion. The course is completed after the qualifier has been approved for it.

A passing NASCLA examination result is not a Louisiana license. The contractor business must complete the application process and satisfy the Board's applicable requirements before a license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Louisiana NASCLA Electrical study plan should begin with the largest exam areas: General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, and Wiring Methods and Materials. Together, these three categories account for half of the scored examination.

Use the 2023 National Electrical Code throughout preparation. Become familiar with the structure of Chapters 1 through 9, the index, definitions, tables, exceptions, cross-references, and the relationship between general requirements and equipment-specific articles.

General Code Requirements preparation should include broad NEC rules involving equipment, installation, working space, conductor identification, listing and labeling concepts, and general provisions that apply across multiple electrical systems.

Wiring and Protection should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, load calculations, and protection requirements.

Wiring Methods and Materials should include conduit, raceways, cable systems, conductors, boxes, fittings, supports, conduit fill, box fill, and installation conditions.

Equipment for General Use accounts for 13 questions. Candidates should review motors, transformers, generators, appliances, luminaires, equipment connections, controllers, and other commonly encountered electrical equipment.

Electrical Theory and Principles contributes 11 questions. Practice Ohm's Law, series and parallel circuits, power formulas, alternating current, voltage, current, resistance, impedance, motors, and transformer relationships.

Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to reinforce theory and calculation skills.

Safety represents 9 questions. OSHA and NFPA 70E should be studied as related but distinct references. OSHA addresses broad construction-jobsite requirements, while NFPA 70E concentrates on electrical workplace safety.

Review shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, energized work, risk assessment, personal protective equipment, temporary electrical systems, ladders, tools, and general construction safety practices.

Project Design and Management accounts for 8 questions. Study project planning, schedules, communication, coordination, quality, resources, estimating concepts, risk, and construction-management processes.

Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions also accounts for 8 questions. Become familiar with NEC rules that apply when electrical installations involve locations or equipment subject to additional requirements.

Practice following NEC cross-references. A question may begin in a general article and then direct the candidate to a more specific requirement elsewhere in the Code.

ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 supports preparation involving energy requirements for commercial buildings. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the standard and with electrical topics such as lighting and control provisions.

Reference navigation should be practiced with every question set. Before opening a book, decide what kind of question you are answering. An NEC installation question, electrical theory problem, workplace-safety question, and project-management question should not all be approached with the same reference.

For NEC questions, identify the primary electrical term and use the index strategically. Move to the referenced article or table, then confirm whether exceptions, notes, or related sections change the answer.

For calculation questions, identify the electrical relationship before searching. Knowing whether the problem involves voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor sizing, motors, or transformers helps narrow the search and improve efficiency.

Because the examination allows 270 minutes for 100 scored questions, timed practice is important. Candidates should learn when to mark a difficult question and move on rather than spending an excessive amount of time on one reference search.

As testing approaches, transition from isolated-topic study into mixed practice. Move among NEC questions, electrical calculations, OSHA, NFPA 70E, project management, equipment, special occupancies, and energy standards to better resemble the pace of the actual examination.
