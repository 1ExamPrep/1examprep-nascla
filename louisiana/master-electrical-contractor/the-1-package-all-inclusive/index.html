Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers an examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Louisiana is a participating jurisdiction, and the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for its Electrical classification.

The NASCLA electrical examination is based on a national job analysis developed around the work performed by experienced electrical professionals. Preparation covers a broad range of subjects rather than relying on the National Electrical Code alone. Candidates should be prepared to work with electrical theory, safety requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment, project design and management, special systems, and other electrical contracting topics.

After passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination, a Louisiana applicant seeking credit for the Electrical trade examination must have the NASCLA transcript transmitted to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors through the NASCLA examination database. The Board does not treat the NASCLA examination as a contractor license. The applicant must still complete the Louisiana application process and satisfy all other licensing requirements.

Louisiana also requires the qualifying party to complete its Business and Law requirement. The Louisiana Business and Law component is completed through the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors' online contractor portal after the qualifying party has been approved. The Business and Law test contains 50 questions, typically takes approximately 30 to 45 minutes, and requires a score of 70 or higher to pass.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program uses authorized reference materials, making effective book navigation an important part of exam preparation.

Candidates benefit from knowing which book applies to a particular subject, understanding where major sections are located, recognizing important terminology, and practicing how to move efficiently between a question and the appropriate reference.

State Requirements

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors regulates contractor licensing in the state. Louisiana recognizes the Electrical classification as a major commercial contractor classification. The classification covers qualifying work involving the installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition of electrical wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and related hardware used to supply electricity to structures, buildings, and projects.

Louisiana requires an applicable contractor license for electrical projects exceeding $10,000, including labor and materials. This threshold differs from the general commercial contractor threshold that applies to many other types of construction work.

Applicants using NASCLA examination credit for Electrical must arrange for the qualifying party's NASCLA transcript to be sent to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. Louisiana specifically allows applicants to request credit from a qualifying NASCLA examination for Electrical, while the state retains authority over the contractor application and licensing decision.

Commercial contractor applicants must also satisfy Louisiana's application requirements. Current state requirements include a financial statement and completion of Louisiana Business and Law by the qualifying party. Business entities such as LLCs and corporations must also maintain the applicable registration with the Louisiana Secretary of State while licensed.

The qualifying party is an important part of a Louisiana contractor license. The qualifying party must satisfy the examination or credential requirements associated with the requested classification and complete the Business and Law requirement. Passing NASCLA provides examination credit for the Electrical trade requirement; it does not remove the other obligations connected with Louisiana licensure.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan for the NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination should combine technical knowledge with consistent reference-book navigation. Because the examination covers several areas of electrical contracting, candidates should avoid spending all their preparation time on one reference or subject.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a central part of your preparation. Practice using the NEC index, locating articles, reading tables, following cross-references, and determining how related rules work together. Become familiar with the organization of the Code so you can move quickly from a question to the appropriate section.

Electrical theory deserves dedicated study time as well. Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to strengthen your understanding of circuits, voltage, current, resistance, power, loads, conductors, calculations, and related concepts. Understanding the principles behind electrical calculations can make it easier to apply them to unfamiliar questions.

Safety preparation should address both construction jobsite safety and electrical workplace safety. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E provide valuable material for developing familiarity with safety concepts encountered in professional electrical work.

Project management should also be included in your study schedule. Electrical contractors work within broader construction projects that require planning, organization, coordination, scheduling, and management. The included project-management and NASCLA contractor references provide additional resources for those areas.

Use your highlighted and tabbed references throughout your preparation. Work practice questions while keeping the actual books nearby. Rather than immediately searching every book, first decide which reference is most likely to contain the answer. This habit can strengthen your ability to choose and navigate the appropriate source efficiently.
