Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. It is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians and is accepted by participating state and local licensing agencies.

The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Louisiana also serves as an approved administration provider within the NASCLA examination program.

The NASCLA electrical examination evaluates professional trade knowledge across several technical areas. Candidates should prepare for National Electrical Code requirements, electrical theory and calculations, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, electrical equipment, safety, project design and management, special installations, energy requirements, and related technical subjects.

The variety of subjects makes a broad reference-based study approach important. Not every question is answered through the National Electrical Code. Construction-safety questions may lead to OSHA Part 1926, while workplace electrical-safety questions can involve NFPA 70E. Electrical theory problems can require textbook knowledge and calculations, while project-management questions may require contractor-business or project-management references.

Louisiana classifies Electrical as a major commercial contractor classification. The Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors identifies this classification as covering installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition involving wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and related hardware for supplying electricity to buildings, structures, and projects within the state's licensing scope.

Louisiana's Electrical classification requires a trade examination unless an accepted credential, such as the applicable NASCLA examination, satisfies the trade-examination component. Candidates must also complete Louisiana's other licensing requirements before a commercial contractor license is issued.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers open-book examinations for Electrical Contractors, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

Open-book preparation should focus on technical understanding and reference navigation at the same time. Candidates should practice recognizing the subject of each question before choosing a book. This approach helps reduce unnecessary searching and makes better use of the reference set.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive significant navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Important study areas include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, special conditions, and specialized systems.

A structured calculation process helps candidates determine what information is provided, identify the quantity being requested, choose the proper formula, complete the calculation, and then apply any relevant Code rule.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction workplace safety, while NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace. Learning the role and organization of both references can make safety-related lookups more efficient.

State Requirements

Commercial contractor licensing in Louisiana is administered by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors. The Board regulates contractor classifications, qualifying parties, examinations, applications, and other requirements associated with commercial contracting work.

Electrical is identified as a major classification. Louisiana requires the applicable commercial contractor license for Electrical projects exceeding $10,000 in value, including labor and materials. This threshold applies specifically to Electrical, Mechanical, and Plumbing work and differs from the general commercial project threshold used for many other classifications.

The Electrical classification covers work involving the installation, construction, alteration, improvement, movement, maintenance, repair, or demolition of wiring, cabling, fixtures, appliances, equipment, and hardware used for supplying electricity to structures, buildings, and projects.

Louisiana contractor applicants must identify a qualifying party for the requested classification. The application requires qualifying-party information and the qualifying party must maintain the relationship to the licensed company required by the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors.

Louisiana participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. This allows an accepted NASCLA result to satisfy the applicable Electrical trade-examination requirement under the Board's current examination framework.

The NASCLA examination is not itself a Louisiana contractor license. Candidates who pass the examination must still apply to the Louisiana State Licensing Board for Contractors and satisfy the additional requirements that apply to the company and qualifying party.

Louisiana contractor licensing includes business and law requirements in addition to trade qualification. Candidates should treat technical electrical preparation and Louisiana contractor-business preparation as separate areas of study because each addresses different responsibilities.

The qualifying-party structure is important throughout the licensing process. The qualifying party is connected to the contractor's classification and must meet the Board's relationship and qualification requirements. Contractor applications include information about the qualifying party's legal identity, position, contact information, and relationship to the company.

Applicants should also distinguish between examination approval and final license approval. An accepted NASCLA result satisfies a trade-examination component, but the Board reviews the complete application before determining whether the contractor license will be issued.
