Exam Details

The Maryland Home Improvement Contractor examination evaluates knowledge of laws and regulations governing the operation of a home improvement business. PSI Services administers the examination for the Maryland Home Improvement Commission.

  • Exam Name: Maryland Home Improvement Contractor Examination
  • Licensing Agency: Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC)
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services
  • Examination Format: Computer-based testing
  • Number of Questions: 55
  • Time Allowed: 150 minutes
  • Passing Score: 39 correct answers
  • Examination Type: Open book
  • Approved NASCLA Reference: Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Maryland Home Improvement Commission, 7th Edition

Maryland MHIC Contractor Examination Topics:

  • Home Improvement Law: 41 questions
  • Door-to-Door Sales Act: 4 questions
  • Labor Laws: 3 questions
  • Safety Regulations: 4 questions
  • Estimating: 3 questions

Home Improvement Law represents the largest examination category, accounting for 41 of the 55 questions. Candidates should devote significant study time to Maryland contractor licensing responsibilities, home improvement regulations, customer protections, and related legal obligations.

The examination also measures knowledge of consumer sales rules, labor requirements, safety regulations, and estimating principles.

Open Book Test

The Maryland Home Improvement Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use the reference publications designated by PSI, including the NASCLA Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Maryland Home Improvement Commission, 7th Edition.

Open-book testing requires candidates to understand the material and navigate the reference efficiently. Highlighting and tabs support organized review and help candidates become familiar with the location of important subjects.

Important PSI Examination Reference Rules:

  • Approved reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed.
  • Books must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain handwritten notes.
  • Loose papers and additional attached pages are not permitted.
  • Tabs must adhere permanently to the pages.
  • Removable Post-it flags and similar detachable markers are prohibited.
  • If a PSI proctor can remove a tab without tearing the page, the tab must be removed before testing.
  • Reference books published by other testing companies are not permitted in the examination center.

Important Tab Acceptance Notice: The testing facility proctor makes the final determination regarding whether tabs are accepted into the examination room. Tabs that do not adhere properly will not be accepted.

The flash cards included in this bundle are intended for preparation before examination day. Flash cards are not approved examination-room reference materials.

Practicing with the highlighted and tabbed publication helps candidates build reference-navigation familiarity while studying the underlying business and legal principles.

Licensing Steps

Individuals pursuing a Maryland Home Improvement Contractor license must complete the examination and licensing requirements established by the Maryland Home Improvement Commission.

Step 1: Review MHIC Licensing Qualifications

Review the contractor experience, education, financial responsibility, insurance, and application requirements. Applicants generally need at least two years of qualifying trade experience or acceptable equivalent educational training.

Step 2: Prepare for the Contractor Examination

Study the NASCLA Maryland Home Improvement Commission, 7th Edition. Review highlighted material, become familiar with the chapter tabs, and incorporate flash-card practice into your study schedule.

Step 3: Apply for Examination Approval

Complete the required online examination application through PSI and submit the supporting documentation needed to establish examination eligibility.

Step 4: Schedule and Complete the Examination

After PSI approves your application, schedule the contractor examination. Bring the required identification and approved reference materials to the examination center and follow all testing rules.

Step 5: Complete the License Application

After successfully passing the examination, submit the appropriate licensing application to the Maryland Home Improvement Commission with the required supporting documents.

Step 6: Obtain Licensing Approval

Complete the Commission's financial, insurance, business registration, and administrative requirements before obtaining the contractor license.

Passing examination results remain valid for two years. Applicants must apply for licensure within that period.

State Requirements

The Maryland Home Improvement Commission regulates covered home improvement work performed on residential properties in Maryland.

Home improvement work generally includes alteration, remodeling, repair, or replacement of a residential building or portions of the building. Covered work also includes certain improvements involving individual condominium units.

Maryland Home Improvement Contractor Licensing Requirements:

  • Trade Experience: At least two years of qualifying home improvement, construction, or related experience, or accepted comparable educational training.
  • Licensing Examination: Successful completion of the required MHIC contractor examination.
  • Financial Responsibility: Documentation demonstrating financial solvency based on the size and scope of the business.
  • Liability Insurance: Maintain general liability insurance of at least $500,000 as required by Maryland law.
  • Business Documentation: Provide applicable corporation, trade name, or other business records.
  • Licensing Application: Submit the required application materials for review and approval by the Commission.

Maryland Home Improvement Contractor licenses are issued for two-year periods. Licensed contractors are responsible for complying with applicable state laws, professional conduct standards, insurance requirements, and license renewal obligations.

Understanding these responsibilities is important for both examination preparation and the operation of a professional home improvement contracting business.
