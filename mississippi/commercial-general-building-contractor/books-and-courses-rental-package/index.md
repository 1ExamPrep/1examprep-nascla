## Test Information and Study Materials

The size of the NASCLA reference library makes a structured study routine valuable. Rather than trying to memorize every reference, begin by learning which book covers each major subject area. This can help you determine where to look when a question involves a specific construction discipline.

**Practice reference navigation regularly.** Become familiar with each book's index, table of contents, major chapters, terminology, and tab structure. The more familiar you become with the library, the less time you may spend deciding where to start a lookup.

**Build construction knowledge before relying on the books.** Reference materials are most useful for confirming details. Understanding the underlying concept can make it easier to identify the correct book and section.

**Use timed practice.** Open-book testing can encourage candidates to spend too much time searching for individual answers. Timed practice helps develop the discipline to move forward when a lookup is taking too long.

**Connect your online course to your books.** When you study a subject in the course, locate that topic inside the relevant reference. This approach reinforces both technical understanding and book navigation.
