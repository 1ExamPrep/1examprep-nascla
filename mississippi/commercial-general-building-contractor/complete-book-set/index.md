## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies, including the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for the Building Construction major classification.

Mississippi applicants pursuing the Building Construction classification may choose the NASCLA accredited examination instead of the Mississippi Building Construction trade examination. The NASCLA result addresses the accepted technical trade-examination component, but candidates must still complete the Mississippi licensing application and all other applicable state requirements.

The NASCLA examination measures knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects. It covers a wide range of construction subjects rather than focusing on one specialty trade.

The listed reference books represent major examination areas involving project management, jobsite administration, estimating, contracts, safety, building codes, accessibility, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, excavation, environmental responsibilities, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical systems.

The Mississippi Business and Law examination is separate from the NASCLA technical examination. It focuses on contractor business practices and state-specific responsibilities. The Mississippi Contractors’ Guide to Business Law and Project Management for Commercial Contractors is the required reference for this examination.

Mississippi contractor examinations are administered through PSI. Applicants must first submit the appropriate licensing application and receive examination eligibility before scheduling the required state examination. Candidates are responsible for registering for the correct examination after receiving approval.

Passing examination results are only one part of the licensing process. A complete application must also satisfy the Mississippi State Board of Contractors’ business, financial, qualifying-party, insurance, classification, and supporting-document requirements before a license is issued.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The examination center does not supply the complete technical library.

The Mississippi Business and Law examination also uses its designated reference material under the applicable examination rules. Candidates should prepare directly from the required Mississippi business guide and become familiar with its organization before test day.

Open-book testing requires more than owning the correct books. The NASCLA library includes thousands of pages across textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty construction guides. Candidates must be able to identify a question’s subject and choose the reference most likely to contain the answer.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a reinforcing-bar placement question may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Questions about concrete field practices may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel construction questions may require the Steel Deck Institute manual or Technical Digest No. 9. Wood-truss questions may involve the BCSI guide, while precast concrete and post-tensioning questions rely on their respective specialty publications.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may involve the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and federal construction safety questions may be answered through 29 CFR Part 1926.

Candidates should practice using the listed editions because page numbers, terminology, code sections, tables, and technical requirements may change between editions. Working directly with the correct books helps build familiarity with the organization expected during the examination.

All references brought into the examination room must comply with current testing rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Mississippi classification.** Applicants seeking commercial general building authority should review the Building Construction major classification and confirm that it matches their intended scope of work.
2. **Identify the qualifying party.** The business must designate the person who will satisfy the examination requirement for the requested classification.
3. **Complete the commercial contractor application.** Submit the required business, ownership, classification, qualifying-party, and contact information to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
4. **Provide supporting documentation.** The application must include the financial, insurance, business, and other materials required for the requested license.
5. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Board reviews the application and notifies PSI when the candidate is approved to schedule the required examinations.
6. **Prepare for Mississippi Business and Law.** Study the Mississippi Contractors’ Guide to Business Law and Project Management for Commercial Contractors.
7. **Choose the accepted trade-examination option.** Building Construction applicants may take the Mississippi trade examination or the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
8. **Apply for and schedule the NASCLA examination.** Follow the applicable NASCLA and testing-provider registration procedures.
9. **Pass the required examinations.** Complete the Mississippi Business and Law examination and the accepted technical trade examination.
10. **Submit the NASCLA result when applicable.** Candidates may transmit their NASCLA transcript through the National Examination Database for use with the Mississippi licensing application.
11. **Complete the Board’s final review.** The Mississippi State Board of Contractors reviews the completed application, examination results, and supporting documents.
12. **Receive the license before contracting.** The company must obtain the appropriate Mississippi license before performing work that requires state contractor licensure.

## State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates commercial and residential contractor licensing in the state. Contractors performing work that falls within Mississippi’s licensing laws must obtain the appropriate license and classification before offering or completing regulated services.

Building Construction is a major commercial classification. Mississippi accepts either its state Building Construction examination or the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the trade-examination component of this classification.

Applicants using the NASCLA examination must still complete Mississippi Business and Law. The NASCLA transcript does not replace the state business examination, contractor application, financial review, insurance requirements, or other licensing documents.

The license is issued to the business entity. The application identifies the person responsible for qualifying the company through the required examinations. Applicants should make sure the company name, ownership information, qualifying-party information, and requested classifications are complete and consistent throughout the application.

The Board reviews the applicant’s financial information and other supporting materials as part of the licensing process. Applicants should submit current documents in the form requested by the Board and respond promptly to requests for missing or corrected information.

Commercial applicants must also provide the insurance documentation required for licensing. The policy and certificate information should correspond with the business entity shown on the application.

Examinations are scheduled only after the Board confirms eligibility with PSI. Candidates should not register for an examination until they have received the appropriate approval notice and should make sure they select only the examination for which they were approved.

Passing an examination does not automatically create a contractor license. The Board must receive and approve the complete application before the license is issued. Contractors are responsible for maintaining the license and completing future renewal requirements.
