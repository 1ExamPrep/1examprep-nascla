## Exam Details

This complete reference package supports preparation for two examinations connected with Mississippi commercial contractor licensing: the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination focuses on the responsibilities involved in operating a contracting company in the state. Candidates should be prepared to review contractor licensing, business organization, financial management, estimating, contracts, employment responsibilities, insurance, safety, project administration, and other business subjects addressed in the Mississippi NASCLA Business Book.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination addresses the technical knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects. The examination covers a broad range of subjects rather than concentrating on one construction specialty.

The listed references address jobsite management, construction project management, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast erection, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, excavation, gypsum construction, masonry, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, federal safety requirements, commercial building codes, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, green building, and mechanical and electrical systems.

Mississippi Building Construction applicants may choose between the Mississippi Building Construction trade examination and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors determines the examinations for which an applicant is eligible.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Mississippi contractor license. The applicant must also pass the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination and complete the applicable business, qualifying-party, financial, insurance, classification, and application requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference books to the testing location. The testing center does not supply the complete technical library.

Open-book testing requires more than having the books available. The library contains thousands of pages across codes, textbooks, standards, manuals, and specialty construction guides. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the books may spend too much time searching unrelated references.

A practical exam strategy begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, or technical tables to locate the relevant material.

A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, while a question about reinforcing-bar placement may be found in Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field practices may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel construction questions may involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or the technical digest covering steel joists and joist girders. Wood-truss questions may require the BCSI guide, while precast and post-tensioning questions rely on their respective specialty publications.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may involve the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and construction safety questions may rely on 29 CFR Part 1926.

The highlighting and permanent tabs in this package support faster navigation, but candidates should still study with the books before test day. Repeated practice helps candidates understand what each tab represents and how the highlighted information fits within the surrounding chapter or code provision.

All references brought into the examination room must comply with current testing rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate information bulletin and avoid loose papers, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Mississippi classification.** Applicants pursuing commercial general building authority should review the Building Construction classification and confirm that it matches their intended work.
2. **Identify the qualifying party.** Each company applying for a Certificate of Responsibility must designate the individual responsible for completing the required examinations.
3. **Complete the Mississippi contractor application.** Submit the requested business, ownership, classification, qualifying-party, and contact information to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
4. **Provide qualifying-party documentation.** The company must provide the employment documentation required to connect the qualifying party with the applicant business.
5. **Submit supporting business documents.** Supply the financial, insurance, and other materials required for the requested commercial license.
6. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Mississippi State Board of Contractors reviews the application and notifies PSI of the examinations the candidate is approved to take.
7. **Prepare for Mississippi Law and Business Management.** Study the Mississippi NASCLA Business Book and practice locating state business information efficiently.
8. **Select the approved trade examination.** Building Construction applicants may choose the NASCLA examination or the Mississippi-specific Building Construction examination when approved.
9. **Schedule the examinations through PSI.** Register only for the examinations listed in the candidate’s eligibility notice.
10. **Pass the required examinations.** Complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination and the approved Building Construction trade examination.
11. **Transmit the NASCLA result when applicable.** A passing NASCLA examination result may be sent to Mississippi through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
12. **Complete the Board’s licensing review.** The Board evaluates the application, examination results, qualifying-party information, financial documents, insurance records, and other required materials.

## State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates commercial and residential contractor licensing in the state. Contractors performing work that falls within Mississippi licensing laws must obtain the appropriate license and classification before offering or performing regulated services.

Building Construction is a major Mississippi commercial classification. Candidates applying for this classification may choose the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors instead of the Mississippi Building Construction trade examination.

All Mississippi applicants must complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. A passing NASCLA result addresses the accepted technical trade-examination component but does not replace the state business examination.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. Each company applying for a Certificate of Responsibility must designate a qualifying individual to complete the applicable examinations. The qualifying individual must remain associated with the company for the license to continue in good standing.

Applicants must submit proof connecting the qualifying party with the business. The Board may accept employment or company records identified in the current application instructions.

New applicants receive a limited eligibility period in which to complete the approved examinations. Candidates who do not complete the required examinations during that period may need to reapply with the Board before receiving new examination eligibility.

The application process also includes financial and insurance requirements. Applicants should submit current documents in the form requested by the Board and make sure the business name is consistent across the application, insurance records, and financial materials.

Passing an examination and purchasing this book set do not create a contractor license. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors must approve the complete application before the business may operate under the requested classification.
