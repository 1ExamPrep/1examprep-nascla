## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It is designed to evaluate the knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial general building projects.

Mississippi recognizes the NASCLA examination for the trade-examination component of the Building Construction major classification. A Mississippi general building contractor candidate may select the NASCLA examination instead of taking the Mississippi-specific Building Construction trade examination.

The NASCLA examination covers multiple construction disciplines rather than one specialty. Candidates should be prepared to review project management, jobsite supervision, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, concrete construction, reinforcing steel, post-tensioning, precast erection, structural metals, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, gypsum systems, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, stormwater controls, exterior insulation systems, and building-service systems.

The extensive scope of the examination is reflected in the reference library. Some publications provide broad commercial construction knowledge, while others address specialty materials, installation methods, safety rules, structural requirements, or code provisions.

Mississippi contractor examinations are administered through PSI. Eligibility for Mississippi examinations is determined through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors application process. Candidates should complete the applicable application requirements and receive authorization before scheduling state examinations.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination is separate from the NASCLA technical examination. All Mississippi contractor applicants are required to satisfy the state law-and-business examination requirement, including candidates who use a passing NASCLA result for the trade portion.

After a candidate passes the NASCLA examination, the result is stored in the NASCLA National Examination Database. The candidate can electronically send an examination transcript to Mississippi or another participating licensing agency.

A passing transcript does not automatically create a contractor license. The applicant must follow the Mississippi State Board of Contractors’ application procedures and satisfy the applicable qualifying-party, business, financial, insurance, classification, and documentation requirements.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing the permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete examination library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for preparation. The reference library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

Searching unfamiliar books without a clear method can use valuable testing time. Candidates should learn the purpose and organization of each reference before examination day. They should also practice identifying the primary subject of a question and selecting the book most likely to contain the answer.

For example, structural concrete questions may require ACI 318, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Questions involving concrete materials, placement, curing, or quality practices may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel construction questions may involve the Steel Deck Institute manual or Technical Digest No. 9. Wood-truss questions may require the BCSI guide. Precast erection and unbonded post-tensioning questions are addressed in their respective specialty references.

Accessibility questions may require ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may involve the International Building Code. Excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and federal construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926.

The online exam prep course helps candidates practice connecting these subjects with the correct publications. Students can develop a repeatable process: read the question, identify the subject, select the reference, locate the applicable section, and evaluate the available answers.

All references brought into the testing room must comply with the current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin before test day and avoid unauthorized loose papers, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Mississippi classification.** Applicants seeking commercial general building authority should review the Building Construction major classification and confirm that it matches their intended scope of work.
2. **Identify the qualifying party.** The applicant business must designate the individual who will qualify the company by completing the required examinations.
3. **Complete the Mississippi contractor application.** Submit the requested company, ownership, classification, qualifying-party, and contact information.
4. **Provide qualifying-party documentation.** Submit the records required to establish the qualifying party’s connection with the applicant business.
5. **Submit supporting business materials.** Provide the applicable financial, insurance, organizational, and application documents required by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
6. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Board reviews the application and determines which examinations the candidate is approved to schedule.
7. **Prepare for Mississippi Law and Business Management.** Study the required Mississippi business reference and become familiar with state contractor responsibilities.
8. **Select the approved Building Construction exam option.** Eligible candidates may choose the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors instead of the Mississippi Building Construction trade examination.
9. **Prepare for the NASCLA examination.** Use the online course with the listed references to review technical subjects and practice open-book navigation.
10. **Schedule and complete the required examinations.** Follow the applicable PSI and NASCLA registration procedures for the examinations the candidate is authorized to take.
11. **Transmit the NASCLA result.** After passing, send the official transcript to Mississippi through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
12. **Complete the licensing review.** The Mississippi State Board of Contractors reviews the application, examination results, qualifying-party records, and supporting materials before issuing a license.

## State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates commercial and residential contractor licensing. A contractor performing regulated work must obtain the appropriate license and classification before offering, bidding, or completing work that requires state licensure.

Building Construction is a major Mississippi commercial classification. It applies to qualifying building-construction activities and requires an approved trade examination. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors accepts either the state Building Construction examination or the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The license is issued to the applicant business. A qualifying party takes the required examinations on behalf of the individual, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or other entity seeking the license.

Mississippi allows more than one qualifying party to be associated with a license. One person may qualify the business for the technical classification while another person completes the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination, subject to the Board’s requirements.

All applicants must complete Mississippi Law and Business Management. Passing the NASCLA examination does not waive this state requirement because NASCLA addresses the accepted technical trade portion rather than Mississippi-specific contractor business law.

The application process may include business-entity records, qualifying-party information, proof of employment or affiliation, financial documentation, insurance records, classification details, and other supporting materials requested by the Board.

Candidates should schedule only the examinations for which they have received eligibility. PSI administers Mississippi contractor examinations after the Board completes the applicable application review and provides testing authorization.

Passing the required examinations does not automatically issue a Mississippi contractor license. The entire application must be complete and approved before the business may operate under the requested classification.
