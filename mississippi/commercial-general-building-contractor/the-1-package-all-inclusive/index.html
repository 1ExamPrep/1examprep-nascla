Exam Details

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors requires applicants to complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. A trade examination may also be required depending on the classification requested. For applicable commercial general building licensing, Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions and provides 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, for completion. Candidates must achieve the required NASCLA passing score for the trade examination.

The NASCLA exam covers major areas of commercial construction, including:

  • Planning and estimating
  • Project and financial management
  • Procurement and contracting
  • Construction safety
  • Site construction
  • Concrete construction
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Wood foundations and framing
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors, windows, and glazing
  • Finishes
  • Mechanical and plumbing systems
  • Electrical systems

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination is separate from the NASCLA trade examination. The Mississippi examination contains 50 questions, requires a 70% passing score, and provides 2 hours for completion.

Mississippi Law and Business Management topics include licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, contracts, business organization, risk management, environmental requirements, and safety.

Candidates preparing for both exams should maintain separate study plans. The NASCLA examination focuses heavily on commercial construction and technical reference material, while the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination concentrates on business operations, state requirements, contracts, financial responsibilities, and contractor law.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open book test. Mississippi's Law and Business Management examination is also an open book test using the approved Mississippi contractor reference.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination uses the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Mississippi, 6th Edition. This publication covers contractor business planning, licensing, insurance, estimating, contracts, project management, financial management, taxation, lien law, safety, and related Mississippi-specific information.

Licensing Steps

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers commercial contractor licensing in Mississippi. Candidates must complete the applicable licensing process before receiving a commercial contractor license.

  1. Choose the appropriate Mississippi classification. Applicants should identify the major or specialty classification that corresponds with the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Establish the business entity. Applicants operating through an LLC, corporation, or LLP must maintain the applicable good-standing status with the Mississippi Secretary of State.
  3. Complete the Mississippi commercial contractor application. The application must include the requested classification, company information, qualifying-party information, and supporting documentation.
  4. Identify the qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual associated with the license who satisfies the applicable examination requirements.
  5. Submit the application before scheduling examinations. Mississippi requires applicants to submit the contractor license application first. MSBOC reviews the application and determines testing eligibility before PSI scheduling.
  6. Pass Mississippi Law and Business Management. This examination is mandatory for Mississippi commercial contractor applicants.
  7. Complete the applicable trade examination. Candidates pursuing an applicable general building classification may use the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination to satisfy the accepted trade requirement.
  8. Provide required financial documentation. Mississippi commercial contractor applicants must submit a reviewed or audited financial statement prepared by a certified public accountant.
  9. Provide required insurance and tax documentation. Applicants must satisfy MSBOC's general liability, workers' compensation when applicable, tax identification, and supporting-document requirements.
  10. Complete Board review. MSBOC issues the contractor license after the application, examinations, financial documentation, insurance, and other requirements have been completed.

State Requirements

Mississippi commercial contractor applicants must satisfy financial, insurance, testing, business-registration, and application requirements administered by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.

Applicants requesting a major classification must demonstrate a minimum net worth of $50,000. Applicants requesting other classifications must demonstrate the applicable financial qualification established by MSBOC. The financial statement must be reviewed or audited by a certified public accountant and completed within the required period. A compilation is not accepted for this requirement.

Commercial applicants must provide proof of current general liability insurance showing at least $300,000 per occurrence and $600,000 aggregate. The insured name on the policy documentation must correspond with the name under which the contractor license is requested.

Workers' compensation insurance documentation is required when applicable. Mississippi's commercial contractor application currently identifies this requirement for applicants with five or more employees.

Corporations, LLCs, and LLPs must provide proof that the business is in good standing with the Mississippi Secretary of State. Applicants must also provide applicable Mississippi tax identification information or a Federal Tax ID number as required by the application.

MSBOC requires the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination for all commercial contractor applicants. A trade examination is required when applicable to the requested classification unless an accepted waiver or reciprocity provision applies.
