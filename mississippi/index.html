Mississippi NASCLA Exam: Your Path to a Contractor License

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 11 min read · 10 official sources cited

Mississippi is one of the states where the NASCLA exam can do real work for you. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for its Building Construction classification, and it accepts NASCLA electrical exams for electrical licensing. Pass once, and you can also send that same score to other participating states.

There is one catch that every applicant runs into: Mississippi requires its own Law and Business Management exam no matter which trade exam you take. This page explains which licenses use NASCLA, what the state exam covers, the financial and insurance rules, published fees, and how to prepare. Does Mississippi Accept the NASCLA Exam?

Yes, for specific classifications. The MSBOC says general building contractors may take the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building instead of the Mississippi Building Construction exam, and master electrical contractors may take the NASCLA electrical exam instead of the state Master Electrical exam. Mississippi is also on NASCLA's participating agencies list for the commercial exam.

On the electrical side, NASCLA's electrical participating agencies page shows Mississippi accepting the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam and the Residential Electrician exam. The Journeyman exam is not listed for Mississippi. The Board's classification page does not spell out a NASCLA option for Residential Electrical, so confirm with the Board before you register for that one.

Passing a NASCLA exam replaces only the trade exam. You still apply to the Board, pass the Mississippi Law and Business Management exam, and meet the financial, insurance and qualifier rules. The Board describes the NASCLA option as especially useful if you plan to work in several states.

Who Issues Contractor Licenses in Mississippi

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors (MSBOC) licenses commercial contractors, residential contractors and roofers. It is a ten member board appointed by the Governor, and it sets licensing standards, reviews applications, and handles complaints and unlicensed contracting.

When you need a license depends on the type and size of the job. Per the Board's FAQ, commercial jobs over $50,000, including equipment installation, require a commercial license for both prime contractors and subcontractors. On the residential side, new home construction over $50,000 and remodeling, additions or roofing over $10,000 require a license. Subcontractors doing residential electrical, mechanical, HVAC or plumbing work must be licensed too.

Mississippi electrical contracting is handled by the same Board. Commercial electrical work falls under the Electrical major classification, and residential electrical work has its own Residential Electrical classification. Cities and counties may still require their own permits, business licenses and inspections on top of the state license.

One detail trips people up: bids, contracts and permits must be signed in the exact name on your license. The Board treats work under another name as unlicensed contracting.

Mississippi Contractor License Types and Which Exam Each Needs

Mississippi uses major and specialty classifications. Here are the ones our products cover. The Board's classifications page lists every classification and its exam.

  • Building Construction (commercial major): commercial buildings and residential structures, plus additions, renovations and site work such as parking and sidewalks. Trade exam: the state Building Construction exam or the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam. Plus Law and Business Management.
  • Electrical (commercial major): installing, repairing, altering or designing electrical wiring, fixtures, raceways and conduit, including plants and substations. Trade exam: the state exam or the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam. Plus Law and Business Management.
  • Residential Electrical: installation, maintenance and repair of residential electrical systems only. A trade exam is required; NASCLA lists Mississippi as accepting its Residential Electrician exam. Plus Law and Business Management.
  • Residential Builder: homes up to 3 floors for 50 or fewer families, and commercial jobs of 7,500 square feet or less. This one uses a state trade exam; no NASCLA option is listed.

How to Get a Mississippi Contractor License, Step by Step

In Mississippi you apply first and test second. The Board must approve your eligibility before PSI will let you schedule.

  1. Pick your classification and decide who your qualifying party will be: an owner, officer or responsible managing employee with hands on experience in that classification.
  2. Register your business. Corporations, LLCs and LLPs need good standing with the Mississippi Secretary of State.
  3. For a commercial license, get a CPA reviewed financial statement and at least three reference letters, one from your bank.
  4. Get proof of general liability insurance, plus workers' compensation if you have 5 or more employees.
  5. Fill out the commercial or residential application, have it notarized, and mail it with the fee. You have 180 days to finish the process.
  6. Wait for MSBOC to notify PSI that you are eligible, then watch for PSI's confirmation email before scheduling the Law and Business Management exam.
  7. If you are using NASCLA for the trade exam, apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org for pre-approval, schedule, and pass.
  8. Send your NASCLA transcript to the Board through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  9. The Board reviews applications weekly. After approval, it says the license usually issues in about 5 working days.

The NASCLA Exam Itself: Format and Rules

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions and a 330 minute time limit, and it is open book. You need 70 percent to pass (81 correct answers out of the 115 scored questions); Mississippi's PSI bulletin lists a minimum passing score of 81. The exam may include unscored pretest questions as well. You apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org, get pre-approval, then schedule with PSI.

The commercial exam draws on a large library: project and jobsite management, concrete and ACI 318, masonry, steel, wood trusses, precast and post-tensioning, roofing, excavation, safety and the NASCLA Basic business guide. Speed with the books is what decides most results.

If you prefer, you can take Mississippi's own Building Construction exam instead. Per the PSI bulletin it has 80 questions in 3 hours, needs 70 percent, and includes topics such as Mississippi One Call. The NASCLA version is longer, but it is the one other states recognize.

The NASCLA electrical exams are also open book and delivered through PSI. NASCLA states they are based on the 2020/2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2025. Expect heavy NEC lookup work, plus OSHA safety, NFPA 70E and business topics. Check NASCLA's electrical exams page and current bulletin for question counts and timing.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management Exam

Every applicant takes this exam, commercial or residential, NASCLA or not. Reciprocity and exam waivers apply only to trade exams, so this one cannot be skipped.

According to the PSI Mississippi bulletin updated April 23, 2026, the Law and Business Management exam has 50 questions, a 2 hour limit, and a 70 percent passing score. It is open book. The reference is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Mississippi edition. Since October 1, 2023, only the 6th Edition is referenced, and NASCLA's Mississippi store page carries that edition.

The content breakdown is Licensing 5, Estimating and Bidding 7, Lien Law 1, Financial Management 5, Tax Laws 5, Labor Laws 5, Project Management 5, Contracts 6, Business Organization 2, Risk Management 4, and Environmental and Safety 5. No single area dominates, so you need to know the whole book's layout.

Mississippi allows more than one qualifying party on a license. One person can take the trade exam while another takes Law and Business Management, which helps companies where the field expert and the business manager are different people.

Mississippi Financial, Insurance and Qualifier Requirements

Commercial applicants submit a financial statement reviewed by a Certified Public Accountant. The Board's published minimum net worth is $50,000 for a major classification such as Building Construction or Electrical, and $20,000 for a specialty classification. Residential applicants do not need a financial statement.

For insurance, commercial applicants need general liability coverage of at least $300,000 per occurrence or $600,000 aggregate, with MSBOC listed to receive cancellation notices. Residential applicants must show proof of coverage, but the Board sets no minimum. Anyone with 5 or more employees must also show workers' compensation coverage.

Each qualifying party needs a U.S. Social Security number and proof of employment with the applicant, such as a check stub, W-2 or Secretary of State listing. Commercial applicants also send at least three reference letters, one of them from a bank or financial institution.

The Board's licensing page does not publish a specific experience hour count, but it expects the qualifier to have hands on experience in the classification. Check the current application for the experience detail it asks you to list.

Mississippi Contractor License Costs and Fees

These are the published figures at the time of writing. Fees change, so confirm with the Board's fee schedule on its forms page before you send payment.

  • Commercial application fee: $400, non-refundable.
  • Residential application fee: $50, non-refundable.
  • PSI fee for Mississippi exams: $120 for one exam portion or $240 for two, per registration, including retakes.
  • NASCLA exam fees are paid separately to NASCLA when you apply for the national exam.

Reciprocity and Working in Other States

The Board has reciprocity agreements with several states, but they cover trade exams only. You still file a full application and take Law and Business Management. Mississippi also offers trade exam waivers for qualified applicants under the Military Family Freedom Act and the Universal Recognition of Occupational Licenses Act.

If you plan to grow across state lines, the NASCLA route is the more portable choice. Your passing score sits in the NASCLA National Examination Database, and you can send a transcript to any participating agency that accepts it. Each state still has its own application and business requirements.

After You Pass: Approval and Annual Renewal

Once your exams are in and your file is complete, the Board reviews it at its weekly review and mails the license, usually about 5 working days after approval. Do not bid or start regulated work until the license is issued.

Mississippi licenses run one year and must be renewed every year, including inactive licenses. Starting in 2026, the Board sends renewal notices only by email, about 60 days before expiration, to the address on file. You can renew late for up to 180 days after the expiration date; after that you must file a new application. Renewal is your responsibility even if the email never arrives.

Renewal fees are on the Board's fee schedule, and continuing education rules are on its continuing education page. Check both before your first renewal, and see the Board's maintaining a license page for the online renewal link.

Mississippi NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does Mississippi accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors in place of its Building Construction trade exam. You still need to pass the Mississippi Law and Business Management exam and meet all other licensing requirements.

Do I still need the Mississippi Law and Business exam if I pass NASCLA?

Yes. Every Mississippi applicant takes the Law and Business Management exam. NASCLA, reciprocity and waivers only replace the trade exam.

How many questions are on the Mississippi Law and Business Management exam?

The current PSI bulletin lists 50 questions in 2 hours, with 70 percent needed to pass. It is open book and uses the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Mississippi 6th Edition.

When do I need a Mississippi contractor license?

Per the Board, commercial jobs over $50,000 need a commercial license. Residential new construction over $50,000 and remodeling or roofing over $10,000 need a residential license. Some specialty work, such as fire sprinklers, has lower thresholds.

Does Mississippi accept NASCLA electrical exams?

NASCLA lists Mississippi as accepting its Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam and its Residential Electrician exam, but not the Journeyman exam. The Board confirms the master electrical option on its site. Confirm the residential electrical route with the Board before you register.

What net worth do I need for a Mississippi commercial license?

The Board lists a minimum net worth of $50,000 for a major classification and $20,000 for a specialty classification, shown on a CPA reviewed financial statement. Residential applicants do not submit a financial statement.

How much does a Mississippi contractor license cost?

The published application fees are $400 for commercial and $50 for residential. PSI charges $120 per exam portion for the state exams. NASCLA exam fees are separate, and all fees can change, so check with the Board.

How long does a Mississippi contractor license last?

One year. Licenses must be renewed annually, and you can renew up to 180 days after expiration before a new application is required.

Can two people qualify one Mississippi license?

Yes. Mississippi allows multiple qualifying parties, so one person can pass the trade exam and another the Law and Business Management exam. Each qualifier needs proof of employment with the company.
