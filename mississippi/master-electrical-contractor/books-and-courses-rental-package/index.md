## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is a standardized examination developed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Participating licensing agencies may accept a passing NASCLA examination result in place of a separate jurisdiction-specific electrical trade examination.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national electrical contractor license. Candidates must still apply to the licensing agency in each jurisdiction where they intend to work and satisfy that agency’s application, experience, financial, insurance, business-law, and approval requirements.

The electrical examination may cover general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, calculations, electrical safety, construction safety, energy standards, project management, and contractor business practices.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is a central technical reference for the examination. Candidates should become familiar with its chapters, articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, conductor requirements, wiring methods, equipment provisions, grounding and bonding rules, and calculation procedures.

The OSHA construction and general industry references support preparation involving electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, temporary power, fall protection, ladders, tools, machinery, material handling, workplace conditions, and employer safety responsibilities.

NFPA 70E supports preparation for workplace electrical safety. Subjects may include shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, risk assessment, electrically safe work conditions, approach boundaries, warning labels, protective equipment, and safety-related maintenance and work practices.

ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 supports study involving energy-efficiency requirements for applicable commercial sites and buildings. Electrical candidates may review lighting, controls, electrical power, equipment, energy use, and related building-performance provisions.

The PMBOK Guide supports project-management preparation involving planning, project teams, stakeholders, delivery, measurement, uncertainty, performance, and project outcomes. Electrical contractors must often coordinate technical work with schedules, costs, labor, materials, communication, risk, and contractual responsibilities.

*Ugly’s Electrical References* provides frequently used electrical formulas, diagrams, conductor information, conversions, calculations, and field data. *Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications* helps connect voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit behavior, and other electrical principles with practical code applications.

The package also includes two contractor business references. The Mississippi-specific NASCLA guide supports preparation for the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination, while the NASCLA Basic guide supports broader contractor business, law, financial-management, and project-management subjects.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor examination is open book. Mississippi contractor examinations administered through PSI are also open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following the current testing rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, markings, notes, and supplemental materials.

Open-book testing still requires extensive preparation. A candidate may have the correct references in the examination room but lose valuable time by selecting the wrong book, searching an ineffective index term, overlooking an exception, or misinterpreting a table.

The rental books in this package are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support more efficient navigation. Highlighting identifies important requirements, calculations, definitions, safety provisions, and business concepts. Tabs divide the materials into major chapters, articles, and frequently reviewed subjects.

Candidates should review every tab before test day and understand what information is located within each prepared section. Practice using the table of contents, indexes, article numbers, formulas, diagrams, and technical terminology throughout the full reference set.

Electrical installation questions may lead to the 2023 National Electrical Code. Questions involving arc-flash hazards, shock protection, energized work, or electrically safe work conditions may require NFPA 70E.

Construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926, while general industry workplace-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1910. Candidates should learn the purpose of each OSHA reference so they can select the correct volume efficiently.

Energy-efficiency questions may require ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022. Project planning, stakeholders, delivery, performance, or project-management questions may lead to the PMBOK Guide.

Electrical formulas, conversions, diagrams, and quick-reference information may be located in *Ugly’s Electrical References*. Theory-based calculations and the principles behind NEC applications may involve *Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications*.

Contractor business and law questions may involve the NASCLA Basic guide or the Mississippi-specific Business and Law guide, depending on the examination being taken. Candidates should not assume that the same business reference is used for every test.

Review the current candidate information bulletin before examination day. The testing provider determines which books, editions, tabs, markings, and materials are permitted in the testing room.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Confirm the appropriate classification.** Determine that the Mississippi Electrical Work classification matches the electrical contracting services the business intends to provide.
2. **Select the qualifying party.** Identify the owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the company and complete the required examinations.
3. **Document electrical experience.** Organize detailed project information showing relevant electrical construction, installation, maintenance, supervision, or project-management experience.
4. **Prepare the Mississippi contractor application.** Complete the applicable filing and gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, financial, insurance, reference, experience, and supporting information.
5. **Submit the application.** Send the completed contractor application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
6. **Respond to application requests.** Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
7. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the applicable NASCLA examination registration process and obtain authorization to schedule the test.
8. **Schedule the examination.** Follow the testing provider’s instructions to select an available testing date and location.
9. **Prepare with the rental references.** Review the NEC, electrical theory, calculations, safety standards, energy requirements, project management, and contractor business subjects.
10. **Prepare for Mississippi Business and Law.** Study the Mississippi-specific reference for the required Law and Business Management examination.
11. **Take the required examinations.** Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials permitted under current testing rules.
12. **Submit examination results.** Complete any score-reporting or transcript steps required for the Mississippi application.
13. **Complete outstanding requirements.** Provide any remaining documents or corrections requested during final application review.
14. **Receive licensing approval.** Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application and supporting records must also be approved.
15. **Maintain the license.** After issuance, the contractor is responsible for renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and continuing compliance.

## State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable commercial contractor licensing process. Electrical Work is recognized as a major classification involving the installation, repair, alteration, addition, or design of electrical wiring, fixtures, appliances, apparatus, raceways, conduit, and related electrical systems.

Applicants should select a classification that accurately reflects the services the business plans to provide. Electrical Work is broader than a limited electrical specialty and may include qualifying work performed within plants, substations, buildings, and other regulated electrical installations.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the Board’s requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration records, ownership details, qualifying-party documentation, project experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials. Information should remain accurate and consistent throughout the application.

Mississippi contractor applicants must complete the Law and Business Management examination. The applicable trade examination is also required unless the Board accepts an approved examination result or grants an applicable waiver.

A passing NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination result may be used to satisfy the trade-examination requirement when accepted by the applicable licensing authority. Candidates must still complete the Mississippi application and remaining state requirements.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not authorize a candidate or business to begin regulated electrical contracting work. The applicable Mississippi contractor credential must be issued before the company performs work that requires the license.

Electrical contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, utility requirements, adopted electrical codes, employee safety, business registrations, insurance, and project-specific approvals.
