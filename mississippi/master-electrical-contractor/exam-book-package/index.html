Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is intended for electrical contractors, master electricians, and professionals seeking an accredited trade-examination credential. Candidates should understand that an examination result is separate from a contractor license. Licensing agencies maintain their own application, experience, financial, insurance, business-law, and approval requirements.

The electrical trade examination may cover general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, calculations, workplace safety, project management, energy standards, and contractor business practices.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is the primary electrical installation reference included in this package. Candidates should become familiar with its organization and learn how to move between definitions, general requirements, wiring methods, equipment articles, special occupancies, special equipment, communications systems, and technical tables.

National Electrical Code preparation may involve conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, service calculations, feeder calculations, branch-circuit requirements, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding, motors, transformers, raceways, boxes, wiring methods, and specialized installations.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving construction-industry safety. Electrical contractors may need to review temporary wiring, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, tools, material handling, excavation, equipment, and other jobsite responsibilities.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1910, addresses general industry safety. Candidates may use this reference to review workplace electrical safety, machinery, personal protective equipment, hazardous materials, emergency planning, walking-working surfaces, employer responsibilities, and other general workplace requirements.

NFPA 70E supports preparation involving electrical safety in the workplace. Topics may include electrical hazard risk assessments, shock protection, arc-flash exposure, approach boundaries, electrically safe work conditions, energized work, protective clothing, personal protective equipment, warning labels, and safety-related maintenance practices.

ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 supports preparation involving energy-efficiency requirements for applicable commercial sites and buildings. Electrical topics may include interior and exterior lighting, lighting power, automatic controls, electrical distribution, equipment efficiency, energy monitoring, and building-system performance.

A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge supports review of project-management principles, stakeholders, project teams, planning, delivery, measurement, uncertainty, performance, communication, and project outcomes. Electrical contractors must coordinate technical work with schedules, budgets, labor, materials, subcontractors, customers, and contractual requirements.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic, 14th Edition supports contractor business preparation. Subjects may include business organization, licensing, estimating, bidding, contracts, project management, financial management, labor responsibilities, safety, insurance, risk management, and contractor operations.

Ugly’s Electrical References provides frequently used electrical formulas, diagrams, conductor information, conversions, calculations, and field data. It can support quick review of electrical relationships and commonly referenced technical information.

Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications helps connect electrical principles with code-based calculations and installations. Candidates may use it to strengthen their understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, loads, and other concepts that support practical National Electrical Code applications.

Mississippi contractor applicants may also need separate preparation for Mississippi-specific business and law requirements. The Mississippi NASCLA Business and Law reference is not included in this trade-examination book package.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors uses an open-book format. Candidates must follow the current examination rules concerning permitted references, editions, markings, highlighting, tabs, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book examination still requires extensive preparation. Having the correct books available does not help when a candidate cannot identify the appropriate reference, locate an effective index term, interpret a technical table, recognize an exception, or complete a calculation within the available time.

Candidates should begin by learning the purpose of every reference. Electrical installation questions may lead to the National Electrical Code. Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, while general industry safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1910.

Questions involving shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, approach boundaries, energized work, protective equipment, or electrically safe work conditions may require NFPA 70E. Energy-efficiency questions may involve ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Project planning, stakeholders, teams, delivery, uncertainty, measurement, or performance questions may lead to the project-management reference. Contractor business, estimating, financial, contract, and project-management questions may require the NASCLA Basic guide.

Electrical formulas, conversions, conductor information, and quick field data may be located in Ugly’s Electrical References. Theory-based questions and the principles behind electrical calculations may involve Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications.

Practice navigating the National Electrical Code by article number, definition, table, equipment type, occupancy, wiring method, conductor, and index term. Learn how general rules interact with more specific requirements and exceptions.

When reading a question, identify the central subject before opening a book. A grounding question may involve grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, grounded conductors, or separately derived systems. Selecting the correct term can reduce unnecessary searching.

Complete timed lookup exercises with the full reference set. Select a subject, choose the most appropriate book, search the table of contents or index, locate the applicable provision, and read enough surrounding material to confirm the answer.

Candidates should review the current candidate information before examination day. The examination provider determines which references, editions, markings, tabs, and materials may enter the testing room.

Licensing Steps

  1. Identify the appropriate contractor classification. Confirm that the electrical contractor classification matches the services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the owner, officer, executive employee, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the business.
  3. Document relevant electrical experience. Organize detailed project information involving electrical installation, maintenance, supervision, design coordination, estimating, or project management.
  4. Choose the business structure. Determine whether the applicant will operate as an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another accepted business entity.
  5. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested ownership, business, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  6. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the applicable Mississippi contractor licensing authority.
  7. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during review.
  8. Register for the trade examination. Complete the required steps for the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors.
  9. Receive authorization to test. Follow the registration and scheduling instructions issued after approval.
  10. Obtain the reference books. Secure the correct titles and editions required for examination preparation.
  11. Study the technical material. Review the National Electrical Code, electrical theory, calculations, safety standards, energy requirements, project management, and contractor business topics.
  12. Practice open-book navigation. Complete timed searches and calculations with the full reference set.
  13. Prepare for Mississippi-specific requirements. Obtain separate preparation for any required state Business and Law examination or other licensing requirements.
  14. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current rules.
  15. Submit examination results. Complete any transcript or score-reporting process required by the licensing authority.
  16. Complete outstanding requirements. Provide any remaining application documents or corrections requested during final review.
  17. Receive final approval. Passing the trade examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. The completed application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  18. Maintain the license. After issuance, the contractor is responsible for renewals, insurance, permits, taxes, business records, safety requirements, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

Mississippi contractor licensing involves more than completing a trade examination. Applicants must submit the appropriate application and satisfy the requirements associated with the requested classification.

The qualifying party must have an accepted relationship with the applicant. Depending on the company structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration information, ownership records, qualifying-party documentation, detailed project experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and additional supporting materials.

Applicants should ensure that names, addresses, ownership information, business details, and qualifying-party records remain consistent throughout the application and supporting documents. Incomplete or inconsistent information can delay the review process.

A passing NASCLA electrical examination result may support the trade-examination portion of a contractor application when accepted by the applicable licensing authority. It does not replace the complete state application, documentation, business-law testing, or final approval process.

Passing the examination does not authorize a candidate or company to begin regulated electrical contracting work. The appropriate contractor credential must be approved and issued before the business performs work that requires the license.

Electrical contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, utility requirements, adopted electrical codes, employee safety, insurance, tax registrations, and project-specific approvals.

Create a study schedule that divides the examination material into National Electrical Code navigation, electrical theory, calculations, construction safety, general industry safety, electrical workplace safety, energy standards, project management, and contractor business topics.

Practice NEC navigation by article, definition, table, and index term. Review services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and common calculations.

Study 29 CFR Part 1926, 29 CFR Part 1910, and NFPA 70E as separate but connected references. Learn which workplace conditions and safety subjects are addressed by each publication.

Use the energy standard to review lighting power, lighting controls, electrical distribution, equipment requirements, and commercial energy provisions. Use the project-management reference to study planning, teams, stakeholders, delivery, measurement, performance, and uncertainty.

Complete calculation practice involving current, voltage, resistance, power, loads, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, and related code applications. Use the electrical theory and field-reference books to reinforce these subjects.

Complete timed reference searches using the full book set. Identify the main subject, select the most appropriate reference, choose an effective search term, and locate the supporting material. Repeat slow searches until navigation becomes more efficient.
