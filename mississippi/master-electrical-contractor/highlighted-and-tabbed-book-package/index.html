Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Participating licensing agencies may accept a passing examination result in place of a separate jurisdiction-specific electrical trade examination.

Passing the examination does not create a national electrical contractor license. Candidates must still apply to each licensing authority where they intend to work and satisfy that jurisdiction’s application, experience, financial, insurance, business-law, fee, and approval requirements.

The examination may address general electrical knowledge, National Electrical Code requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, calculations, electrical theory, workplace safety, project management, energy standards, and contractor business practices.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is a central technical reference within this package. Candidates should become familiar with the NEC’s organization, chapters, articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, conductor requirements, wiring methods, grounding provisions, equipment rules, and calculation procedures.

NEC questions may involve determining conductor sizes, applying ampacity requirements, identifying overcurrent protection, reviewing grounding and bonding rules, selecting wiring methods, calculating loads, or locating requirements for specific electrical equipment and occupancies.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Electrical contractors may need to review temporary power, personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, tools, equipment, electrical hazards, material handling, and other construction-site responsibilities.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1910, supports preparation involving general industry workplace safety. Topics may include electrical safety, machinery, hazardous materials, personal protective equipment, emergency planning, workplace conditions, and employer responsibilities.

NFPA 70E supports preparation involving electrical safety in the workplace. Candidates may review shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, risk assessments, approach boundaries, protective equipment, electrically safe work conditions, warning labels, energized work, and safety-related maintenance practices.

ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 supports study involving energy-efficiency requirements for applicable commercial sites and buildings. Electrical topics may include lighting power, lighting controls, electrical power systems, equipment, energy monitoring, and building energy performance.

A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge supports preparation involving project principles, planning, stakeholders, teams, delivery, performance, measurement, uncertainty, and project outcomes. Electrical contractors often coordinate labor, materials, schedules, costs, communication, risks, and contractual responsibilities.

The NASCLA Basic contractor guide supports broader business, law, financial-management, safety, and project-management preparation connected with the accredited examination. It helps candidates review the administrative and operational responsibilities involved in running construction projects and contractor businesses.

Ugly’s Electrical References provides frequently used electrical formulas, conversions, conductor information, calculations, diagrams, and field data. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications connects electrical principles with practical code questions and calculation methods.

Mississippi contractor applicants must also complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination as part of the state licensing process. The Mississippi-specific Business and Law reference is not included in this trade-examination book package.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is open book. Candidates may bring permitted references into the examination room, subject to the current rules established by NASCLA and the testing provider.

Open-book testing still demands extensive preparation. Having the correct books does not help when a candidate cannot identify the relevant reference, locate an effective index term, understand the code structure, or interpret the applicable rule within the available testing time.

The references in this package are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support efficient navigation. Highlighting identifies important requirements, formulas, definitions, exceptions, tables, safety provisions, and business topics. Tabs organize major chapters, articles, and frequently reviewed subjects.

Candidates should review every tab before examination day. Learn what each tab identifies, practice moving between sections, and become familiar with the surrounding material. A tab should guide a candidate to a known subject rather than replace knowledge of the book.

Electrical installation questions may lead to the 2023 National Electrical Code. Candidates should practice navigating the NEC by article number, table, definition, equipment type, occupancy, wiring method, and index term.

Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, while general industry safety questions may lead to 29 CFR Part 1910. Candidates should understand the purpose of each OSHA reference so they can select the proper volume without unnecessary searching.

Questions involving electrical shock, arc-flash exposure, protective boundaries, energized work, or electrically safe work conditions may require NFPA 70E. Energy-related questions may involve ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Project planning, stakeholder coordination, team performance, delivery, measurement, or uncertainty questions may lead to the PMBOK Guide. Contractor operations and business questions may involve the NASCLA Basic contractor guide.

Electrical formulas, conversions, quick tables, and commonly used field information may be found in Ugly’s Electrical References. Questions involving the principles behind voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and NEC calculations may require Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications.

Practice using related search terms because an examination question may not use the exact wording found in the reference. A grounding question may require searching grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, separately derived systems, or grounded conductors.

Candidates should review the current NASCLA Candidate Information Bulletin before testing. The testing provider determines which book editions, markings, highlighting methods, tabs, and supplemental materials are permitted in the examination room.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate Mississippi classification. Determine whether the Electrical Work classification matches the electrical contracting services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Document electrical experience. Organize detailed information showing relevant electrical construction, installation, supervision, maintenance, or project-management experience.
  4. Prepare the Mississippi application. Complete the applicable contractor filing and gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, financial, insurance, reference, experience, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. Send the completed contractor application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  6. Respond to application requests. Provide missing records, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  7. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the applicable registration process for the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors.
  8. Receive examination authorization. Follow the instructions issued after the examination application is approved.
  9. Schedule the test. Select an available examination date and authorized testing location.
  10. Study the reference set. Review the NEC, electrical theory, calculations, safety regulations, energy standards, project management, and contractor business subjects.
  11. Practice reference navigation. Complete timed lookups using the highlighted and tabbed books so the full reference set becomes familiar.
  12. Prepare for Mississippi Business and Law. Obtain the separate Mississippi-specific reference and complete the required state business-law preparation.
  13. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials permitted under the current testing rules.
  14. Submit examination results. Complete any transcript or score-reporting steps required for the Mississippi contractor application.
  15. Complete outstanding requirements. Provide any remaining application records or corrections requested during final review.
  16. Receive final licensing review. Passing the examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. The application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  17. Maintain the license. After issuance, the contractor is responsible for renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable commercial contractor licensing process. Electrical Work is recognized as a major contractor classification.

Applicants should select a classification that accurately reflects the services their business plans to provide. Electrical Work can involve wiring, fixtures, electrical equipment, raceways, conductors, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, controls, and related systems within the approved classification scope.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the Board’s requirements. Depending on the business structure, that person may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The application may require business-registration records, ownership information, qualifying-party documentation, detailed electrical experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and additional supporting materials. Information should be accurate and consistent across the application and company documents.

Mississippi applicants must complete the Law and Business Management examination. An applicable trade examination is also required unless the Board accepts an approved result or grants an available trade-examination waiver.

A passing NASCLA electrical examination result may satisfy the trade-examination component when accepted by the licensing authority. Candidates must still complete the Mississippi application, Business and Law examination, documentation, and approval process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not authorize the candidate or business to begin regulated electrical contracting work. The applicable Mississippi contractor credential must be issued before performing work that requires the license.

Electrical contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, utility requirements, adopted electrical codes, employee safety, tax registrations, insurance, and project-specific approvals.
