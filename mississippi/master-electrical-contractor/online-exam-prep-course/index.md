## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Participating licensing agencies may accept a passing NASCLA examination result in place of a separate jurisdiction-specific electrical trade examination.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national contractor license. Candidates must apply to the licensing authority in each jurisdiction where they intend to work and satisfy that authority’s application, experience, business-law, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

The examination may address general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, equipment, calculations, workplace safety, project management, energy standards, and contractor business practices.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is a central technical reference for the examination. Candidates should become familiar with its chapters, articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, wiring methods, conductor requirements, equipment rules, grounding provisions, and calculation procedures.

NEC preparation may involve services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, voltage drop, overcurrent protection, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, motors, transformers, raceways, boxes, special occupancies, emergency systems, and specialized equipment.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Electrical contractors may need to review temporary power, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, tools, equipment, material handling, and other construction-site responsibilities.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1910, supports preparation involving general industry safety. Topics can include electrical safety, workplace conditions, machinery, personal protective equipment, hazardous materials, emergency planning, lockout and control procedures, and employer responsibilities.

NFPA 70E supports electrical workplace-safety preparation. Candidates may review shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, risk assessments, approach boundaries, electrically safe work conditions, protective equipment, warning labels, energized work, and safety-related maintenance practices.

ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 supports preparation involving energy-efficiency requirements for applicable commercial sites and buildings. Electrical topics may involve lighting power, lighting controls, electrical power systems, equipment, energy monitoring, and building energy performance.

*A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge* supports review involving project principles, teams, stakeholders, planning, project work, delivery, measurement, uncertainty, performance, and project outcomes. Electrical contractors frequently coordinate labor, materials, schedules, budgets, communication, risks, and contractual responsibilities.

The NASCLA Basic contractor guide supports broader contractor business, law, financial-management, safety, estimating, and project-management preparation associated with the accredited examination.

*Ugly’s Electrical References* provides commonly used formulas, conversions, conductor information, calculations, diagrams, and field data. *Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications* helps candidates connect current, voltage, resistance, power, circuits, and other electrical principles with practical code applications.

Mississippi contractor applicants must also complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. This online course focuses on preparation for the NASCLA electrical trade examination. Mississippi-specific Business and Law preparation is separate unless included in another product.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is open book. Candidates may bring permitted references into the examination room, subject to the current rules established by NASCLA and the testing provider.

Mississippi contractor examinations administered through PSI are also open book. Candidates should review the current candidate information for every examination they plan to take because the approved references and preparation rules may differ between the NASCLA trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination.

Open-book testing requires strong navigation skills. Candidates may have the correct reference available but still lose valuable time by selecting the wrong book, searching an ineffective term, overlooking an exception, misreading a table, or applying a provision outside its intended scope.

The course helps candidates develop a repeatable lookup process. Begin by identifying the main subject of the question. Determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer. Search the index, table of contents, article number, chapter heading, definition, formula, or technical table. Then read the surrounding material to confirm that the provision applies.

Electrical installation questions may lead to the 2023 National Electrical Code. Candidates should practice finding information by article, equipment type, occupancy, wiring method, conductor, calculation, definition, and index term.

Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, while general industry safety questions may lead to 29 CFR Part 1910. Candidates should learn the purpose and organization of both OSHA references so they can select the proper volume efficiently.

Questions involving electrical shock, arc-flash exposure, approach boundaries, protective equipment, energized work, or electrically safe work conditions may require NFPA 70E. Energy-related questions may involve ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Questions involving project teams, stakeholders, planning, delivery, performance, uncertainty, or project outcomes may lead to the PMBOK Guide. Contractor business and management questions may require the NASCLA Basic contractor guide.

Electrical formulas, conversions, diagrams, conductor data, and quick-reference information may be found in *Ugly’s Electrical References*. Questions involving the principles behind electrical calculations may require *Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications*.

Candidates should practice related search terms because examination wording may differ from the terminology used in a reference. A grounding question may involve grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, or separately derived systems.

Review the current NASCLA Candidate Information Bulletin before examination day. The testing provider determines which references, editions, markings, highlighting methods, tabs, and supplemental materials are permitted in the examination room.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Confirm the appropriate Mississippi classification.** Determine whether the Electrical Work classification matches the electrical contracting services the business intends to provide.
2. **Select the qualifying party.** Identify the owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the company and complete the required examinations.
3. **Document electrical experience.** Organize detailed information showing relevant electrical construction, installation, maintenance, supervision, or project-management experience.
4. **Prepare the Mississippi contractor application.** Complete the applicable filing and gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
5. **Submit the state application.** Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
6. **Respond to application requests.** Provide missing records, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
7. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the applicable registration process for the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors.
8. **Receive authorization to test.** Follow the instructions issued after the examination application is approved.
9. **Schedule the examination.** Select an available testing date and authorized location through the testing provider.
10. **Obtain the listed references.** Secure the applicable books and editions before beginning the reference-navigation portions of the course.
11. **Prepare with the online course.** Review the NEC, electrical theory, calculations, safety regulations, energy standards, project management, and contractor business topics.
12. **Complete timed reference practice.** Develop the ability to select the correct book, locate the applicable information, and answer questions efficiently.
13. **Prepare for Mississippi Business and Law.** Obtain separate preparation for the required Mississippi Law and Business Management examination.
14. **Take the required examinations.** Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials permitted under the current testing rules.
15. **Submit examination results.** Complete the transcript or score-reporting process required for the Mississippi application.
16. **Complete outstanding requirements.** Provide any remaining application documents or corrections requested during final review.
17. **Receive licensing approval.** Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
18. **Maintain the license.** After issuance, contractors are responsible for renewals, insurance, business records, taxes, permits, safety obligations, and continued compliance.

## State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable commercial contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the state application process and receive approval before performing electrical contracting work that requires the applicable credential.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that meets the Board’s requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration records, ownership information, qualifying-party documentation, detailed electrical experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials. Application information should be accurate and consistent with the company’s official records.

Mississippi applicants are required to complete the Law and Business Management examination. A trade examination may also be required unless the Board accepts an approved examination result or grants an applicable waiver.

A passing NASCLA electrical examination result may be used to satisfy the trade-examination requirement when accepted by the licensing authority. Candidates must still complete the Mississippi application, Business and Law examination, documentation, and final approval process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not authorize the candidate or business to begin regulated electrical contracting work. The applicable Mississippi contractor credential must be issued before the company performs work requiring the license.

Electrical contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, utility requirements, adopted electrical codes, employee safety, tax registrations, insurance, and project-specific approvals.
