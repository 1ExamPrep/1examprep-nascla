Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides a standardized trade examination for Electrical Contractors, also identified as Master or Unlimited Electricians. Mississippi administers and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination through the NASCLA program.

Passing the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor examination can allow a candidate to submit an examination transcript to participating agencies that accept the credential. Each agency retains control over its licensing requirements, applications, business-law testing, experience standards, financial documentation, insurance requirements, fees, and final approval.

The NASCLA examination is not a national contractor license. It is an accredited trade examination that may be accepted in place of a separate state-specific trade examination by participating licensing agencies. Candidates must still apply for the appropriate contractor license in every jurisdiction where they intend to work.

The electrical examination may address general electrical knowledge, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, equipment, grounding and bonding, motors, transformers, calculations, overcurrent protection, electrical safety, jobsite safety, energy requirements, project management, and electrical theory.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is a central reference for electrical installation requirements. Candidates should become familiar with its organization, chapters, articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, informational notes, calculations, and equipment-specific provisions.

The OSHA construction and general industry references support preparation involving workplace safety, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, fall protection, ladders, tools, equipment, lockout procedures, temporary power, construction operations, and employer responsibilities.

NFPA 70E supports electrical workplace safety preparation. Topics can include shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, risk assessment, electrically safe work conditions, protective equipment, approach boundaries, warning labels, and safety-related work practices.

The energy standard supports preparation involving energy-efficient building and site requirements for applicable commercial construction. The project-management reference supports review involving project principles, planning, delivery, teams, stakeholders, performance, uncertainty, measurement, and project outcomes.

Ugly’s Electrical References provides compact electrical formulas, tables, calculations, diagrams, and field information. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications supports the connection between electrical principles and practical National Electrical Code applications.

The package also includes two contractor business references. The Mississippi edition supports the required Mississippi Law and Business Management examination, while the NASCLA Basic guide supports broader contractor business, law, and project-management preparation associated with the accredited electrical examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor examination is open book. Mississippi contractor examinations administered through PSI are also open book. Candidates must bring the permitted references and follow the current rules for approved titles, editions, tabs, highlighting, notes, markings, and supplemental materials.

Open-book testing requires more than owning the reference set. Candidates need to recognize the subject of each question, select the correct book, locate an effective search term, and interpret the applicable provision within the available testing time.

The included books are professionally highlighted and tabbed to support this process. Highlighting identifies important code requirements, safety provisions, business topics, calculations, tables, and technical concepts. Tabs help candidates move between major sections and frequently reviewed subjects.

Candidates should study every prepared tab before test day. Review the table of contents, index, chapter structure, definitions, diagrams, formulas, and tables in each book. Prepared references are most effective when candidates have already practiced using them.

Electrical installation questions may lead to the 2023 National Electrical Code. Workplace electrical-safety questions may require NFPA 70E, while construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926. General industry safety subjects may require 29 CFR Part 1910.

Energy-efficiency questions may involve ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022. Project-management questions may lead to the PMBOK Guide. Electrical formulas, conversions, and quick-reference information may be located in Ugly’s Electrical References.

Questions involving the principles behind current, voltage, resistance, power, circuits, and code calculations may require Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. Contractor business questions may involve either the NASCLA Basic guide or the Mississippi-specific Business and Law guide, depending on the examination being taken.

Candidates should review the current candidate bulletin before testing. Only references and preparation methods permitted by the examination provider may enter the testing room.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the license classification. Determine that the Mississippi Electrical Work classification matches the electrical contracting services the business intends to offer.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the owner, officer, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Choose a business structure. Decide whether the company will operate as an LLC, corporation, or another accepted business type.
  4. Form the business entity. Establish the LLC or corporation and organize its official ownership and registration records.
  5. Obtain the EIN. File for the Employer Identification Number used for tax administration, business banking, employee hiring, and company operations.
  6. Document electrical experience. Organize detailed project information showing relevant electrical construction, installation, maintenance, supervision, or management experience.
  7. Prepare the Mississippi application. Complete the applicable contractor application and gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, insurance, financial, reference, and supporting information.
  8. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  9. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional records requested during the review.
  10. Receive examination eligibility. After the qualifying party is approved to test, follow the examination provider’s registration instructions.
  11. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Use the course and included reference books to study electrical code, theory, safety, energy standards, project management, and contractor business topics.
  12. Prepare for Mississippi Business and Law. Review the Mississippi-specific contractor business and law material required for state licensing.
  13. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials allowed under current testing rules.
  14. Submit examination results. Complete any transcript or score-reporting steps required for the Mississippi application.
  15. Complete remaining requirements. Provide any outstanding information requested during final application review.
  16. Receive Board approval. Passing an examination does not automatically issue a license. The application and supporting records must also be approved.
  17. Maintain the business and license. Track renewals, insurance, taxes, company records, permits, safety requirements, and ongoing contractor compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates applicable commercial contractor licensing. Electrical Work is one of the major classifications recognized by the Board.

Applicants must complete the Mississippi contractor application before they can schedule state-administered examinations. The Board reviews the filing, identifies missing requirements, and sends eligible candidate information to the testing provider.

The qualifying party must have an accepted relationship with the applicant. Depending on the company’s structure, that person may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

Mississippi applicants must complete the Law and Business Management examination. The applicable trade examination is also required unless the Board accepts an approved examination transcript or grants an applicable waiver.

Mississippi administers and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Candidates who pass must still complete all Mississippi application and licensing requirements before performing work that requires the state credential.

The application may require business-registration documents, ownership information, qualifying-party records, project experience, references, financial information, insurance documentation, and other supporting materials. Company information should remain consistent across official filings.

Business formation does not create a contractor license. Forming an LLC or corporation and obtaining an EIN establishes the company’s legal and administrative structure, but the business must still receive the required contractor credential before performing regulated work.

Electrical contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, utility requirements, adopted codes, employee safety, insurance, tax registrations, and project-specific approvals.
