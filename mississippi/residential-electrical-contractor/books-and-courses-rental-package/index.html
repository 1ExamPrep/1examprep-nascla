Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical examinations that participating licensing agencies can administer or accept in place of their own trade examinations. Mississippi currently accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for the residential electrical trade-examination component.

The Mississippi Residential Electrical classification is a residential license classification covering the installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems. The scope is limited to residential electrical work, making this pathway different from broader commercial or master electrical classifications.

Preparation for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination should include a broad range of residential electrical subjects. Candidates should be comfortable working with technical code material and practical electrical concepts rather than relying on memorization alone.

Mississippi applicants must also prepare separately for the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The State Board of Contractors requires all applicants to take the Law and Business Management exam, regardless of whether the applicable trade examination is taken directly or waived through an accepted examination pathway.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors coordinates examination eligibility with PSI. Applicants first submit the applicable license application to the Board. Once the applicant is eligible for testing, the Board sends the qualifying party's information to PSI, and PSI provides the scheduling process for the required examination.

Open Book Test

The applicable Mississippi contractor examinations are open book tests. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors states that its examinations are open book and provides candidates with a Candidate Information Bulletin explaining testing procedures, scheduling information, and which books may be used for the specific examination.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is also an open-book examination. Open-book testing makes organized reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice recognizing what a question is asking, identifying which book contains the information, locating the applicable provision, formula, definition, table, or safety rule, and applying it correctly.

All rental books in this package are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support this type of study. Tabs create visible navigation points throughout large technical references, while highlighting helps focus attention on important material during review.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically create a Mississippi residential electrical license. Applicants must complete the licensing process through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors and satisfy all applicable requirements.

  1. Confirm the Residential Electrical classification. Make sure the Mississippi residential electrical classification matches the scope of work you intend to perform.
  2. Complete the Mississippi license application. Submit the required contractor application and supporting documentation to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
  3. Identify the qualifying party. Mississippi uses a qualifying-party system for contractor licensing, and the qualifying party is responsible for satisfying the applicable examination requirement.
  4. Use the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination when applicable. Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for the residential electrical trade requirement.
  5. Prepare for the trade examination. Study the residential electrical references, calculations, electrical theory, safety requirements, and construction topics included in this package.
  6. Prepare for Mississippi Law and Business Management. All Mississippi applicants must complete the Law and Business Management examination.
  7. Follow PSI scheduling procedures. Once MSBOC submits the qualifying party's testing information to PSI, schedule the required examination through PSI.
  8. Submit all remaining documentation. Ensure the Board has received the required application materials, licensing documentation, insurance information when applicable, and examination results.
  9. Receive Board approval. Once all required documents and passing scores are received, the application is submitted for Board review and approval.
  10. Maintain the license. Mississippi contractor licenses must be renewed annually, and beginning in 2026 MSBOC sends annual renewal notices by email.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates commercial and residential contractors in Mississippi. Residential electrical contractors performing regulated residential electrical work must hold the applicable Mississippi license.

The Residential Electrical classification involves the installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems. The holder of this classification is limited to residential electrical work only.

Mississippi requires applicants to complete an application before testing. After the Board receives and reviews the application, the qualifying party's information is forwarded to PSI so the individual can schedule the required examination.

All applicants must take the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. Depending on the classification and examination pathway, a trade exam may also be required. Mississippi's acceptance of the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination allows the accepted NASCLA result to address the applicable residential electrical trade-examination component.

The Board's examinations are open book. Candidates receive a Candidate Information Bulletin containing examination procedures, scheduling information, reference-book rules, and other testing instructions. Candidates should follow the current bulletin when determining exactly which materials can be used at a specific Mississippi examination appointment.

Applicants must satisfy the Board's full application requirements before a license is issued. The Board reviews the application, documentation, and passing examination results before approving the license.

Mississippi also requires appropriate insurance documentation for active residential licenses. General liability insurance is part of the residential contractor licensing process, and workers' compensation documentation applies when required by the applicant's business circumstances.

Contractor licenses are issued to the approved entity and must be used in the name shown on the license. Mississippi instructs contractors to conduct business, sign bids, contracts, permits, and other documents using the licensed business name.

Mississippi contractor licenses are renewed annually. Beginning in 2026, the Mississippi State Board of Contractors sends annual renewal notices by email only. License holders remain responsible for renewing on time and maintaining current contact information with the Board.
