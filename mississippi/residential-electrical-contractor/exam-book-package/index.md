## Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Mississippi Residential Electrical contractor license. Candidates must complete the Mississippi State Board of Contractors licensing process.

1. **Choose the Residential Electrical classification.** This Mississippi classification covers installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems.
2. **Complete the residential contractor application.** Submit the current Mississippi State Board of Contractors application and required supporting information.
3. **Document experience.** Mississippi requires applicants to demonstrate experience in each requested classification and currently asks applicants to list at least three completed jobs in the requested classification.
4. **Identify the qualifying party.** The qualifying party is the individual responsible for taking required examinations for the applicant.
5. **Provide qualifying-party employment documentation.** When applicable, Mississippi requires proof establishing the qualifying party's relationship with the applicant.
6. **Complete the Law and Business Management examination.** Mississippi requires this examination for residential contractor applicants.
7. **Complete the accepted residential electrical trade examination requirement.** Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination.
8. **Provide insurance and business documentation.** Submit the applicable proof of insurance, tax identification information, business-entity documents, and other materials required by the Board.
9. **Complete Board review.** The Mississippi State Board of Contractors determines whether the applicant has satisfied all licensing requirements before issuing the Residential Electrical license.

## State Requirements

The **Mississippi State Board of Contractors** regulates residential contractor licensing. The Board's Residential Electrical classification is a test-required classification covering installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems. The holder is limited to residential electrical work.

The current Mississippi residential contractor application requires applicants to demonstrate experience in the classification requested. Applicants are instructed to list at least **three completed jobs** in that classification as part of the experience section.

Mississippi uses a **qualifying party** structure for contractor examinations. The qualifying party is the person who takes the required examination or examinations on behalf of the applicant. The qualifying party must be appropriately associated with the applicant as an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

When proof of employment is required, Mississippi accepts documentation such as a check stub, W-2, or business records showing the qualifying party as an owner or officer. Proof of employment is not required when the application is submitted in an individual's own name.

The current residential application requires a **Mississippi Law and Business Management examination** in addition to the applicable trade examination. The Board states that its examinations are open book and that examination eligibility is established through the contractor application process before scheduling.

Mississippi's current residential application fee is **$50**, which includes one trade classification. Additional trade classifications carry an additional fee. The Law and Business Management examination is required but is not treated as a trade classification.

Residential applicants must also provide proof of **general liability insurance**. Mississippi does not currently specify the same minimum general-liability coverage amount for residential applicants that applies to commercial applicants, but proof of coverage is required for active residential licensing.

Applicants with five or more employees may also need to submit proof of workers' compensation coverage. Corporations, limited liability companies, and limited liability partnerships must provide applicable documentation showing good standing with the Mississippi Secretary of State.

Mississippi contractor licensing is separate from simply passing an examination. The applicant must receive the Residential Electrical license from the Mississippi State Board of Contractors before performing work requiring that state credential.
