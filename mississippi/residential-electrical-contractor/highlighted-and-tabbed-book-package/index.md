## State Requirements

The **Mississippi State Board of Contractors** regulates commercial and residential contractor licensing in Mississippi. Residential Electrical is a specific test-required classification administered by the Board.

The Residential Electrical classification covers the **installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems**. The holder of this classification is limited to residential electrical work only. Contractors who intend to perform work outside that residential scope must obtain the appropriate classification for the work being performed.

Mississippi residential contractor applicants must complete an application and provide the documentation requested by the Board. The Board reviews experience, examination records, qualifying-party information, insurance, business structure, and other materials before licensing.

Mississippi uses a **qualifying party** system. The qualifying party is the individual responsible for satisfying the applicable examination requirement on behalf of the applicant. The qualifying party must have an acceptable relationship with the applying business or individual under Mississippi licensing requirements.

Mississippi's application process also requires applicants to demonstrate experience in the classification requested. Applicants should be prepared to provide project and work-history information supporting experience in residential electrical work.

All applicants must take the **Law and Business Management examination**. Depending on the classification, a trade examination may also be required. For Residential Electrical, Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an accepted trade-exam pathway.

Mississippi states that its contractor examinations are **open book**. Candidate information materials provide details about scheduling, authorized books, and examination procedures. Candidates should follow the current examination bulletin when preparing references for the testing room.

Residential applicants must also comply with applicable insurance requirements. General liability insurance is required for active residential contractor licensing. Workers' compensation coverage must be provided when applicable under Mississippi law.

Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, and other business entities may also need to provide documentation regarding business registration and good standing as part of the licensing process.

Mississippi licenses must be maintained through the Board's renewal process. Beginning in 2026, the Mississippi State Board of Contractors uses email as its method for sending annual renewal notices to current licensees. License holders remain responsible for renewing on time and keeping their contact information current with the Board.
