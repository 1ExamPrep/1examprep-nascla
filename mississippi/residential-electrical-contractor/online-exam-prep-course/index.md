## State Requirements

The **Mississippi State Board of Contractors** licenses and regulates contractors within Mississippi. The Board maintains separate commercial and residential contractor classifications, including a specific **Residential Electrical** classification.

The Residential Electrical classification covers the **installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems**. A contractor holding this classification is limited to residential electrical work.

Mississippi law specifically addresses residential subcontractors performing life-safety trades such as electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and mechanical work on residential construction and residential improvement projects. Contractors should obtain the classification required for the type and scope of residential electrical work they perform and comply with applicable state and local permitting requirements.

The Mississippi residential contractor application requires an applicant to demonstrate experience in each requested classification. The current application instructs applicants to list at least **three completed jobs** involving the classification for which they are applying.

Mississippi also uses a **qualifying party** system. The qualifying party is the individual responsible for taking required examinations on behalf of the applicant. The qualifying party must be properly associated with the applicant, and the Board requires proof of employment when applicable.

Current Mississippi residential licensing application requirements include proof of general liability insurance. Residential applicants must provide proof of coverage, although the Board does not specify the same minimum liability coverage amount that applies to commercial applicants.

If the applicant has five or more employees, workers' compensation documentation may also be required. Corporations, limited liability companies, and limited liability partnerships must provide the applicable proof of good standing with the Mississippi Secretary of State.

Mississippi's current residential contractor application lists a **$50 application fee**, which includes one trade classification. An additional fee applies when an applicant requests additional trade classifications. The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination is required but is not treated as a trade classification.

Mississippi requires applicants to complete the licensing process in addition to passing examinations. An examination result alone does not authorize a contractor to perform regulated work. The applicant must receive the applicable license from the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.

Local permitting and electrical requirements may also apply. Contractors should perform work only within the scope of the license held and in accordance with the requirements of the jurisdiction where the project is located.
