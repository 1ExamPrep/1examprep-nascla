Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized electrical trade examinations accepted or administered by participating licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for the residential electrical classification. Candidates using this examination still must satisfy Mississippi's separate licensing requirements before a license can be issued.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around the knowledge used in residential electrical work. Candidates should prepare for subjects involving electrical theory, code navigation, calculations, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, circuit protection, equipment, safety, project management, and other areas associated with residential electrical systems.

Important preparation areas include:

  • Electrical theory and calculations
  • National Electrical Code navigation
  • Residential electrical services
  • Feeders and branch circuits
  • Dwelling-unit load calculations
  • Conductor sizing and ampacity
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Residential wiring methods
  • Boxes, cables, raceways, and enclosures
  • Electrical equipment and appliances
  • Tools and test equipment
  • Construction-industry safety
  • Electrical workplace safety
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Residential construction requirements
  • Project design and management
  • Contractor business and law

Mississippi also requires applicants to complete a Law and Business Management examination. The state business and law examination is separate from the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor technical examination. The Mississippi, 6th Edition book included in this package supports preparation for the state-specific business and law component.

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors uses PSI for contractor examinations. After the applicable licensing application information is submitted, MSBOC provides examination eligibility and scheduling instructions through the testing process.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. Mississippi contractor examinations, including the required Law and Business Management examination, are also administered as open-book examinations under the applicable testing rules.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation an important part of exam preparation. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question, selecting the correct reference, locating the relevant code section, table, formula, definition, safety standard, or business-law provision, and applying the information accurately.

All rental books included in The 1 Package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting helps focus attention on important study material, while permanent tabs create visible navigation points throughout larger codes, standards, manuals, and technical references.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial study attention. Candidates should become comfortable using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, chapter organization, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Residential study should include services, feeders, branch circuits, load calculations, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, receptacles, equipment, appliances, motors, and related electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be reviewed alongside code navigation. Study voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, circuit relationships, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and practical electrical calculations. Understanding the electrical concept behind a problem can help candidates determine which formula or code provision applies.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-safety preparation, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system preparation. The International Residential Code provides residential construction context, while ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide and Mississippi, 6th Edition provide contractor business and law preparation. Candidates should devote separate study time to Mississippi's Law and Business Management requirement instead of treating the technical NASCLA examination as the entire licensing process.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates residential contractors performing covered work in Mississippi. Residential Electrical is a specific residential license classification for installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems.

Mississippi uses a qualifying party system. The qualifying party is the individual who takes the required examinations on behalf of the applicant. A qualifying party may be an owner, officer, member of the executive staff, or responsible managing employee.

For business applicants, proof of employment for the qualifying party is required. Current MSBOC application instructions identify documents such as a check stub, W-2 form, or Mississippi Secretary of State listing for an owner or officer as acceptable forms of proof. Proof of employment is not required when the application is filed in an individual name.

Mississippi requires the Law and Business Management examination for all residential contractor applicants. Residential Electrical applicants must also satisfy the residential electrical examination requirement. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is accepted by Mississippi for this trade-examination purpose.

Residential contractor applicants must also provide proof of general liability insurance. Mississippi does not currently specify a minimum liability coverage amount for residential applicants, but proof of coverage must be submitted as part of the licensing process.

If the applicant has five or more employees, proof of workers' compensation coverage is required. Applicants organized as a Corporation, LLC, or LLP must also meet Mississippi business-registration requirements applicable to their entity.

Mississippi's current residential application fee is $50. The state licensing fee is separate from the price of The 1 Package and separate from examination-related charges.

Mississippi residential licenses must be renewed annually. The current residential renewal fee is $100. Beginning in 2026, MSBOC sends annual renewal notices by email to the address of record approximately 60 days before the expiration date shown on the certificate of licensure.

A license may be processed as a late renewal for up to 180 days after its expiration date. If it has been expired for more than 180 days, a new application for licensure is required.

Business Formation included with The 1 Package helps establish the business entity, but forming an LLC or Corporation does not itself grant authority to perform residential electrical contracting. The contractor license, qualifying party, examination requirements, insurance, registration, and other applicable obligations must be completed separately.
