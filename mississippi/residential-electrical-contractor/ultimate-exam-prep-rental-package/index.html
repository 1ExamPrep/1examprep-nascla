Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized trade examinations for participating licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

Mississippi currently accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination. As an accepting state agency, Mississippi can use a passing NASCLA result as a waiver in place of its applicable state-specific residential electrical trade examination. Candidates must still apply to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors and satisfy all additional licensing requirements.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is built around the knowledge associated with electrical work in residential environments. Preparation should combine technical electrical knowledge with code navigation, calculations, safety, residential construction, and practical problem solving.

Important residential electrical preparation areas include:

  • Electrical theory and calculations
  • National Electrical Code navigation
  • Residential services and service equipment
  • Feeders and branch circuits
  • Dwelling-unit load calculations
  • Conductor sizing and ampacity
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Residential wiring methods
  • Boxes, raceways, cables, and enclosures
  • Electrical equipment and appliances
  • Construction-industry safety
  • Electrical workplace safety
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Residential construction requirements
  • Product safety signs and labels
  • Contractor business and project management

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the National Examination Database. The current NASCLA application fee is $65. Applications are typically processed within seven business days, and an approved application remains valid for one year from the approval date.

After passing an accepted NASCLA examination, candidates can electronically send the examination transcript to participating state agencies through the National Examination Database. Mississippi can then pair that transcript with the applicant's contractor license application.

Mississippi also requires applicants to pass the separate Law and Business Management examination. This requirement remains even when the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is used as the accepted trade-exam waiver.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies the Residential Electrical Contractor examination as one of its open-book electrical trade examinations.

Mississippi contractor examinations are also conducted as open-book examinations under the state's examination procedures. Candidates should follow the current candidate bulletin and testing rules regarding which materials may be used during a particular examination appointment.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should know how to identify the subject of a question, choose the appropriate reference, locate the applicable code section, table, formula, definition, or safety requirement, and apply that information correctly.

All rental books in this Ultimate package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting can help direct attention toward important provisions and study areas, while permanent tabs create visible navigation points throughout larger technical references.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial study attention. Candidates should practice using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Residential study should include services, feeders, branch circuits, load calculations, grounding and bonding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, receptacles, lighting, appliances, equipment, and special electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be reviewed alongside code navigation. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and practical calculations. Understanding the electrical principle behind a problem can make it easier to determine which formula or code provision applies.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-safety preparation, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system study. The International Residential Code provides broader residential construction context, while ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide and Mississippi, 6th Edition provide business-law and contractor-management material. Because Mississippi requires the Law and Business Management examination in addition to the applicable trade requirement, candidates should plan dedicated study time for business topics as well as technical electrical subjects.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Mississippi residential electrical contractor license. Candidates must complete the licensing process through the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.

  1. Confirm the Residential Electrical classification. Make sure the Mississippi residential electrical classification matches the work you intend to perform.
  2. Complete the Mississippi contractor application. Submit the required application and supporting documentation to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
  3. Identify the qualifying party. Mississippi uses a qualifying-party structure for contractor licensing and examination purposes.
  4. Use the NASCLA examination for the applicable trade waiver. Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination.
  5. Transmit the NASCLA transcript. Send the passing NASCLA transcript electronically through the National Examination Database to the Mississippi licensing agency.
  6. Prepare for Mississippi Law and Business Management. All applicants must complete the state's required Law and Business Management examination.

  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Submit any additional experience, business, insurance, financial, or other documentation required by the Board.
  11. Receive Board approval. The license is issued only after all applicable Mississippi requirements have been completed and approved.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates residential and commercial contractor licensing in Mississippi. Residential electrical contractors performing regulated residential electrical work must hold the appropriate Mississippi contractor license.

The Mississippi Residential Electrical classification covers the installation, maintenance, and repair of residential electrical systems. The classification is limited to residential electrical work.

Mississippi accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as a trade-examination waiver. The accepted NASCLA result can satisfy the applicable trade requirement, but it does not function as a contractor license and does not replace Mississippi's application process.

All contractor applicants must take the Law and Business Management examination. Mississippi's examination materials explain that a trade examination may also be required depending on the classification. When an accepted NASCLA result satisfies the trade requirement, the separate Law and Business Management examination remains applicable.

The Board requires applicants to complete the licensing application before the examination process is finalized. Once the application has been reviewed and the qualifying party is approved for testing, the examination process is coordinated through PSI.

Mississippi contractor examinations are open book. Candidates should use the current Candidate Information Bulletin to determine examination procedures and the specific materials allowed for the state examination they are taking.

Residential contractor licensing also includes insurance requirements. Applicants for an active residential license must provide applicable general liability insurance documentation, and workers' compensation requirements apply when required by the business circumstances.

The license is issued to the approved business entity and should be used in the licensed name. Contractors are responsible for maintaining the license, meeting renewal requirements, and remaining within the authorized residential electrical classification.

Mississippi contractor licenses are renewed annually. Beginning in 2026, the Mississippi State Board of Contractors sends annual renewal notices by email. License holders remain responsible for maintaining current contact information and completing renewal requirements by the applicable deadline.
