Missouri NASCLA Electrical Exam and Statewide Electrical Contractor License

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 10 min read · 8 official sources cited

Missouri does not license electrical contractors the way many states do. Most cities and counties run their own electrical licensing, and the state layers an optional statewide electrical contractor license on top. That statewide license is issued by the Office of Statewide Electrical Contractors (OSEC), and the exam it relies on is the NASCLA accredited electrical contractor exam, given by PSI.

This page walks you through what the Missouri NASCLA electrical exam covers, who can sit for it, the hours and insurance you need, what the state charges, and how to study for an open book test built around nine references. Does Missouri accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes, for electrical work. NASCLA lists the Missouri Office of Statewide Electrical Contractors as a participating agency in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program, covering the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam and the Journeyman Electrician exam. Our Missouri materials focus on the Electrical Contractor (Master/Unlimited) exam, which is the one you take for the statewide electrical contractor license.

Missouri law requires a passing score on a standardized, nationally accredited electrical assessment that a third party creates and administers. The state rule adds that the exam must be based on the National Electrical Code and given by a testing agency that is not tied to the state or to any city or county. The NASCLA exam, delivered through PSI, fits that description, and the division posts its list of accepted exams on its website.

Missouri is not on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the NASCLA commercial building contractor exam. If you are a general contractor, your requirements come from the city or county where you build, not from a state board. This page is about the electrical license.

Who issues the Missouri statewide electrical contractor license

The Office of Statewide Electrical Contractors sits inside the Missouri Division of Professional Registration in Jefferson City. It was created by Senate Bill 240, effective August 28, 2017, and the law now lives at Sections 324.900 to 324.945 of the Missouri statutes. OSEC started taking license applications on July 1, 2019.

Two features make Missouri different. First, the statewide license is optional. If you only work in places where you already hold the local license, or where no license is required, state law says you do not need the statewide one. The value of the statewide credential is that it lets you work across jurisdictions that recognize it instead of collecting a separate local license in each one.

Second, OSEC does not issue permits or inspect work. Permits still come from the city or county, and any bond a local government requires stays posted with that local government. You give OSEC proof that the bond exists when you apply.

Each electrical contracting firm needs at least one statewide licensed contractor employed at a supervisory level, and one licensee can represent only one firm at a time. You can reach OSEC at OSEC@pr.mo.gov or 573-522-3280.

License types and which exam each needs

OSEC issues one license: the statewide electrical contractor license. The state rule on approved exams says accepted tests can cover the electrical contractor, master electrician and journeyman electrician levels, and NASCLA lists both the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician and Journeyman Electrician exams for Missouri.

For the contractor license, the exam you plan for is the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors (Master/Unlimited). Missouri's PSI bulletin gives the details for it, and that is what our Missouri Master Electrical Contractor prep is built around.

If you already hold a local license, look at the other routes before you book a test. Missouri accepts a verified local or state license that itself required a division-approved exam as proof of passing. The statute also has a path for holders of a Missouri city or county electrical contractor or master electrician license, based on a standardized NEC exam given by an independent testing agency plus hours or years of local licensure. Apprentice, journeyman and residential licenses for day-to-day work remain local matters, so check with your city or county.

How to get a Missouri electrical contractor license, step by step

The order matters in Missouri, because OSEC approves you before PSI will let you schedule. Here is the usual sequence, based on the PSI bulletin for Missouri and the state rules.

  1. Confirm which experience or education path you qualify under (see the requirements section below).
  2. Get the application from OSEC. The office now uses the MOPRO online system, which opened for registration on January 14, 2025, for applications, renewals and payments.
  3. Gather your Certificate of Insurance showing at least $500,000 in liability coverage, proof of any city or county bonds, and documentation of your hours or degree.
  4. Submit the application. OSEC alone decides eligibility and returns incomplete files with the missing items listed.
  5. Once approved, you get payment and scheduling instructions. Pay the published $125 exam fee and book a seat with PSI online or by phone.
  6. Take and pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor (Master/Unlimited) exam at a PSI center in Missouri or Kansas.
  7. Finish any remaining items OSEC asks for. Applications not completed within 24 months of first submission become invalid.

The Missouri NASCLA electrical exam: format and test day

According to Missouri's PSI candidate bulletin, the Electrical Contractor exam has 100 scored questions, and you need 75 correct to pass. You get 270 minutes. The exam also carries 10 unscored pretest questions, and the time for those is already built into the allowance.

It is open book. The approved references are the NEC (2020 or 2023 edition), 29 CFR 1926, 29 CFR 1910, the PMBOK Guide 7th edition, ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022, the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management Basic 14th edition, NFPA 70E 2024, Ugly's Electrical References 2023, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. PSI hands out a diagram and blueprint packet at the center. Two key word indexes (Ferm's Fast Finder and Tom Henry's) are optional extras. Code questions come only from the listed code editions.

The bulletin is not consistent on calculators. One section mentions a silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator, while another says you use an on-screen calculator and cannot bring your own. Ask PSI before test day.

Your OSEC eligibility lasts 2 years. If you fail, you can retest as often as you like inside that window, paying the fee each time. To keep your fee when you change plans, cancel or reschedule at least 2 days ahead through PSI's website or phone line, and bring one current government photo ID with a signature.

The business and law portion

Missouri does not give a separate state business and law exam for the statewide electrical license, and we did not find a Missouri edition of NASCLA's business and law guide in the NASCLA store. Instead, business and project management questions are part of the NASCLA electrical contractor exam itself.

That is why two of the nine approved references are management books: the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management (Basic 14th edition) and the PMBOK Guide. Expect questions on contracts, estimating, scheduling, risk, records and running a contracting company alongside the code work. Many electricians spend all their study time in the NEC and lose easy points here, so give these books their own practice sessions.

Missouri electrical contractor license requirements: hours, insurance and bonds

Section 324.920 and rule 20 CSR 2117-2.010 set out what you need. Insurance comes first: proof of liability coverage of at least $500,000, shown on a Certificate of Insurance issued by an insurance company authorized in Missouri. The rule specifically says a certificate issued by an agent will not be accepted. You also need proof that any bond required by a political subdivision where you work has been posted.

Then you need a passing score on a division-approved exam and one of these experience or education paths:

  • 12,000 verifiable practical hours installing equipment and associated wiring.
  • 10,000 verifiable practical hours plus a journeyman certificate from a U.S. Department of Labor approved electrical apprenticeship.
  • 8,000 verifiable practical hours plus an associate's degree from a state-accredited program.
  • 4,000 verifiable practical hours supervising installation work plus a qualifying four-year electrical engineering degree.
  • A Missouri city or county electrical contractor or master electrician license that was current and active for 6 of the previous 8 years, verified by the issuing body.

Costs and fees

The fees below are the published figures in Missouri rule 20 CSR 2117-1.070 and the PSI bulletin. They can change, so confirm them with OSEC before you pay. State fees are nonrefundable.

Licensing costs and exam fees are separate from what you spend on books and courses. Missouri also offers fee waivers for military families and low-income applicants, and veterans may be able to get exam fees reimbursed; OSEC's site has the details.

  • License application: $200
  • Triennial renewal: $200 (inactive renewal $100)
  • Reactivation: $100; reinstatement: $250
  • NASCLA exam through PSI: $125, valid for one year from payment

Reciprocity and licenses from other places

Missouri does not run a formal state-to-state reciprocity program for this license. The 2017 statute allowed for it, and OSEC's FAQ invites contractors to email the office with the name of a state they want considered.

What the rules do allow is using a verified license from another state, or from a city in another state, as evidence toward the exam and hours requirements, as long as that license required a division-approved exam and the hours can be documented. If you passed the NASCLA electrical contractor exam elsewhere, your results sit in NASCLA's National Examination Database at ned.nascla.org, and you can send a transcript to a state agency from there. Ask OSEC how it will treat your file before you book a new exam.

After you pass: renewal and keeping your license

Once OSEC issues your license, renewals run on a three-year cycle. Under rule 20 CSR 2117-2.030, the first renewal was due September 30, 2020, and renewals are due by September 30 every third year after that. OSEC mails a reminder, but not receiving one does not excuse a late renewal.

A license that is not renewed on time becomes inactive and lapsed. If it stays unrenewed for two years, it is void, which means starting over. You must report changes to your legal name, mailing address or phone number in writing within thirty days. The statute allows OSEC to adopt continuing education by rule, but we did not find a published requirement, so ask the office when you renew. Keep your local licenses and registrations current too, since cities and counties still control permits.

Missouri NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Is a statewide electrical contractor license required in Missouri?

No. State law makes it optional. If you work only where you already hold the local license, or where no license is required, you can operate without it. Contractors who work in many jurisdictions use it to avoid stacking local licenses.

What exam does Missouri use for the statewide electrical contractor license?

Missouri uses the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors (Master/Unlimited), given by PSI. You must be approved by OSEC before PSI will schedule you.

How many questions are on the Missouri NASCLA electrical contractor exam?

Missouri's PSI bulletin lists 100 scored questions plus 10 unscored pretest items, with 270 minutes to finish. You need 75 correct answers to pass.

Is the Missouri electrical contractor exam open book?

Yes. You may bring the approved references, including the NEC, OSHA 1926 and 1910, NFPA 70E, Ugly's, the PMBOK Guide, ASHRAE 90.1 and the NASCLA Contractors Guide. A blueprint packet is provided at the test center.

How many hours of experience do I need for a Missouri electrical contractor license?

The base path is 12,000 verifiable practical hours. That drops to 10,000 with a DOL-approved apprenticeship journeyman certificate, 8,000 with an accredited associate's degree, or 4,000 supervisory hours with a four-year electrical engineering degree.

What insurance does Missouri require for electrical contractors?

You need liability insurance of at least $500,000, shown on a Certificate of Insurance issued by an insurer authorized in Missouri. A certificate issued by an agent is not accepted. You also need proof of any bonds that local governments require.

How much does the Missouri statewide electrical license cost?

The published application fee is $200, and renewal is $200 every three years. The NASCLA exam fee through PSI is $125. Confirm current fees with OSEC before you apply.

Does a Missouri statewide license replace city permits?

No. OSEC does not issue permits or inspect work. You still pull permits from the city or county and keep any local bonds posted there.

Can I retake the exam if I fail?

Yes. You can retest as many times as you need during your 2-year eligibility period. Each attempt requires paying the exam fee again.
