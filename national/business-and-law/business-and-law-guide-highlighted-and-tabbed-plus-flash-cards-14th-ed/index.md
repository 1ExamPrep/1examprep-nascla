## Open Book Test

The **NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test**. The NASCLA Basic 14th Edition is one of the publications identified for use with this examination.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved references while answering examination questions. However, the ability to locate information efficiently remains an important preparation skill.

The highlighted and tabbed NASCLA guide helps candidates become familiar with the publication's organization and practice finding important material.

**Important Examination Reference Rules:**

- Only approved reference publications may be used during the examination.
- Designated references may be highlighted and underlined.
- Permanent indexing tabs are permitted when they comply with examination requirements.
- Temporary tabs and removable sticky notes are prohibited.
- Reference materials must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain handwritten notes.
- Loose papers and unauthorized additional materials are not permitted.
- Testing center personnel determine whether reference materials meet examination requirements.

**Important Tab Acceptance Notice**

The testing facility proctor is the deciding factor on whether or not tabs will be accepted and allowed into the exam. If the tabs do not adhere properly, they will not be accepted.

**Flash Cards:** The included flash cards are supplemental preparation materials and are not approved examination-room references.

The open-book designation above applies to the NASCLA Accredited Commercial General Building Contractor examination. Other examination programs have their own rules concerning reference materials and testing procedures.

## Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Basic 14th Edition is used by candidates pursuing contractor licensing through different examination programs. The licensing process depends on the jurisdiction and contractor classification.

**Step 1: Identify Your Contractor Classification**

Determine the professional classification associated with the construction services you intend to perform. These may include commercial general building, electrical, residential electrical, HVAC, plumbing, or other regulated contracting activities.

**Step 2: Review Licensing Qualifications**

Review the experience, education, qualifying-party, business documentation, insurance, and financial responsibilities established by the applicable licensing authority.

**Step 3: Identify Required Examinations**

Determine whether your selected classification requires a NASCLA Accredited Examination, a business and law examination, a technical trade examination, or a combination of examinations.

**Step 4: Prepare Your Examination References**

Study the NASCLA Basic 14th Edition for applicable business and management subjects. Use the highlighted material and tabs to practice locating information, and incorporate flash-card review into your preparation schedule.

**Step 5: Complete Examination Registration**

Follow the application and scheduling procedures established by the licensing agency or testing provider responsible for your examination.

**Step 6: Complete Your Examination**

Attend your scheduled examination with the required identification and permitted reference materials. Follow the examination provider's testing procedures.

**Step 7: Complete the Licensing Process**

After satisfying examination requirements, submit the necessary licensing documents and complete the approval process established by the applicable authority.

Passing a NASCLA Accredited Examination does not automatically grant a contractor license. Individual jurisdictions maintain their own licensing requirements.
