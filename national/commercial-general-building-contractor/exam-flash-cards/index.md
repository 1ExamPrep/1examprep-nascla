## Exam Details

The official examination associated with this study product is the **NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors**. NASCLA developed the examination as a standardized trade examination that can be accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies.

The examination contains **115 scored questions**. Candidates must answer **81 questions correctly** to achieve the required passing score. The examination provides **330 minutes**, or 5½ hours, of testing time.

In addition to the scored questions, candidates are administered **10 non-scored pretest questions**. These experimental questions are used in examination development and do not count toward the final score. Candidates are not identified which questions are scored and which are pretest questions.

The examination is administered by **PSI Services LLC** and is delivered by computer. Candidates must first receive approval through NASCLA before scheduling the examination with PSI.

The current examination content outline includes:

- **General Requirements:** 25 questions
- **Site Construction:** 15 questions
- **Concrete:** 6 questions
- **Masonry:** 4 questions
- **Metals:** 6 questions
- **Wood:** 5 questions
- **Thermal and Moisture Protection:** 5 questions
- **Doors, Windows, and Glazing:** 4 questions
- **Finishes:** 5 questions
- **Mechanical and Plumbing Systems:** 6 questions
- **Electrical Systems:** 3 questions
- **Procurement and Contracting Requirements:** 31 questions

Procurement and Contracting Requirements represents the largest single portion of the examination, while General Requirements and Site Construction also account for significant numbers of questions. Candidates should build a balanced study plan rather than concentrating only on hands-on construction subjects.

The examination also involves plans, diagrams, and construction documentation. A physical diagram and blueprint packet is provided at the testing center for use during the examination.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **open-book test**. Candidates may use the approved references identified in the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin.

Open book does not mean unrestricted materials are allowed. Only approved reference materials may be used. The references are not supplied at the examination center, so candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted books they plan to use.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. They must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain written-in notes or additional loose or attached papers.

References may be tabbed or indexed with **permanent tabs only**. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are prohibited and must be removed before the examination begins.

A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator that meets PSI testing requirements may also be used.

Because there are numerous approved books, book navigation should be a major part of NASCLA exam preparation. Learn the index, table of contents, chapters, tables, and major subject areas of the references you expect to use most often. Practice locating information under time pressure instead of waiting until examination day to become familiar with the books.

## Licensing Steps

The NASCLA examination is designed to streamline the trade-examination portion of contractor licensing in participating jurisdictions. It is not itself a contractor license. Candidates must still meet the licensing requirements of the state or jurisdiction where they intend to become licensed.

1. **Determine whether the licensing jurisdiction accepts the NASCLA examination.** Participating agencies may accept NASCLA examination results in place of a state-specific trade examination for an applicable contractor classification.
2. **Apply with NASCLA for examination approval.** Candidates must be pre-approved before they can schedule the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination.
3. **Receive eligibility to test.** Once NASCLA approves the application, candidate eligibility information is provided to PSI.
4. **Schedule the examination with PSI.** Approved candidates can select an available examination date and testing location through PSI.
5. **Prepare the approved reference books.** Organize permitted references with highlighting, underlining, and permanent tabs in accordance with the examination rules.
6. **Take and pass the NASCLA examination.** Candidates must earn at least 81 correct answers out of 115 scored questions.
7. **Access the NASCLA National Examination Database.** Passing examination results are entered into the NASCLA National Examination Database, commonly known as NED.
8. **Send an examination transcript to the applicable licensing agency.** Candidates can electronically request that their NASCLA transcript be sent to participating agencies when applying for licensure.
9. **Complete the jurisdiction’s contractor license application.** The applicant must satisfy the additional requirements imposed by that particular licensing authority.
10. **Complete additional examinations when required.** A jurisdiction may require a separate business and law examination or other state-specific testing in addition to accepting the NASCLA trade examination.

NASCLA examination eligibility currently lasts **one year from the application approval date**. An eligible candidate may sit for the examination a maximum of three times during that one-year eligibility period. Candidates who do not pass within those attempts may reapply after the eligibility period expires.

## State Requirements

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is accepted by participating state and territorial licensing agencies, but acceptance of the examination does not create a national contractor license.

After passing the NASCLA examination, candidates must apply directly to the licensing authority where they want to become licensed. Each participating agency establishes its own contractor classifications, experience requirements, financial requirements, insurance requirements, application procedures, business and law testing requirements, fees, and other licensing standards.

Participating jurisdictions include contractor licensing agencies in Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, among others represented through the current NASCLA Commercial Exam program.

Some agencies administer the NASCLA examination as an option for a contractor classification, while others accept a passing NASCLA score obtained through another participating agency. The exact license classification available to a candidate depends on the rules of the licensing jurisdiction.

Passing the NASCLA exam can therefore reduce duplicate trade testing for contractors pursuing licenses in multiple participating jurisdictions, but applicants still need to complete each jurisdiction’s separate licensing process.

## Test Information and Study Materials

With 115 scored questions, 10 additional pretest questions, a 330-minute testing period, and an extensive reference library, the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam rewards organized preparation.

Use the **500 flash cards** in smaller sections instead of trying to work through the entire deck at once. Study a manageable group, test yourself without immediately checking the answer, and separate missed cards for additional review. Continue mixing previously learned cards into later sessions to reinforce long-term recall.

Pay particular attention to Procurement and Contracting Requirements, General Requirements, and Site Construction because these three areas account for a substantial portion of the scored examination. At the same time, continue reviewing concrete, masonry, metals, wood, roofing and moisture protection, finishes, mechanical systems, plumbing, and electrical systems.

Practice using your approved reference books while studying. When you miss a flash card, locate that subject in the relevant reference. This helps you reinforce the concept while learning where information is located.

Plan reading and blueprint interpretation should also be included in your study routine. Because a diagram and blueprint packet is provided during the exam, candidates should be comfortable reading construction documents and relating them to code, estimating, and construction questions.

Time management matters as well. With a large number of questions and references, avoid spending excessive time searching one book for one difficult answer. Practice identifying the subject first, selecting the most likely reference, and navigating directly to the applicable chapter, table, index entry, or code section.
