Exam Details

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrical exam evaluates knowledge associated with journeyman-level electrical work. Preparation should include electrical installation requirements, workplace safety, electrical theory, calculations, wiring methods, wiring protection, equipment, fire alarm systems, plans, specifications, testing, and troubleshooting.

A major part of preparation involves the National Electrical Code. Candidates should understand how to navigate the code and how general requirements interact with rules for specific equipment, occupancies, systems, and installation conditions.

Common areas of electrical code study include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, transformers, equipment installation, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency systems, and communication systems.

Electrical safety preparation should include both OSHA regulations and NFPA 70E. Candidates should be able to distinguish between construction-industry requirements, general-industry requirements, and electrical safety-related work practices.

Electrical theory remains important even though the exam is open book. Candidates should review voltage, current, resistance, power, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, formulas, and common electrical calculations.

Testing and troubleshooting preparation should focus on systematic problem-solving. Candidates may need to recognize likely electrical faults, select appropriate testing methods, interpret circuit behavior, and identify safe corrective actions.

Plans and specifications may require candidates to interpret electrical symbols, schedules, notes, equipment information, circuit designations, conductor details, and written project requirements. Study should include both technical knowledge and the ability to locate supporting information in the permitted references.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrical exam is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following all current examination rules governing books, tabs, highlighting, calculators, identification, and testing procedures.

An open-book format does not mean candidates can depend on searching every answer from the beginning. Time can be lost quickly when a student does not know which book applies, where the subject is located, or how a requirement should be interpreted.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books are intended to support more efficient navigation. Highlighting helps useful provisions, tables, definitions, and safety requirements stand out. Permanent tabs make it easier to move to major sections without repeatedly searching through the full publication.

Candidates should use the books throughout the entire six-month course-access period. Regular use helps students learn the layout of each publication, understand the purpose of each reference, and develop a repeatable method for locating answers.

A practical open-book process is to identify the subject of the question, select the most likely reference, use the index or tabs to locate the section, read the complete requirement, check for exceptions or table notes, and confirm the answer before moving forward.

Testing rules can change, and candidates are responsible for reviewing the current candidate information before exam day. All references should remain compliant with the testing provider’s requirements.

State Requirements

Journeyman electrician licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction. A state or local agency may establish its own experience standards, education requirements, application procedures, license classifications, fees, renewal rules, and continuing education obligations.

An agency may accept the NASCLA exam while still requiring the applicant to complete additional licensing steps. These requirements may include documenting electrical work experience, providing employer verification, submitting apprenticeship records, completing an application, and paying jurisdiction-specific fees.

Some locations regulate journeyman electricians through a statewide agency. Others use city, county, or municipal licensing systems. Candidates should identify the exact jurisdiction and license classification before registering for the exam.

A passing NASCLA exam result should not be treated as a national electrician license. The receiving licensing authority reviews the complete application and determines whether the candidate satisfies all applicable requirements.
