## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is designed to evaluate knowledge associated with the installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair of electrical systems in accordance with applicable codes, standards, and accepted trade practices.

The examination covers a range of journeyman-level electrical subjects. These areas may include interpreting plans and specifications, basic electrical safety, electrical theory and principles, testing and troubleshooting, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

A large portion of exam preparation involves learning how to apply the National Electrical Code. Candidates should be comfortable navigating NEC articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and requirements for specific types of equipment and installations. Questions may require candidates to connect general rules with more specific requirements found elsewhere in the code.

Safety preparation is also important. The included OSHA and NFPA 70E references address different aspects of occupational and electrical safety. Candidates should understand which reference is most appropriate for a question involving construction activities, general industry, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, safe work practices, or workplace responsibilities.

Electrical theory preparation may involve calculations and the practical application of concepts such as voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, transformers, motors, and alternating-current systems. Understanding the principle behind a formula can make it easier to recognize the correct calculation method during an examination.

Additional subject areas may require candidates to interpret drawings, identify electrical symbols, use schedules and notes, evaluate system conditions, select appropriate testing methods, and troubleshoot common electrical problems. A balanced preparation plan should address both code navigation and the underlying trade knowledge expected of a journeyman electrician.

## State Requirements

Electrical licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction. A state, county, city, or other regulatory authority may establish its own experience standards, education requirements, apprenticeship rules, application procedures, fees, and license classifications.

A jurisdiction that accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination may use the passing result to satisfy its trade-testing requirement. The applicant may still need to complete additional steps before receiving a license. These steps can include documenting qualifying work experience, providing employer verification, completing an application, paying licensing fees, or meeting other regulatory requirements.

Some jurisdictions regulate journeyman electricians at the state level, while others rely on local licensing authorities. The permitted scope of work, supervision requirements, renewal process, continuing education rules, and license portability may differ depending on the location.

Candidates should identify the exact license and jurisdiction they intend to pursue before registering for an examination. This helps ensure that the selected NASCLA examination is accepted for the intended credential and that all eligibility requirements can be completed.

Passing a NASCLA examination should not be interpreted as a national electrician license. Licensing authority remains with the participating state or local agency, and each agency determines whether an applicant meets its complete licensing standards.

## Reference Books

- **National Electrical Code, 2023**
The 2023 National Electrical Code contains electrical installation requirements covering wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems. It is a central reference for NEC-based exam preparation.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations**
This OSHA publication addresses federal workplace safety requirements for construction activities. It supports study of construction hazards, employee protection, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, and safe jobsite practices.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910 - OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards**
This reference contains federal occupational safety and health standards for general industry. It supports preparation involving workplace conditions, electrical hazards, equipment, employee protection, and employer safety responsibilities.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety-related work practices intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards. Topics include risk assessment, approach boundaries, shock protection, arc-flash protection, personal protective equipment, and energized work considerations.
- **Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023**
This compact electrical field reference contains commonly used formulas, tables, conversions, conductor information, motor information, and practical electrical data. This included book will not be highlighted or tabbed.
- **NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2022 Edition**
NFPA 72 contains requirements for fire alarm systems, signaling systems, emergency communications, initiating devices, notification appliances, inspection, testing, and related system functions.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
This reference connects electrical theory with practical code applications. It supports study involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, formulas, calculations, and the principles used to evaluate electrical systems.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Begin exam preparation by reviewing the official content outline and organizing study time by subject. Code-heavy areas should receive substantial attention, but safety, electrical theory, troubleshooting, plans, specifications, special installations, and communication systems should remain part of the study plan.

For National Electrical Code preparation, practice using both the index and table of contents. Learn how definitions affect code interpretation and how general requirements interact with rules for specific equipment, occupancies, and conditions. Pay close attention to exceptions, table notes, and cross-references.

For wiring and protection, review conductors, branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and equipment protection. For wiring methods and materials, study raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, conductor installation, support requirements, fill calculations, and permitted uses.

Safety study should include both OSHA volumes and NFPA 70E. Candidates should learn which publication applies to a particular situation and practice identifying requirements related to workplace hazards, protective equipment, electrical safety-related work practices, construction activities, and general industry.

Electrical theory study should include formulas and practical problem-solving. Work through examples involving Ohm’s law, power, series circuits, parallel circuits, transformers, motors, and alternating-current principles. Practice selecting the correct formula before entering values into a calculator.

Timed study sessions can reveal where additional practice is needed. Arrange the books within easy reach, answer mixed questions, and record how long it takes to locate each answer. Review whether missed questions resulted from limited subject knowledge, slow navigation, calculation errors, or incorrect interpretation of a code provision.

Repeated use of the highlighted and tabbed books can build familiarity with the package. Over time, candidates can develop a consistent process: identify the topic, select the appropriate reference, use the index or tab system, read the full requirement, check for exceptions, and confirm the final answer.
