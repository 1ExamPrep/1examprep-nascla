## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. It is intended for individuals who have developed the knowledge and skills needed to perform electrical installation, maintenance, and repair work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

The examination evaluates journeyman-level electrical knowledge across multiple trade areas. Preparation may involve interpreting electrical plans and specifications, applying basic electrical safety practices, solving theory questions, selecting testing procedures, troubleshooting electrical systems, and using code requirements for different types of installations.

National Electrical Code preparation is a major part of studying for a journeyman electrician examination. Candidates should understand how the NEC is organized and how to use its index, table of contents, articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references.

Code questions may involve branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, equipment, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency systems, and communication systems. Candidates should learn how general requirements interact with rules that apply to a particular installation or piece of equipment.

Safety preparation may involve both OSHA regulations and NFPA 70E. Candidates should be able to distinguish between construction-industry requirements, general-industry requirements, and electrical safety-related work practices. Study areas may include hazard recognition, employee protection, personal protective equipment, safe work procedures, electrical shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, and workplace responsibilities.

Electrical theory remains essential even in an open-book exam. Candidates may need to work with voltage, current, resistance, power, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, and common electrical formulas. Understanding why a formula applies is just as important as entering numbers into a calculator.

Plans and specifications may require candidates to interpret symbols, schedules, notes, circuit information, equipment locations, conductor information, and written project requirements. Troubleshooting topics may require the candidate to evaluate circuit behavior, identify likely faults, select testing equipment, and apply a logical diagnostic process.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for following the current examination rules governing permitted references, markings, tabs, calculators, identification, and testing procedures.

Open-book testing does not mean that every answer can be found quickly without preparation. Candidates must recognize the subject, choose the correct book, locate the applicable section, read the requirement carefully, and determine how it applies to the question. A candidate who is unfamiliar with the references may lose significant time searching through the wrong publication or section.

The online exam prep course supports a study method centered on understanding and navigation. Candidates can strengthen their ability to connect electrical questions with the appropriate code article, safety regulation, theory concept, table, formula, or fire alarm requirement.

Reference navigation should become part of regular study. Candidates should practice using the index and table of contents, locating major articles, reading definitions, identifying exceptions, checking table notes, and following cross-references. The goal is to build a repeatable process for finding and confirming information under timed conditions.

Each book serves a different purpose. The National Electrical Code addresses installation requirements. OSHA publications address federal workplace safety standards. NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety-related work practices. NFPA 72 addresses fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly’s Electrical References provides compact field information, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications supports the principles and calculations behind electrical work.

Candidates should review the current candidate information before test day. Examination policies may regulate highlighting, underlining, permanent tabs, loose papers, handwritten notes, removable tabs, inserts, and other modifications to reference materials.

## State Requirements

The NASCLA Accredited Examination Program is not a national electrician license. It provides an examination result that may be used in place of a state-specific trade examination by participating regulatory agencies.

Journeyman electrician requirements vary by jurisdiction. A licensing authority may require documented work experience, completion of an apprenticeship program, technical education, employer affidavits, examination approval, application fees, identification documents, or other supporting information.

Some states regulate journeyman electricians through a statewide licensing agency. Other locations may use county, city, or municipal licensing systems. The scope of work, supervision rules, renewal requirements, continuing education obligations, and license classifications may differ.

Candidates should confirm the requirements for the exact jurisdiction in which they intend to work. An agency may accept the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination while still requiring the applicant to complete additional state or local steps.

A passing examination result demonstrates that the candidate met the testing standard. The receiving licensing agency remains responsible for reviewing the full application and determining whether the applicant qualifies for a license.

## Reference Books

- **National Electrical Code, 2023**
The 2023 National Electrical Code contains requirements for electrical installations, including wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations**
This OSHA reference addresses federal workplace safety requirements for construction activities. It supports study involving construction hazards, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, employee protection, and safe work practices.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910 - OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards**
This publication contains federal occupational safety and health standards for general industry. It supports preparation related to workplace conditions, electrical hazards, equipment, employee protection, and employer responsibilities.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety-related work practices, including hazard assessment, shock protection, arc-flash protection, approach boundaries, personal protective equipment, and energized work considerations.
- **Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023**
Ugly’s Electrical References is a compact field resource containing electrical formulas, tables, conversions, conductor information, motor data, and practical reference material.
- **NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2022 Edition**
NFPA 72 provides requirements for fire alarm systems, signaling systems, initiating devices, notification appliances, emergency communications, inspection, testing, and related system functions.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
This publication connects electrical theory with practical NEC applications. It supports study involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, formulas, calculations, and electrical problem-solving.

The books listed above are reference materials associated with the examination and course study. They are not stated as included with this online exam prep course. Candidates should obtain the required publications separately and use the editions specified for their examination.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine technical review, reference navigation, calculation practice, and timed problem-solving. Reading alone may not be enough to build the speed and judgment needed for an open-book electrical examination.

Begin by learning the organization of each publication. Identify the purpose of the book, the types of questions it can answer, and the fastest way to locate information. Practice selecting the appropriate reference before searching for the answer.

For NEC preparation, study definitions, branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, equipment, motors, transformers, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency systems, and communication systems.

For theory preparation, work through electrical formulas and calculations. Review Ohm’s law, power, resistance, series circuits, parallel circuits, voltage relationships, current relationships, transformers, motors, and common trade calculations. Check units carefully and practice recognizing the information provided in each problem.

For safety preparation, compare OSHA construction standards, OSHA general industry standards, and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand how the references differ and which publication applies to a particular workplace or electrical safety question.

Timed practice can help reveal whether a candidate is struggling with technical knowledge, book navigation, code interpretation, or calculation accuracy. When reviewing an incorrect answer, determine why it was missed and return to the applicable reference or concept.

A useful open-book process is to identify the subject, select the most likely reference, locate the section, read the complete requirement, review exceptions and notes, and then confirm the answer. Practicing this process repeatedly can improve efficiency and confidence.
