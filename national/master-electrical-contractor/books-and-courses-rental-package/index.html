Exam Details

The NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical exam is intended for experienced electrical professionals preparing for contractor-level or master-level licensing in participating jurisdictions. The exam addresses technical electrical knowledge as well as the management, safety, business, and energy-related responsibilities associated with electrical contracting.

Preparation should include general electrical code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, electrical theory, project management, workplace safety, contractor business practices, and energy-efficiency standards.

The 2023 National Electrical Code is a central reference for the exam. Candidates should be comfortable navigating definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Major preparation areas may include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, transformers, luminaires, appliances, and specialized electrical installations.

Project design and management are also important at the contractor level. Candidates should understand planning, scheduling, project delivery, stakeholder coordination, communication, cost awareness, procurement, quality, risk, contracts, and other responsibilities involved in managing electrical work.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction regulations, OSHA general-industry standards, and NFPA 70E. Candidates should learn which publication applies to a particular workplace, task, hazard, or electrical safety practice.

Energy-standard preparation should include the organization and use of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1. Candidates may need to locate requirements involving electrical power, lighting systems, controls, and energy-efficient building systems.

Electrical theory remains essential even in an open-book exam. Candidates should review voltage, current, resistance, power, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, and commonly used electrical formulas. Understanding the principle behind a calculation makes it easier to choose the correct method and confirm whether the final result is reasonable.

Contractor-level candidates should also prepare to interpret plans, schedules, symbols, equipment information, conductor details, notes, and project specifications. Open-book success depends on combining trade knowledge with accurate reference use.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical exam is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references and following all current testing rules governing book condition, highlighting, tabs, calculators, identification, security, and prohibited materials.

An open-book format does not mean candidates can depend on slowly searching for every answer. The exam covers several subject areas and uses multiple references. Candidates must be able to recognize which book applies and locate the necessary information efficiently.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books are prepared to support this process. Highlighting helps useful code language, tables, definitions, formulas, safety provisions, and contractor topics stand out. Permanent tabs help candidates reach major sections more quickly.

Candidates should practice using the books exactly as they plan to use them during the exam. Begin by learning the purpose of each publication, reviewing its table of contents and index, and identifying the types of questions it can answer.

A practical open-book method is to identify the topic, select the most likely reference, use the index or permanent tabs to locate the section, read the complete provision, check related definitions and exceptions, perform any required calculation, and confirm the answer.

Testing rules may restrict handwritten notes, loose papers, removable tabs, inserted material, or other modifications. Candidates should review the current exam bulletin and testing-provider instructions before test day.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical exam does not automatically issue an electrical contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician license. The exam may satisfy the trade-testing requirement for a participating jurisdiction, but candidates must still complete the licensing process established by the applicable regulatory agency.

  1. Select the licensing jurisdiction. Identify the state or regulatory agency where you intend to apply for an electrical contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician credential.
  2. Confirm exam acceptance. Verify that the licensing authority accepts the NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical examination for the exact classification you are pursuing.
  3. Review eligibility requirements. Determine whether the jurisdiction requires documented electrical experience, supervisory experience, education, business registration, insurance, financial information, or a qualifying individual.
  4. Complete the application process. Submit the forms and supporting documentation required for examination approval or licensing eligibility.

  7. Schedule and take the exam. Follow all current registration, identification, calculator, reference-book, security, and testing-center requirements.
  8. Submit the passing result. Complete the required process for transmitting or documenting your exam result with the accepting licensing agency.
  9. Complete the licensing application. Submit any remaining forms, fees, experience records, financial documents, insurance information, or business credentials.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction. A state or local agency may accept the NASCLA exam while still requiring applicants to meet separate experience, education, financial, insurance, business, and administrative standards.

Some jurisdictions issue individual master electrician or unlimited electrician licenses. Others license electrical contracting businesses and require a designated qualifying individual to pass the trade exam. Candidates should confirm the exact credential and scope of work before registering.

A separate business and law exam may be required by the licensing authority. The inclusion of contractor business and project-management content in this package does not automatically replace a state-specific business-law requirement.

Applicants may also need to provide employer verification, work-history documentation, apprenticeship records, financial statements, business-entity information, insurance certificates, background information, or other supporting materials.

The NASCLA exam should not be treated as a national electrical contractor license. Each jurisdiction independently reviews the complete application and determines whether the candidate qualifies for licensing.
