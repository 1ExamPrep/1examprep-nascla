## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is intended for master electricians and unlimited electricians who conduct business involving the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

The examination contains 100 scored questions. Candidates must answer at least 75 questions correctly to meet the minimum passing score. The examination allows 270 minutes, providing four hours and thirty minutes to complete the scored questions and any additional experimental items.

Ten non-scored experimental questions are administered in addition to the scored examination. These questions are used in the development of future licensing examinations and do not count toward the candidate’s final result.

The scored examination is divided among the following subject areas:

- **Project Design and Management:** 8 questions
- **Safety:** 9 questions
- **Electrical Theory and Principles:** 11 questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 17 questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 17 questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 13 questions
- **Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, and Special Conditions:** 8 questions
- **Communication Systems:** 1 question

General code requirements, wiring and protection, and wiring methods and materials account for a substantial portion of the examination. Candidates should devote significant study time to NEC organization, conductor requirements, branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, and installation rules.

Contractor-level preparation should also address project design and management. Candidates may need to apply concepts involving project planning, scheduling, communication, procurement, contracts, quality, cost, risk, and coordination. The included project-management and business-law references support these areas.

A physical diagram or blueprint packet is provided at the testing center. Candidates should be prepared to interpret plans, specifications, schedules, symbols, equipment information, and project requirements. A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator may be used during the examination.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their approved references to the testing center because the books are not provided at the examination site.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs may be used to identify chapters, articles, tables, and other important sections. Temporary tabs, including removable notes and Post-it-style products, are not allowed. Books must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain handwritten notes, loose papers, or attached additional sheets.

The open-book format rewards candidates who understand both the subject matter and the organization of the references. Searching every question from the beginning of a book is not an effective strategy for a timed contractor examination. Candidates should know which publication is most likely to contain the answer and how to reach the relevant section quickly.

Begin by learning the table of contents, index, chapter arrangement, article structure, definitions, major tables, and appendices in each book. Practice identifying whether a question relates to the NEC, OSHA construction regulations, OSHA general-industry standards, NFPA 70E, project management, energy requirements, business and law, field calculations, or electrical theory.

Permanent tabs should support an established navigation system. They are most useful when candidates have already practiced with the books and understand why each section matters. During study sessions, use the references exactly as they will be used on exam day and practice moving between multiple books without electronic search tools.

## Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue an electrical contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician license. The examination may satisfy the trade-testing requirement for participating regulatory agencies, but every applicant must complete the licensing process required by the state or jurisdiction where the credential will be used.

1. **Select the licensing jurisdiction.** Identify the state or regulatory agency where you intend to apply for an electrical contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician license.
2. **Confirm examination acceptance.** Verify that the jurisdiction accepts the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors for the desired classification.
3. **Review eligibility requirements.** Determine whether the agency requires documented electrical experience, supervisory experience, education, financial information, insurance, business registration, or other qualifications.
4. **Apply for NASCLA examination eligibility.** Complete the current application process through the NASCLA National Examination Database and submit the required application materials.
5. **Receive examination approval.** After approval, follow the instructions provided for scheduling the examination through the testing provider.
6. **Prepare with the approved books.** Study the examination outline and become familiar with all nine references in this package.
7. **Schedule and take the examination.** Follow the testing provider’s requirements for identification, calculators, reference books, arrival time, security, and prohibited materials.
8. **Access the examination record.** After a passing result is processed, create or access the appropriate account in the NASCLA National Examination Database.
9. **Send the transcript.** Purchase and electronically transmit the examination transcript to a participating state agency.
10. **Complete the state licensing application.** Submit all additional forms, fees, business documents, experience records, and supporting materials required by the jurisdiction.

## State Requirements

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is accepted by participating regulatory agencies as an alternative to certain state-specific trade examinations. Acceptance does not eliminate the remaining requirements for an electrical contractor or master electrician license.

Each state determines the classifications for which it accepts the examination. A jurisdiction may require applicants to document electrical work experience, supervisory experience, education, financial responsibility, insurance coverage, business registration, or the designation of a qualifying individual.

A separate state business and law examination may also be required. The business and project-management material included in the NASCLA electrical exam does not automatically replace a jurisdiction’s separate business-law testing requirement.

Some agencies issue individual master electrician credentials, while others regulate electrical contracting businesses and require a qualified individual to pass the trade examination. License names, scopes of work, renewal rules, continuing education requirements, and financial standards vary by jurisdiction.

After passing the examination, candidates can use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send their results electronically to participating agencies. The receiving agency evaluates the complete application and determines whether the applicant satisfies its licensing requirements.

## Reference Books

- **National Electrical Code, 2023**
The 2023 National Electrical Code provides the principal installation requirements for electrical systems. It covers wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations**
This OSHA reference addresses federal safety requirements for construction work. It supports preparation involving electrical hazards, jobsite conditions, employee protection, personal protective equipment, equipment use, and safe construction practices.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910 - OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards**
This publication contains federal occupational safety and health standards for general industry. It addresses workplace hazards, electrical safety, employee protection, equipment, training, and employer responsibilities.
- **A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge, 2021, 7th Edition**
This project-management reference presents principles and performance areas that support planning, delivery, measurement, uncertainty management, stakeholder coordination, teamwork, and project value.
- **ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1, 2022 Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings - I-P Edition**
This standard provides energy-efficiency requirements for applicable sites and buildings. It includes provisions affecting electrical power, lighting systems, equipment, controls, and energy-conscious building design.
- **NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition**
This guide supports contractor-level preparation involving business planning, project management, contracts, risk management, safety, estimating, scheduling, financial administration, and other responsibilities associated with operating a contracting business.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety-related work practices, including risk assessment, shock protection, arc-flash protection, approach boundaries, personal protective equipment, energized work, and safe maintenance practices.
- **Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023**
This compact field reference contains commonly used electrical formulas, tables, conversions, conductor information, motor data, and practical information that supports calculation and field-knowledge review.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
This publication connects foundational electrical theory with practical NEC applications. It supports study involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, transformers, motors, calculations, and electrical problem-solving.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Build a study plan around the official examination content outline. General code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, and equipment for general use represent most of the scored questions. These subjects deserve substantial preparation, but candidates should not ignore project management, safety, theory, energy standards, special installations, or communication systems.

Review definitions, conductor ampacity, branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, emergency systems, and communication systems.

Electrical theory preparation should include Ohm’s law, power relationships, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, and common contractor calculations. Practice identifying the correct formula, maintaining consistent units, and checking whether a result is reasonable.

Safety preparation should combine the two OSHA references with NFPA 70E. Learn which publication applies to construction work, general-industry conditions, or electrical safety-related work practices. Review hazard recognition, protective equipment, shock protection, arc-flash protection, employee training, and safe work procedures.

Project-management study should include planning, scheduling, stakeholder communication, procurement, quality, cost control, risk, contracts, and project delivery. Use the PMBOK Guide and NASCLA Contractors Guide together to understand both general project-management principles and contractor-focused business practices.

Energy-standard preparation should focus on locating requirements in ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1. Become familiar with its organization and provisions related to electrical power, lighting, controls, and applicable building-energy requirements.

Complete timed practice sessions with all nine references nearby. Track how long it takes to determine the subject, choose a book, locate the section, and confirm the answer. Review missed questions to identify whether the problem involved technical knowledge, book navigation, calculation accuracy, or interpretation.
