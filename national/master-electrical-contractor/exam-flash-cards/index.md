## Exam Details

NASCLA’s electrical examination program includes the **Electrical Contractors examination for Master Electricians and Unlimited Electricians**. NASCLA defines this category as an electrical contractor who conducts business in the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

The NASCLA Master Electrician examination contains **100 scored questions**. Candidates must earn a minimum score of **75** to pass. The testing period is **270 minutes**, or 4½ hours.

The examination also includes **10 non-scored experimental questions**. These questions are used as part of the examination-development process and do not count toward the candidate’s overall score.

The current official examination content outline distributes the 100 scored questions across the following areas:

- **Project Design & Management:** 8 questions
- **Safety:** 9 questions
- **Electrical Theory & Principles:** 11 questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 17 questions
- **Wiring & Protection:** 17 questions
- **Wiring Methods & Materials:** 16 questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 13 questions
- **Special Occupancies, Special Equipment & Special Conditions:** 8 questions
- **Communication Systems:** 1 question

General Code Requirements, Wiring & Protection, and Wiring Methods & Materials make up a substantial portion of the scored examination. However, candidates should not overlook theory, safety, project design, equipment, or special electrical installations when building a study schedule.

The examination is administered by computer through PSI. Candidates receive their score information after completing the examination. Candidates who do not pass receive diagnostic information showing relative strengths and weaknesses by examination area.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Master Electrician examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center in accordance with the current PSI examination rules.

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Program Plan Set is provided at the test site. A physical diagram or blueprint packet is also provided onsite for examination use.

Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. They must otherwise remain unmarked. Written notes are not permitted in the references, and candidates may not add loose or attached papers.

Permanent tabs may be used to organize approved references. Temporary tabs, including Post-it-style notes, are not permitted and must be removed before testing begins.

A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator may be used during the examination.

Because the NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination is open book, efficient reference navigation should be part of your study plan. Practice finding code articles, definitions, tables, conductor requirements, overcurrent protection provisions, grounding and bonding information, wiring methods, equipment rules, and special occupancy requirements without searching randomly through your books.

The 250 flash cards can support this process. When a flash card relates to a code requirement or technical subject, practice identifying the likely reference before checking the answer. This helps connect trade knowledge with the book-navigation skills needed on examination day.

## Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program can help candidates satisfy the electrical trade-examination requirement for participating licensing agencies. Passing the NASCLA examination is not itself an electrical contractor license or master electrician license.

1. **Determine whether the licensing agency accepts the NASCLA electrical examination.** NASCLA works with participating jurisdictions that administer or accept its Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.
2. **Apply for examination eligibility.** Candidates applying through NASCLA use the NASCLA National Examination Database to submit their examination application.
3. **Receive examination approval.** Once the application is approved, the candidate receives scheduling instructions and a NASCLA candidate identification number.
4. **Schedule the examination.** Approved candidates schedule the applicable NASCLA electrical examination through PSI.
5. **Prepare approved references.** Candidates should obtain and organize the permitted books before the examination, following all rules regarding highlighting, indexing, tabs, and written material.
6. **Take the Master Electrician examination.** Complete the 100 scored questions and additional non-scored experimental items within the 270-minute testing period.
7. **Earn the required passing score.** The NASCLA Master Electrician examination requires a minimum score of 75.
8. **Access examination results through the NASCLA National Examination Database.** Passing results can be associated with the candidate’s NASCLA record after testing.
9. **Send a transcript to the appropriate regulatory agency.** Candidates can electronically submit a NASCLA transcript to a participating agency when pursuing licensure.
10. **Complete the licensing agency’s separate requirements.** The applicant must satisfy the jurisdiction’s experience, application, business, financial, insurance, background, and other requirements that apply to the desired license.

NASCLA currently allows candidates applying through its examination process **three attempts within a one-year eligibility period**. Examination eligibility remains valid for one year from the date the application is approved.

## State Requirements

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program is designed to provide a standardized trade examination that participating regulatory agencies can administer or accept. It does not create a single national electrical license.

After passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination, candidates must still apply to the electrical licensing agency in the jurisdiction where they want to become licensed. Each jurisdiction determines which license classification corresponds to the NASCLA exam and what additional requirements an applicant must satisfy.

Current participating agencies include electrical licensing authorities in Alabama, Arizona, the District of Columbia, Florida, Idaho, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, New Mexico, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia, and participating local jurisdictions such as Vanderburgh County, Indiana.

Some participating agencies administer the NASCLA examination directly as an examination option, while others accept a passing NASCLA result in place of their own trade examination. Candidates may still need to complete state-specific business and law examinations or other tests required by the applicable licensing agency.

Experience requirements also vary by jurisdiction. A NASCLA passing score does not override a state’s requirements for documented electrical experience, master-level qualifications, contractor business registration, insurance, financial responsibility, bonding, continuing education, or other licensing conditions.

NASCLA stores passing examination information in the National Examination Database, allowing candidates to electronically send transcripts to participating regulatory agencies. The regulatory agency then uses the NASCLA result as part of its own licensing process.

## Reference Books

The current NASCLA Master Electrician examination bulletin identifies the following references for the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination:

- **National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023**
The primary electrical code reference for general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, and other NEC-based examination content.
- **Code of Federal Regulations – 29 CFR 1926 – OSHA Construction Industry Regulations, 2024**
A federal construction safety reference addressing workplace hazards, electrical construction safety, protective practices, and jobsite requirements.
- **Code of Federal Regulations – 29 CFR 1910 – OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards, 2024**
A federal workplace safety reference covering general industry occupational safety and health requirements relevant to electrical work.
- **A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge, 7th Edition, 2021**
A project management reference covering planning, project organization, management processes, coordination, and project delivery principles.
- **ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022, Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings**
An energy standard addressing requirements for commercial buildings and related electrical and energy-efficiency considerations.
- **NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic, 14th Edition, 2024**
A NASCLA reference covering contractor business practices, law, project management, contracts, planning, and related management subjects.
- **NFPA 70E – Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
An electrical safety reference addressing safe work practices, electrical hazards, shock protection, arc-flash considerations, and workplace electrical safety.
- **Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023 Edition**
A compact electrical reference containing formulas, tables, electrical calculations, conversions, and frequently used technical information.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
An electrical theory reference designed to connect electrical principles and calculations with practical National Electrical Code applications.

The NASCLA bulletin also identifies optional electrical indexes that may be used under separate testing-room conditions. These optional materials are not sources for examination questions.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination combines code knowledge, electrical theory, safety, project management, and practical installation requirements. An effective study plan should reflect that broad scope.

Start with the **250 flash cards** and divide them into manageable groups. Instead of working through the complete deck every time, review a smaller number each day. Cards you answer correctly can move into a maintenance group, while cards you miss should return to your active study rotation more frequently.

Give particular attention to National Electrical Code navigation. General Code Requirements, Wiring & Protection, Wiring Methods & Materials, and Equipment for General Use together account for a large portion of the scored examination. Candidates should become comfortable finding information quickly in the NEC instead of depending on memory alone.

Electrical theory and principles should also be practiced regularly. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, single-phase and three-phase relationships, transformers, motors, conductors, loads, and other electrical concepts applicable to master-level work. When a topic involves mathematics, solve the problem rather than only reading the formula.

Safety represents another important examination area. Use the OSHA and NFPA 70E references to review electrical hazards, safe work practices, jobsite requirements, protective equipment, and procedures that apply to electrical work.

Project Design & Management accounts for eight scored questions. Preparation should therefore include project planning, management principles, coordination, estimating-related concepts, and the ability to interpret electrical project information.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions should not be ignored. Candidates should be familiar with the structure of the NEC articles that address installations requiring specialized rules and should know how to locate those requirements efficiently.

Plan interpretation should also be included in your study routine because the examination provides an electrical program plan set and blueprint material at the testing site. Practice reading electrical layouts, panel information, circuits, equipment details, and project documentation while connecting the plans to code requirements.

Use flash cards as a starting point for deeper review. When you miss a question, locate the subject in the applicable approved reference. This approach helps you reinforce both the answer and the location of the supporting information, making each missed card useful for developing book-navigation skills.
