## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for individuals performing or supervising contractor-level electrical work. It addresses knowledge associated with electrical system installation, maintenance, repair, project planning, safety, code compliance, and contractor operations.

Exam preparation should include general electrical code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communication systems, electrical theory, workplace safety, project management, and energy-efficiency requirements.

The National Electrical Code is a central reference for the examination. Candidates should understand how the NEC is organized and how its general requirements interact with rules for specific equipment, locations, systems, and installation conditions. Useful preparation areas include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and electrical equipment.

Contractor-level candidates should also prepare for project design and management topics. These subjects can involve planning, scheduling, cost awareness, quality, communication, procurement, risk, contracts, coordination, and project delivery. The included project management and NASCLA contractor references help support this area of study.

Safety preparation should include construction requirements, general-industry standards, and electrical safety-related work practices. Candidates should learn how the OSHA construction regulations, OSHA occupational safety standards, and NFPA 70E differ so they can select the appropriate book for a safety question.

The included energy standard supports preparation involving energy-efficient building systems, electrical power, lighting, controls, and related requirements for applicable buildings and sites. Candidates should learn the structure of the standard and practice locating provisions instead of attempting to memorize the entire publication.

Electrical theory remains important in an open-book contractor examination. Candidates should review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and commonly used formulas. Strong theory knowledge helps candidates understand what a question is asking before searching for a code section or table.

Plan and specification questions may require candidates to interpret symbols, equipment schedules, circuit information, notes, conductor details, and written requirements. Practice should combine blueprint review with the ability to confirm code and project requirements in the appropriate references.

## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing requirements vary by state and jurisdiction. An agency may accept the NASCLA Electrical Contractors examination while still requiring the applicant to meet separate experience, education, financial, insurance, business, and administrative requirements.

Some jurisdictions issue individual master electrician or unlimited electrician licenses. Others regulate electrical contracting businesses and require a designated qualifying individual to pass the trade examination. Candidates should confirm the exact credential and scope of work before registering.

A state may require a separate business and law examination even though this NASCLA exam package includes contractor business and project management references. Acceptance of the trade examination does not automatically waive a jurisdiction’s separate business-law requirement.

Applicants may also be required to document qualifying work history, provide employer verification, submit financial statements, register a business entity, obtain liability insurance, or complete a background review. These requirements are determined by the licensing authority rather than by the book package.

The NASCLA examination should not be treated as a national contractor license. A passing result may be transmitted to participating agencies, but each agency independently reviews the applicant and determines whether the licensing requirements have been satisfied.

## Reference Books

- **National Electrical Code, 2023**
The 2023 National Electrical Code contains requirements for electrical installations, including wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1926 - OSHA Construction Industry Regulations**
This OSHA reference addresses federal workplace safety requirements for construction activities. It supports preparation involving construction hazards, employee protection, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, and safe jobsite practices.
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR 1910 - OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards**
This publication contains federal occupational safety and health standards for general industry, including workplace conditions, equipment, electrical hazards, employee protection, training, and employer responsibilities.
- **A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge, 2021, 7th Edition**
This project management reference addresses principles and performance areas associated with planning, delivery, stakeholders, teams, measurement, uncertainty, adaptation, and project value.
- **ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1, 2022 Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings - I-P Edition**
This standard provides energy-efficiency requirements for applicable sites and buildings, including provisions involving electrical power, lighting, controls, equipment, and energy-conscious building systems.
- **NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition**
This contractor-focused reference supports preparation involving business organization, project management, contracts, estimating, scheduling, safety, risk, financial administration, and professional contractor responsibilities.
- **NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024**
NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety-related work practices, including hazard assessment, shock protection, arc-flash protection, approach boundaries, personal protective equipment, and energized work considerations.
- **Ugly’s Electrical References, 2023**
This compact field reference includes commonly used electrical formulas, tables, conversions, conductor information, motor information, and practical trade data. This book will not be highlighted or tabbed.
- **Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications**
This publication connects foundational electrical theory with practical NEC applications. It supports review involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, formulas, and electrical calculations.
