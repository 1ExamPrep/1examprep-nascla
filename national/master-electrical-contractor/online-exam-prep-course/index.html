Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is intended for individuals performing, supervising, or qualifying electrical contracting work. It evaluates knowledge related to the installation, maintenance, repair, management, and safe operation of electrical systems in accordance with applicable codes and standards.

The examination contains 100 scored questions. Candidates must answer at least 75 scored questions correctly to meet the minimum passing standard. The examination allows 270 minutes, which provides four hours and thirty minutes to complete the test.

Ten additional non-scored experimental questions may also be administered. These questions are used for future examination development and do not count toward the final score.

The examination content includes the following major areas:

  • Project Design and Management: Planning, coordination, project delivery, schedules, stakeholders, risk, quality, and contractor responsibilities.
  • Safety: Construction safety, general-industry safety, electrical hazards, protective equipment, and electrical safety-related work practices.
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: Voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, formulas, and calculations.
  • General Code Requirements: NEC organization, definitions, general installation rules, and code interpretation.
  • Wiring and Protection: Branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding, bonding, conductors, and overcurrent protection.
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: Raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, conductor installation, support, and permitted uses.
  • Equipment for General Use: Motors, transformers, appliances, luminaires, switches, receptacles, and other electrical equipment.
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Requirements for specialized locations, systems, and installations.
  • Communication Systems: Code requirements affecting applicable communication circuits and systems.

General code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, and equipment for general use represent a substantial portion of the contractor examination. Candidates should dedicate meaningful study time to these subjects while continuing to prepare for project management, safety, theory, energy requirements, and specialized electrical installations.

A physical diagram or blueprint packet may be provided at the testing center. Candidates should be prepared to interpret plans, specifications, equipment schedules, symbols, conductor information, notes, and project requirements. A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator may be used during the examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted reference books to the testing center because the publications are not supplied onsite.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs may be used to identify major chapters, articles, tables, and frequently referenced sections. Temporary tabs, removable notes, handwritten study notes, loose sheets, and attached additional papers are not permitted.

The open-book format places significant importance on reference familiarity. Candidates may need to move between the National Electrical Code, OSHA regulations, NFPA 70E, the energy standard, project-management guidance, contractor business material, field references, and electrical theory content during the same testing session.

A productive preparation method begins with learning the purpose and organization of each publication. Candidates should know which book is most likely to address a particular question before beginning to search. This reduces wasted time and helps create a more controlled examination strategy.

For the National Electrical Code, practice using the index, table of contents, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references. For OSHA and NFPA 70E, learn how construction safety, general-industry safety, and electrical safety-related work practices differ.

For project management, contractor business, and energy-standard questions, become familiar with the chapters, subject divisions, terminology, and indexes within the applicable references. Repeated practice is more useful than attempting to memorize every page.

Candidates should study with the physical books they plan to use during the examination. The online course supports technical preparation and navigation practice, but it does not replace the approved references.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Master Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician license. The exam may satisfy a trade-testing requirement for a participating jurisdiction, but applicants must still meet the licensing standards established by that agency.

  1. Select the licensing jurisdiction. Identify the state or regulatory authority where you intend to apply for an electrical contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician credential.
  2. Confirm examination acceptance. Verify that the agency accepts the NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors for the classification you are pursuing.
  3. Review eligibility requirements. Determine whether the jurisdiction requires documented electrical experience, supervisory experience, education, financial information, business registration, insurance, or a designated qualifying individual.
  4. Complete the applicable application. Submit the forms and supporting documents required for examination approval or licensing eligibility.
  5. Obtain the approved references. Purchase or otherwise obtain the listed editions before beginning intensive course study.
  6. Use the online exam prep course. Follow an organized plan for electrical code, safety, theory, project management, energy standards, business practices, calculations, and reference navigation.
  7. Register and schedule the examination. Follow the current scheduling instructions from the examination administrator.
  8. Take the examination. Comply with all testing-center rules concerning identification, calculators, references, arrival time, security procedures, and prohibited materials.
  9. Transmit the passing result. Complete the process for sending the examination transcript to a participating licensing agency.
  10. Finish the licensing application. Submit all remaining jurisdiction-specific forms, fees, experience records, financial documents, insurance information, and business credentials.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing requirements vary by state and jurisdiction. A regulatory agency may accept the NASCLA examination while still requiring applicants to meet separate experience, education, financial, business, and administrative requirements.

Some jurisdictions issue individual master electrician or unlimited electrician licenses. Others license electrical contracting businesses and require a qualifying individual to pass the trade examination. Candidates should confirm the exact classification and scope of work associated with the credential they intend to obtain.

A separate state business and law examination may also be required. The inclusion of business and project-management subjects on the NASCLA electrical examination does not automatically replace a jurisdiction’s separate business-law testing requirement.

Applicants may need to document qualifying electrical work, supervisory experience, education, apprenticeship completion, financial responsibility, insurance coverage, or business registration. A licensing agency may also require application fees, background information, tax documentation, or other supporting records.

The NASCLA examination should not be treated as a national electrical contractor license. Passing results may be sent to participating agencies, but each agency independently decides whether an applicant meets its complete licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan should combine course review, book navigation, calculations, code interpretation, and timed practice. Reading a reference from beginning to end without applying the material may not develop the speed needed for a contractor-level open-book exam.

For National Electrical Code study, focus on definitions, general requirements, conductor sizing, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, emergency systems, and communication systems.

Practice reading complete code provisions rather than stopping after the first matching sentence. Exceptions, table notes, definitions, and related articles can change how a requirement applies.

For project management, review stakeholders, planning, schedules, project work, delivery, quality, procurement, communication, cost, uncertainty, and risk. Use both the PMBOK Guide and the NASCLA Contractors Guide to connect general management principles with contractor-focused responsibilities.

For safety preparation, compare OSHA construction regulations, OSHA general-industry standards, and NFPA 70E. Learn which publication applies to a particular work environment, hazard, or electrical safety practice.

For energy-standard preparation, become familiar with the organization of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES Standard 90.1. Practice locating requirements involving electrical power, lighting systems, controls, equipment, and applicable building-energy provisions.

Electrical theory study should include Ohm’s law, power relationships, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and common contractor calculations. Practice selecting the correct formula, maintaining consistent units, and checking whether the final result is reasonable.

Timed practice can help candidates identify whether difficulty comes from technical knowledge, reference navigation, calculation accuracy, or code interpretation. Keep all nine references nearby and practice moving between them as questions change subjects.

A useful exam process is to identify the topic, select the correct publication, locate the section, read the complete requirement, review exceptions or notes, complete any necessary calculation, and confirm the answer before moving forward.
