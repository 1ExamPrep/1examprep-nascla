# Nebraska NASCLA Electrical Exam: Contractor, Journeyman and Residential Licenses

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 11 min read · 10 official sources cited

Nebraska licenses electricians at the state level through the Nebraska State Electrical Division (NSED), and it uses the NASCLA accredited electrical exams for three of its licenses: electrical contractor, journeyman electrician and residential journeyman electrician. The state does not just accept a NASCLA score you earned somewhere else. NSED administers the exam itself, through PSI, and only counts results from tests taken through the Division on a date after August 1, 2025.

This page covers how the Nebraska NASCLA electrical exam works for each license, the experience the State Electrical Board expects, the published fees, reciprocity, renewal and continuing education, and how to study for an open book test. ## Does Nebraska accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes, for electrical licenses. NASCLA lists the [Nebraska State Electrical Division](https://www.nascla.org/electrical-exams-participating-state-agencies/nebraska-state-electrical-division) as an agency that administers its Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician and Residential Electrician exams. Two conditions come with that listing. The exam has to be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and the exam date must be after August 1, 2025 for Nebraska to accept it.

In practice, that means you apply to NSED first, NSED sends your eligibility to PSI, and you test under Nebraska's program. A NASCLA electrical score from another state's program, or from a sitting before that date, should not be counted on. Ask NSED before you register if you have an older result.

Nebraska is not on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the [NASCLA commercial building contractor exam](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies). Nebraska has no statewide general contractor license; instead, contractors register with the Nebraska Department of Labor. Everything on this page is about electrical licensing.

## Who issues electrical licenses in Nebraska

The [Nebraska State Electrical Division](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/) carries out the Nebraska State Electrical Act (Sections 81-2101 through 81-2144) for the State Electrical Board. It handles licenses, apprentice registration, permits and inspections, and it runs exam approvals. The office is at 1220 Lincoln Mall, Suite 125, Lincoln, and the phone number is (402) 471-3550.

Under the Act, anyone who wires or installs electrical work for someone else must hold a contractor-level license (electrical contractor, Class B electrical contractor, Class A master electrician or fire alarm installer). People who do the wiring or supervise apprentices need a journeyman-level license and must work for a contractor-level licensee. A licensee may not lend a license to anyone else.

NSED moved applications, renewals and other transactions to a new online licensing and permitting system. Every applicant for apprenticeship or licensure, including exam applicants, also has to submit a United States Citizenship Attestation form, so include it with your first application to avoid delays after you pass.

## Nebraska license types and which NASCLA exam each needs

Three Nebraska licenses line up with the three NASCLA electrical exams. Pick the exam that matches the license you are applying for, because NSED approves you for a specific test.

- Electrical Contractor: take the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam. See our [Nebraska Master Electrical Contractor prep](https://passyournasclaexam.com/nebraska/master-electrical-contractor/).
- Journeyman Electrician: take the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician exam. See our [Nebraska Journeyman Electrician prep](https://passyournasclaexam.com/nebraska/journeyman-electrician/).
- Residential Journeyman Electrician (Type RW): take the NASCLA Residential Electrician exam. See our [Nebraska Residential Electrical Contractor prep](https://passyournasclaexam.com/nebraska/residential-electrical-contractor/). The RW license covers single-family and two-family homes and multifamily dwellings no more than three stories tall.

## How to get a Nebraska electrical license, step by step

Nebraska is strict about order: no exam is scheduled until NSED approves your application. Here is the path as the Division describes it on its [exam scheduling page](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/exam-scheduling).

1. Choose your license and confirm you meet its experience requirement (see below).
2. Complete the state electrical license application with employer verification letters on company letterhead, any degree you are using for credit, the citizenship attestation and the application fee.
3. Submit an exam application. You need a new exam application each time you want to test.
4. Wait for approval. NSED emails you, sends your eligibility to PSI, and includes a short quiz on the State Act and Board Rules that you return once you pass.
5. Use the link in PSI's email to pay, pick a test center and schedule.
6. Take and pass the NASCLA exam through the Nebraska program.
7. Pay the license fee and finish any remaining paperwork so NSED can issue the license.
8. If you will run a business, register with the Nebraska Department of Labor as a contractor and carry the insurance the Board requires.

## The Nebraska NASCLA electrical exam: format and references

All three NASCLA electrical exams are open book. NASCLA's national [candidate bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/6677) lists 100 questions for each. It gives 270 minutes for the Electrical Contractor and Residential exams and 300 minutes for the Journeyman exam. That bulletin lists 75 correct as passing for Electrical Contractor and 70 for the other two, while Nebraska's [Board rules](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/STATE%20ELECTRICAL%20BOARD%20RULES%204-23-24.pdf) set a 75 percent minimum for state license exams. Confirm which standard applies to your sitting with NSED.

Approved references differ by exam. The contractor exam uses the NEC, OSHA 29 CFR 1926 and 1910, the PMBOK Guide, ASHRAE 90.1-2022, the NASCLA Contractors Guide (Basic 14th edition), NFPA 70E 2024, Ugly's 2023 and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. The journeyman list drops the business and energy books and adds NFPA 72 (2022). The residential list adds the 2024 International Residential Code and ANSI Z535.4-2023. A blueprint packet is handed out on site.

Nebraska has tested on the 2023 NEC since August 1, 2024. NSED also lists exam subjects as the 2023 NEC, basic electricity and theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits including fire alarm circuits. Retesting is covered by NSED Policy 37, effective July 11, 2025, and Board rules say you must reapply after a failed exam. Passing an exam also earns 6 hours of continuing education credit.

## Business and law content in Nebraska

Nebraska does not give a separate business and law exam for electrical licenses, and we did not find a Nebraska edition of NASCLA's business and law guide in the NASCLA store. The business material is built into the NASCLA exams themselves.

For the contractor and residential exams, the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management (Basic 14th edition) is an approved reference, and the contractor exam adds the PMBOK Guide. Expect questions on contracts, estimating, scheduling, risk, payroll and project control. On the state side, NSED's approval email includes a quiz on the Nebraska State Electrical Act and Board Rules, so read the Act before you test, not just the NEC.

## Nebraska electrical license requirements: experience and insurance

The experience rules come from the Act and the current [state application](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/2026%20updated%20Application%20%28002%294.1.2026_1.pdf). Experience must be acceptable to the State Electrical Board, and time registered as an apprentice can serve as evidence of it.

If you own and run an electrical contracting firm as an electrical contractor or master, Board Rule 7 requires liability coverage of $100,000 per person, $300,000 per accident and $100,000 for property damage. When more than one licensee owns the firm, only one needs to carry it. Any contractor with employees must also keep a workers' compensation certificate on file with the [Department of Labor](https://dol.nebraska.gov/LaborStandards/Contractors) as part of contractor registration.

- Electrical Contractor: one year as a licensed journeyman electrician; or a four-year electrical engineering degree plus one year of journeyman experience; or five years planning, laying out, supervising and installing electrical wiring and equipment.
- Journeyman Electrician: four years of verifiable experience, or three years plus a qualifying two-year electrical technology degree (Board Rule 8 grants one year of credit).
- Residential Journeyman (Type RW): three years of verifiable experience, or two years plus a qualifying electrical technology degree.

## Nebraska electrical license costs and fees

These are the published figures from the state application and NSED's [2025-2026 fee page](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/2025-2026-license-registration-fees). License fees are charged per year of the two-year period, so the amount owed depends on whether you are licensed in an even or odd year. Check with NSED for current amounts before you pay; PSI exam fees are paid at scheduling and are separate.

- Application fee: Electrical Contractor $180; Journeyman Electrician $90; Residential Journeyman (RW) $90.
- License fee per year (2025-2026): Electrical Contractor $187.50; Journeyman and Residential Wireman $37.50; Apprentice registration $35.
- Reciprocity applicants pay the application fee plus the license fee.
- Department of Labor contractor registration: annual fee increases to $40 effective August 1, 2026.

## Reciprocity and working in other states

Nebraska has journeyman reciprocity with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Iowa, Minnesota, Montana, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas and Wyoming, according to NSED's [reciprocal information page](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/node/26). Only some of those states, such as Iowa, Minnesota, South Dakota and Texas, are marked for contractor licenses as well; the rest are journeyman only. The page warns that restrictions vary by state, so confirm before you apply.

The general conditions: you need a score of 75 percent or more on the state exam for the license, and you must have held the license in good standing for at least one year. Nebraska licensees seeking reciprocity elsewhere need 4 years of registration and 8,000 hours of work in Nebraska. Reciprocity is one-time; if a reciprocal license lapses, you must test to get it back.

Because NASCLA stores results in its National Examination Database (ned.nascla.org), a NASCLA score taken through Nebraska can also help when another participating state reviews your application.

## After you pass: renewal and continuing education

Nebraska electrical licenses run on a two-year cycle, and all licenses and registrations expire on December 31 of each even-numbered year. NSED's fee page states the current licenses expire December 31, 2026, and the Division lists the renewal window as October 1 through December 31, 2026. Under the Act, renewals after November 30 carry an extra 10 percent, and if you have not renewed by April 1 after expiration you must retest.

To renew, you need 12 contact hours of [Board-approved continuing education](https://electrical.nebraska.gov/node/19), with at least 6 of them on the NEC. The statute sets the deadline as January 1 of each odd-numbered year, and a contact hour is 50 minutes of class. Keep your certificates for your renewal application. Your passing exam can count for 6 of those hours.

If you open a contracting business, keep your Department of Labor registration and workers' compensation certificate current, along with the liability coverage the Board requires.

## Nebraska NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Does Nebraska accept NASCLA electrical exam scores from other states?

Nebraska's NASCLA listing says the exam must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division with an exam date after August 1, 2025. Plan on applying to NSED and testing under its program. Ask NSED directly if you hold an older or out-of-state result.

### Who issues electrical licenses in Nebraska?

The Nebraska State Electrical Division, acting for the State Electrical Board, issues electrical contractor, journeyman, residential journeyman and specialty licenses and registers apprentices. It also handles permits and inspections.

### How do I schedule the Nebraska NASCLA electrical exam?

Submit an exam application to NSED and wait for approval. NSED then sends your eligibility to PSI, and PSI emails a link to pay and choose a test center. No exam can be scheduled before NSED approves you.

### How much experience do I need for a Nebraska journeyman electrician license?

Four years of verifiable experience acceptable to the Board, or three years plus a qualifying two-year electrical technology degree. Time registered as an apprentice can serve as evidence of experience.

### What are the requirements for a Nebraska electrical contractor license?

You need one year as a licensed journeyman, a four-year electrical engineering degree plus one year of journeyman experience, or five years of planning, layout, supervision and installation experience. Firm owners must also carry the liability insurance set by Board Rule 7.

### What does a Nebraska Type RW residential license cover?

It covers residential installations, meaning single-family and two-family dwellings and multifamily dwellings no more than three stories high. Applicants need three years of acceptable experience, or two years plus a qualifying electrical technology degree.

### Is the Nebraska electrician exam open book?

Yes. The NASCLA electrical exams allow approved references, led by the NEC, and Nebraska tests on the 2023 NEC. A blueprint packet is provided at the test center.

### How much does a Nebraska electrical contractor license cost?

The published application fee is $180, and the 2025-2026 license fee is $187.50 per year of the two-year period. Exam fees are paid separately to PSI. Check current amounts with NSED.

### What continuing education does Nebraska require?

You need 12 contact hours of Board-approved courses each two-year cycle, with at least 6 hours on the NEC. Passing a license exam earns 6 hours of credit.

### Does Nebraska have electrical license reciprocity?

Yes. Nebraska has journeyman reciprocity with 13 states and contractor reciprocity with a smaller group. You generally need a 75 percent score on the state exam and a license held in good standing for at least one year.
