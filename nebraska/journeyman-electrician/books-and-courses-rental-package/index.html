Exam Details

The Nebraska State Electrical Division administers journeyman electrician licensing and examination eligibility in Nebraska. Approved candidates are sent to PSI for exam scheduling. Candidates must first submit an exam application and receive approval from the Nebraska State Electrical Division before PSI scheduling can be completed.

Nebraska currently uses the 2023 National Electrical Code for electrical examinations. The State Electrical Division adopted the 2023 NEC beginning August 1, 2024, and electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 code at that time.

Nebraska's examination information identifies several major subject areas candidates should be prepared to study, including:

  • National Electrical Code - 2023
  • Basic Electricity and Electrical Theory
  • Nebraska State Electrical Act
  • Emergency Circuits, including Fire Alarm Circuits

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination also covers a broad range of journeyman-level electrical knowledge. Preparation should include interpreting plans and specifications, basic electrical safety, electrical theory and principles, testing and troubleshooting, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and communication systems.

National Electrical Code preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, cables, wiring methods, motors, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also be comfortable working with formulas, calculations, electrical theory, troubleshooting concepts, and safety standards.

Passing the examination is only one part of the Nebraska licensing process. The Nebraska State Electrical Division separately reviews applicant qualifications, experience, education when applicable, application materials, and licensing eligibility before issuing a Journeyman Electrician license.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license. Licensing is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and candidates must satisfy the State Electrical Board's requirements.

  1. Review Nebraska Journeyman Electrician qualifications. Determine whether your electrical work experience and education meet the State Electrical Board's requirements.
  2. Document qualifying experience. Nebraska generally requires at least four years, or 8,000 hours, of verifiable electrical trade experience acceptable to the Board.
  3. Submit an exam application. Candidates must apply through the Nebraska State Electrical Division before testing.
  4. Receive approval to test. The Nebraska State Electrical Division reviews the application and sends approved eligibility to PSI.
  5. Prepare for the examination. Study the 2023 NEC, basic electricity and theory, Nebraska electrical requirements, safety, fire alarm circuits, calculations, troubleshooting, wiring methods, and equipment.
  6. Schedule through PSI. After approval, candidates receive scheduling instructions from PSI.
  7. Take the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination through Nebraska. For Nebraska acceptance, the exam must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division.
  8. Complete the remaining licensing process. Submit any outstanding licensing documentation and fees required by the State Electrical Division.
  9. Maintain the license. Follow Nebraska's current renewal and continuing-education requirements after licensure.

State Requirements

The Nebraska State Electrical Division regulates electrical licensing in Nebraska. The State Electrical Board oversees several license and registration categories, including Journeyman Electricians, Electrical Contractors, Residential Journeymen, specialty licensees, and apprentices.

An applicant for a Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license generally must have at least four years, or 8,000 hours, of verifiable electrical trade experience acceptable to the Board. Registration as an apprentice electrician for those years may serve as evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also allows an education-based experience credit. Successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical program resulting in an Associate's degree in Electrical Technology can provide one year of work-experience credit. Under that pathway, an applicant may qualify with three years of acceptable verifiable experience together with the qualifying degree.

Qualified electrical experience obtained through military service may also receive work-experience credit when accepted by the Board. Applicants using education or military experience should provide the supporting documentation required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Nebraska also distinguishes between unrestricted and restricted Journeyman Electrician licenses based on in-state experience. If an applicant has fewer than 4,000 hours of verifiable electrical experience in Nebraska, the resulting Journeyman Electrician license is restricted and is not eligible for reciprocity with another state.

The Nebraska State Electrical Board maintains reciprocal Journeyman Electrician agreements with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Idaho, Minnesota, Montana, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming. Reciprocity is a separate licensing pathway and has its own requirements.

Nebraska currently lists a $90 application fee for the Journeyman Electrician license. License fees vary depending on whether the license is issued in an even-numbered or odd-numbered year. These government licensing fees are separate from the price of this Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep rental package.

Applicants for Nebraska electrical registration and licensing are also required to complete the applicable lawful-presence attestation associated with the state's licensing procedures.

After an applicant is approved for testing, both the Nebraska State Electrical Division and PSI send eligibility information. Candidates then schedule and pay for the examination through PSI. Nebraska requires a new exam application and approval each time a candidate seeks another examination attempt.
