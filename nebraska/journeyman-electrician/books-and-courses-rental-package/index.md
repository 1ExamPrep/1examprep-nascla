## State Requirements

The **Nebraska State Electrical Division** regulates electrical licensing in Nebraska. The State Electrical Board oversees several license and registration categories, including Journeyman Electricians, Electrical Contractors, Residential Journeymen, specialty licensees, and apprentices.

An applicant for a Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license generally must have at least **four years, or 8,000 hours, of verifiable electrical trade experience** acceptable to the Board. Registration as an apprentice electrician for those years may serve as evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also allows an education-based experience credit. Successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical program resulting in an **Associate's degree in Electrical Technology** can provide one year of work-experience credit. Under that pathway, an applicant may qualify with three years of acceptable verifiable experience together with the qualifying degree.

Qualified electrical experience obtained through military service may also receive work-experience credit when accepted by the Board. Applicants using education or military experience should provide the supporting documentation required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Nebraska also distinguishes between unrestricted and restricted Journeyman Electrician licenses based on in-state experience. If an applicant has fewer than **4,000 hours of verifiable electrical experience in Nebraska**, the resulting Journeyman Electrician license is restricted and is not eligible for reciprocity with another state.

The Nebraska State Electrical Board maintains reciprocal Journeyman Electrician agreements with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Idaho, Minnesota, Montana, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming. Reciprocity is a separate licensing pathway and has its own requirements.

Nebraska currently lists a **$90 application fee** for the Journeyman Electrician license. License fees vary depending on whether the license is issued in an even-numbered or odd-numbered year. These government licensing fees are separate from the price of this Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep rental package.

Applicants for Nebraska electrical registration and licensing are also required to complete the applicable lawful-presence attestation associated with the state's licensing procedures.

After an applicant is approved for testing, both the Nebraska State Electrical Division and PSI send eligibility information. Candidates then schedule and pay for the examination through PSI. Nebraska requires a new exam application and approval each time a candidate seeks another examination attempt.
