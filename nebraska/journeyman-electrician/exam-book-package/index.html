NASCLA developed its electrical examinations through a national occupational analysis involving electrical industry subject matter experts.

  • Testing and troubleshooting
  • General electrical code requirements
  • Wiring and protection
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Equipment for general use
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Special conditions and communication systems

State Requirements

Nebraska Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The state regulates electrical contractors, journeyman electricians, residential journeyman electricians, fire alarm installers, special installers, and apprentice electricians.

An applicant for a Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license generally must have at least four years, or 8,000 hours, of verifiable electrical trade experience acceptable to the State Electrical Board.

Nebraska also recognizes an education-based pathway. A candidate may receive one year of work-experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course resulting in an Associate's degree in Electrical Technology. Under that pathway, an applicant may qualify with three years of acceptable verifiable experience together with the qualifying degree.

Applicants using the education credit must provide documentation of the Associate's degree when submitting the licensing application. Nebraska may also grant work-experience credit for qualified electrical experience obtained through military service.

Work-experience verification is an important part of the application process. Applicants must provide complete mailing information for current and previous employers, and verification letters from employers must be submitted on company letterhead. Incomplete experience documentation can result in application problems.

Nebraska also distinguishes between unrestricted and restricted Journeyman Electrician licenses based on qualifying experience obtained within the state. If an applicant has fewer than 4,000 hours of verifiable electrical experience in Nebraska, the resulting Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license is restricted and is not eligible for reciprocity with another state.

The Nebraska State Electrical Board maintains reciprocal Journeyman Electrician licensing agreements with several other states. Reciprocity is separate from the NASCLA examination pathway and depends on the applicant's existing license history and Nebraska's current reciprocal licensing requirements.

Nebraska electrical examinations use the 2023 National Electrical Code. The State Electrical Division adopted the 2023 NEC beginning August 1, 2024, and current electrical exam preparation should reflect that code edition.
