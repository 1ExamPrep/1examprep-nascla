## State Requirements

Nebraska Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered by the **Nebraska State Electrical Division**. The State Electrical Board establishes licensing qualifications and regulates several electrical classifications, including electrical contractors, journeyman electricians, residential journeymen, fire alarm installers, special installers, and apprentices.

An applicant for a Nebraska Journeyman Electrician license must generally have at least **four years, or 8,000 hours, of verifiable experience** in the electrical trade that is acceptable to the Board. Registered apprentice electrician experience may be used as evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also recognizes qualifying electrical education. Completion of a two-year post-high-school electrical program resulting in an **Associate's degree in Electrical Technology** can provide one year of work-experience credit. Qualified electrical experience gained during military service may also receive work-experience credit.

Experience verification is an important part of the licensing process. Nebraska applicants should maintain accurate information about their employers, electrical work history, dates of employment, and the type of work performed so that qualifying experience can be properly documented.

Nebraska also distinguishes between unrestricted and restricted Journeyman Electrician licenses based on in-state experience. If an applicant has fewer than **4,000 hours of verifiable electrical experience in Nebraska**, the resulting Journeyman Electrician license is restricted and cannot be used for reciprocity with another state.

The Nebraska State Electrical Board maintains reciprocal Journeyman Electrician agreements with several other states. Reciprocity is a separate licensing route from the NASCLA examination pathway and has its own eligibility and documentation requirements.

For the NASCLA pathway, Nebraska specifically requires the examination to be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. NASCLA identifies an examination date after August 1, 2025 as a condition for Nebraska acceptance.

Nebraska currently uses the **2023 National Electrical Code**. The 2023 NEC became the state's minimum electrical standard on August 1, 2024, and Nebraska electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC on that same date.

Nebraska electrical license and registration applicants are also subject to the State Electrical Division's current application documentation requirements. Applicants should complete all required forms and provide accurate experience, education, and licensing information when applying.
