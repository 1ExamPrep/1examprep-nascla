Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes a dedicated examination for Journeyman Electricians. Nebraska is a participating administering jurisdiction, and the exam must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division for Nebraska administration.

Nebraska's electrical examination program covers subjects including the 2023 National Electrical Code, basic electricity and theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, blueprint reading, emergency circuits, and fire alarm circuits.

Nebraska State Electrical Board rules provide that state electrical examinations contain a minimum of 50 questions and require a minimum passing grade of 75%. Candidates approved for testing receive scheduling information through the state's current testing process.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division transitioned journeyman electrician examinations to PSI. After the State Electrical Division approves a candidate to test, eligibility is submitted to PSI and the candidate receives scheduling information and access to the applicable candidate bulletin.

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is intended to evaluate broad electrical competency rather than residential work alone. Preparation should therefore include code requirements and electrical practices applicable to a wider range of journeyman-level installations, maintenance, troubleshooting, safety, and repair.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies its Journeyman Electrician examination as one of the open-book examinations within the Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes familiarity with the references essential. Candidates should know how to locate information through the NEC index, article structure, tables, definitions, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references rather than attempting to read the code from beginning to end during the exam.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be at the center of exam preparation. Candidates should develop familiarity with branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, equipment, special occupancies, special equipment, and other commonly tested NEC subjects.

Safety-related questions may require knowledge from 29 CFR Part 1926, 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand both construction-related safety and electrical workplace safety, including shock hazards, arc-flash hazards, personal protective equipment, safe work practices, and energized electrical work considerations.

Fire alarm and emergency-circuit preparation may involve NFPA 72 together with applicable NEC requirements. Electrical theory questions may require direct application of formulas and principles rather than simple reference lookup.

A practical strategy is to classify each question first. Determine whether it involves NEC code application, theory, safety, fire alarm systems, grounding and bonding, motors, conductors, overcurrent protection, calculations, or another topic. Then move directly to the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Licensing Steps

Journeyman electrician licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Candidates must satisfy the state's experience, application, examination, and licensing requirements.

  1. Obtain qualifying electrical experience. Nebraska requires journeyman electrician applicants to document qualifying experience acceptable to the State Electrical Board.
  2. Maintain appropriate apprentice registration. Apprentices working toward licensure should comply with Nebraska's registration and supervision requirements.
  3. Complete the Nebraska electrical license application. Applicants submit the required application and supporting employment information to the Nebraska State Electrical Division.
  4. Document experience. Current and previous employers must be properly identified, and Nebraska requires complete employment verification information.
  5. Receive examination approval. The State Electrical Division reviews the application before submitting examination eligibility.
  6. Prepare for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician exam. Study NEC requirements, electrical theory, safety, fire alarm systems, emergency circuits, tools, calculations, and other journeyman-level subjects.
  7. Practice open-book reference navigation. Work regularly with the NEC and supporting references so that important articles, tables, formulas, and safety standards become easier to locate.
  8. Schedule the examination through the approved testing process. Once approved, candidates receive scheduling instructions through PSI.
  9. Pass the required examination. Candidates must earn the passing score required by the applicable Nebraska examination rules.
  10. Complete the licensing process. After successful examination, candidates must satisfy the remaining Nebraska State Electrical Division requirements before the license is issued.

State Requirements

Nebraska requires an applicant for a Journeyman Electrician license to have at least four years of verifiable electrical trade experience, equivalent to 8,000 hours, acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board.

Nebraska recognizes registered apprenticeship experience as evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board. The state may also grant one year of work-experience credit for completion of a qualifying two-year post-high-school electrical program resulting in an Associate's degree in electrical technology.

Qualified electrical experience gained through military service may also be considered for work-experience credit under Nebraska's licensing requirements.

If a journeyman applicant has fewer than 4,000 hours of verifiable experience in Nebraska, the resulting Nebraska license may be restricted and may not qualify for reciprocity with another state.

Nebraska currently uses the 2023 National Electrical Code. The state adopted the 2023 NEC for covered installations and switched electrical examinations to the 2023 code beginning August 1, 2024.

Nebraska participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program for the Journeyman Electrician examination. For Nebraska recognition, the examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and NASCLA identifies exams taken after August 1, 2025 as accepted under the participating-state arrangement.

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not by itself issue a Nebraska electrical license. Applicants must still meet Nebraska's experience, application, lawful-presence attestation, licensing, and other applicable State Electrical Division requirements.

Nebraska also maintains reciprocal licensing agreements for the Journeyman Electrician license with several states, subject to the state's reciprocity requirements and the applicant's licensing history.

    Supports review of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, electrical calculations, and other electrical fundamentals while connecting theory with NEC applications.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nebraska NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Nebraska specifically identifies thorough knowledge of the NEC as an important part of successful examination preparation.

Rather than reading the NEC passively, candidates should practice answering questions that require them to locate specific requirements. Use the index, article numbers, tables, definitions, exceptions, and cross-references repeatedly until navigation becomes familiar.

Branch circuits and feeders should receive regular study. Review conductor sizing, ampacity, required circuits, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, voltage considerations, and the relationship between conductors and protective devices.

Services are another important journeyman-level subject. Candidates should understand service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, grounding, bonding, and related installation requirements.

Grounding and bonding frequently requires both code knowledge and careful terminology. Review grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, separately derived systems, and sizing requirements.

Wiring methods should include raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, support requirements, physical protection, conductor fill, box fill, and installation requirements for common wiring systems.

Motors and equipment require familiarity with conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, overload protection, disconnecting means, motor calculations, and related NEC tables. Candidates should practice recognizing when different motor calculations apply.

Electrical theory is another core preparation area. Review Ohm's law, power formulas, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating current concepts, voltage, current, resistance, and other basic electrical relationships.

Use Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications and Ugly's Electrical References to reinforce calculations and practical electrical formulas. Work through calculations repeatedly rather than relying only on memorization.

Construction safety should be reviewed using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Journeyman electricians may encounter ladders, scaffolds, tools, temporary wiring, excavation, energized systems, material-handling hazards, and other construction-site conditions.

General-industry safety preparation should include applicable subjects from 29 CFR 1910. Candidates should understand how workplace electrical-safety requirements differ from construction-specific OSHA rules.

NFPA 70E should be used to reinforce electrical workplace safety concepts. Review shock risk, arc-flash risk, approach boundaries, protective equipment, work practices, and establishing electrically safe work conditions.

For fire alarm systems and emergency circuits, study relevant NEC requirements together with NFPA 72. Nebraska specifically identifies emergency circuits, including fire alarm circuits, among the subjects covered by its electrical examination program.

Blueprint reading is also identified as a Nebraska electrical examination subject. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting symbols, circuits, electrical layouts, equipment locations, and other information found on construction drawings.

When practicing, separate knowledge errors from navigation errors. If a question is missed because you selected the wrong NEC article, the solution is more code-navigation practice. If the problem is a calculation error, additional theory and math practice may be needed.

Timed practice is useful even for an open-book examination. Candidates should become comfortable determining when an answer is known immediately and when reference lookup is necessary. Efficient use of the books can preserve time for calculations and more difficult code questions.
