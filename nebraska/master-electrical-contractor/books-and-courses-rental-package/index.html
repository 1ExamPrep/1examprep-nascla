Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program provides a standardized examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians that may be accepted by participating state and local licensing agencies.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division participates in the NASCLA program and administers the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination, Journeyman Electrician examination, and Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Candidates pursuing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician route in Nebraska must take the examination through the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Nebraska specifically requires the NASCLA examination date to be after August 1, 2025 for the result to be accepted through its current NASCLA pathway. Candidates preparing for Nebraska licensure should therefore follow the Nebraska State Electrical Division's application and examination procedures rather than independently taking the NASCLA electrical examination through another participating jurisdiction.

The examination covers professional electrical knowledge involving electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, safety, wiring practices, equipment, circuit protection, grounding and bonding, calculations, project design and management, special electrical systems, and related technical subjects.

Nebraska adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2024. Electrical examinations administered through the Nebraska State Electrical Division transitioned to the 2023 NEC at that time. Candidates preparing for Nebraska electrical licensing should become thoroughly familiar with the 2023 NEC and its organization.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book examination. Approved reference books may be used during testing according to the current NASCLA and examination-provider rules.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the Nebraska State Electrical Board. The state regulates electrical contractors, journeyman electricians, residential electrical workers, specialty installers, apprentices, and other electrical credentials.

The current Nebraska Electrical Contractor qualification information provides two principal pathways for examination eligibility. An applicant may be a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university and have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician, or the applicant may have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

Nebraska requires examination applicants to complete the applicable licensing application and obtain approval before testing. Once approved, candidates receive information concerning examination scheduling and the examination-provider process.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2024. Electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC at the same time, making familiarity with the 2023 Code an important part of current Nebraska electrical license preparation.

Nebraska participates directly in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician NASCLA exam may be used through the Nebraska pathway, but Nebraska places a specific condition on acceptance: the examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and the exam date must be after August 1, 2025.

Candidates should not assume that a NASCLA Electrical Contractor result taken elsewhere automatically satisfies Nebraska's requirement. Following the Nebraska State Electrical Division's application and scheduling process is important for candidates who intend to use the NASCLA examination for Nebraska licensure.

Nebraska electrical licenses are subject to state renewal requirements. Electrical license holders must complete continuing education during the licensing period, including required National Electrical Code education, to maintain an eligible license.

Passing the NASCLA examination fulfills an accepted examination component but does not automatically issue the Nebraska Electrical Contractor license. Applicants remain responsible for satisfying experience, application, documentation, and other requirements established by the Nebraska State Electrical Division.
