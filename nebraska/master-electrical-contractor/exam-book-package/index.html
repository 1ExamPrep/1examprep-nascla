Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program and is accepted or administered by participating licensing agencies, including the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination evaluates a broad range of electrical contractor knowledge. Candidates should prepare for electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, grounding and bonding, circuit protection, wiring methods, electrical equipment, special systems, tools and test equipment, and energy-related subjects.

The 270-minute testing period gives candidates four hours and thirty minutes to work through the examination. Effective preparation should include both technical review and time-management practice. Candidates need to decide when a question can be answered from trade knowledge and when a reference lookup or calculation is necessary.

Nebraska has adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code as the state's minimum electrical wiring standard. Nebraska electrical testing transitioned to the 2023 NEC beginning August 1, 2024. Candidates preparing for Nebraska electrical licensing should therefore be comfortable working with the 2023 NEC and understanding how its requirements apply to common electrical installations.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials in accordance with the current NASCLA, Nebraska State Electrical Division, and PSI examination rules.

Open-book testing still requires extensive study. Electrical reference materials contain thousands of pages of technical information, and a candidate who must search blindly through unfamiliar books can lose valuable testing time. Preparation should therefore include repeated work with the references before exam day.

The National Electrical Code deserves significant navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Common study areas include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, and special installations.

Supporting references should also become familiar tools. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction-industry safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. Electrical theory references support calculations and fundamental electrical principles. Project-management and contractor references address planning, communication, coordination, risk, and other professional responsibilities.

NASCLA examination rules permit approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed. References must otherwise comply with current testing rules and may not contain unapproved loose or attached papers. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their books according to the current candidate information bulletin before testing.

Timed reference practice can be particularly useful. Read a practice question, identify the subject, select the book most likely to contain the answer, locate the supporting information, and record the time required. Repeated practice helps develop faster navigation and a clearer understanding of how the references work together.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division under the Nebraska State Electrical Board. The state regulates electrical contractors, journeyman electricians, residential journeyman electricians, specialty license holders, and other electrical classifications.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division is an administering agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. Nebraska administers the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor NASCLA examinations.

Nebraska's current NASCLA participation rules require the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination to be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The examination date must be after August 1, 2025, for the result to be accepted under Nebraska's current NASCLA pathway.

An applicant for a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license may qualify through specific education and experience pathways. Nebraska recognizes an applicant who is a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course at an accredited college or university and has at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

Nebraska also recognizes an applicant who has at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. State law additionally provides an experience pathway based on at least five years of acceptable experience in planning, laying out, supervising, and installing wiring, apparatus, or equipment for electrical light, heat, and power.

Nebraska's examination scheduling process begins with approval from the State Electrical Division. Candidates cannot simply schedule an exam directly with PSI without first submitting an examination application and receiving approval. Once approved, the Division sends eligibility information to PSI and the candidate receives scheduling instructions.

Nebraska adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2024. Electrical examinations administered through the Nebraska system transitioned to the 2023 NEC on that date, making familiarity with the current Code particularly important for applicants.

Nebraska electrical applicants must also complete the state's lawful-presence attestation requirements as part of the licensing process. This documentation should be submitted with the licensing materials so issuance is not delayed after the candidate satisfies the examination requirement.

Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska electrical contractor license. The examination satisfies an accepted examination component, while the Nebraska State Electrical Board remains responsible for determining whether an applicant meets the experience, application, documentation, and other licensing requirements.

  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace-safety standard addressing practices and procedures intended to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while working on or around electrical systems and equipment.
  • Ugly's Electrical References
    A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, conversions, calculations, tables, and technical information useful for electrical problem solving.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference connecting electrical theory, calculations, and fundamental electrical principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

This makes it easier to determine whether a question involves general construction safety, electrical workplace safety, or another safety-related requirement.

Project-management preparation should include planning, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, documentation, and other responsibilities associated with professional electrical contracting. The NASCLA contractor reference and project-management material help candidates prepare for subjects beyond electrical installation alone.

The ASHRAE/IES energy standard adds an energy-efficiency component to the reference set. Candidates should become comfortable with its terminology and organization so relevant requirements can be located efficiently when needed.

Timed practice can bring the entire reference collection together. Choose questions from several subject areas, identify the correct reference, locate the supporting information, complete any necessary calculations, and measure the time required. Topics that repeatedly require long searches can then receive additional study attention.
