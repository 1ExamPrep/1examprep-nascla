Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized trade examinations that participating jurisdictions can use as part of their licensing process.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The current NASCLA electrical examination content includes electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and special equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-efficiency or energy-management material.

The exam is designed to measure practical electrical knowledge and the ability to apply technical information. Some questions may be solved through direct trade knowledge, while others may require a calculation or a search through an approved reference. Candidates should be prepared to move between the National Electrical Code, OSHA regulations, NFPA 70E, electrical theory resources, project-management materials, and supporting technical references.

The 270-minute testing period makes pacing important. Candidates should practice determining whether a question requires a reference lookup or can be answered directly. When a lookup is necessary, identifying the subject first can help narrow the search and make the highlighted and tabbed organization more effective.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may bring approved references into the examination center in accordance with current examination rules.

NASCLA permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed. References must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain loose or attached additional papers. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary tabs such as removable notes are not allowed.

This makes a highlighted and tabbed package particularly useful for candidates who want to build reference-navigation habits before exam day. Permanent indexing can make major sections easier to reach, while highlighting helps important provisions stand out during study and technical review.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable locating definitions, branch-circuit requirements, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment requirements, special occupancies, and special electrical systems.

Safety references should also become familiar tools. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 covers federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. Understanding how the two references differ and how each is organized can make safety-related research more efficient.

Electrical theory references should be used actively for calculations. Practice solving voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, and related electrical problems. Develop a repeatable process for identifying known values, selecting the correct formula, completing the calculation, and determining whether a Code requirement affects the final result.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The Division regulates electrical contractors, electricians, specialty installers, apprentice registrations, and electrical inspection requirements throughout the state.

Nebraska participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and administers the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Nebraska specifically requires the examination to be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and the examination date must be after August 1, 2025 for the NASCLA result to be accepted.

Nebraska's Electrical Contractor license has specific qualification requirements. An applicant may qualify by graduating from a four-year electrical engineering program at an accredited college or university and having at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

An applicant may also qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. The applicant must document the qualifying background as required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division during the application process.

Nebraska uses the 2023 National Electrical Code as the minimum electrical wiring standard, subject to Nebraska amendments. The 2023 NEC became effective in Nebraska on August 1, 2024, and Nebraska electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC beginning on that date.

Nebraska electrical examination applicants must also complete the state licensing application process. The Nebraska State Electrical Division provides online licensing applications through its current licensing and permitting system, as well as application information for candidates pursuing electrical licenses.

Nebraska's electrical examination information states that state exams may cover the 2023 National Electrical Code, basic electrical theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits, including fire-alarm circuits. Candidates pursuing Nebraska licensure should therefore distinguish between NASCLA trade-examination preparation and any Nebraska-specific material required through the state's licensing process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license. The NASCLA result can satisfy an accepted examination requirement, while the Nebraska State Electrical Division remains responsible for determining whether the applicant satisfies the qualifications and application requirements associated with the license.

Nebraska also maintains reciprocal licensing agreements for the Electrical Contractor license with certain other states. Reciprocity is a separate licensing pathway and should not be confused with taking the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination through Nebraska.

Test Information and Study Materials

A highlighted and tabbed reference package becomes most effective when candidates use the books throughout the entire preparation process. The purpose of highlighting and indexing is not simply to make the books look organized. The real benefit comes from repeated practice using that organization to locate technical information efficiently.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice navigating services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and special systems. Learn how to use the index, tables, article structure, notes, exceptions, and cross-references.

Electrical theory should be reinforced through active calculations. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, motors, transformers, and other electrical concepts. For each calculation, identify what the question provides, determine what must be solved, select the correct formula, calculate carefully, and then determine whether the NEC changes the final answer.

Safety review should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand the role of each reference and become comfortable locating safety-related topics without searching through the wrong book.

Project-management and contractor-business references should remain part of the study schedule. Electrical contracting includes planning, coordination, estimating concepts, communication, scheduling, risk, documentation, and professional management responsibilities in addition to technical installation knowledge.

Energy-efficiency material should also be reviewed. Become familiar with the structure and terminology of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 so related requirements can be found efficiently if they appear during the examination.

Timed practice is especially useful. Work through sample questions with the highlighted and tabbed reference set. Track how quickly you identify the topic, select the correct reference, locate the relevant section, and determine the answer. Over time, this can reveal which books and subjects require additional preparation.
