## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may bring approved references into the examination center in accordance with current examination rules.

NASCLA permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed. References must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain loose or attached additional papers. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary tabs such as removable notes are not allowed.

This makes a highlighted and tabbed package particularly useful for candidates who want to build reference-navigation habits before exam day. Permanent indexing can make major sections easier to reach, while highlighting helps important provisions stand out during study and technical review.

## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The Division regulates electrical contractors, electricians, specialty installers, apprentice registrations, and electrical inspection requirements throughout the state.

Nebraska participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and administers the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Nebraska specifically requires the examination to be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and the examination date must be after August 1, 2025 for the NASCLA result to be accepted.

Nebraska's Electrical Contractor license has specific qualification requirements. An applicant may qualify by graduating from a four-year electrical engineering program at an accredited college or university and having at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

An applicant may also qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. The applicant must document the qualifying background as required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division during the application process.

Nebraska uses the 2023 National Electrical Code as the minimum electrical wiring standard, subject to Nebraska amendments. The 2023 NEC became effective in Nebraska on August 1, 2024, and Nebraska electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC beginning on that date.

Nebraska electrical examination applicants must also complete the state licensing application process. The Nebraska State Electrical Division provides online licensing applications through its current licensing and permitting system, as well as application information for candidates pursuing electrical licenses.

Nebraska's electrical examination information states that state exams may cover the 2023 National Electrical Code, basic electrical theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits, including fire-alarm circuits. Candidates pursuing Nebraska licensure should therefore distinguish between NASCLA trade-examination preparation and any Nebraska-specific material required through the state's licensing process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license. The NASCLA result can satisfy an accepted examination requirement, while the Nebraska State Electrical Division remains responsible for determining whether the applicant satisfies the qualifications and application requirements associated with the license.

Nebraska also maintains reciprocal licensing agreements for the Electrical Contractor license with certain other states. Reciprocity is a separate licensing pathway and should not be confused with taking the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination through Nebraska.
