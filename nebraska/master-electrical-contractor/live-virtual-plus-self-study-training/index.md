## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program consists of open-book electrical trade examinations, including the Electrical Contractor examination. Authorized references may be used according to the applicable examination and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Electrical codes, safety standards, theory textbooks, and project-management resources contain a large amount of information. Candidates who know how their books are organized can approach reference questions more efficiently than candidates who begin every lookup from an unfamiliar starting point.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive substantial navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special systems.

A useful process is to identify what information is provided, determine what must be solved, select the correct formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC provision affects the final result.

Safety preparation should include OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. Understanding how each reference is organized can help candidates determine where a particular safety topic is most likely to be found.

## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The Division regulates electrical contractors, electricians, specialty license holders, apprentices, permits, inspections, and electrical licensing requirements throughout the state.

Nebraska participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and administers the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Nebraska requires candidates using this examination for state licensing purposes to take it through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, with an examination date after August 1, 2025.

Nebraska's Electrical Contractor license has multiple qualification pathways. One pathway allows an applicant to qualify as a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course at an accredited college or university with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

Another pathway allows an applicant to qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician. Nebraska's current state electrical license application also identifies an experience pathway based on at least five years of acceptable experience in planning for, laying out, supervising, and installing wiring, apparatus, or equipment for electrical light, heat, and power.

Nebraska adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2024. Electrical permits received by the Division on or after that date are subject to the 2023 NEC with Nebraska amendments, while existing permits remain under the Code edition applicable when the permit was issued.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division also states that electrical examinations are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code. State examination material may include the NEC, basic electricity and theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits, including fire-alarm circuits.

These Nebraska-specific subjects should be distinguished from the standardized NASCLA trade-examination preparation. Candidates should understand whether they are preparing for the NASCLA examination, a Nebraska-specific examination, or additional licensing requirements connected with the credential they are seeking.

Passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license. Applicants must still satisfy the applicable experience qualifications, complete the state licensing application, submit required documentation, and receive approval from the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Nebraska also has reciprocal licensing agreements for the Electrical Contractor license with certain states. Reciprocity is a separate pathway from taking the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination through Nebraska and should be treated as a different licensing option.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should combine structured technical instruction with repeated independent practice. Live virtual classes help candidates organize a broad examination scope into focused subjects, while self-study provides additional time to reinforce difficult topics and build stronger reference-navigation habits.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code and use it as a working reference. Practice using the index, following cross-references, locating tables, reviewing definitions, and moving between related articles. Rather than relying entirely on memorized section numbers, learn how the NEC is structured so you can find requirements efficiently.

Electrical theory should be reinforced through calculations. Practice circuit relationships, voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, and other fundamental electrical problems. Focus on understanding how the calculation works and when Code requirements must be applied.

Safety study should involve direct use of OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn the purpose of each reference and practice locating relevant material. This can help candidates avoid searching through the wrong book during open-book practice.

Project-management and contractor-business references should also remain part of the study schedule. Electrical contractors work within projects involving planning, coordination, documentation, risk management, communication, and professional responsibility. Energy-efficiency material should be reviewed so candidates become familiar with the organization and terminology of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Timed reference drills are especially useful for open-book preparation. Select a practice question, identify the subject, decide which reference is appropriate, locate the supporting information, and measure how long the process takes. Repeated practice can reveal which books and subjects require additional attention.

Live training can help establish the study framework, while self-study gives candidates the repetition needed to make that framework useful. Candidates can revisit technical material discussed during class, practice NEC lookups, repeat electrical calculations, and spend additional time on weaker areas between live sessions.
