Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials during the test according to current NASCLA, Nebraska State Electrical Division, and PSI examination rules.

An open-book exam still requires extensive preparation. The National Electrical Code and supporting references contain a large amount of technical material, and searching unfamiliar books during a timed test can quickly consume valuable minutes. Candidates should prepare by learning the structure of each major reference before exam day.

Reference navigation should be practiced alongside technical study. Become familiar with the NEC index, definitions, chapters, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice locating requirements involving branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, and special electrical installations.

Supporting references are also important. Electrical theory questions may require formulas or principles found in electrical textbooks. Safety questions can involve OSHA construction regulations or NFPA 70E. Project-management questions may draw from project-management and contractor references, while energy-related material may involve the applicable ASHRAE/IES standard.

NASCLA examination guidance allows approved reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed while otherwise remaining unmarked. Temporary tabs and additional loose or attached papers are not permitted. Candidates should follow the current examination bulletin when preparing books for testing.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division under the State Electrical Board. Nebraska licenses electrical contractors, journeyman electricians, residential journeyman electricians, and other regulated electrical classifications.

An applicant for a Nebraska Electrical Contractor license may qualify through multiple experience pathways. One pathway allows a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering program at an accredited college or university to qualify with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a journeyman electrician.

Another pathway allows an applicant with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician to qualify for the electrical contractor examination. Nebraska's current licensing application also identifies a pathway based on at least five years of experience acceptable to the Board involving planning, laying out, supervising, and installing wiring, apparatus, or equipment for electrical light, heat, and power.

Nebraska has adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code. Electrical permits received by the State Electrical Division on or after August 1, 2024, are subject to the 2023 NEC, and Nebraska electrical examinations transitioned to the 2023 NEC beginning on that date.

Nebraska requires electrical license applicants to complete the state's lawful-presence attestation requirements as part of the licensing process. Examination applicants should complete the required documentation so that licensing can proceed after successful completion of the examination.

Applicants using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination must take the exam through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska's NASCLA participating-agency requirements state that the examination date must be after August 1, 2025, for the NASCLA examination result to be accepted under the current program.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create a Nebraska electrical contractor license. The examination satisfies an accepted examination component, while Nebraska separately evaluates experience, application documentation, and other state licensing qualifications.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan for the Nebraska NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination should combine Code review with electrical theory, calculations, safety, project management, and repeated reference navigation. Passive reading alone may not provide the practice needed to perform efficiently on a timed open-book examination.

Begin with foundational NEC subjects. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Practice locating the actual Code provision that controls the answer rather than relying exclusively on memory.

Electrical theory should be studied through problem solving. For each calculation, identify the information provided, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate electrical relationship or formula, maintain consistent units, and complete the calculation carefully. Then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the final answer.

Safety preparation should include familiarity with both OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E electrical workplace safety concepts. Learn the role of each reference and practice recognizing which source applies to different types of safety questions.

Project design and management should also receive regular attention. Electrical contractors are responsible for more than installation work. Planning, estimating, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, and project execution are important professional responsibilities represented in NASCLA electrical preparation materials.

Use timed reference practice as the examination approaches. Select a practice problem, identify the subject, choose the most likely reference, locate the information, and track the time required. If certain topics repeatedly take too long to find, spend additional time learning that portion of the reference.

Nebraska candidates should also become familiar with state-specific requirements outside the NASCLA technical examination. The Nebraska State Electrical Division identifies the Nebraska State Electrical Act and state rules as important material within its broader electrical licensing process. Candidates should complete all state requirements associated with their examination authorization and licensing application.
