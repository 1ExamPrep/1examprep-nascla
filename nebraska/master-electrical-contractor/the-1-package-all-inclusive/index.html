Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes an examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Nebraska participates in the program through the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

Nebraska electrical examinations use the 2023 National Electrical Code. Nebraska adopted the 2023 NEC for electrical permitting and examination purposes effective August 1, 2024. The Nebraska State Electrical Division identifies the 2023 NEC, basic electricity and theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits including fire alarm circuits among the subjects covered by state electrical examinations.

The NASCLA electrical examination is built around the knowledge used by experienced electrical professionals and covers a broader range of subjects than code memorization alone. Candidates should prepare for electrical theory, safety, wiring systems, grounding and bonding, equipment, project design and management, special systems, energy-related requirements, and other professional electrical topics.

Nebraska's participation in the NASCLA electrical program gives candidates an additional examination pathway, but passing an examination does not automatically issue an Electrical Contractor license. The Nebraska State Electrical Division determines whether an applicant meets the state's qualifications and licensing requirements.

Nebraska's current Electrical Contractor application requires applicants to document qualifying education or electrical experience. Candidates should organize this information before submitting an examination or licensing application so the State Electrical Division can evaluate eligibility.

State Requirements

The Nebraska State Electrical Division administers electrical licensing and electrical safety regulation in Nebraska. The state regulates Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, Residential Wiremen, Fire Alarm Installers, and other electrical license or registration categories.

Nebraska provides multiple qualification pathways for an Electrical Contractor applicant. Under current state requirements, an applicant may qualify as a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university with qualifying journeyman experience, through qualifying licensed journeyman experience, or through qualifying experience involving the planning, layout, supervision, and installation of electrical wiring, apparatus, or equipment.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division's current examination application also identifies an experience pathway based on at least five years of experience acceptable to the Board in planning for, laying out, supervising, and installing wiring, apparatus, or equipment for electrical light, heat, and power.

Nebraska's electrical licensing program currently uses the 2023 National Electrical Code. Exams switched to the 2023 NEC beginning August 1, 2024, making familiarity with the 2023 code particularly important for candidates preparing for Nebraska electrical licensing.

Applicants must complete the forms and supporting documentation required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska also requires applicable applicants to complete the state's lawful-presence attestation requirements as part of the licensing process.

After licensure, electrical contractors must continue to follow Nebraska's electrical laws, adopted code requirements, permitting procedures, and licensing rules. Examination preparation is therefore only one part of the larger process of becoming and remaining a properly licensed electrical professional.
