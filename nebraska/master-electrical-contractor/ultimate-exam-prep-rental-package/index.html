Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes an examination for Electrical Contractors, also identified for Master/Unlimited Electricians. NASCLA developed the examination using a national job analysis intended to represent knowledge and tasks performed by electrical professionals across participating jurisdictions.

Nebraska is a participating state for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Nebraska requires candidates using this pathway to take the examination through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska indicates that the exam date must be after August 1, 2025 for the NASCLA examination to be accepted under this participating-state arrangement.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination evaluates knowledge across several major areas of professional electrical work. Published NASCLA content domains include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy efficiency or management.

This broad content scope makes preparation about more than memorizing individual code sections. Candidates benefit from knowing how the references are organized, understanding electrical principles, recognizing terminology, and practicing how to identify the right source for a particular type of question. A structured preparation strategy should therefore combine code review, theory, calculations, safety concepts, project-management knowledge, and repeated reference-book navigation.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA examination is an examination credential, not a national electrical license. Passing a NASCLA Accredited Examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska electrical contractor or master electrician license. Candidates must complete the licensing process required by the Nebraska State Electrical Division.

  1. Review Nebraska qualification requirements. Determine whether you satisfy the experience or education requirements applicable to the Nebraska Electrical Contractor license examination.
  2. Submit the required Nebraska exam application. Nebraska requires candidates to apply and receive approval before scheduling the examination.
  3. Receive exam eligibility. Approved candidates receive eligibility information used to proceed with examination scheduling.
  4. Schedule the examination. Nebraska uses PSI as its third-party testing provider for Electrical Contractor, Journeyman, and Residential Wireman examinations.
  5. Prepare for the examination. Use your course, highlighted and tabbed references, electrical theory review, code-navigation practice, and practice-oriented study sessions to build familiarity with the tested material.
  6. Take and pass the applicable examination. Follow all current examination-day rules and instructions provided by Nebraska and the testing provider.
  7. Complete Nebraska licensing requirements. Passing an examination is one component of the licensing process. Complete the remaining state requirements before performing work that requires licensure.

State Requirements

For the Nebraska Electrical Contractor examination pathway, state rules establish applicant qualification requirements. An applicant for an Electrical Contractor license examination must either be a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a journeyman electrician, or have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

Nebraska's State Electrical Division administers the state's licensing process and requires candidates to obtain approval before testing. The state has also adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code for its electrical program. Nebraska began using the 2023 NEC effective August 1, 2024.

For candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in Nebraska, NASCLA identifies the Nebraska State Electrical Division as a participating state agency and states that the examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The qualifying examination date must be after August 1, 2025.

Candidates should distinguish between passing the examination and receiving a license. NASCLA examination results can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, but Nebraska retains control over its own licensing qualifications, applications, approvals, and other regulatory requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Successful NASCLA electrical exam preparation should combine technical understanding with fast reference navigation. Because the examination covers multiple subject areas, an effective study plan should rotate among code work, theory, calculations, safety, management concepts, and practical reference exercises.

Start with the 2023 NEC and develop familiarity with its layout. Practice using the index, locating relevant articles, and following cross-references. When working a practice question, avoid immediately looking for an answer key. First determine what the question is asking, identify the likely reference, locate the supporting section, and then confirm the answer. This builds the search process needed for an open-book trade examination.

Electrical theory deserves separate attention. Topics such as electrical quantities, circuit relationships, power, conductors, loads, and calculations become easier to apply when the underlying concepts are understood. Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications provide resources for reinforcing those fundamentals.

Safety preparation should include both construction jobsite safety and electrical workplace safety. The included OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 reference and NFPA 70E provide important sources for reviewing safety-oriented subjects associated with the NASCLA examination content domains.

Project design, estimating, management, and energy-efficiency topics also form part of the broader examination blueprint. This is where the included project-management, NASCLA business and project-management, and ASHRAE/IES energy references can become important components of a complete study routine.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books throughout your course preparation rather than saving them for the end. Repeated navigation builds familiarity with where information lives. When studying, make a habit of identifying both the correct answer and the reference location that supports it. Over time, this can make the book set feel less like nine separate resources and more like one organized examination library.
