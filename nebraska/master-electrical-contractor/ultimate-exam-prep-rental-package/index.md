## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes an examination for Electrical Contractors, also identified for Master/Unlimited Electricians. NASCLA developed the examination using a national job analysis intended to represent knowledge and tasks performed by electrical professionals across participating jurisdictions.

Nebraska is a participating state for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Nebraska requires candidates using this pathway to take the examination through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska indicates that the exam date must be after August 1, 2025 for the NASCLA examination to be accepted under this participating-state arrangement.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination evaluates knowledge across several major areas of professional electrical work. Published NASCLA content domains include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy efficiency or management.

This broad content scope makes preparation about more than memorizing individual code sections. Candidates benefit from knowing how the references are organized, understanding electrical principles, recognizing terminology, and practicing how to identify the right source for a particular type of question. A structured preparation strategy should therefore combine code review, theory, calculations, safety concepts, project-management knowledge, and repeated reference-book navigation.

## State Requirements

For the Nebraska Electrical Contractor examination pathway, state rules establish applicant qualification requirements. An applicant for an Electrical Contractor license examination must either be a graduate of a four-year electrical engineering course from an accredited college or university with at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a journeyman electrician, or have at least one year of experience acceptable to the Board as a licensed journeyman electrician.

Nebraska's State Electrical Division administers the state's licensing process and requires candidates to obtain approval before testing. The state has also adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code for its electrical program. Nebraska began using the 2023 NEC effective August 1, 2024.

For candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in Nebraska, NASCLA identifies the Nebraska State Electrical Division as a participating state agency and states that the examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The qualifying examination date must be after August 1, 2025.

Candidates should distinguish between passing the examination and receiving a license. NASCLA examination results can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, but Nebraska retains control over its own licensing qualifications, applications, approvals, and other regulatory requirements.
