The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical trade examinations for participating licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division administers the Residential Electrical Contractor examination under this program. Nebraska requires candidates using the NASCLA examination for state acceptance to take the examination through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The examination date must be after August 1, 2025 for the NASCLA result to be accepted in Nebraska.

Nebraska electrical examinations cover subjects that include the 2023 National Electrical Code, basic electricity and theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits including fire alarm circuits. Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska residential electrical license. Applicants must complete Nebraska's licensing process and meet the qualifications for the credential they are pursuing.

  1. Identify the Nebraska residential credential. Nebraska licenses Residential Journeyman Electricians under the Type RW classification.
  2. Review the experience requirements. Type RW applicants must satisfy the experience requirements established by the Nebraska State Electrical Board.
  3. Submit the examination application. Candidates must apply through the Nebraska State Electrical Division before they can be approved to test.
  4. Receive examination approval. The State Electrical Division reviews the application before sending eligibility information to PSI.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is administered by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Residential electrical work is covered by the Residential Journeyman Electrician Type RW credential when the work falls within the authorized residential scope.

A Type RW applicant must have at least three years of experience acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board in the electrical trade. Registration as an apprentice electrician during those years can serve as evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also permits an education-based pathway. The Board may allow one year of experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course. The current license application identifies a pathway of two years of verifiable experience plus successful completion of a qualifying post-high-school electrical wiring program resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology.

The current application fee for a Residential Journeyman Electrician, Type RW is $90. Nebraska's current 2025-2026 licensing schedule lists the Residential Wireman license fee at $37.50 per year of the two-year licensing period. Current licenses in this cycle expire December 31, 2026.

A Residential Journeyman Electrician is authorized to wire for or install electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for residential installations and to supervise apprentice electricians within the residential scope.

Nebraska defines a residential installation as one intended for a single-family or two-family residential dwelling or a multifamily residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height. Nebraska specifically notes that clubhouses at multifamily complexes must be wired by Type JA journeymen rather than under the Type RW classification.

Nebraska adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code beginning August 1, 2024. Electrical permits received by the State Electrical Division on or after that date are based on the 2023 NEC with applicable Nebraska provisions, and state electrical examinations moved to the 2023 NEC on the same date.

Electrical license applicants must also complete the applicable Nebraska citizenship attestation requirement associated with lawful-presence verification. Examination applicants are instructed to submit the required attestation so license issuance is not delayed after passing the examination.

Nebraska requires complete work-experience documentation. Applicants must provide complete employer information, and employer verification letters must be on company letterhead when required.

Nebraska electrical licenses expire on December 31 of even-numbered years. Renewal requires at least 12 hours of approved continuing education, with at least six of those hours covering the National Electrical Code.
