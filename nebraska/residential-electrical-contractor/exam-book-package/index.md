## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an **open book test**. NASCLA identifies the Residential Electrical Contractor examination as one of the open-book examinations offered through its Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of exam preparation. Candidates should develop a repeatable method for identifying the subject of a question, selecting the appropriate reference, finding the relevant provision, table, definition, formula, or technical requirement, and applying the information correctly.

The **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023** deserves significant attention. Nebraska adopted the 2023 NEC effective August 1, 2024, and electrical examinations administered through the state moved to the 2023 NEC beginning on that date. Candidates should be comfortable using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, chapters, individual articles, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references.

Residential NEC preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, dwelling-unit load calculations, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, receptacles, lighting outlets, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, appliances, motors, equipment, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be practiced alongside code navigation. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, electrical loads, and practical calculations. Understanding the underlying electrical principle often helps a candidate determine where to look for the applicable requirement.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-jobsite safety study, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. The International Residential Code provides broader residential construction context.

A productive open-book routine is to answer practice-oriented questions with the reference library available. Even when you believe you know the answer, locate the supporting information. Repeated navigation builds familiarity with the books while reinforcing technical knowledge.

## Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska Residential Journeyman Electrician license. Applicants must complete the Nebraska State Electrical Division licensing process and satisfy the state's experience and application requirements.

1. **Review the Type RW license requirements.** Nebraska licenses Residential Journeyman Electricians under the Type RW classification.
2. **Document qualifying electrical experience.** Applicants generally need three years of verifiable electrical experience acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board.
3. **Use qualifying education when applicable.** Nebraska permits an approved post-high-school electrical technology program to provide experience credit under the Board's rules.
4. **Submit the Nebraska electrical license and examination application.** Nebraska must approve the applicant before examination eligibility is sent to PSI.
5. **Prepare for the residential electrical examination.** Study the 2023 NEC, theory, safety, residential wiring, calculations, emergency and fire alarm circuits, and applicable state requirements.
6. **Take the NASCLA examination through Nebraska.** For Nebraska acceptance, the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division and must have an examination date after August 1, 2025.
7. **Complete Nebraska's state requirements.** Follow the Nebraska State Electrical Division instructions associated with the State Electrical Act and Board Rules.
8. **Receive the license.** The Nebraska State Electrical Division issues the Residential Journeyman Electrician license after all applicable licensing requirements are satisfied.

## State Requirements

The **Nebraska State Electrical Division** regulates statewide electrical licensing. Nebraska's Residential Journeyman Electrician classification is identified as **Type RW**.

An applicant for a Type RW Residential Journeyman Electrician license generally must have at least **three years of verifiable experience** acceptable to the Board in the electrical trade. Registration as an apprentice electrician during those years may constitute evidence of experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also provides an education-based pathway. The current state application identifies eligibility with **two years of verifiable experience plus successful completion of a qualifying post-high-school electrical wiring course resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology**. Board rules permit one year of experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course.

The Type RW license is valid only for **residential installations**. Nebraska defines a residential installation as an installation intended for a single-family or two-family residential dwelling or a multifamily residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division notes that clubhouses at multifamily complexes must be wired by Type JA journeymen rather than under the residential Type RW classification.

The current Nebraska application fee for a Residential Journeyman Electrician Type RW is **$90**. License issuance fees vary depending on the year in which the license is issued. For licenses issued in 2026, Nebraska currently lists a Type RW license fee of **$37.50**.

Nebraska adopted the **2023 National Electrical Code** effective August 1, 2024. Electrical permits received by the State Electrical Division on or after that date are based on the 2023 NEC with Nebraska amendments, and electrical examinations moved to the 2023 NEC at the same time.

The state also requires electrical license applicants to complete the applicable citizenship attestation requirement associated with verification of lawful presence before license issuance.

Nebraska states that candidates who successfully pass a state electrical examination receive **six hours of continuing education credit** toward the continuing education needed for license renewal.

The NASCLA examination pathway is not a substitute for Nebraska's licensing review. Experience, application approval, state requirements, and the scope of the Type RW license remain controlled by the Nebraska State Electrical Division.
