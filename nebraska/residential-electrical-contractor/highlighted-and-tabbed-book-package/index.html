Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Nebraska electrical license. Applicants must complete the Nebraska State Electrical Division licensing process and satisfy the qualifications associated with the credential they are pursuing.

  1. Identify the Nebraska residential electrical credential. Nebraska licenses Residential Journeyman Electricians under the Type RW classification.
  2. Review the experience requirements. Applicants generally need three years of verifiable electrical experience acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board.
  3. Use qualifying education when applicable. Nebraska provides an education-based pathway that can reduce the required practical experience when an applicant completes a qualifying post-high-school electrical technology program.
  4. Submit the state electrical license application. The Nebraska State Electrical Division reviews experience and application documentation before examination eligibility is established.
  5. Prepare with the highlighted and tabbed references. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, OSHA safety, NFPA 70E, fire alarm systems, residential construction, and contractor business subjects.
  6. Practice open-book navigation. Use the books actively while working technical questions so that finding information becomes part of the study routine.
  7. Complete the applicable examination. Follow Nebraska State Electrical Division and testing-provider requirements for scheduling and examination procedures.
  8. Complete Nebraska-specific requirements. Review the Nebraska State Electrical Act and other state electrical requirements associated with licensure.
  9. Receive the license after approval. The State Electrical Division issues the credential after all applicable requirements have been satisfied.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is regulated by the Nebraska State Electrical Division. Nebraska licenses several categories of electrical professionals, including Electrical Contractors, Journeyman Electricians, Residential Journeyman Electricians, Special Installers, Fire Alarm Installers, and Apprentice Electricians.

The residential journeyman license is identified as Type RW. A Residential Journeyman Electrician is licensed to wire for or install electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for residential installations and to supervise apprentice electricians within the authorized residential scope.

Nebraska currently requires a Type RW applicant to have at least three years of verifiable experience acceptable to the Board in the electrical trade. Registration as an apprentice electrician during those years may serve as evidence of experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also provides an education-based qualification pathway. A candidate may qualify with two years of verifiable experience plus successful completion of a post-high-school electrical wiring program resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology. Nebraska Board rules allow one year of experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course.

The Type RW credential is valid only for residential installations. Nebraska defines a residential installation as an installation intended for a single-family or two-family residential dwelling or a multifamily residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height.

Nebraska specifically notes that clubhouses at multifamily complexes must be wired by Type JA journeymen rather than under the Type RW residential classification.

The current Nebraska application fee for a Residential Journeyman Electrician Type RW is $90. For a license issued in an even-numbered year, the current license fee is $37.50. For a license issued in an odd-numbered year, the current license fee is $75.

Nebraska adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code effective August 1, 2024. Electrical permits received by the State Electrical Division on or after that date are based on the 2023 NEC with Nebraska amendments, and state electrical examinations moved to the 2023 NEC beginning on that same date.

Applicants for electrical licensing must also complete the applicable Nebraska citizenship attestation requirement associated with verification of lawful presence. Examination applicants are instructed to submit the required attestation so that license issuance is not delayed after a successful examination result.

Work-experience verification must be properly documented. Nebraska requires complete employer information, and verification letters from employers must be provided on company letterhead when required as part of the application process.
