Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes three electrical trade examinations: Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor. Nebraska is an administering state for all three examination categories.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around the knowledge and skills associated with electrical installation, service, repair, and maintenance commonly encountered in permanent dwelling units. Residential electrical candidates should be prepared to apply code requirements, electrical theory, calculations, safety practices, and practical field knowledge to residential electrical situations.

Major content areas identified for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination include:

  • Electrical Theory
  • Project Design and Management
  • Personal and Jobsite Safety
  • General Code Requirements
  • Grounding and Bonding
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Practices
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions
  • Tools and Test Equipment
  • Special Systems and Equipment Installations
  • Communication Systems
  • Energy Efficiency and Management

Nebraska's own electrical examination information identifies additional subjects applicants should study. State electrical examinations cover the 2023 National Electrical Code, basic electricity and electrical theory, the Nebraska State Electrical Act, and emergency circuits, including fire alarm circuits.

Nebraska transitioned its electrical examinations to the 2023 National Electrical Code beginning August 1, 2024. The Nebraska State Electrical Division specifically emphasizes thorough knowledge of the NEC as an important part of examination preparation.

For the NASCLA pathway, candidates must follow Nebraska's examination approval process. Once an applicant is approved to test, the Nebraska State Electrical Division submits eligibility information to PSI. PSI then provides examination scheduling instructions.

NASCLA also maintains a National Examination Database for its accredited examinations. NASCLA currently lists a $65 application fee when applying directly through the NASCLA examination system. An approved NASCLA application is valid for one year, with up to three examination attempts available during the eligibility period.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies all three examinations in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program as open-book trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an essential part of preparation. Candidates should learn how to recognize the subject of a question, select the appropriate book, locate the relevant rule, table, formula, definition, or technical requirement, and apply that information to the problem.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be one of the most frequently used references during study. Candidates should become comfortable with the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapter structure, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references.

Residential electrical preparation should include branch circuits, required dwelling-unit circuits, services, feeders, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, equipment, appliances, motors, and special electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be practiced alongside code navigation. Understanding voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, electrical loads, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, and common calculations helps candidates determine which code provision or formula applies to a particular question.

Safety references should also be used during study. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-industry safety preparation, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety practices. Candidates should learn where different categories of safety information are located rather than treating every safety question as a general NEC question.

A productive open-book routine is to answer practice-oriented questions while actively using the references. Identify the topic first, choose the correct book, navigate to the applicable section, confirm the technical requirement, and then solve the problem. Repeated navigation helps build familiarity with the reference library.

State Requirements

Nebraska licenses residential electrical professionals through the Nebraska State Electrical Division. The state's residential journeyman credential is the Residential Journeyman Electrician, Type RW license.

An applicant for a Nebraska Residential Journeyman Electrician license must generally have at least three years of experience acceptable to the State Electrical Board in the electrical trade. Nebraska's examination-approval policy describes this as three years or approximately 6,000 hours of employment history installing electrical equipment while employed by an electrical contractor.

Registration as an apprentice electrician during those years may constitute evidence of qualifying experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also permits education to provide experience credit. Successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course may provide one year of experience credit. The current state licensing application identifies a pathway of two years of verifiable experience together with successful completion of a qualifying post-high-school electrical wiring course resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology.

The Residential Journeyman Electrician license is limited to residential installations. Nebraska defines residential installations as electrical installations intended for a single-family or two-family residential dwelling, or a multi-family residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height.

A Type RW Residential Journeyman Electrician may install electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for residential installations and may supervise apprentice electricians within the scope authorized by the license.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division currently lists a $90 application fee for a Residential Journeyman Electrician license. License fees depend on the year in which the credential is issued. These state fees are separate from NASCLA examination-related charges and from this online exam prep product.

Nebraska also requires applicants for electrical apprenticeship and licensing to complete the applicable lawful-presence or citizenship attestation associated with the state's licensing process.

After a candidate is approved for a Nebraska electrical examination, the State Electrical Division submits eligibility to PSI. The candidate receives information from both the State Electrical Division and PSI regarding the next steps for examination scheduling.

Nebraska also awards six hours of continuing-education credit toward license renewal when an applicant successfully passes a state electrical examination.
