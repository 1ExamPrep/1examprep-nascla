Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical examinations used by participating licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division administers all three NASCLA electrical examination categories, including the Residential Electrical Contractor examination. For Nebraska acceptance, the examination must be taken through the Nebraska State Electrical Division, and Nebraska currently specifies that the qualifying examination date must be after August 1, 2025.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around electrical work typically encountered in permanent dwelling units. Preparation should include technical understanding, code navigation, electrical calculations, safety, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, project management, and residential electrical systems.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies the Residential Electrical Contractor exam as one of the three open-book examinations in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes organized reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice recognizing what a question is asking, selecting the correct reference, locating the applicable code section, table, definition, formula, or safety requirement, and applying that information accurately.

All rental books included in The 1 Package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting can help direct attention toward important information during review, while permanent tabs create visible navigation points throughout large codes, standards, manuals, and technical references.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial study attention because Nebraska's current electrical examinations are based on the 2023 NEC. Candidates should become familiar with Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, article organization, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references.

Residential NEC study should include services, feeders, branch circuits, dwelling-unit load calculations, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, receptacles, lighting, appliances, motors, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside NEC navigation. Nebraska specifically identifies Basic Electricity and Theory as an examination subject. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and practical calculations.

NFPA 72 supports preparation for fire alarm and emergency-circuit material. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction safety, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. The International Residential Code provides residential construction context, and ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels.

State Requirements

The Nebraska State Electrical Division administers electrical licensing and inspection under the Nebraska State Electrical Act. The Residential Journeyman Electrician, Type RW, is the Nebraska license specifically associated with qualifying residential electrical installations.

A Type RW applicant must have at least three years of experience acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board in the electrical trade. Registration as an apprentice electrician during those years may constitute evidence of the required experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska may allow one year of experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course. Current application materials also provide an education pathway involving two years of acceptable verifiable experience plus successful completion of a post-high-school electrical wiring program resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology.

The current application fee for a Residential Journeyman Electrician, Type RW is $90. During the 2025-2026 licensing period, Nebraska lists the Residential Wireman license fee at $37.50 per year. Nebraska electrical licenses in the current cycle expire on December 31, 2026.

The Type RW license authorizes the licensee to wire for or install electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for residential installations and to supervise apprentice electricians within that residential scope.

Nebraska defines a residential installation as an installation intended for a single-family or two-family residential dwelling or a multifamily residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height. Clubhouses at multifamily complexes require a Type JA journeyman rather than Type RW authority.

Nebraska currently uses the 2023 National Electrical Code as the minimum electrical code standard under the State Electrical Act, with applicable state amendments. Electrical examinations have been based on the 2023 NEC since August 1, 2024.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division currently processes applications, licenses, renewals, and related transactions through its online licensing and permitting system. Applicants must still meet the applicable qualification requirements before they are approved to test or receive a license.

Electrical license renewal requires 12 contact hours of approved continuing education, including at least six hours devoted to the National Electrical Code. Nebraska electrical licenses expire on December 31 of even-numbered years.

Candidates who successfully pass a Nebraska electrical license examination receive six hours of continuing education credit toward the continuing education total required for renewal.

Business Formation and EIN Filing included in The 1 Package establish important business components, but neither service replaces Nebraska electrical licensing. The license holder and business must still comply with all applicable licensing, electrical code, permitting, inspection, tax, employment, and business requirements.
