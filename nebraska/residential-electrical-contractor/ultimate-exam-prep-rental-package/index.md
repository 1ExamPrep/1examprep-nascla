## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Nebraska is regulated by the **Nebraska State Electrical Division**. The Residential Journeyman Electrician Type RW credential authorizes qualifying residential electrical work within the scope established by the State Electrical Act and State Electrical Board rules.

A Type RW applicant must have at least **three years of experience** acceptable to the Nebraska State Electrical Board in the electrical trade. Registration as an apprentice electrician for those years may constitute evidence of the required experience when approved by the Board.

Nebraska also allows educational credit toward the residential journeyman experience requirement. The Board may allow one year of experience credit for successful completion of an approved two-year post-high-school electrical course.

The current Nebraska application also identifies a pathway involving **two years of verifiable experience acceptable to the Board plus successful completion of a post-high-school electrical wiring program resulting in a Degree in Electrical Technology**.

The current application fee for a **Residential Journeyman Electrician, Type RW** is **$90**. For a license issued in an even-numbered year, the current Type RW license fee is **$37.50**. A license issued in an odd-numbered year carries the applicable higher amount for the two-year licensing period.

A Nebraska Residential Journeyman Electrician is licensed to wire for or install electrical wiring, apparatus, and equipment for residential installations and to supervise apprentice electricians within that residential scope.

Nebraska defines a residential installation as an installation intended for a **single-family or two-family residential dwelling or a multifamily residential dwelling not larger than three stories in height**. Clubhouses at multifamily complexes must be wired by Type JA journeymen rather than under the Type RW classification.

The Nebraska State Electrical Division's current examination information states that all license examinations other than apprentice registration cover subjects including the **National Electrical Code 2023, Basic Electricity and Theory, Nebraska State Electrical Act, and Emergency Circuits including Fire Alarm Circuits**.

Applicants must provide complete work-experience information. Nebraska requires complete employer addresses, and verification letters from employers must be on company letterhead when experience verification is required.

After a candidate successfully passes a Nebraska electrical examination, the State Electrical Division currently awards **six hours of continuing education credit** toward the total required for license renewal.

Nebraska electrical licenses expire on **December 31 of even-numbered years**. License renewal requires at least **12 hours of approved continuing education**, including at least six hours devoted to the National Electrical Code.
