# Nevada NASCLA Exam: How to Get Your Nevada General Contractor License

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 13 min read · 9 official sources cited

If you want to run commercial or general building work in Nevada, you need a license from the Nevada State Contractors Board. For B General Building and B-2 Residential and Small Commercial applicants, passing the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors can stand in for the Nevada trade exam. That makes the Nevada NASCLA exam one of the most practical routes to a Nevada general contractor license, especially if you may also want to work in other states.

## Does Nevada accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. The Nevada State Contractors Board appears on [NASCLA's list of participating agencies](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies) that accept the NASCLA commercial exam. The Board's own [FAQ page](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=757) says that passing the NASCLA Accredited General Building Exam can waive the Nevada trade exam if you are applying for the B (General Building) or B-2 (Residential and Small Commercial) classification.

The waiver covers only the trade exam. You still have to pass Nevada's business and law exam, which the Board calls the CMS exam, and you still have to meet the experience, financial, bond and background requirements that apply to every applicant. The NASCLA result does not replace any of those.

To use your score, you buy a NASCLA transcript and have it sent electronically to the Board. NASCLA stores results in its National Examination Database, and the [Board's endorsement page](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=1400) explains that applicants verify NASCLA scores through that database and send the transcript to the Board.

Keep in mind that the Board says it can require any applicant to take and pass an examination, so read the current application instructions and call licensing staff if your situation is unusual. If you are applying for a specialty classification such as electrical or plumbing, the NASCLA commercial exam does not help you, and you will take the Nevada trade exam for that classification.

## Who issues the Nevada general contractor license

Contractor licensing in Nevada is handled by the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/), often shortened to NSCB. The Board has offices in Las Vegas and Reno. Its listed licensing staff numbers are 702-486-1100 in Las Vegas and 775-688-1141 in Reno.

The Board licenses businesses, not just people. A sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC or corporation can hold a license as long as it has a qualifying party who has the required experience and passes the required exams. Corporations, limited partnerships and LLCs must be registered with the Nevada Secretary of State and must stay in good standing to keep the license. Everyone listed on the application must be at least 18.

The Board also sets a monetary limit on each license, which is the most you can contract for on a single site or subdivision for a single client. That limit drives the bond amount and the kind of financial statement you have to submit, so it is worth deciding early how large your typical project will be.

The Board runs a free Business Assistance Program over Zoom on the third Tuesday of each month, which is a useful way to ask questions before you apply.

## Nevada B General Building license and other classifications

Nevada defines its classifications in the Nevada Administrative Code, sections 624.140 through 624.572. There is no handyman license. The two classifications that matter for the NASCLA exam are B and B-2.

The B General Building classification covers building or remodeling structures that shelter or enclose people, animals or property. Under the Board's [classification document](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/07-06-26-General-Building-and-Specialty-Contractor-Classifications.pdf), a B project must involve three or more unrelated building trades, the B contractor must be the prime contractor, and building construction or remodeling must be the main purpose of the job. A B contractor cannot bid or perform single-trade projects, and cannot do plumbing, electrical, refrigeration and air conditioning, or fire protection work without the matching specialty license.

B-2 Residential and Small Commercial is a subclassification of B. It covers homes and smaller structures, and it carries the same prime contractor rules.

Here is how the exams line up:

- B General Building: Nevada CMS exam, plus either the Nevada B trade exam or a passing NASCLA commercial exam.
- B-2 Residential and Small Commercial: Nevada CMS exam, plus either the Nevada trade exam or a passing NASCLA commercial exam.
- A General Engineering and C specialty classifications: Nevada CMS exam plus the Nevada trade exam for that classification, unless another waiver applies.

## How to get a Nevada general contractor license, step by step

The order below keeps you from paying for things you cannot use yet. The Board only lets you schedule the Nevada CMS exam after it has reviewed your application, while the NASCLA exam runs on its own approval track through NASCLA.

1. Pick your classification (B or B-2) and decide on a monetary limit, since that affects your bond and financial statement.
2. Set up your business entity and, if it is a corporation, LLC or limited partnership, register it with the Nevada Secretary of State.
3. Apply for the NASCLA commercial exam at ned.nascla.org. NASCLA must pre-approve you before you can schedule with PSI.
4. Study, schedule and pass the NASCLA exam, then buy your transcript and send it to the Nevada State Contractors Board.
5. Gather proof of four years of experience in the last ten, usually four Certification of Work Experience forms completed by people other than you.
6. Prepare a financial statement that meets the Board's rules for your monetary limit.
7. Submit the Board application with the fee and the fingerprint background waiver form. Wait for the Board to authorize fingerprints before sending them.
8. When the Board sends your exam eligibility letter, schedule and pass the CMS exam with PSI.
9. Post your contractor's bond, pay the license fee and any Residential Recovery Fund fee that applies, and receive your license.

## The Nevada NASCLA exam: format, references and scheduling

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions and you get 330 minutes. It is open book, and you need 70 percent to pass (81 correct answers out of the 115 scored questions). The exam may also include a few unscored pretest questions mixed in with the real ones.

You apply online at ned.nascla.org. According to [NASCLA's PSI candidate bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/6393), applications are processed within about seven business days, and once you are approved NASCLA passes your name to PSI, which emails you scheduling instructions. You have one year from approval to test and up to three attempts in that year. If you do not pass in three tries, you can reapply after the year runs out.

Because the test is open book, the reference list matters as much as your construction knowledge. NASCLA publishes the approved reference list for the commercial exam, and you bring your own copies. NASCLA's bulletin limits how books may be marked, and handwritten notes are not allowed, so read the current rules before you highlight or tab anything.

With 330 minutes for 115 questions, you have a little under three minutes per question. Most people who struggle do not run out of knowledge, they run out of time looking things up. That is the reason tabbing and highlighting your books, and practicing with them under a timer, makes such a difference.

PSI schedules online or by phone. Cancel at least two days before your appointment if you need to move it, and arrive about 30 minutes early with valid government photo ID, or you can lose your fee.

## The Nevada contractors board exam for business and law (CMS)

Every Nevada applicant takes the business and law exam, which the Board and PSI call the CMS (Contractor Management Survey) exam. The NASCLA exam never waives it, and neither does licensure by endorsement.

Nevada does not use a NASCLA business and law guide for this test. Per the [PSI Nevada bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/304), the reference is the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada, published by PSI. The exam is open book. You bring your own copy, and you may highlight, underline and add permanent tabs, but you cannot write notes in it or use sticky notes.

The bulletin lists 60 scored questions plus 3 unscored ones, with 120 minutes for the scored portion plus a few extra minutes for the unscored items. You need 45 correct, which is 75 percent. Content areas include licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax and labor laws, project management, contracts, risk management, and environmental and safety rules. Financial management carries the most questions.

The published fee is $95 for one exam portion. If you fail, you wait two weeks before retesting. The Board's [exam page](https://nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=3268) says you get three attempts, a third failure voids your application, and you can reapply after 30 days. Scores come right after you finish.

## Experience, financial statement and bond requirements

Experience. Your qualifying party needs at least four years of experience within the ten years before you apply, working as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee or contractor in the classification you want. The Board's usual proof is four Certification of Work Experience forms, completed by someone other than you, adding up to at least 1,460 days. A current master certification from a government agency or documented military experience can also count. Accredited college or Board-accepted training can cover up to three of the four years if you document it.

Financial statement. The Board's [license limit page](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=23042) says a financial statement is required no matter how small a limit you request. For a limit of $25,000 or less you can use a Board form, a CPA-prepared statement or a GAAP balance sheet with an affidavit. Above $25,000 you need a CPA compiled, reviewed or audited statement, and at $1,000,000 or more only a CPA reviewed or audited statement is accepted. Compiled and self-prepared statements must be no older than six months, and reviewed or audited ones no older than a year. If your own finances fall short, the Board allows an optional indemnification from another person or company.

Bond. Every license needs a contractor's bond. The Board sets the amount between $1,000 and $500,000 based on your monetary limit and overall finances. You can use a surety bond from an insurer authorized in Nevada with an A rating or better, or post a cash bond, which carries a $200 administration fee at filing and at each renewal. If a bond lapses, the license is suspended until a new one is in place.

Background. Officers and qualified individuals are fingerprinted for a background check after the Board authorizes it.

## Costs and fees for a Nevada general contractor license

The Board publishes these figures. Confirm them on the [Board's FAQ page](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=757) before you pay, since fees can change.

- Application fee: $300, the same for every classification and not refundable.
- License fee: $600 for a two-year license. Nevada does not issue one-year licenses.
- Residential Recovery Fund: required if you do residential work, with the amount tied to your monetary limit and due at issuance and each renewal.
- Cash bond administration fee: $200 if you post a cash bond instead of a surety bond.
- CMS exam at PSI: $95 for one exam portion, per the PSI Nevada bulletin.
- NASCLA exam: paid to NASCLA and PSI when you apply and schedule. Check ned.nascla.org for the current amount, plus the transcript fee.

## Licensure by endorsement and working in other states

Nevada also offers licensure by endorsement. If the same qualified individual has held an active license in good standing in another state for the past four years, with no discipline or pending actions anywhere, the Board may waive the trade exam and the experience requirement. You still pass the Nevada business and law exam and submit everything else the application asks for.

The Board's [State Equivalency Chart](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/?p=1400) lists 12 states: Alabama, Arizona, California, Connecticut, Florida, Hawaii, Louisiana, New Mexico, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia. Equivalency is set classification by classification, and for some states the chart still requires the Nevada trade exam. Your other state completes a verification form that you send with your application.

The NASCLA exam works the other way. Once you pass, your result sits in the NASCLA database and you can send a transcript to any participating agency. Nevada is one of many states that accept it for commercial or general building work, so one passing score can support applications in several places, each with its own business and law test and other rules.

## A study plan for the Nevada NASCLA exam

Start by getting your books tabbed, whether you buy them prepared or do it yourself. Then read the NASCLA content outline so you know which references carry the most questions. Spend most of your time on the books that cover the largest share of the test.

Next, practice looking things up under time pressure. Take timed sets of questions with your actual books open, and keep a pace close to three minutes per question. Track which references slow you down and add tabs where you keep searching.

In the last week, take at least one full practice run that lasts the whole 330 minutes. It builds stamina, and it shows you whether you need to skip and return to hard questions. After you pass, switch to the PSI Nevada manual and prepare for the CMS exam, which is shorter but needs a higher 75 percent score.

## After you pass: license, renewal and keeping it active

Once your NASCLA transcript and CMS score are on file and the Board approves your experience, finances and background, you post your bond and pay the license fee. Nevada licenses run for two years and renew every two years.

Keep the license in good shape between renewals. The bond must stay continuous; insurers must give 60 days notice before canceling, and a lapse suspends the license. If your qualifying party leaves, notify the Board in writing within 10 days, and under NRS 624.285 you have 30 days to replace that person. Corporations and LLCs must stay in good standing with the Secretary of State. If you want to take on bigger jobs later, the Board lets you apply to raise your monetary limit with an updated financial statement.

## Nevada NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Does Nevada accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. The Nevada State Contractors Board accepts a passing NASCLA Accredited General Building Exam in place of the Nevada trade exam for B and B-2 applicants. You still take the Nevada CMS business and law exam and meet the other requirements.

### Do I still need the Nevada business and law exam if I pass NASCLA?

Yes. Every qualifying party takes the CMS exam, even when the trade exam is waived by NASCLA or by endorsement. It is open book and uses the PSI Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada.

### How much experience do I need for a Nevada B license?

The Board asks for at least four years of experience in the last ten years in the classification you want, as a journeyman, foreman, supervisor or contractor. Approved education can count for up to three of those years if you document it.

### How much does a Nevada contractor license cost?

The Board publishes a $300 application fee and a $600 fee for a two-year license. You also pay exam fees, bond costs, possibly a Residential Recovery Fund fee, and the cost of any CPA financial statement. Check the Board's site for current amounts.

### How big is the Nevada contractor bond?

The Board sets the bond between $1,000 and $500,000 based on your monetary limit and finances. You can post a surety bond or a cash bond, and a cash bond carries a $200 administration fee.

### Is the Nevada NASCLA exam open book?

Yes. The NASCLA commercial exam is open book, with 115 scored questions in 330 minutes and a 70 percent passing mark. You bring your own copies of the approved references, marked only as NASCLA's current rules allow.

### How many times can I retake the Nevada CMS exam?

You can retake it two weeks after a failure. The Board says a third failure voids your application, after which you can reapply after 30 days.

### Can I get a Nevada license by reciprocity?

Nevada offers licensure by endorsement for people licensed for the last four years in one of 12 states on its equivalency chart. It can waive the trade exam and experience requirement, but you still pass the Nevada business and law exam.

### Do I need a financial statement for a small Nevada license?

Yes. The Board requires a financial statement at every monetary limit. For $25,000 or less you can use a Board form or a GAAP balance sheet with an affidavit, while larger limits require CPA statements.
