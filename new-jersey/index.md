## Does New Jersey accept the NASCLA exam?

Not for licensing in the way many states do. [NASCLA's list of participating agencies](https://www.nascla.org/page/ParticipatingAgencies) for the commercial general building exam does not include New Jersey, so a passing NASCLA commercial score does not replace a New Jersey trade exam.

That does not mean NASCLA is irrelevant here. NASCLA publishes a New Jersey edition of its Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, and the state's PSI candidate bulletins name it as the reference for the business and law exam. If you are working toward a New Jersey electrical contractor license or a master plumber license, the business and law portion of your exam is built on this book.

The edition matters. [NASCLA's store](https://store.nascla.org/products/new-jersey-1st-edition) says testing centers allowed either the 1st or 2nd Edition through May 31, 2025, and that from June 1, 2025 the exams are based on the New Jersey 2nd Edition only. If you have an older copy, replace it before you test.

One exception to watch: the PSI bulletin we reviewed for the Master HVACR Contractor exam lists a different NASCLA reference, the Basic edition, rather than the New Jersey edition. If you are an HVACR candidate, read the current bulletin for your exam before you buy books.

## Who issues contractor licenses in New Jersey

New Jersey licenses its regulated trades through boards within the Division of Consumer Affairs. The ones tied to the New Jersey business and law exam are:

- The State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors, which issues the electrical contractor license and business permits. Board phone listed in its materials: (973) 504-6410.
- The State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, which issues the master plumber license.
- The State Board of Examiners of Heating, Ventilating, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors, which issues the Master HVACR Contractor license. Board phone listed in its bulletin: (973) 504-6250.

## New Jersey general contractors and home improvement work

New Jersey handles general and home improvement contractors differently from the licensed trades. Businesses that sell or perform home improvements on residential property must register every year with the Division of Consumer Affairs, according to the [Division's home improvement FAQ](https://njconsumeraffairs.gov/hic/Pages/FAQ.aspx). That FAQ does not list an exam for registration.

To register, you need commercial general liability insurance of at least $500,000 per occurrence, workers' compensation unless you are exempt, and additional security such as a bond or letter of credit of $10,000, $25,000 or $50,000 depending on your contract values. The published fees are $110 for an initial application and $90 to renew by March 31, with higher late and reinstatement fees.

If you plan to bid on state construction projects, the [Division of Property Management and Construction](https://nj.gov/www.nj.gov/treasury/dpmc/pub_how_to_do_business_with_division.shtml) requires contractors to be classified before they can work on DPMC-administered jobs. That process looks at financial ability, experience and bonding capacity rather than an exam. Local permits and other rules may also apply, so check with your municipality and the Division for your specific work.

## New Jersey license types and which exam each needs

Each board runs its own set of exams through PSI. Here is how they line up, based on the PSI candidate bulletins:

- Electrical contractor: the electrical contractor trade exam, the alarm systems exam, and the business and law exam. The [electrical bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/3984) names the NASCLA New Jersey 2nd Edition as the business and law reference.
- Master plumber: the master plumber trade exam, the master plumber practical exam, and the business and law exam. The [plumbing bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/3985) names the NASCLA New Jersey 2nd Edition. First-time candidates take all three on the same day.
- Master HVACR contractor: the HVACR trade exam and the business and law exam. First-time candidates take both on the same day. The [HVACR bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/3986) we reviewed lists a NASCLA Basic edition and certain New Jersey statutes, so confirm the reference with PSI or the board.

## How to get licensed in New Jersey, step by step

The exact paperwork differs by board, but the path for a licensed trade follows the same pattern. You cannot schedule anything with PSI until your board approves you.

1. Confirm which board licenses your work and read its current application and the PSI candidate bulletin for your exams.
2. Complete the experience your board requires. For electrical and plumbing, that generally means a four-year approved apprenticeship followed by a year of journeyman work.
3. Submit the exam application and fee to the board with proof of your experience.
4. Wait for your Examination Eligibility Notice or approval letter, which tells you how to pay and schedule.
5. Buy the NASCLA New Jersey 2nd Edition guide and any trade references, and prepare them with permanent tabs and highlighting.
6. Schedule with PSI online or by phone, then take your exams.
7. Retake only the parts you fail, within the board's limits.
8. Once you pass every required exam, complete the licensing packet the board sends you, including any business permit.

## The New Jersey business and law exam: format and rules

Across the electrical, plumbing and HVACR bulletins, the New Jersey business and law exam has the same shape: 50 questions in 130 minutes, and you need 70 percent, which is 35 correct. It is open book.

You bring your own copy of the reference. The electrical bulletin says you may highlight, underline or index the book with permanent tabs, but you may not write notes in it. Removable sticky tabs are not allowed. That is why many candidates buy the guide already highlighted and tabbed, or buy pre-printed tabs that are made to be permanent.

With 130 minutes for 50 questions, you have about two and a half minutes per question. That is plenty if you know where things are in the book and tight if you do not. The guide covers the business, legal and project management side of running a contracting company, and the New Jersey edition is written for contractors working under New Jersey rules.

The published business and law exam fee is $47 in each of the three bulletins. PSI fees are not refundable or transferable, and they are good for one year from payment. To reschedule without losing your fee, cancel at least two days before your appointment. Arrive about 30 minutes early with valid photo ID that matches your registration.

## Retakes and time limits

Retake rules vary by board, so read your own bulletin.

For electrical contractor candidates approved on or after March 16, 2015, the [electrical bulletin](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/3984) says your first attempt must be within two years of your eligibility date. If you fail, you retake only the parts you failed, after waiting six months from your last test date. You must pass all three exams within three attempts and within five years of eligibility, or you reapply to the board.

For master plumber and HVACR candidates, the bulletins allow unlimited attempts, with a six-month wait between them.

## Experience and other requirements

Electrical contractor. The [board's FAQ](https://njconsumeraffairs.gov/elec/Pages/FAQ.aspx) says applicants must complete a four-year apprenticeship through a program approved by the U.S. Department of Labor, followed by one additional year as a journeyman. Citizenship and a Social Security number are not required if you meet the other requirements. Each licensee can serve as the qualifier for only one electrical contracting business, and each party to a joint venture doing electrical work must hold a business permit.

Master plumber. Under [N.J.A.C. 13:32-2.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-32-2-1), you must be at least 21 to sit for the plumbing exam. [N.J.A.C. 13:32-2.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-32-2-2) requires either a four-year Department of Labor approved apprenticeship or a bachelor's degree in mechanical, plumbing or sanitary engineering, and either way the program must include propane education. After that, you need one year of journeyman hands-on experience in New Jersey totaling at least 1,200 hours. Supervising, engineering and estimating time does not count.

Insurance and bond rules for licensed trades are set by each board's regulations. We did not find them stated in the materials we reviewed, so check with your board before you apply.

## Costs and exam fees

- Master plumber trade exam: $87.
- Master HVACR trade exam: $84.
- Electrical late renewal within 30 days of expiration: $50, per the electrical board FAQ.

## How to study for the New Jersey business and law exam

Start by learning the layout of the NJ guide. Read the table of contents and index, and make sure every major chapter has a tab you can find without looking.

Next, work practice questions with the book open and a timer running. Aim for about two minutes per question so you leave time to check your answers. When a question takes too long, note where the answer was and add a highlight or move a tab.

Pay extra attention to material that is specific to New Jersey, since that is where general contracting knowledge will not carry you. Any question that involves a calculation deserves a slow, careful read.

If you are taking a trade exam on the same day, split your study time and do at least one practice session that covers both tests back to back so you are ready for a long day.

## After you pass: licensing, renewal and continuing education

Once you pass every required exam, your board sends licensing information. Electrical contractors also receive a pressure seal that belongs only to the licensee and must be returned if the license is suspended, revoked, made inactive, or the licensee stops serving as qualifier.

Electrical licenses and business permits expire on March 31 every three years. The electrical board FAQ lists a $50 late fee if you renew within 30 days after expiration, and renewing later than that leads to suspension. You can also renew as inactive.

For continuing education, the [IAEI's New Jersey page](https://www.iaei.org/page/new-jersey-electrical-ceus) reports 34 hours per three-year cycle for electrical contractors, with at least 9 hours on the current NEC and 1 hour on state rules. The master plumber rules include a continuing education subchapter, so confirm the current hours with the plumbing board.

## New Jersey NASCLA exam and license FAQ

### Does New Jersey accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

New Jersey is not on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the commercial general building exam. New Jersey does use the NASCLA New Jersey 2nd Edition guide as the reference for its business and law exam for electrical contractors and master plumbers.

### Which book do I need for the New Jersey business and law exam?

The electrical and plumbing PSI bulletins name the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, New Jersey 2nd Edition. NASCLA says exams have used the 2nd Edition only since June 1, 2025, so older editions are not allowed.

### How many questions are on the New Jersey business and law exam?

The exam has 50 questions and you get 130 minutes. You need 70 percent, or 35 correct, to pass.

### Is the New Jersey business and law exam open book?

Yes. You bring your own copy of the approved guide. You can highlight, underline and use permanent tabs, but you cannot write notes in it.

### How much does the New Jersey business and law exam cost?

The published PSI fee is $47. Fees are not refundable or transferable and are valid for one year from payment.

### Do I need a license to be a general contractor in New Jersey?

Businesses that do home improvement work on residential property must register each year with the Division of Consumer Affairs and carry required insurance. Contractors on state projects must also be classified by DPMC. Check with the Division for the rules that apply to your work.

### What experience do I need for a New Jersey electrical contractor license?

The electrical board asks for a four-year apprenticeship through a U.S. Department of Labor approved program, plus one more year as a journeyman. You then pass the trade, alarm systems and business and law exams.

### How often can I retake the New Jersey business and law exam?

Plumbing and HVACR candidates can retake as often as needed with a six-month wait between attempts. Electrical candidates must wait six months between attempts and pass everything within three attempts and five years of eligibility.

### Are pre-printed tabs allowed in the New Jersey exam?

The bulletins allow permanent tabs, and sticky notes or removable tabs are not allowed. Our pre-printed tabs for the NJ 2nd Edition are made to be applied permanently.
