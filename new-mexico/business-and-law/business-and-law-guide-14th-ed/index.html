Open Book Test

The New Mexico Business and Law examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved reference publications according to the examination provider's rules.

The NASCLA Contractor's Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition, is identified as one of the reference books for this examination.

Open-book testing requires candidates to understand the examination subjects and locate relevant information efficiently. Familiarity with chapter titles, the table of contents, and important terminology can make reference navigation more productive.

New Mexico Open-Book Examination Rules:

  • Only designated reference materials may be used during the examination.
  • Approved references may be highlighted and underlined before the examination.
  • Permanent reference tabs are permitted when they comply with PSI requirements.
  • Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes, are not permitted.
  • Handwritten notes and other unauthorized writing inside references are prohibited.
  • Candidates may not add writing to reference materials during the examination.
  • Loose papers and unauthorized attached materials are not permitted.
  • Testing personnel determine whether reference materials comply with examination requirements.

For effective preparation, practice identifying a subject, locating the appropriate chapter, and reviewing the relevant passage without spending unnecessary time searching.

Licensing Steps

New Mexico contractor licensing is regulated by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department.

The licensing process involves selecting an appropriate classification, establishing a qualifying party, satisfying examination requirements, and completing the company licensing application.

Step 1: Determine Your Contractor Classification

Identify the classification corresponding to the construction services your business intends to perform. New Mexico maintains separate classifications for different construction trades and specialties.

Step 2: Establish Your Qualifying Party

Identify the individual who will serve as the qualifying party for the requested contractor classification. This individual must satisfy the applicable qualification and examination requirements.

Step 3: Complete the Qualifying Party Application

Prepare the qualifying party application and submit the required experience documentation through the established licensing process.

Step 4: Satisfy the Examination Requirements

Complete the applicable trade examination and satisfy the Business and Law requirement through the examination or an approved course, as applicable.

Step 5: Prepare with the NASCLA Basic 14th Edition

Study the approved NASCLA guide for Business and Law subjects. Review New Mexico-specific regulations and practice locating information in the permitted references.

Step 6: Submit the Company License Application

After the qualifying party satisfies the applicable requirements, the contracting business must submit its licensing application and supporting documentation.

Step 7: Complete Licensing Approval

Provide the required business documentation, bonding information, tax registration, and other materials applicable to the requested contractor license.

Contractors must complete the approval process before operating under a license requiring authorization from the Construction Industries Division.
