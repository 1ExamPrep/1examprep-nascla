Open Book Test

The New Mexico Business and Law examination is an open-book test. Candidates may consult approved publications according to PSI examination requirements.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic 14th Edition, is an approved reference for the examination.

Open-book testing requires preparation because candidates must understand the question, identify the relevant subject, and locate supporting information within the permitted references.

The included chapter tabs support practice with reference navigation and help readers become more familiar with the book's organization.

Important PSI Reference Material Rules:

  • Only approved reference materials are permitted in the examination room.
  • Permitted references may be highlighted and underlined before the examination.
  • Reference books may be indexed with permanent tabs.
  • Temporary tabs, including removable sticky notes, are not allowed.
  • Reference materials containing unauthorized writing are prohibited.
  • Candidates may not write in references during the examination.
  • Loose papers and unauthorized additional attached materials are not permitted.
  • Testing personnel determine whether reference materials comply with examination requirements.

Licensing Steps

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department.

The process generally involves establishing a qualifying party, satisfying the applicable examination requirements, and submitting the business licensing application.

Step 1: Identify Your Contractor Classification

Determine the classification appropriate for the construction services you intend to provide. New Mexico maintains separate classifications for general construction, electrical, mechanical, and specialty work.

Step 2: Establish Your Qualifying Party

Identify the individual who will satisfy the experience and examination requirements for the requested classification.

Step 3: Submit the Qualifying Party Application

Complete the required application and work experience affidavits. The qualifying party must receive approval before scheduling the applicable examinations.

Step 4: Satisfy the Business and Law Requirement

Complete the New Mexico Business and Law examination or an approved Business and Law course.

Step 5: Complete Any Required Trade Examination

Prepare for and complete the technical examination required for your contractor classification.

Step 6: Prepare the Company Application

Submit the appropriate contractor license application with the required business documentation, qualifying-party information, and supporting materials.

Step 7: Complete Licensing Approval

Provide the applicable bonding, tax registration, business entity, and other required documentation to complete the licensing process.

Contractors must satisfy the applicable licensing requirements before performing regulated contracting work in New Mexico.

State Requirements

The New Mexico Construction Industries Division regulates construction contracting and administers the state's contractor licensing requirements.

Licensed businesses must operate within their approved classifications and maintain compliance with applicable construction laws and regulations.

Important New Mexico Contractor Licensing Responsibilities:

  • License Classification: Select the classification corresponding to the work performed.
  • Qualifying Party: Maintain an appropriately certified individual responsible for the licensed classification.
  • Work Experience: Satisfy the experience requirements associated with the requested classification.
  • Business and Law Knowledge: Complete the examination or approved course requirement.
  • Trade Examination: Pass the applicable technical examination when required.
  • Business Application: Submit the completed company licensing application.
  • License Bond: Provide the required bonding documentation.
  • Tax Registration: Complete applicable New Mexico tax registration requirements.
  • Business Entity Documentation: Provide supporting organizational records when applicable.
  • Workers' Compensation: Maintain the coverage required for licensed contracting businesses.
