Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Candidates may use approved reference materials according to the applicable examination rules.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who understand both the construction material and the organization of their reference library. Having dozens of books available will not help if every question requires a long search through several unrelated references. A stronger approach is to recognize the subject first, identify the most appropriate book, and use the reference to confirm a specific technical detail.

The books included in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Candidates should use those features as navigation aids while studying. Become familiar with the table of contents, index, definitions, chapters, code sections, technical tables, and terminology used by each reference.

New Mexico's open-book examination rules allow approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References containing writing are not permitted. Additional loose or attached papers are also prohibited. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing, while temporary tabs such as removable Post-It notes are not allowed.

Regular reference practice can turn a large commercial construction library into a more manageable tool. When you study concrete, work with the concrete references. When you study accessibility, practice navigating the applicable accessibility standard. Apply the same approach to safety, masonry, steel, roofing, building codes, project management, and other exam subjects.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate New Mexico contractor classification. New Mexico contractor licenses are classification-specific, so applicants should identify the scope of work for which they intend to qualify.
  2. Identify the Qualifying Party. A contractor business must have an appropriately certified Qualifying Party for the classification under which it intends to operate.
  3. Meet the applicable experience requirements. Qualifying Party applicants must demonstrate the experience required for the classification being requested.
  4. Submit the applicable application. Provide the information and documentation required through the New Mexico contractor licensing process.
  5. Receive approval for testing. Qualifying Party applicants must complete the applicable approval process before scheduling required examinations.
  6. Complete the technical examination requirement. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for applicable commercial building licensing purposes.
  7. Satisfy the Business & Law requirement. New Mexico Qualifying Party applicants must also satisfy the state's applicable business and construction law knowledge requirement.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. After the required qualification and examination steps are completed, the contractor business must finish the applicable New Mexico licensing process.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. Contractor licenses are issued according to classifications and scopes of work, with qualifying requirements tied to the work the contractor intends to perform.

New Mexico requires an appropriately qualified individual to serve as the Qualifying Party for a licensed contractor business. Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy applicable experience and examination requirements for the classification they are seeking.

New Mexico law requires applicants for classifications such as general building to demonstrate technical knowledge and familiarity with applicable codes and minimum standards through an approved examination. Applicants must also satisfy requirements involving general business knowledge, division rules and regulations, and the Construction Industries Licensing Act.

The NASCLA commercial exam can satisfy an accepted technical trade-examination requirement for participating jurisdictions such as New Mexico. The NASCLA examination is not itself a contractor license. Candidates must still complete the applicable New Mexico licensing process.
