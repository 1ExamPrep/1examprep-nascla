Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is designed to evaluate knowledge across multiple areas of commercial construction. New Mexico Construction Industries Division is one of the participating licensing agencies that accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building examination.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The exam includes subjects associated with general requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, doors and glazing, finishes, mechanical and plumbing systems, electrical systems, and procurement and contracting requirements.

Because commercial building work involves many different disciplines, candidates should prepare across the entire exam scope. Strong field knowledge in one trade may not be enough when questions extend into project administration, building codes, accessibility, OSHA safety, structural systems, contracts, or another commercial construction area.

Pacing should also be part of the preparation process. Candidates have 330 minutes to work through 115 questions. Practicing how to identify questions that can be answered directly and questions requiring reference confirmation can help make more effective use of the available testing time.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination in accordance with the applicable testing rules.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity especially important. Having a reference available does not necessarily make a question easier if you do not know where to search. Candidates who become familiar with the organization of the books during preparation can approach lookups more efficiently.

Start by learning the table of contents and index of each major reference. Identify which publications deal with construction management, accessibility, structural concrete, OSHA safety, masonry, carpentry, roofing, steel deck, building codes, and other frequently studied topics. When you encounter a practice question, determine which reference is most likely to contain the supporting information before opening a book.

Use references primarily to find and confirm specific information. Construction knowledge can help you recognize the subject, narrow the possible answers, and identify the correct reference. The book can then be used to verify a requirement, standard, code provision, table, definition, or technical detail.

Practicing this approach before exam day can help reduce unnecessary searching. The goal is to develop a repeatable system: identify the subject, choose the reference, locate the relevant section, confirm the information, and move to the next question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the New Mexico contractor classification. New Mexico contractor licensing is classification-specific, so applicants should identify the classification that corresponds with the work they intend to perform.
  2. Identify the Qualifying Party. New Mexico issues contractor licenses to qualified business entities that employ or are owned by one or more properly certified Qualifying Parties.
  3. Meet the applicable experience requirement. Qualifying Party applicants must have the experience required for the classification being sought.
  4. Submit the Qualifying Party application. The applicant must complete the applicable application and work-experience documentation before scheduling examinations.
  5. Receive approval to test. New Mexico requires Qualifying Party applicants to be preapproved before scheduling the applicable examinations.
  6. Complete the required technical examination. Applicants must pass the examination applicable to their requested classification. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for applicable commercial building purposes.
  7. Complete the Contractor's Business and Law requirement. Qualifying Party applicants must also satisfy New Mexico's Business and Law requirement.
  8. Complete the business license application. After the Qualifying Party satisfies the applicable examination requirements, the business entity completes the remaining New Mexico contractor licensing process.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. Contractor businesses must operate under the appropriate classification and have a properly certified Qualifying Party for the scope of work the business intends to perform.

A Qualifying Party is the individual responsible for satisfying the qualification requirements associated with the applicable contractor classification. New Mexico requires Qualifying Party applicants to have the necessary experience and complete the examinations applicable to their requested classification.

Applicants must be preapproved before scheduling their examinations. New Mexico also requires Qualifying Party applicants to satisfy the Contractor's Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable technical examination.

After the applicable examinations are completed, the contractor business must continue through the licensing process. New Mexico's licensing requirements can include a contractor license application, license bond, tax registration documentation, applicable business-entity documentation, and proof of the Qualifying Party's examination or certification status.

The NASCLA exam is an examination credential rather than a contractor license. Passing it can help satisfy the applicable trade examination requirement with participating agencies such as New Mexico, but candidates must still complete the remaining state requirements before performing work that requires a New Mexico contractor license.
