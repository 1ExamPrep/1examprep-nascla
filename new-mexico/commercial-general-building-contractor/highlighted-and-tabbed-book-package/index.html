Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade exam accepted by participating licensing agencies. New Mexico Construction Industries Division is included among the agencies that accept the NASCLA commercial examination.

  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Exam Type: Commercial General Building Contractor trade examination

The current NASCLA examination content outline distributes questions among General Requirements, Site Construction, Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Wood, Thermal and Moisture Protection, Doors, Windows and Glazing, Finishes, Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, Electrical Systems, and Procurement and Contracting Requirements.

This broad content structure means candidates should prepare across multiple construction disciplines rather than relying exclusively on experience in one specialty. A contractor who is very comfortable with field operations may still need additional review in building codes, project administration, accessibility, safety standards, concrete requirements, or another area represented on the examination.

Pacing is also important. With 115 questions in 330 minutes, candidates need to balance construction knowledge with efficient reference use. Spending too long searching for a single answer can reduce the time available for the rest of the examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Approved references may be used during testing according to the applicable examination rules.

An open-book format makes reference familiarity valuable, but it does not replace studying. Candidates who approach every question by searching several books from the beginning can quickly lose time. A more efficient approach is to understand the construction concept first, determine which reference is most likely to contain the supporting information, and perform a focused lookup when confirmation is necessary.

Your highlighted and tabbed reference books can help support this process. Highlighting can draw attention to significant areas, while tabs can help organize sections and make navigation more deliberate. Regular practice with these books allows you to become familiar with the terminology and organization used by each publication.

During preparation, practice using tables of contents, indexes, definitions, chapter headings, code sections, technical tables, and standards. Associate common subjects with their primary references. For example, learn which book you would reach for when a question involves accessibility, structural concrete, OSHA safety, steel deck construction, masonry, roofing, or commercial project management.

The goal is not to memorize the location of every answer. The goal is to build enough familiarity that you know where to begin searching when a reference lookup is required.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Construction Industries Division licenses and regulates contractors across multiple license classifications.

New Mexico Construction Industries Division participates in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination program. The NASCLA examination is a trade examination rather than a national contractor license. Passing it can satisfy the trade examination requirement for a participating licensing agency, but candidates must still apply for the applicable state license and meet that agency's remaining requirements.

New Mexico regulates general building work through classification-specific licensing. Candidates should determine the classification that corresponds with the construction work they intend to perform and follow the qualification, experience, examination, and business licensing requirements associated with that classification.

  • Pipe and Excavation Contracting

  • Principles and Practices of Commercial Construction, 11th Edition
    Provides broad coverage of commercial construction materials, building systems, methods, sequencing, and project execution.
  • Roofing Construction and Estimating, Daniel Atcheson, 1995
    Reviews roofing systems, materials, installation methods, construction practices, and estimating concepts.
  • Placing Reinforcing Bars
    Provides guidance concerning reinforcing steel placement, handling, detailing, and reinforced concrete field practices.
  • SDI Steel Deck Institute Manual of Construction with Steel Deck
    Covers steel deck handling, installation, construction, and related field procedures.
  • The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction, 4th Edition
    Reviews concrete planning, placement, finishing, quality practices, and practical construction procedures.
  • ANSI/EIMA 99-A-2017: Standard for Exterior Insulation and Finish Systems (EIFS) and EIFS with Drainage, 2017
    Provides standards addressing exterior insulation and finish systems and EIFS with drainage.

    Provides construction-site guidance for developing and implementing stormwater pollution prevention plans.
