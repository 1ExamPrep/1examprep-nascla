Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination, subject to the applicable testing-center rules.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity. A candidate who understands the subject and knows which reference applies can usually locate information more efficiently than someone attempting to research every question from the beginning.

Reference navigation should therefore become part of your regular study routine. Practice using each book's table of contents, index, chapter headings, definitions, technical tables, code sections, and major subject areas. Over time, this helps build an association between a question topic and the book most likely to contain the answer.

For example, accessibility questions may direct you toward the accessibility standard, while structural concrete questions may require a concrete code or concrete construction reference. OSHA questions require familiarity with construction safety regulations, while building-code questions may require quick navigation through the International Building Code.

The goal is not to memorize every page. The goal is to know enough about the reference library that you can locate supporting information efficiently when needed.

Licensing Steps

  1. Choose the applicable New Mexico contractor classification. Contractor licensing in New Mexico is based on specific classifications and scopes of work.
  2. Identify the appropriate Qualifying Party. New Mexico contractor businesses must have a properly qualified individual associated with the applicable classification.
  3. Complete the required application process. Submit the documentation and information required for the classification being pursued.
  4. Receive approval for required testing. Applicants must complete the applicable eligibility process before scheduling examinations.
  5. Prepare for the accepted trade examination. For applicable commercial building licensing purposes, candidates may use the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
  6. Complete applicable New Mexico Business & Law requirements. Candidates must also satisfy the state-specific requirements associated with the licensing process.
  7. Complete required examinations. Schedule and take the applicable contractor examinations after approval.
  8. Finish the licensing process. Submit any remaining documents and complete the requirements associated with the contractor license and classification.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. Licensing requirements vary according to the classification and scope of construction work being performed.

New Mexico uses a Qualifying Party structure for contractor licensing. The Qualifying Party is the individual who satisfies applicable experience and examination requirements for the classification under which the contractor business operates.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for applicable commercial building licensing purposes. Passing the NASCLA exam can satisfy the accepted trade examination component, but candidates must still complete all remaining New Mexico licensing requirements.

New Mexico also requires applicants to satisfy the applicable Business & Law requirement. The licensing process may include application review, experience verification, qualifying-party approval, testing, and submission of required business documentation.
