Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. New Mexico's Construction Industries Division is a participating agency in the NASCLA commercial examination program.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions. Candidates must answer at least 81 questions correctly to pass. Candidates receive 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, to complete the examination.

The NASCLA examination covers a broad range of commercial construction knowledge, including:

  • Planning and estimating
  • Financial and project management
  • Construction safety
  • Site construction
  • Concrete construction
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Wood foundations and framing
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors, windows, and glazing
  • Finishes
  • Specialty equipment and conveyance systems

The examination requires candidates to work across practical construction concepts, building codes, safety requirements, technical standards, project-management principles, and specialized trade information. Field experience can provide a strong foundation, but exam preparation should also include familiarity with the terminology and structure of the approved reference materials.

New Mexico Qualifying Party applicants must also complete the Contractor Business and Law requirement. Business and Law preparation addresses New Mexico-specific contractor licensing requirements and the responsibilities associated with operating a licensed contracting business.

Preparing for these requirements as separate but coordinated study areas can make the process more manageable. NASCLA preparation should focus primarily on commercial construction knowledge and technical reference navigation, while New Mexico Business and Law preparation should emphasize contractor regulation, licensing requirements, and business responsibilities.

Licensing Steps

Contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the Regulation and Licensing Department. Businesses seeking a contractor license must employ or be owned by a properly certified Qualifying Party for the scope of work the business intends to perform.

  1. Determine the appropriate New Mexico classification. Applicants should select the classification that corresponds with the construction work the company intends to perform. For broad residential and commercial building work, New Mexico maintains the GB-98 General Building classification.
  2. Identify the Qualifying Party. The business must employ or be owned by a Qualifying Party who meets the experience and examination requirements for the requested classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Qualifying Party applicants must document the required experience for the classification, with qualifying experience falling within the period required by New Mexico rules.
  4. Apply for Qualifying Party approval. The Qualifying Party application and required experience documentation must be submitted before scheduling applicable New Mexico examinations.
  5. Complete the applicable trade examination requirement. New Mexico participates in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor program and accepts NASCLA for applicable commercial general building trade qualification.
  6. Complete Contractor Business and Law. New Mexico requires Qualifying Party applicants to satisfy the Contractor Business and Law examination requirement.
  7. Establish and register the contractor business. The licensing entity must complete the applicable New Mexico business, tax, and entity-registration requirements.
  8. Obtain the required license bond. The company must secure the contractor license bond required for New Mexico licensure.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The company provides the required application, Qualifying Party documentation, examination results, bond, tax registration information, and applicable entity documentation.
  10. Maintain required workers' compensation coverage. New Mexico requires licensed entities to obtain workers' compensation coverage as applicable to the licensing requirements.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division. Contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more properly certified Qualifying Parties.

The Qualifying Party must have the experience required for the requested classification. New Mexico requires that qualifying experience be obtained within the 10 years immediately preceding the application. The number of years required depends on the classification.

The GB-98 General Building classification requires four years of qualifying experience. This classification covers qualifying residential and commercial building work within the scope established under New Mexico contractor classification rules.

New Mexico's licensing process also requires the Qualifying Party to complete the applicable classification examination and Contractor Business and Law examination. Candidates must obtain approval before scheduling the New Mexico examinations associated with Qualifying Party certification.

After the Qualifying Party has satisfied the applicable requirements, the business applies for the contractor license. New Mexico requires the licensed company to secure a license bond and complete applicable tax and business registrations. Corporations and LLCs must provide the entity documentation required for their business structure, while limited partnerships and limited liability partnerships must satisfy the applicable Secretary of State registration requirements.

Workers' compensation coverage is required for licensed entities under New Mexico's contractor licensing process. Applicants should coordinate business formation, tax registration, bonding, Qualifying Party certification, and insurance requirements as part of the overall licensing plan.
