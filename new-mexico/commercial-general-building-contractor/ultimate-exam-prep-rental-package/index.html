Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. New Mexico Construction Industries Division is one of the agencies that accepts this NASCLA examination.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers

The current NASCLA exam content outline distributes questions across multiple construction categories. General Requirements account for 25 questions, while Procurement and Contracting Requirements account for 31. The examination also includes Site Construction, Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Wood, Thermal and Moisture Protection, Doors, Windows and Glazing, Finishes, Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, and Electrical Systems.

This broad distribution means candidates should prepare across commercial construction disciplines rather than relying only on experience in one area. A contractor may have strong field knowledge but still need additional review in project administration, accessibility, OSHA safety, building codes, concrete, estimating, contracts, steel construction, or other subjects represented on the exam.

Time management is also a major part of preparation. With 115 questions and 330 minutes, candidates need to balance construction knowledge with efficient reference use. Training yourself to recognize whether a question can be answered directly or requires a book lookup can help protect valuable exam time.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination in accordance with applicable testing-center requirements.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity especially important. A large collection of books is useful only when you understand which reference applies to a particular construction subject and how that book is organized. Searching several unfamiliar references for every answer can quickly consume examination time.

The rental books included with this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Those features provide an organized starting point, but candidates should still practice regularly with the references. Learn the indexes, chapter structures, definitions, technical tables, code sections, and subject terminology used throughout the library.

For New Mexico open-book contractor examinations, approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. References containing writing are not permitted. Candidates may not bring additional loose or attached papers with approved references, and permanent tabs are required when books are tabbed. Temporary removable tabs are not permitted.

A useful preparation method is to practice a find-and-confirm workflow. First, determine what the question is testing. Next, identify the most likely reference. Then locate and confirm the specific provision, table, standard, requirement, or construction detail you need. The goal is to use the books strategically rather than treating each question like a research project.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate New Mexico contractor classification. Contractor licensing is classification-specific, and the scope of work you intend to perform determines the classification you should pursue.
  2. Identify the Qualifying Party. New Mexico contractor businesses operate under qualifying individuals who satisfy the applicable requirements for the classification.
  3. Meet the applicable experience requirements. The Qualifying Party must demonstrate experience appropriate to the classification being requested.
  4. Complete the applicable application process. Submit the required forms, experience documentation, and other requested information.
  5. Receive approval for testing. New Mexico candidates must complete the applicable approval process before scheduling required contractor examinations.
  6. Complete the accepted trade examination requirement. For applicable commercial general building licensing purposes, the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors can satisfy the trade examination requirement.
  7. Satisfy New Mexico Business & Law requirements. The state licensing process includes applicable business and law requirements in addition to the technical examination component.
  8. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. After qualification and examination requirements are satisfied, the contractor business must complete the remaining licensing steps for the applicable classification.

State Requirements

New Mexico contractor licensing is administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. PSI provides examination services for New Mexico Construction Industries licensing.

The New Mexico contractor licensing structure uses Qualifying Parties. A licensed business must have the appropriate qualifying individual for the classification of work being performed. The Qualifying Party is responsible for satisfying the experience and examination requirements applicable to that classification.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as part of its participation in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program. The NASCLA examination can eliminate the need to retake the commercial building trade examination in participating jurisdictions, subject to each jurisdiction's licensing requirements.

Passing NASCLA does not automatically create a New Mexico contractor license. Candidates must still complete the applicable New Mexico classification, application, qualifying-party, business, and licensing requirements before becoming licensed.
