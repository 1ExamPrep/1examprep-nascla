## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts to represent the knowledge and skills associated with journeyman electrical work.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains **100 scored questions** and **10 pre-test questions**. The minimum passing score is **70**, and candidates are allowed **270 minutes, or 4.5 hours**, to complete the examination.

The current examination content outline includes:

- **Interpreting Plans and Specifications:** 6 questions
- **Basic Electrical Safety:** 8 questions
- **Electrical Theory and Principles:** 10 questions
- **Testing and Troubleshooting:** 7 questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 15 questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 18 questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 10 questions
- **Special Occupancies and Special Equipment:** 8 questions
- **Special Conditions and Communication Systems:** 2 questions

Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements account for a large portion of the scored examination. Candidates should therefore devote substantial preparation time to National Electrical Code navigation while also reviewing theory, troubleshooting, safety, equipment, special occupancies, plans, and communication systems.

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. An approved application is valid for one year. During that eligibility period, candidates currently receive up to **three attempts** to take and pass the examination. Approved candidates receive scheduling information from PSI.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national electrical license. The examination may be used to waive the applicable trade-exam requirement for participating agencies, while the licensing agency remains responsible for determining whether the applicant satisfies its additional requirements.

## State Requirements

Journeyman electrical certification in New Mexico is administered through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. New Mexico maintains several journeyman electrical classifications based on the type of work performed.

The **EE-98J Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical** classification requires **four years of experience**. A person holding the EE-98J classification can work under EE-98, ER-1, and electrical specialty licenses, subject to the scope of the contractor license under which the journeyman is working.

New Mexico also maintains other electrical journeyman classifications, including residential wiring, electrical distribution systems, electrical signs and outline lighting, cathodic protection and lightning protection systems, and sound, intercommunication, electrical alarm systems, and systems operating at 50 volts and under.

The experience requirement varies by classification. Candidates should identify the exact classification needed for their intended work before submitting an application.

New Mexico's rules also recognize qualifying technical training. Electrical training from accredited colleges, universities, technical vocational institutions, manufacturers' accredited training programs, and approved apprenticeship programs may be applied under the state's certification rules when the applicable requirements are met.

For candidates using NASCLA, New Mexico accepts the **NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in lieu of the trade examination for the Journeyman Certificate**. New Mexico also requires a **letter of good standing from another state agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year**.

The NASCLA pathway should not be confused with reciprocity. New Mexico separately maintains reciprocal journeyman agreements with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming. Reciprocity has its own application and qualification requirements.

Applicants using a reciprocal pathway must hold a qualifying journeyman certificate or license obtained through a mandatory examination after completing a four-year apprenticeship program or four years of equivalent experience as determined by the licensing state.

A New Mexico Journeyman Certificate is also separate from a New Mexico contractor license. Contractor licensing involves separate requirements associated with the contractor entity and qualifying party. Journeyman certification identifies the individual's authorization to work within the applicable trade classification.
