Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is designed to measure the knowledge and skills needed to properly perform electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work in accordance with applicable standards and codes. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis involving electrical subject matter experts from multiple jurisdictions.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains 100 scored questions. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of 70, and the current examination allows 270 minutes, or 4.5 hours, for completion.

The published NASCLA content outline distributes the 100 scored questions across the following areas:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions

Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements account for a large portion of the examination. Candidates should therefore make National Electrical Code navigation a central part of preparation while also reviewing theory, safety, troubleshooting, plans, equipment, and specialty systems.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in place of the applicable state trade examination for the Journeyman Certificate pathway. Passing NASCLA does not by itself create a New Mexico certificate. The Construction Industries Division continues to determine whether an applicant satisfies the state's experience, documentation, certification, and other requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program consists of open-book electrical trade examinations using authorized reference materials under the applicable testing rules.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates need to recognize the topic being tested, identify the book most likely to contain the answer, locate the applicable rule or technical information, and apply it correctly within the available examination time.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should become one of your most familiar references. Practice using the index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, tables, cross-references, and equipment-specific articles. Become comfortable locating requirements involving branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, motors, equipment, and special occupancies.

The other references should be incorporated into regular study. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-industry safety review, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 addresses general-industry workplace standards. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems.

Ugly's Electrical References provides formulas, calculations, tables, and practical electrical information, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications reinforces the concepts behind electrical calculations and code-related problems.

A useful preparation method is to identify the subject of a practice question before opening a reference. Choose the book most likely to contain the information, locate the relevant section, and then confirm the answer. Repeating this process helps develop a more efficient open-book examination strategy.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an accepted trade-examination pathway, but it is not a New Mexico Journeyman Certificate of Competence by itself. New Mexico journeyman certification is administered through the Construction Industries Division.

  1. Determine the applicable New Mexico journeyman classification. Identify the electrical certificate classification that matches the work you intend to perform.
  2. Document qualifying electrical experience or training. New Mexico requires applicants to provide proof of the experience or approved training required for the journeyman certification pathway.
  3. Apply for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Complete the NASCLA examination application process through the National Examination Database.
  4. Prepare with the reference books. Study the 2023 NEC, OSHA safety requirements, NFPA standards, electrical theory, calculations, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Achieve the minimum passing score required by NASCLA.
  6. Meet New Mexico's NASCLA acceptance condition. Candidates using the NASCLA pathway must provide the required letter of good standing from another state agency documenting at least one year of licensure.
  7. Submit the New Mexico journeyman application. Complete the certification process through the Construction Industries Division and provide the required documentation.
  8. Work within the scope of the issued certificate. After certification, perform electrical work within the classification and scope authorized by New Mexico.
  9. Maintain the certificate. Follow New Mexico's renewal and continuing-education requirements for journeyman electricians.

State Requirements

Journeyman electrician certification in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. New Mexico requires individuals performing regulated journeyman electrical work to hold the applicable Certificate of Competence.

New Mexico's journeyman certification rules generally require applicants to provide proof of qualifying experience in the classification for which certification is sought. Current rules require journeyman applicants to provide proof of four years of work experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application, except where another approved pathway applies.

Training classified as technical training by an accredited college, university, manufacturer's accredited training program, technical vocational institute, or accredited apprenticeship program may be considered in lieu of work experience under New Mexico's certification rules.

New Mexico law specifically recognizes electrical apprenticeship and accredited electrical training pathways for journeyman electrician applicants. The Construction Industries Division evaluates the submitted experience and training documentation as part of the certification process.

For candidates using NASCLA, New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in lieu of the trade exam for the Journeyman Certificate. The state also requires a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year.

A New Mexico Journeyman Certificate is different from a contractor license. Journeyman certification authorizes an individual to work within the applicable trade classification, while contractor licensing involves separate business entities, qualifying parties, business-law requirements, bonding, registrations, and other contractor-specific obligations.

New Mexico also maintains reciprocal journeyman arrangements with certain states. For the EE-98J electrical journeyman classification, reciprocity is currently available with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming when the applicant satisfies New Mexico's reciprocal licensing conditions.

Journeyman electricians in New Mexico are subject to continuing-education requirements. The Construction Industries Division requires at least 16 hours of continuing education during each three-year period between electrical code updates, including 8 hours of code-change instruction and 8 hours of other approved industry-related instruction.

    A study reference focused on strengthening electrical theory knowledge and connecting electrical principles with practical National Electrical Code applications and calculations.
