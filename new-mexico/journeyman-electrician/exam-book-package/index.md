## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is designed to measure the knowledge and skills needed to properly perform electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work in accordance with applicable standards and codes. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis involving electrical subject matter experts from multiple jurisdictions.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains **100 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of **70**, and the current examination allows **270 minutes, or 4.5 hours**, for completion.

The published NASCLA content outline distributes the 100 scored questions across the following areas:

- **Interpreting Plans and Specifications:** 6 questions
- **Basic Electrical Safety:** 8 questions
- **Electrical Theory and Principles:** 10 questions

- **General Code Requirements:** 15 questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 18 questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 10 questions

Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements account for a large portion of the examination. ## State Requirements

Journeyman electrician certification in New Mexico is administered by the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. New Mexico requires individuals performing regulated journeyman electrical work to hold the applicable Certificate of Competence.

New Mexico's journeyman certification rules generally require applicants to provide proof of qualifying experience in the classification for which certification is sought. Current rules require journeyman applicants to provide proof of **four years of work experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application**, except where another approved pathway applies.

Training classified as technical training by an accredited college, university, manufacturer's accredited training program, technical vocational institute, or accredited apprenticeship program may be considered in lieu of work experience under New Mexico's certification rules.

New Mexico law specifically recognizes electrical apprenticeship and accredited electrical training pathways for journeyman electrician applicants. The Construction Industries Division evaluates the submitted experience and training documentation as part of the certification process.

For candidates using NASCLA, New Mexico accepts the **NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in lieu of the trade exam for the Journeyman Certificate**. The state also requires a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year.

A New Mexico Journeyman Certificate is different from a contractor license. Journeyman certification authorizes an individual to work within the applicable trade classification, while contractor licensing involves separate business entities, qualifying parties, business-law requirements, bonding, registrations, and other contractor-specific obligations.

New Mexico also maintains reciprocal journeyman arrangements with certain states. For the EE-98J electrical journeyman classification, reciprocity is currently available with Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming when the applicant satisfies New Mexico's reciprocal licensing conditions.

Journeyman electricians in New Mexico are subject to continuing-education requirements. The Construction Industries Division requires at least **16 hours of continuing education during each three-year period between electrical code updates**, including 8 hours of code-change instruction and 8 hours of other approved industry-related instruction.
