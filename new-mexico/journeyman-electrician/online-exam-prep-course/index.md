## Exam Details

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**. Authorized reference materials may be used during the examination according to NASCLA and testing-provider rules.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation a major part of effective preparation. Candidates need to recognize the electrical topic being tested, determine which reference contains the relevant information, locate the correct code section or technical rule, and apply the information accurately within the available exam time.

The **2023 National Electrical Code** should become a regular part of your study routine. Practice using the index, finding Article 100 definitions, locating tables, navigating chapters and articles, following cross-references, and identifying where general requirements give way to more specific rules.

Safety references should also be used actively. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-site safety preparation, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 covers general-industry workplace requirements. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References provides formulas, calculations, tables, and practical field information, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications supports electrical theory and code-related problem solving.

A useful study method is to identify the subject of a practice question before opening a reference. Decide which book is most likely to contain the answer, locate the supporting information, and then confirm your solution. Repeating this process helps build both technical understanding and efficient open-book navigation skills.

## State Requirements

New Mexico journeyman certification is administered through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. State law requires individuals working as journeymen in regulated trades to hold the applicable Certificate of Competence.

New Mexico law establishes experience requirements for individuals seeking journeyman certification. A person generally must have qualifying experience in the occupation or trade or complete an approved training pathway before becoming eligible for the applicable examination and certificate.

For journeyman electricians specifically, New Mexico law recognizes qualifying electrical training and apprenticeship experience. The Construction Industries Division evaluates the applicant's documentation as part of the certification process.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the Journeyman Certificate pathway. However, NASCLA exam passage alone does not replace the state's certification requirements.

Candidates using the NASCLA examination must also provide a **letter of good standing from another state agency showing at least one year of licensure**. This is an important New Mexico-specific requirement for applicants using the NASCLA examination pathway.

A Journeyman Certificate of Competence is different from a contractor license. A journeyman is certified to perform work in the trade classification covered by the certificate, while contracting businesses and qualifying parties are regulated under separate licensing provisions.

New Mexico's Construction Industries Licensing Act establishes the regulatory framework for contractor licensing, qualifying parties, journeymen, examinations, and certificates of competence. Candidates should understand that the NASCLA exam serves as an accepted trade-exam pathway while the Construction Industries Division retains authority over certification and licensing decisions.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong New Mexico NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine National Electrical Code navigation with electrical theory, calculations, troubleshooting, safety, and practical trade knowledge. Because the examination covers ten content areas, candidates benefit from a balanced study routine.

Begin with the **2023 National Electrical Code**. Wiring and Protection contains 18 scored questions, Wiring Methods and Materials contains 16, and General Code Requirements contains 15. These areas together represent nearly half of the scored exam.

Practice locating rules for services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, and electrical equipment. Become familiar with how general code rules interact with specific equipment and occupancy provisions.

Electrical Theory and Principles accounts for 10 questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, conductors, and common electrical calculations. Understanding the principles behind a calculation is more valuable than memorizing one example.

Testing and Troubleshooting accounts for 7 questions. Review how voltage, current, resistance, continuity, and test instruments are used to evaluate electrical systems. Practice approaching troubleshooting questions logically rather than relying on trial and error.

Safety is another important exam area. Study OSHA construction safety, general-industry safety, and NFPA 70E electrical safety practices. Pay attention to the differences among jobsite requirements, workplace requirements, and electrical hazard controls.

Interpreting Plans and Specifications should also be part of your preparation. Practice recognizing electrical symbols, circuits, equipment, locations, and installation requirements from plans. Journeyman electricians routinely work from drawings and specifications in the field.

Special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm systems should not be ignored. These categories contain fewer questions than general wiring topics, but unfamiliar reference sections can consume valuable exam time if you have not practiced locating them before test day.
