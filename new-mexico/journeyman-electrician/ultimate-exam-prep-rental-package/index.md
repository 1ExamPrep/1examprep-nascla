## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program provides a standardized electrical trade examination that participating state licensing agencies may accept instead of requiring an applicant to repeat their state-specific trade examination.

New Mexico is a participating jurisdiction through the **Construction Industries Division**. NASCLA identifies New Mexico as accepting the Journeyman Electrician examination, and the result may be used in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the New Mexico Journeyman Certificate pathway.

The NASCLA electrical examination program is built around the knowledge and skills associated with professional electrical work. Candidates should prepare for subjects that include:

- Interpreting plans and specifications
- Basic electrical safety
- Electrical theory and principles
- Testing and troubleshooting
- General electrical code requirements
- Wiring and protection
- Wiring methods and materials
- Equipment for general use
- Special occupancies and special equipment
- Special conditions and communication systems

Preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, calculations, workplace safety, troubleshooting, and electrical theory.

NASCLA examination applications may be submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. NASCLA currently lists an application fee of **$65**, and an approved application is valid for **one year** from the approval date. Candidates using the NASCLA pathway must still complete New Mexico's separate certification process.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national electrician license. New Mexico determines whether an applicant satisfies the requirements for a New Mexico Journeyman Certificate.

## State Requirements

Journeyman electrical certification in New Mexico is administered through the **Construction Industries Division**. New Mexico maintains multiple electrical journeyman classifications based on the scope of work performed.

The principal residential and commercial classification is **EE-98J – Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical**. New Mexico requires **four years of experience** for this classification. The EE-98J certificate allows work under an EE-98, ER-1, and applicable electrical specialty contractor license, subject to the scope of the contractor license under which the journeyman is working.

New Mexico also maintains additional electrical journeyman classifications. These include ER-1J for residential wiring, EL-1J for electrical distribution systems including transmission lines, ES-1J for electrical signs and outline lighting, ES-2J for cathodic protection and lightning protection systems, ES-3J for sound, intercommunication, electrical alarm systems, and systems operating at 50 volts and under, and other specialty classifications.

Experience requirements differ by classification. EE-98J and EL-1J require four years of experience, while several specialty journeyman classifications require two years of experience. Candidates should pursue the classification that corresponds to the electrical work they intend to perform.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination in lieu of the trade examination for the Journeyman Certificate pathway. However, NASCLA currently states that New Mexico also requires a **letter of good standing from another state agency showing at least one year of licensure** for candidates using this acceptance pathway.

New Mexico also maintains separate reciprocity provisions for EE-98J. The Construction Industries Division identifies EE-98J as the electrical journeyman classification eligible for reciprocity and states that it requires **8,000 hours of experience** for the reciprocal pathway.

New Mexico currently recognizes EE-98J reciprocity from **Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming**. Reciprocity is a separate process from using the NASCLA examination as a trade-exam waiver.

A New Mexico Journeyman Certificate is also different from an electrical contractor license. Journeyman certification applies to the individual performing work within the permitted classification, while contractor licensing carries separate business, qualifying-party, financial, and classification requirements.

EE-98J certificate holders are also subject to New Mexico's applicable electrical continuing-education requirements. Certificate holders should maintain their certification according to the current renewal procedures established by the Construction Industries Division.
