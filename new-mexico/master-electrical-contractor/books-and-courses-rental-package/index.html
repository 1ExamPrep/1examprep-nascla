Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. It is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians and provides participating licensing agencies with a standardized electrical trade examination.

New Mexico's Construction Industries Division accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license when the candidate satisfies the state's NASCLA acceptance requirements. New Mexico also accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination for the applicable journeyman certificate pathway.

The NASCLA electrical examination covers professional electrical knowledge across multiple subject areas. Candidates should prepare for National Electrical Code application, electrical theory, calculations, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, electrical equipment, safety, project design and management, special electrical systems, energy requirements, and other trade-related topics.

A balanced study plan should use all relevant references rather than concentrating only on the National Electrical Code. Some questions may require Code research, while others may involve OSHA construction-safety requirements, NFPA 70E workplace safety, electrical calculations, project-management concepts, or supporting technical references.

New Mexico's licensing process also includes a separate Contractor's Business and Law requirement for qualifying parties. The technical electrical examination and the Business and Law requirement serve different purposes, so candidates should organize their preparation to address both components.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved references according to the rules established for the examination and testing provider.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation a key study skill. Technical books can contain hundreds or thousands of pages, so candidates should become familiar with the structure of each reference before test day. Practice should include recognizing the subject of a question, selecting the most appropriate book, finding the applicable chapter or Code section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable working with definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, cross-references, branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special electrical systems.

Candidates should practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related calculations. Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the New Mexico classification you need. Candidates pursuing residential and commercial electrical contracting generally review the requirements for the EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying party. New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more qualifying parties certified for the scope of work the business intends to perform.
  3. Document qualifying experience. A qualifying-party applicant must document the work experience required for the requested classification. Experience used for qualification must fall within the period recognized by New Mexico's licensing rules.
  4. Submit the qualifying-party application and experience documentation. The qualifying party must complete the required application process before becoming eligible to schedule applicable New Mexico examinations.
  5. Use the NASCLA pathway when eligible. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable EE-98 trade examination when the state's NASCLA acceptance conditions are met.
  6. Complete the Contractor's Business and Law requirement. New Mexico qualifying parties must pass the Contractor's Business and Law examination or satisfy the approved alternative allowed by the state.
  7. Prepare with the rental package. Use the highlighted and tabbed books with 6 months of course access to study electrical theory, NEC application, safety, calculations, project management, business law, and reference navigation.
  8. Complete the applicable examination requirements. Follow the approved NASCLA and New Mexico testing procedures associated with your licensing pathway.
  9. Apply for the contractor business license. After the qualifying party has satisfied the applicable requirements, the business submits the license application and supporting documentation required by the Construction Industries Division.
  10. Complete business registration and licensing requirements. The company must satisfy applicable bonding, tax registration, workers' compensation, entity-registration, and other requirements before the license is issued.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in New Mexico is regulated by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Division licenses contractors and enforces the laws, regulations, and standards governing construction contracting throughout the state.

The EE-98 classification covers residential and commercial electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less and includes electrical work within the scope defined by New Mexico's contractor-classification rules. The EE-98 classification requires four years of qualifying experience.

New Mexico uses a qualifying-party system. Contractor licenses are issued to qualified business entities that employ or are owned by one or more qualifying parties who hold valid certification in the classification in which the business intends to operate.

A qualifying-party applicant must provide satisfactory work-experience documentation. Under New Mexico's qualifying-party rules, the required experience must generally be obtained within the ten years immediately preceding the application. Technical education and qualifying training may receive experience credit under the conditions established by state rules.

New Mexico also requires qualifying-party applicants to satisfy the Contractor's Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable trade examination requirement. The state allows an approved Business and Law course to be used in lieu of the Business and Law examination when completed through an approved provider.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the trade examination for the EE-98 license. For this NASCLA acceptance pathway, New Mexico requires candidates to provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year.

The NASCLA pathway does not eliminate New Mexico's other contractor licensing requirements. The qualifying party and contractor business must still complete the state application process and satisfy the Business and Law, experience, bond, business-registration, tax-registration, and other requirements that apply.

After a qualifying party has completed the applicable examination requirements, the contractor business submits its license application. New Mexico requires licensed entities to maintain workers' compensation coverage as applicable, and the company must secure the required license bond and complete applicable agency registrations.

Passing an examination does not by itself issue a New Mexico contractor license. The Construction Industries Division retains responsibility for determining whether the qualifying party and business entity satisfy all requirements for licensure.
