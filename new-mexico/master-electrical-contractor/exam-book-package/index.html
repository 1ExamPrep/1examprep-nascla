Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The examination is developed around the technical knowledge and professional responsibilities expected of experienced electrical professionals.

The exam covers multiple subject areas associated with electrical contracting, including electrical theory, safety, general Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, project design and management, special occupancies and conditions, and communication systems.

Because the examination is designed around broad trade competency, candidates should prepare for both direct knowledge questions and questions requiring reference research. Some problems may involve an electrical calculation, while others may require locating a specific NEC provision, safety requirement, project-management concept, or technical standard.

The NASCLA electrical examination is also intended to support license mobility among participating jurisdictions. A passing examination result can be used with participating licensing agencies that accept the applicable NASCLA electrical examination, subject to the licensing requirements established by each individual jurisdiction.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program uses open-book trade examinations, including the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Candidates should prepare with the applicable reference books and follow the current examination rules governing permitted materials.

Open-book testing requires a different study strategy from a closed-book exam. Rather than attempting to memorize every Code section or technical detail, candidates should develop a strong understanding of the subject matter while also learning how to locate precise information efficiently.

The National Electrical Code should receive significant navigation practice. Candidates should become familiar with the NEC index, definitions, chapter structure, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Common study areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, and special installations.

The supporting references are equally important. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction safety requirements. NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. Electrical theory references support calculations and fundamental concepts, while project-management and contractor references address planning, business responsibilities, coordination, risk, and professional project execution.

Candidates should practice looking up information during study sessions. Read a practice question, identify the subject, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the controlling information, and apply it to the question. Repeating this process can build both knowledge and navigation speed.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Electrical Bureau enforces applicable electrical codes, industry standards, and licensing requirements within the state's construction regulatory system.

New Mexico participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. NASCLA identifies the examination as accepted in lieu of the trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway.

The EE-98 classification covers residential and commercial electrical work. New Mexico separately regulates journeyman electricians through certificate-of-competence requirements, so candidates should distinguish between contractor licensing and individual journeyman certification.

New Mexico's NASCLA participation includes an additional condition for candidates using this route. Candidates must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for one year. This requirement has applied to the New Mexico NASCLA pathway since January 13, 2023.

The Construction Industries Division licenses contractors in multiple construction classifications, including electrical work. Anyone engaged in construction-related contracting within the scope regulated by New Mexico must obtain the appropriate license before performing regulated contracting work.

New Mexico also uses a qualifying-party structure for contractor licensing. The qualifying party is associated with the contractor license and must satisfy the experience and qualification requirements for the classification being requested. The contractor business and qualifying party must both satisfy the applicable state requirements before the license can be issued.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national contractor license and does not automatically issue a New Mexico EE-98 license. NASCLA provides an accepted examination credential, while New Mexico separately evaluates licensing eligibility, qualifying-party qualifications, application documentation, business requirements, and other conditions established by the Construction Industries Division.

New Mexico also maintains reciprocity rules for certain journeyman classifications. The only journeyman classification currently reciprocated by New Mexico is EE-98J, residential and commercial electrical. That reciprocity process is separate from the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination pathway.

Test Information and Study Materials

The strongest way to use this exam book package is to work with the references throughout your preparation. Candidates should learn the purpose and organization of each book instead of waiting until the examination to decide where information may be located.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Practice using the NEC index and following cross-references when several sections may apply to one question.

Electrical theory should be studied through active calculations. Use the theory references to reinforce voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other electrical fundamentals. Develop a consistent process for identifying known values, selecting the correct formula, completing the calculation, and checking whether a Code provision affects the final answer.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. These references serve different purposes, so candidates should learn which one applies to general construction safety and which addresses electrical workplace safety practices. Familiarity with each reference can make safety-related research faster and more accurate.

Project-management study should include planning, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, documentation, and other professional responsibilities associated with electrical contracting. The project-management and NASCLA contractor references help candidates prepare for subjects that extend beyond installation work alone.

The ASHRAE/IES energy standard adds an energy-efficiency component to the reference collection. Candidates should become comfortable with its terminology and organization so relevant information can be located without beginning an unfamiliar search during the examination.

Timed reference-navigation practice can bring all of these skills together. Choose a question, identify the subject, select the reference, locate the supporting information, complete any necessary calculation, and record how long the process takes. If a topic consistently requires excessive search time, spend additional study time learning that section of the applicable book.
