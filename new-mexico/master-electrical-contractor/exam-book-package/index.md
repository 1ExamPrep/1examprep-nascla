## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Electrical Bureau enforces applicable electrical codes, industry standards, and licensing requirements within the state's construction regulatory system.

New Mexico participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. NASCLA identifies the examination as accepted in lieu of the trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway.

The EE-98 classification covers residential and commercial electrical work. New Mexico separately regulates journeyman electricians through certificate-of-competence requirements, so candidates should distinguish between contractor licensing and individual journeyman certification.

New Mexico's NASCLA participation includes an additional condition for candidates using this route. Candidates must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for one year. This requirement has applied to the New Mexico NASCLA pathway since January 13, 2023.

The Construction Industries Division licenses contractors in multiple construction classifications, including electrical work. Anyone engaged in construction-related contracting within the scope regulated by New Mexico must obtain the appropriate license before performing regulated contracting work.

New Mexico also uses a qualifying-party structure for contractor licensing. The qualifying party is associated with the contractor license and must satisfy the experience and qualification requirements for the classification being requested. The contractor business and qualifying party must both satisfy the applicable state requirements before the license can be issued.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not create a national contractor license and does not automatically issue a New Mexico EE-98 license. NASCLA provides an accepted examination credential, while New Mexico separately evaluates licensing eligibility, qualifying-party qualifications, application documentation, business requirements, and other conditions established by the Construction Industries Division.

New Mexico also maintains reciprocity rules for certain journeyman classifications. The only journeyman classification currently reciprocated by New Mexico is EE-98J, residential and commercial electrical. That reciprocity process is separate from the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination pathway.
