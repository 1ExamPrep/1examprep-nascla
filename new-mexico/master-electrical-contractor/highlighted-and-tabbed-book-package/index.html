Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The examination is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized electrical trade examinations accepted by participating regulatory agencies.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination is developed around the work performed by experienced electrical professionals. Candidates should expect questions that require technical understanding and practical application rather than simple memorization of Code article numbers or isolated definitions.

Major examination areas include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and special equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-efficiency or energy-management concepts.

This broad examination scope requires candidates to work with several different references. A grounding or wiring-method question may require the National Electrical Code, while a workplace-safety question may involve OSHA or NFPA 70E. Other questions may require electrical calculations, project-management concepts, or information from an energy standard.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the New Mexico electrical classification you are pursuing. Electrical contractor licensing is administered through the New Mexico Construction Industries Division.
  2. Review the EE-98 licensing pathway. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway.
  3. Meet the qualifying-party requirements. New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more certified Qualifying Parties who are authorized for the scope of work involved.
  4. Document qualifying experience. A Qualifying Party applicant must document the required experience for the requested classification, and the qualifying experience must fall within the period recognized by New Mexico.
  5. Meet the additional NASCLA acceptance condition. Candidates using the New Mexico NASCLA pathway must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year.
  6. Complete the required examination process. Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy the applicable trade examination requirement and New Mexico's Contractor Business and Law requirement.
  7. Prepare with the applicable reference books. Study the NEC, electrical theory, OSHA requirements, NFPA 70E, project-management concepts, energy-efficiency standards, and supporting electrical materials.
  8. Take and pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Passing the examination can satisfy the accepted trade-exam component for the EE-98 pathway.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the New Mexico licensing process and submit the required contractor documentation.
  10. Complete business licensing requirements. New Mexico requires licensed entities to satisfy applicable bond, tax-registration, business-registration, workers' compensation, and qualifying-party requirements.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Division licenses contractors and enforces the laws, rules, standards, and codes governing construction contracting throughout the state.

New Mexico participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway. The state also accepts other NASCLA electrical examinations for applicable classifications and certificates.

The EE-98 classification is associated with residential and commercial electrical contracting work. New Mexico separately regulates individual journeyman electricians, so candidates should distinguish between the contractor license, the Qualifying Party certification connected to the contractor, and individual journeyman certification.

New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more Qualifying Parties who are properly certified for the scope of work performed by the licensed business. The Qualifying Party represents the technical qualification associated with the contractor's classification.

Qualifying Party applicants must document the required experience for the classification they are seeking. New Mexico requires the qualifying experience to fall within the ten years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying Party applicants must also pass the examination or examinations applicable to the requested classification as well as the Contractor Business and Law requirement.

Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for the New Mexico pathway have an additional condition. New Mexico requires a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year.

After the Qualifying Party requirements are satisfied, the contractor business must complete the license application. New Mexico requires the business to submit applicable license fees and supporting documentation, including a license bond, tax registration documentation, qualifying-party certification or examination documentation, and business-entity documentation when applicable.

Workers' compensation coverage is required for licensed entities under the conditions established by New Mexico law. Contractor businesses must also complete the applicable registration requirements associated with their legal business structure.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a New Mexico contractor license. The NASCLA result can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, while the Construction Industries Division remains responsible for evaluating qualifying-party status, business documentation, bonding, licensing eligibility, and final license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A highlighted and tabbed reference package is most useful when the books become part of the entire study routine. Candidates should use them while reviewing technical subjects, solving calculations, and working through practice questions so the organization of the references becomes familiar before examination day.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice locating requirements involving services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, equipment, and special installations. Learn to follow cross-referenced Code sections and tables instead of relying entirely on memorized article numbers.

Electrical theory should be studied through active calculations. Use the theory references to strengthen understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other electrical principles. Develop a consistent process for identifying known values, choosing the appropriate formula, completing the calculation, and determining whether an NEC requirement affects the final result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learning how each reference is organized can help candidates quickly determine whether a question involves general construction safety, electrical workplace safety, or another technical requirement.

Project-management preparation should include planning, estimating, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, documentation, and other professional responsibilities associated with electrical contracting. The project-management and NASCLA contractor references broaden preparation beyond installation knowledge alone.

Energy-efficiency material should also remain part of the study plan. Candidates should become familiar with the terminology and organization of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022 so related information can be found efficiently when needed.

Use the highlighting and tabs during timed practice. Select a question, identify the subject, choose the correct reference, move to the relevant tabbed section, locate the applicable material, and track how long the process takes. This type of practice helps candidates combine technical knowledge with practical open-book navigation.
