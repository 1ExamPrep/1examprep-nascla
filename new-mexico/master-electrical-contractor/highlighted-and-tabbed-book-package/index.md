## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Division licenses contractors and enforces the laws, rules, standards, and codes governing construction contracting throughout the state.

New Mexico participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license pathway. The state also accepts other NASCLA electrical examinations for applicable classifications and certificates.

The EE-98 classification is associated with residential and commercial electrical contracting work. New Mexico separately regulates individual journeyman electricians, so candidates should distinguish between the contractor license, the Qualifying Party certification connected to the contractor, and individual journeyman certification.

New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more Qualifying Parties who are properly certified for the scope of work performed by the licensed business. The Qualifying Party represents the technical qualification associated with the contractor's classification.

Qualifying Party applicants must document the required experience for the classification they are seeking. New Mexico requires the qualifying experience to fall within the ten years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying Party applicants must also pass the examination or examinations applicable to the requested classification as well as the Contractor Business and Law requirement.

Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for the New Mexico pathway have an additional condition. New Mexico requires a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year.

After the Qualifying Party requirements are satisfied, the contractor business must complete the license application. New Mexico requires the business to submit applicable license fees and supporting documentation, including a license bond, tax registration documentation, qualifying-party certification or examination documentation, and business-entity documentation when applicable.

Workers' compensation coverage is required for licensed entities under the conditions established by New Mexico law. Contractor businesses must also complete the applicable registration requirements associated with their legal business structure.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a New Mexico contractor license. The NASCLA result can satisfy an accepted trade-examination requirement, while the Construction Industries Division remains responsible for evaluating qualifying-party status, business documentation, bonding, licensing eligibility, and final license issuance.
