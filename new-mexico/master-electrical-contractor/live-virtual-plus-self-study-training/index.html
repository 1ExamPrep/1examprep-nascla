Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized trade examinations that participating jurisdictions may accept as part of their licensing process.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Pretest Questions: 10 non-scored questions may be included
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The examination content is based on occupational analysis of work performed by electrical professionals. Candidates should prepare for practical application of electrical knowledge rather than relying solely on memorized Code references.

Current examination subject areas include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-related concepts.

The examination spans several references, which makes reference recognition an important skill. Candidates should practice determining which source applies before beginning a search. Electrical theory problems may require calculations, while installation questions may require NEC research. Safety questions may involve OSHA or NFPA 70E, and project-management questions may require a different reference entirely.

The 270-minute testing period also makes pacing important. Candidates can improve preparation by practicing both direct-response questions and timed reference searches so they become more comfortable deciding when a lookup is necessary and when technical knowledge is sufficient.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Approved references may be used during testing in accordance with current NASCLA and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity especially important. Electrical codes and technical books contain extensive information, and searching every question without a plan can consume valuable time. Candidates should practice recognizing the subject first, selecting the correct reference, and moving directly to the area most likely to contain the answer.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice for NASCLA preparation. Candidates should become comfortable using the index, definitions, article organization, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Important study areas include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and other specialty installations.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related calculations. Develop a repeatable method: identify the known information, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, perform the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC provision affects the final result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 addresses construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning the purpose and organization of each reference can help candidates decide which book is appropriate for a particular safety question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the New Mexico electrical classification you need. Electrical contractor licensing is administered through the New Mexico Construction Industries Division.
  2. Review the EE-98 licensing pathway. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in connection with the EE-98 contractor licensing pathway.
  3. Meet the Qualifying Party requirements. New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualified business entities that employ or are owned by one or more certified Qualifying Parties authorized for the scope of work performed.
  4. Document qualifying experience. Qualifying Party applicants must meet the experience requirements for the requested classification, and the qualifying experience must fall within the period recognized by New Mexico.
  5. Meet the NASCLA good-standing requirement. New Mexico requires candidates using the NASCLA pathway to provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing they have been licensed for at least one year.
  6. Prepare for the examination. Use live virtual training and self-study to review electrical theory, Code application, calculations, safety, project management, and reference navigation.

  8. Complete the Contractor Business and Law requirement. New Mexico Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy the Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable trade-examination requirement.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the New Mexico licensing application and provide the required supporting documentation.
  10. Complete business licensing requirements. The applicant business must satisfy applicable bonding, registration, workers' compensation, tax, and Qualifying Party requirements.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Division licenses contractors, enforces state construction licensing laws, and regulates the classifications under which contracting businesses operate.

New Mexico is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The Construction Industries Division accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor NASCLA examinations.

For the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination, New Mexico accepts the NASCLA credential in connection with the EE-98 licensing pathway. The EE-98 classification covers residential and commercial electrical contracting work under New Mexico's contractor licensing structure.

New Mexico contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities that employ or are owned by one or more Qualifying Parties certified by the Construction Industries Division. The Qualifying Party is responsible for the technical scope associated with the contractor classification.

Qualifying Party applicants must have the required experience for the classification being sought. New Mexico requires that qualifying experience fall within the ten years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying Party applicants must also pass the applicable classification examination or satisfy an accepted examination pathway, as well as the Contractor Business and Law requirement.

Candidates using the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination must also meet New Mexico's additional good-standing condition. The state requires a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that the candidate has been licensed for at least one year.

After the Qualifying Party has satisfied the applicable requirements, the contractor business completes the license application. New Mexico requires licensed companies to obtain a license bond and submit applicable business documentation. Workers' compensation coverage is required for licensed entities, and tax and entity-registration documentation may also be required depending on the business structure.

New Mexico separately certifies journeyman electricians. The EE-98J journeyman certificate is a separate credential from the EE-98 contractor classification. Candidates should distinguish between a contractor license, a Qualifying Party certificate, and an individual journeyman certificate when planning their licensing path.

New Mexico currently identifies the 2020 New Mexico Electrical Code and National Electrical Code as the state's adopted electrical code for field enforcement. This state code adoption is separate from the 2023 NEC reference used in the current NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination preparation materials listed for this course.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a New Mexico contractor license. Candidates must still complete the Qualifying Party process, Business and Law requirement, contractor application, business documentation, and other state licensing requirements before a license is issued.

    A technical study reference connecting electrical theory and calculation principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine structured instruction with active independent practice. Live virtual classes can help organize a broad range of electrical material, while self-study gives candidates additional time to reinforce difficult topics and practice using the reference books.

Begin with National Electrical Code navigation. Practice using the index, following cross-references, locating tables, reviewing definitions, and moving between related articles. Open-book preparation is more effective when the NEC becomes a working reference rather than a book candidates only open on test day.

Electrical calculations should also be practiced consistently. Work through voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, circuits, and other electrical problems. Focus on understanding the method behind the calculation and identifying when a Code provision must be applied to the result.

Safety study should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn which subjects are associated with construction safety and which address workplace electrical safety. Familiarity with both references can make safety-related lookups more efficient.

Project-management and contractor-business material should remain part of preparation. Electrical contractors operate within projects involving planning, coordination, estimating, communication, documentation, scheduling, risk, and professional responsibility. Energy-efficiency standards should also be reviewed so candidates understand how the reference is organized.

Timed reference drills are especially useful. Select a practice question, identify the subject, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the applicable information, and measure the time required. Repeat the process until selecting and navigating the correct reference becomes more familiar.

Live training can help establish the study framework, while self-study gives candidates the repetition needed to make that framework useful. Candidates can revisit subjects covered in class, repeat difficult calculations, and spend additional time on technical areas where they need more confidence.
