Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is the NASCLA examination designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. NASCLA describes the classification as a professional who conducts business involving the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Experimental Questions: 10 non-scored items may be included
  • Minimum Passing Score: 75
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The NASCLA examination blueprint organizes the 100 scored questions into nine major content domains:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 questions
  • Safety: 9 questions
  • Electrical Theory: 11 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 17 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 13 questions
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions: 8 questions
  • Communication Systems: 1 question

The weighting of these categories is useful when building a study schedule. General Code Requirements and Wiring and Protection each contain 17 scored questions, while Wiring Methods and Materials contains 16. Equipment for General Use contains another 13. These four categories represent a significant portion of the examination, making strong NEC navigation and Code application especially important.

Electrical Theory contributes 11 scored questions and requires candidates to understand the principles behind electrical work rather than relying entirely on book lookup. Safety, project design and management, special occupancies, and communication systems complete the examination blueprint and should remain part of a comprehensive study plan.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates should treat reference navigation as an exam skill rather than assuming that an open-book format makes preparation unnecessary. With 100 scored questions and additional experimental items possible within a 270-minute testing period, inefficient searching can consume valuable exam time.

During preparation, become familiar with the structure of the National Electrical Code and the other references used for the examination. Practice locating definitions, tables, exceptions, conductor requirements, grounding and bonding rules, overcurrent protection requirements, wiring-method provisions, motor requirements, transformer information, special occupancy rules, safety provisions, and project-management information.

A useful study method is to practice questions with the books open while timing each lookup. Read the question, identify the subject being tested, determine which reference should contain the answer, find the appropriate section, and confirm the answer from the source. Repeating that process builds navigation speed and helps develop the judgment needed to decide whether a question should be answered from knowledge, calculation, or reference research.

Open-book candidates should also understand the current NASCLA and PSI rules regarding which editions and reference materials may be brought into the testing center and how those materials may be prepared. Follow the current testing rules when highlighting, underlining, indexing, or tabbing books used for exam-day reference.

State Requirements

The New Mexico Construction Industries Division is the state agency responsible for regulating construction trades, including electrical classifications. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license path.

New Mexico's acceptance of a NASCLA examination does not make the NASCLA credential a New Mexico license. Candidates must satisfy the Construction Industries Division's licensing requirements and complete the state's application process. NASCLA likewise states that its Accredited Examination Program does not create a national contractor license. Each participating state remains responsible for its own experience, application, qualifying-party, business, financial, background, and other licensing requirements.

New Mexico's NASCLA participation information states that candidates using this pathway must provide a letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing that they have been licensed for one year. Applicants should incorporate that documentation into their planning when pursuing the NASCLA examination acceptance route in New Mexico.

The EE-98 classification is associated with residential and commercial electrical work in New Mexico. A contractor-license application may involve requirements that are separate from individual journeyman certification. Candidates should distinguish between the contractor classification, qualifying-party requirements, and journeyman certificate requirements when preparing their state licensing documentation.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive NASCLA study strategy begins with the examination blueprint. Instead of treating every subject as equally weighted, organize study time around both the number of questions in each domain and your current level of knowledge.

General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Equipment for General Use account for most of the scored examination. Candidates should build confidence finding and applying NEC provisions involving services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, equipment, motors, transformers, and other common installation requirements.

Electrical theory deserves dedicated calculation practice. Work through problems methodically and become comfortable identifying known values, selecting the correct formula, maintaining units, and determining whether a Code provision also affects the answer. Electrical licensing questions frequently test application rather than simple recall, so understanding why a calculation method works is valuable.

Safety preparation should include both construction-safety principles and electrical workplace safety. Project-management review should cover the types of planning and coordination responsibilities that an electrical contractor may encounter while managing work. Special occupancies and special equipment should also receive focused review because these questions can require candidates to navigate NEC sections they use less frequently in everyday work.

Practice under timed conditions as the examination approaches. A 270-minute exam requires consistent pacing. Some questions may be answered quickly from experience, while calculations and unfamiliar Code searches can take considerably longer. Developing a habit of moving past a difficult question and returning later can help preserve time for questions that can be answered more efficiently.
