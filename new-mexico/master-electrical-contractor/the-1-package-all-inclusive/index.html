Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes an examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. New Mexico participates in the program through the Construction Industries Division and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the trade examination for the EE98 classification when applicable New Mexico requirements are met.

New Mexico also places an additional condition on candidates using the NASCLA electrical examination pathway. Candidates must provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year. Passing the NASCLA examination by itself does not issue a New Mexico EE98 contractor license. The applicant must still complete the qualifying-party, Business and Law, application, and contractor licensing requirements established by New Mexico.

NASCLA electrical preparation requires broad technical knowledge. Candidates should prepare for subjects involving electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, electrical equipment, grounding and bonding, calculations, electrical safety, project management, special occupancies, special equipment, and other concepts associated with professional electrical contracting.

The electrical references included with The 1 Package allow candidates to prepare across several knowledge areas rather than relying on a single code book. The 2023 National Electrical Code supports code navigation, while references such as Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications reinforce theory, calculations, practical electrical concepts, and code application.

Safety preparation is supported by OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Project-management and business references help students prepare for responsibilities beyond electrical installation, including planning, coordination, contracts, estimating, financial organization, safety management, and professional business operations.

Licensing Steps

The NASCLA examination provides an accepted trade-examination pathway, but New Mexico electrical contractor licensing involves additional requirements. The Construction Industries Division regulates contractor licensing, while licensing and examination services are handled through PSI.

  1. Identify the appropriate classification. The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is accepted in lieu of the trade examination for the New Mexico EE98 classification when applicable requirements are satisfied.
  2. Prepare for the NASCLA electrical examination. Study electrical theory, NEC requirements, safety, wiring, equipment, calculations, project management, and other examination subjects using the included references and course materials.
  3. Complete the NASCLA examination process. Candidates must follow the applicable NASCLA examination application and testing procedures.
  4. Meet New Mexico's NASCLA acceptance condition. Candidates using this pathway must provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing at least one year of licensure.
  5. Complete the qualifying-party requirements. New Mexico requires the qualifying party to meet applicable experience, documentation, examination, and application requirements.
  6. Satisfy the Contractor Business and Law requirement. New Mexico contractors must satisfy the applicable Business and Law requirement in addition to the trade requirement.
  7. Complete the contractor license application. Once the qualifying-party requirements are satisfied, the business entity proceeds through the applicable New Mexico contractor licensing process.
  8. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation support for an LLC or Corporation.
  9. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included to support business banking, taxes, employee hiring, and professional business operations.
  10. Organize contractor compliance. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to help customers understand responsibilities associated with operating a licensed electrical contracting business in New Mexico.

Application Service is included to help organize the licensing paperwork involved in moving through the process. This allows candidates to work on exam preparation while receiving support with the administrative side of the contractor licensing path.

State Requirements

New Mexico construction contracting is regulated by the Construction Industries Division of the Regulation and Licensing Department. Businesses performing regulated construction work must operate under the appropriate contractor classification and meet the requirements connected with that license.

A qualifying party is the individual whose experience and qualifications support the licensed business entity for a particular classification. Electrical contractor applicants must follow the experience, application, examination, and documentation requirements applicable to the classification they are pursuing.

For the EE98 pathway covered by this package, New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the state trade examination when the applicable acceptance requirements are satisfied. Candidates using this pathway must also provide the required letter of good standing from another state agency documenting at least one year of licensure.

The Contractor Business and Law requirement remains an important part of New Mexico contractor qualification. Contractors need to understand licensing obligations, contracts, financial management, project responsibilities, safety, business organization, and compliance in addition to possessing technical electrical knowledge.

Passing the NASCLA electrical examination should not be interpreted as receiving a national electrical contractor license. NASCLA provides an examination credential accepted by participating jurisdictions. New Mexico retains authority over whether an applicant meets the state's EE98 licensing requirements.

Project-management material should also receive dedicated study time. Electrical contractors may coordinate employees, materials, schedules, subcontractors, safety responsibilities, documentation, and customer requirements. Understanding project-management concepts helps connect technical electrical knowledge with professional contractor operations.
