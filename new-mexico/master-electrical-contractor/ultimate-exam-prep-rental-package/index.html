Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program offers an open-book examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians and is accepted by participating licensing agencies.

The New Mexico Construction Industries Division is a participating agency. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license when the state's acceptance requirements are satisfied.

The NASCLA electrical examination covers professional electrical knowledge across multiple areas. Candidates should prepare for electrical theory, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, grounding and bonding, equipment for general use, safety, project design and management, special electrical systems, and other technical subjects associated with advanced electrical work.

The range of exam material makes a complete reference-based study approach important. The 2023 National Electrical Code is central to preparation, but supporting references address electrical theory, construction safety, workplace electrical safety, project management, energy standards, and professional contractor responsibilities.

New Mexico separately requires qualifying-party applicants to satisfy the Contractor's Business and Law requirement. This means candidates preparing for the EE-98 licensing pathway should treat technical electrical preparation and New Mexico Business and Law preparation as related but distinct parts of the licensing process.

Applicants must receive preapproval before scheduling the examinations applicable to the classification they are seeking. New Mexico's Construction Industries Division uses PSI as part of the qualifying-party application and examination process.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Approved references may be used according to the examination rules established for the NASCLA electrical program and the applicable testing provider.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for strong technical preparation. Candidates must still understand electrical concepts, recognize which type of information a question requires, select the appropriate reference, and locate the relevant material within the available testing period.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become familiar with the NEC index, definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be reinforced through active problem solving. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, and related calculations. A consistent process can help candidates identify the known values, determine what must be solved, select the proper formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC requirement changes the final result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on construction workplace safety, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Candidates should become comfortable distinguishing the purpose of the two references and locating information efficiently within each one.

Highlighted and tabbed references can be especially helpful when candidates use them consistently throughout preparation. Rather than waiting until test day, candidates can practice moving between books, following tabs, using indexes, locating tables, and identifying important sections during normal study sessions.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the New Mexico electrical classification you need. Candidates pursuing residential and commercial electrical contracting generally review the EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical classification.
  2. Identify the Qualifying Party. New Mexico issues contractor licenses to qualified business entities that employ or are owned by one or more Qualifying Parties certified for the scope of work the business intends to perform.
  3. Document the required work experience. Qualifying Party applicants must have the required experience for the classification, and the qualifying experience must fall within the period recognized by New Mexico licensing rules.
  4. Submit the Qualifying Party application. The applicant must submit the required application and work-experience documentation and obtain preapproval before scheduling applicable examinations.
  5. Use the NASCLA electrical pathway when eligible. New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the EE-98 trade examination when the applicable acceptance requirements are satisfied.
  6. Complete the Contractor's Business and Law requirement. Qualifying Party applicants must satisfy New Mexico's Business and Law requirement in addition to the applicable trade qualification.

  8. Use the included Application Service. Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to provide support with the application component of the licensing pathway.
  9. Complete the contractor business license application. After the Qualifying Party satisfies the applicable requirements, the company submits the contractor license application and required supporting documents.

  11. Receive licensing approval. Passing an examination does not by itself issue a New Mexico contractor license. The Construction Industries Division must approve the complete licensing application.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in New Mexico is administered by the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. The Division licenses contractor businesses and certifies Qualifying Parties for the classifications in which those businesses intend to operate.

The EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical classification covers electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less within the scope defined by New Mexico's classification rules. The EE-98 classification requires four years of qualifying experience.

New Mexico's licensing structure centers on the Qualifying Party. A contractor license is issued to a qualified business entity that employs or is owned by one or more Qualifying Parties who hold valid certification for the applicable scope of work.

Qualifying Party applicants must document the required work experience for the classification. New Mexico requires the qualifying experience to be within the ten years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying Party applicants must also pass the examinations applicable to the classification being requested as well as the Contractor's Business and Law examination. Applicants must receive preapproval before scheduling examinations.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination in lieu of the trade examination for the EE-98 license. For this NASCLA acceptance pathway, candidates must also provide a letter of good standing from another state agency showing that they have been licensed for at least one year.

The NASCLA examination does not eliminate New Mexico's Business and Law requirement or its other licensing requirements. Candidates must still satisfy the qualifying-party application process and the business entity must complete the contractor license application.

After a Qualifying Party has passed the applicable examinations, the contractor business must submit the required license application. Current New Mexico licensing information requires the company to provide a license bond, tax registration documentation, applicable entity registration documents, and a copy of the Qualifying Party certificate or applicable test scores.

Workers' compensation coverage is required for licensed entities under New Mexico's current contractor licensing process. Business entities must also complete the agency registrations applicable to the organization type.

The NASCLA pathway can satisfy an accepted trade-examination component, but final licensing authority remains with the New Mexico Construction Industries Division. Candidates should therefore prepare for both the technical electrical examination and the additional state licensing requirements that apply to the Qualifying Party and contractor business.
