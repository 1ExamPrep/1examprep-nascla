## State Requirements

Electrical licensing in New Mexico is administered through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. The Division regulates contractors and journeymen performing electrical work under the state's construction licensing system.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination along with the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician and Journeyman Electrician examinations. The accepted NASCLA Residential examination may be used in lieu of the applicable trade exam for the EE-98 license and journeyman certificate pathway.

A significant New Mexico requirement applies to candidates using NASCLA: the applicant must provide a **letter of good standing from another state licensing agency demonstrating at least one year of licensure**. This requirement applies in addition to the accepted NASCLA examination.

The **EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical Wiring** contractor classification requires four years of qualifying experience. Its scope includes electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less, along with work included in specified electrical specialty classifications and residential electrical wiring included in the ER-1 classification.

New Mexico also maintains the **ER-1 Residential Electrical Wiring** classification, which requires two years of experience. ER-1 covers qualifying one- and two-family dwelling units and certain multifamily residential installations within the classification's defined limitations. It does not authorize commercial-use wiring such as hotels or motels.

Contractor classifications and journeyman certificates are separate credentials. A contractor license authorizes the licensed business to contract within the approved classification, while individual journeyman certification establishes trade qualifications for work performed under the applicable licensing structure.

New Mexico also maintains a reciprocal journeyman pathway for **EE-98J Residential and Commercial Electrical**. The state currently identifies EE-98J as its only reciprocated journeyman classification and requires 8,000 hours of experience. Reciprocity is accepted from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming.

Reciprocity is separate from the NASCLA examination pathway. Applicants using reciprocity follow the reciprocal journeyman process, while candidates using NASCLA must satisfy the applicable NASCLA acceptance conditions and New Mexico licensing requirements.

New Mexico currently enforces the **2020 New Mexico Electrical Code/National Electrical Code** for electrical installations. Candidates should distinguish this adopted state code from the NEC 2023 reference included in this NASCLA examination preparation package.
