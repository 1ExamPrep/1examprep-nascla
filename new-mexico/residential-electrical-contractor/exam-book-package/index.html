Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized electrical trade examinations for participating licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The New Mexico Construction Industries Division currently accepts all three NASCLA electrical examination categories, including the Residential Electrical Contractor examination. The examination may be used in place of the applicable trade examination for New Mexico's EE-98 license and journeyman certificate pathway, but the applicant must still meet state licensing requirements.

The Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around electrical installations, service, repair, maintenance, safety, and other responsibilities commonly associated with residential electrical work. Candidates should prepare across a broad range of subjects, including:

  • Electrical theory and practical calculations
  • Personal and jobsite safety
  • Project design, estimating, and management
  • National Electrical Code requirements
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Circuit protection and installation
  • Residential wiring methods and practices
  • Electrical tools and test equipment
  • Special electrical systems and equipment
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Residential construction requirements
  • Contractor business and project management concepts

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The current application fee is $65. NASCLA states that applications are typically processed within seven business days, and approved applications are valid for one year from the approval date.

Candidates currently receive three attempts to take the examination during the one-year eligibility period. After an application is approved, the testing provider sends the candidate scheduling information and the applicable test-taker identification information.

After passing, candidates can have their NASCLA transcript electronically transmitted to a participating regulatory agency through the National Examination Database. New Mexico then pairs the transcript with the applicant's licensing materials and evaluates the remaining requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies all three examinations in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program as open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should become familiar not only with the content of the books but also with how each reference is organized. The goal is to recognize a question's subject, choose the correct book, locate the applicable provision, table, definition, formula, or technical requirement, and apply that information correctly.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial attention. Candidates should practice using the index, Article 100 definitions, individual articles, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Residential electrical questions can involve services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, equipment, and special systems.

Electrical theory should also be studied actively. Understanding voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current fundamentals, motors, transformers, and electrical loads can make it easier to determine which formula or NEC requirement applies to a problem.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving construction-industry safety. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system study. The International Residential Code provides broader residential construction context.

Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications support formulas, calculations, and electrical principles. ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels, while the NASCLA Contractors Guide supports study of contractor business, law, and project-management concepts.

A useful open-book study routine is to work practice-oriented questions with the books nearby. Identify the technical subject first, choose the appropriate reference, find the supporting information, and then solve or confirm the answer. Repeated navigation builds familiarity with the library while reinforcing trade knowledge.

State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing and journeyman certification in New Mexico are administered through the Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department. New Mexico maintains several electrical contractor and journeyman classifications covering different scopes of electrical work.

The EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical contractor classification is a broad electrical classification covering qualifying residential and commercial electrical work within the scope established by New Mexico law. New Mexico currently requires four years of experience for the EE-98 contractor classification.

New Mexico also maintains the ER-1 Residential Electrical Wiring contractor classification. This classification is limited to qualifying residential electrical wiring and currently requires two years of experience. Its scope includes one- and two-family dwelling units and certain qualifying multifamily dwelling configurations. It does not authorize commercial-use wiring such as hotels, motels, and similar occupancies.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is accepted by New Mexico in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license and journeyman certificate pathway. Applicants using NASCLA must also provide the required letter of good standing showing at least one year of licensure through another state agency.

New Mexico contractor licensing and individual journeyman certification are separate credentials. A contractor license applies to the contracting business and qualifying party, while a journeyman certificate applies to an individual performing electrical work within the scope of the applicable certificate.

New Mexico also maintains a reciprocity pathway for the EE-98J Residential and Commercial Electrical journeyman classification. The Construction Industries Division identifies EE-98J as the only journeyman classification currently reciprocated and requires 8,000 hours of experience for the reciprocity pathway.

New Mexico currently recognizes EE-98J reciprocity from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming. Reciprocity is separate from NASCLA examination acceptance and should not be confused with the NASCLA trade-exam waiver process.

Applicants must perform electrical work only within the scope of the credential issued and comply with applicable New Mexico permitting, inspection, code, and licensing requirements.
