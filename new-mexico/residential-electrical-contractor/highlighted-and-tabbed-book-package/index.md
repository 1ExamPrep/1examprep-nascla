## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing and journeyman certification in New Mexico are administered through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. The Division regulates construction contracting and requires appropriate licensure for individuals and businesses performing regulated construction contracting work.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination along with the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician and Journeyman Electrician examinations. For the Residential Electrical Contractor pathway, the accepted examination may be used in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license and journeyman certificate pathway.

A key New Mexico condition applies to NASCLA candidates: the applicant must provide a **letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing at least one year of licensure**. This state-specific requirement remains applicable even when the NASCLA examination has already been passed.

The NASCLA examination is not itself a contractor license or journeyman certificate. It satisfies an accepted trade-examination requirement, while New Mexico continues to review the applicant's licensing qualifications and supporting documentation.

New Mexico also maintains the **EE-98J Residential and Commercial Electrical journeyman classification**. The Construction Industries Division identifies EE-98J as the only journeyman classification currently available for reciprocity and requires **8,000 hours of experience** for that reciprocal pathway.

New Mexico currently recognizes EE-98J reciprocity from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming. Reciprocity is a separate process from NASCLA examination acceptance and should not be treated as the same licensing pathway.

Applicants using reciprocity must follow the state's reciprocal journeyman application process, while applicants using the NASCLA examination pathway must follow the NASCLA trade-exam waiver requirements and the applicable New Mexico licensing process.

Electrical work must be performed only within the scope of the contractor license or journeyman certificate issued. Licensees are also responsible for complying with applicable permitting, inspection, adopted code, and construction requirements enforced by the Construction Industries Division and other authorities having jurisdiction.
