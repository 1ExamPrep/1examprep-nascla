## State Requirements

Electrical contracting and journeyman certification in New Mexico are regulated through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**. Electrical examinations associated with New Mexico Construction Industries licenses are administered by PSI.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination in lieu of the applicable trade examination for the **EE-98 license and Journeyman Certificate pathway**. However, examination acceptance does not remove New Mexico's separate qualification requirements.

Candidates using the NASCLA pathway must provide a **letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing at least one year of licensure**. This requirement is an important distinction between simply passing the NASCLA examination and being eligible to use that examination result for New Mexico licensing.

The **EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical** contractor classification is New Mexico's broad electrical contractor classification. Contractor licensing and individual journeyman certification are separate credentials. A contractor license concerns the licensed contracting business and qualifying party, while a journeyman certificate applies to an individual performing electrical work within the applicable classification.

New Mexico also maintains several electrical journeyman classifications. The principal residential and commercial journeyman classification is **EE-98J – Journeyman Residential and Commercial Electrical**. New Mexico currently identifies four years of experience as the requirement for EE-98J.

New Mexico separately maintains a reciprocity pathway for EE-98J. The state identifies EE-98J as the only journeyman classification currently reciprocated and requires 8,000 hours of experience for that reciprocal pathway. Reciprocity is accepted from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming.

Reciprocity should not be confused with NASCLA acceptance. The NASCLA pathway concerns acceptance of an accredited examination in place of the applicable trade examination, while reciprocity is a separate method of obtaining certification based on an existing license from a state with which New Mexico has a reciprocal agreement.

New Mexico's currently adopted electrical code is the **2020 New Mexico Electrical Code/National Electrical Code**. State electrical work must comply with the code edition and New Mexico amendments currently adopted by the Construction Industries Division. The 2023 NEC listed with this online exam prep is part of the provided NASCLA study-reference set and should not be confused with New Mexico's currently adopted statewide electrical code.

Applicants and license holders must also comply with applicable permitting, inspection, renewal, and continuing-education requirements associated with the credential held and the work being performed.
