## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an **open book test**. NASCLA identifies the Residential Electrical Contractor examination as one of the open-book examinations in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question, determining which reference contains the needed information, locating the correct code provision, table, definition, formula, or safety requirement, and applying that information correctly.

## State Requirements

The **New Mexico Construction Industries Division** licenses contractors and administers construction-related regulations throughout the state. Electrical contractor classifications are defined by the authorized scope of work and the experience required for each classification.

The **EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical Wiring** contractor classification requires **four years of experience**. The classification covers electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less and includes qualifying residential electrical wiring as well as specified electrical specialty work.

The **ER-1 Residential Electrical Wiring** contractor classification requires **two years of experience**. Its scope is limited to qualifying residential electrical wiring. The classification is intended for residential work within the boundaries established by New Mexico's classification rules and does not extend to commercial uses such as hotels or motels.

New Mexico contractor licenses and journeyman certificates are separate credentials. A contractor license authorizes a qualifying business to contract within an approved classification, while a journeyman certificate recognizes the individual trade qualification required for applicable electrical work.

For the NASCLA pathway, New Mexico requires a **letter of good standing from another state licensing agency showing at least one year of licensure**. This requirement has applied to NASCLA candidates since January 13, 2023.

The state also maintains a reciprocal journeyman process. New Mexico currently reciprocates only the **EE-98J Residential and Commercial Electrical** journeyman classification. The reciprocal pathway requires **8,000 hours of experience** and is currently recognized from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming.

The reciprocal journeyman route is separate from the NASCLA examination pathway. Candidates should follow the process appropriate to their current credentials and the New Mexico classification they intend to obtain.

New Mexico currently enforces the **2020 New Mexico Electrical Code**, which adopts the 2020 National Electrical Code with New Mexico amendments. The NEC 2023 book included with The 1 Package is provided for the NASCLA examination preparation described for this product.

Business Formation included with The 1 Package establishes an LLC or Corporation, but business formation alone does not provide authority to perform electrical contracting. The business must still satisfy applicable New Mexico licensing, qualifying-party, permitting, inspection, tax, employment, and other regulatory requirements.
