## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing and journeyman certification in New Mexico are administered through the **Construction Industries Division of the New Mexico Regulation and Licensing Department**.

New Mexico accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an alternative to the applicable trade examination for the EE-98 license and journeyman certificate pathway. Applicants must still satisfy the state's additional licensing requirements.

A key New Mexico requirement applies to NASCLA candidates: the applicant must provide a **letter of good standing from another state licensing agency confirming at least one year of licensure**. This requirement applies in addition to the passing NASCLA examination result.

The **EE-98 Residential and Commercial Electrical Wiring** contractor classification requires **four years of experience**. Its scope covers electrical wiring operating at 5,000 volts nominal or less and includes residential electrical wiring within the ER-1 classification along with specified electrical specialty work.

New Mexico also maintains the **ER-1 Residential Electrical Wiring** classification. ER-1 requires **two years of experience** and covers qualifying one- and two-family dwelling units and certain qualifying multifamily residential installations. It does not authorize wiring for commercial uses such as hotels or motels.

The contractor classification being sought determines the applicant's authorized scope. An EE-98 contractor has a broader residential and commercial electrical scope, while ER-1 is limited to residential electrical wiring within the boundaries established by New Mexico rules.

New Mexico contractor licenses and journeyman certificates are separate credentials. A contractor license authorizes the licensed business to contract within an approved classification, while journeyman certification establishes an individual's trade qualification for work performed under the applicable licensing structure.

New Mexico also has a reciprocal journeyman pathway for **EE-98J Residential and Commercial Electrical**. The state currently identifies EE-98J as the only reciprocated journeyman classification and requires **8,000 hours of experience**. Reciprocity is currently recognized from Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, and Wyoming.

Reciprocity is separate from the NASCLA pathway. A candidate using reciprocity follows the reciprocal journeyman process, while a candidate relying on NASCLA must satisfy the NASCLA acceptance requirements and all applicable New Mexico application rules.

New Mexico currently enforces the **2020 New Mexico Electrical Code**, which adopts the 2020 National Electrical Code with state amendments for electrical work performed under Construction Industries Division jurisdiction. The NEC 2023 book included with this package is provided for the NASCLA examination preparation described here.
