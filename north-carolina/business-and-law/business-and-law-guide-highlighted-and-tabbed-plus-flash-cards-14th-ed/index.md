## Exam Details

The **North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors** regulates electrical contractor licensing and establishes the examination requirements for the state's electrical contracting classifications.

PSI Services administers the Board's examinations. These examinations assess technical electrical knowledge together with applicable laws, administrative rules, and business practices.

**North Carolina Electrical Examination Information:**

- **Licensing Authority:** North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors
- **Testing Provider:** PSI Services
- **Examination Delivery:** Computer-based testing
- **Passing Score:** 70%
- **NASCLA Study Reference:** Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, North Carolina Electrical, 14th Edition
- **Business and Law Subjects:** Contractor licensing, Board rules, business operations, and construction management

**Examination Formats by Classification:**

- **Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited:** 100 multiple-choice questions with six hours allowed.
- **Special Restricted Single-Family Detached and other applicable Special Restricted classifications:** 40 multiple-choice questions with three hours allowed.
- **Special Restricted Fire Alarm/Low Voltage:** 50 multiple-choice questions with three hours allowed.
- **Special Restricted Swimming Pool Bonding:** 25 multiple-choice questions with one hour allowed.

Examination subjects vary by classification. The applicable examination includes technical questions associated with the electrical work authorized under the requested license, along with business, law, and regulatory material.

The NASCLA guide supports the business and management portion of preparation. Candidates must also study the electrical codes and other technical subjects required for their specific examination.

## Closed Book Test

**Questions based on the NASCLA North Carolina Electrical guide are closed book.** The North Carolina electrical contractor examinations permit designated technical code references, but the Board's examination handbook identifies questions concerning the NASCLA guide, the Electrical Contractors Licensing Law, and the Board's administrative rules as closed-book material.

That means candidates must study business, law, and management concepts in advance and answer the related examination questions without consulting the NASCLA publication.

The examinations allow specified electrical code references during testing. The Board's handbook identifies the 2020 National Electrical Code and, for certain classifications, the 2013 National Fire Alarm Code.

**Important Examination Reference Rules:**

- Only designated technical reference publications may enter the examination room.
- The NASCLA North Carolina Electrical guide is a preparation reference for closed-book examination subjects.
- Permitted technical references must meet the Board's binding and reference-material requirements.
- Approved technical references may contain highlighting, underlining, and permitted annotations made before testing.
- Permanent tabs must comply with the examination rules.
- Temporary sticky tabs, stapled tabs, and divider tabs are not permitted.
- Additional loose papers and unauthorized attached materials are prohibited.
- Candidates may not write in their approved reference publications during the examination.

**IMPORTANT: TESTING FACILITY TAB ACCEPTANCE**

Please note that the Testing Facility Proctor is the deciding factor on whether or not tabs will be accepted and allowed into the exam. If the tabs do not adhere properly, they will not be accepted.

**NASCLA Study Guide:** The highlighted and tabbed NASCLA book in this bundle is intended for examination preparation. The Board's examination handbook does not include it among the books permitted inside the examination room.

The included flash cards are also preparation materials and are not permitted examination-room references.

Because business and law questions are closed book, focus on understanding key concepts and practicing recall instead of relying solely on locating information within the guide.

## Licensing Steps

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is administered through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The process involves selecting a classification, meeting eligibility requirements, completing the examination, and applying for the appropriate license.

**Step 1: Select Your Electrical Contractor Classification**

Identify the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, or Special Restricted classification corresponding to your intended electrical contracting work.

**Step 2: Review Examination Qualifications**

Study the Board's eligibility and experience requirements for the classification you intend to pursue.

**Step 3: Submit Your Examination Application**

Complete the required application and provide supporting experience documentation and other information requested by the Board.

**Step 4: Obtain Examination Approval**

After approval, the Board issues a Notice of Approval identifying the examination classification and authorization period.

**Step 5: Schedule Through PSI**

**Step 6: Prepare for Business and Technical Subjects**

**Step 7: Complete the Examination**

**Step 8: Complete the Licensing Application**

After satisfying the examination requirements, complete the Board's licensing application and any remaining qualification procedures.
