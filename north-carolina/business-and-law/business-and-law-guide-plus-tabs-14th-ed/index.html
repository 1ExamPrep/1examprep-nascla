Exam Details

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors regulates electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina. PSI Services administers the Board's examinations.

Examination content includes technical electrical subjects as well as North Carolina licensing law, administrative rules, and construction business management concepts.

The NASCLA North Carolina Electrical 14th Edition is a designated study reference for the business and law content associated with these examinations.

North Carolina Electrical Examination Information:

  • Licensing Authority: North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services
  • Examination Method: Computer-based testing
  • Passing Grade: 70%
  • Business and Law Study Reference: NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, North Carolina Electrical, 14th Edition
  • Major Study Areas: Electrical codes, licensing regulations, Board rules, business practices, and project management

Examination requirements differ among the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications. Each candidate should prepare for the classification associated with the electrical work they intend to perform.

North Carolina electrical examinations test an applicant's technical understanding alongside their knowledge of professional laws and business responsibilities. The NASCLA guide addresses the business management portion of this preparation rather than replacing electrical code study.

Closed Book Test

Questions concerning the NASCLA North Carolina Electrical guide are closed book. The North Carolina electrical contractor examinations allow designated electrical code references, but the Board's examination handbook identifies NASCLA business and law questions as closed-book material.

This means candidates study the NASCLA publication before examination day and answer related business, law, and management questions from their knowledge. The NASCLA book itself is not one of the publications permitted inside the examination room under the handbook's reference-material rules.

The examinations are open book only for specified technical references. The examination handbook identifies the 2020 National Electrical Code as a permitted publication and the 2013 National Fire Alarm Code for the applicable classifications.

Important Reference Rules:

  • Only designated technical reference publications may be brought into the examination room.
  • Permitted code references may be highlighted, underlined, and annotated before testing under the applicable rules.
  • Permanent tabs are permitted on approved examination-room references when they meet the testing requirements.
  • Temporary sticky tabs, stapled tabs, and divider tabs are not permitted.
  • Loose papers and unauthorized additional materials are prohibited.
  • Writing in reference publications during the examination is prohibited.
  • Testing personnel determine whether examination materials meet the applicable requirements.

IMPORTANT: TESTING FACILITY PROCTOR AND TAB ACCEPTANCE

Please note that the Testing Facility Proctor is the deciding factor on whether or not tabs will be accepted and allowed into the exam. If the tabs do not adhere properly, they will not be accepted.

Licensing Steps

North Carolina electrical contractor licensing is administered by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The process involves establishing eligibility, completing the appropriate examination, and applying for the applicable license.

Step 1: Select Your Classification

Identify the Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, or applicable Special Restricted classification corresponding to your intended electrical contracting work.

Step 2: Review the Experience Requirements

Study the Board's eligibility requirements for the selected classification and assemble the experience documentation required for the examination application.

Step 3: Submit Your Examination Application

Complete the Board's application and provide the required supporting information.

Step 4: Receive Examination Approval

Approved candidates receive an examination authorization from the Board identifying the approved classification and authorization period.

Step 5: Schedule Through PSI

After receiving the required eligibility communication, schedule the electrical contractor examination through PSI Services.

Step 6: Study the NASCLA Guide and Electrical Codes

Review the business and law content in the NASCLA 14th Edition. Use the included tabs to organize your study sessions, and prepare with the technical electrical references associated with your examination classification.

Step 7: Complete the Examination

Attend the examination with the required identification and permitted technical reference publications.

Step 8: Complete Your Licensing Application

After passing, submit the appropriate license application and satisfy the Board's applicable licensing requirements.

State Requirements

North Carolina electrical contracting is regulated under the state's Electrical Contractors Licensing Law and the administrative rules of the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

Contractors must hold the appropriate classification for the electrical contracting work they perform and comply with the Board's licensing requirements.

Important areas of professional responsibility include licensing qualifications, applicable electrical code requirements, business administration, contracts, financial management, and compliance with state regulations.

The NASCLA guide supports preparation for business-related concepts while the Board's laws, rules, and technical references address additional state-specific requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA guide is organized into three major sections, giving electrical contracting professionals a structured way to study business management principles.

Part 1: Business Planning and Startup

This section addresses the foundations of establishing a construction business. Topics include preparing a business plan, selecting a business structure, understanding licensing and insurance requirements, and developing management and marketing skills.

Part 2: Construction Business Operations

The second section covers the responsibilities involved in operating a construction business. Topics include estimating, contract management, scheduling, project management, safety, environmental considerations, and working relationships with employees, subcontractors, and customers.

Part 3: Business Administration

The final section focuses on financial management, taxation fundamentals, and lien laws. These subjects help contractors understand the importance of maintaining financial records and managing administrative obligations.
