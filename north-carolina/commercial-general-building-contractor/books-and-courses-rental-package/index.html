Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating state licensing agencies. The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors accepts the NASCLA exam for the Building classification.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • North Carolina Classification: Building

The NASCLA examination is designed around broad commercial general building knowledge rather than a single specialty trade. Preparation may involve general construction requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, building systems, safety, project management, and procurement and contracting responsibilities.

With 115 questions and 330 minutes available, candidates should prepare for both technical knowledge and exam pacing. A long reference search on one difficult question can affect the time available for the rest of the examination. Practice should therefore include identifying questions you can answer from knowledge and recognizing questions where a focused reference lookup is appropriate.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Candidates may use approved references during the examination according to the applicable testing rules.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Access to a large library is most useful when you already understand how the books are organized and which reference applies to the construction subject being tested.

A practical open-book strategy is to identify the topic first, determine the most relevant reference, locate the necessary information, confirm the answer, and move forward. This approach is more efficient than searching several books for every question.

Practice using tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, definitions, technical tables, code sections, and standards. Build associations between specific topics and their corresponding references. Accessibility questions should direct you toward the accessibility standard, while OSHA questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may involve ACI materials, while commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

Regular practice with the rental reference library can help make these decisions more natural. The objective is not to memorize every page, but to know enough about each book that you have a clear starting point when a lookup is needed.

State Requirements

General contractor licensing in North Carolina is administered by the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors. The Board organizes contractor licenses by classification and limitation. Classification determines the type of construction work a contractor may perform, while limitation determines the size of projects the contractor may undertake.

The North Carolina Building classification includes commercial, industrial, institutional, residential, and other building construction within the scope established by the Board. The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for this classification.

North Carolina applicants must submit a license application and satisfy the applicable qualification requirements. The Board requires applicants to identify a qualifier or qualifying party who has passed an examination approved by the Board. Applicants must also satisfy applicable financial responsibility, application, and background requirements.

North Carolina's licensing examination requirements also address knowledge of state contractor law, construction requirements, and sedimentation pollution control. The North Carolina-specific references included in this package provide materials covering these important state topics.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a North Carolina contractor license. NASCLA is an accepted trade examination, while licensure is issued through the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors after the applicable state requirements have been completed.
