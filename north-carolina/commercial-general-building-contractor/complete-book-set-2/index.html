Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Candidates use approved reference materials during the examination according to the applicable NASCLA and testing-center requirements.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the reference library. Having access to many books does not automatically make the examination easier. Searching multiple unfamiliar references for every question can quickly consume valuable testing time.

State Requirements

General contractor licensing in North Carolina is administered by the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors. A person or business seeking licensure must complete the Board's application process and satisfy the requirements associated with the desired classification and limitation.

North Carolina contractor licenses are divided into classifications and limitations. The classification determines the type of contracting work the license holder may perform, while the limitation determines the permitted project size.

The Building classification includes private, public, commercial, industrial, and residential buildings of all types. The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors specifically identifies the NASCLA exam as an accepted examination for the Building classification.

Applicants must also satisfy North Carolina's application requirements. These include being at least 18 years of age, meeting the Board's character and financial responsibility requirements, submitting the required application fee, and consenting to a criminal background check when required by the Board.

The Board requires an applicant to identify a qualifier or qualifying party who has successfully passed an examination approved by the Board. Passing NASCLA does not itself issue a North Carolina contractor license. The NASCLA examination satisfies an accepted examination component, while the applicant must still complete the Board's licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions and provides 330 minutes for completion. Candidates must achieve a minimum score of 70%, equal to 81 correct answers.

Organize study around the exam categories. General Requirements and Procurement and Contracting Requirements represent significant portions of the examination, but candidates should also prepare for site work, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, building-envelope topics, finishes, and building systems.

Match each subject to a reference. Build clear associations between construction topics and books. Accessibility should point you toward the accessibility standard, OSHA safety toward 29 CFR Part 1926, structural concrete toward ACI materials, and broad commercial code questions toward the International Building Code.

Practice find-and-confirm navigation. Read a practice question and determine the subject before reaching for a book. Choose the most relevant reference and search for the specific detail you need. This process develops a more deliberate open-book workflow.

Learn the indexes and tables of contents. Efficient book navigation starts with understanding how information is organized. Spend time learning the language used by each publication and the keywords most likely to direct you toward the correct section.

Train for pace. Timed practice helps candidates avoid spending too much time on individual questions. If a difficult lookup begins consuming excessive time, practice moving forward and returning to the question later.

Review project management and contracting topics. The NASCLA exam is not limited to physical construction methods. Candidates should also prepare for general requirements, procurement, contracting, management, and other responsibilities associated with commercial general contracting.
