Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate North Carolina classification. The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors issues licenses according to classifications and limitations. Candidates using the NASCLA commercial examination for North Carolina should be pursuing the Building classification for which the Board accepts NASCLA.
  2. Choose the applicable license limitation. North Carolina license limitations determine the project value a contractor may undertake.
  3. Submit the North Carolina general contractor license application. Applicants begin the licensing process through the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors.
  4. Identify the qualifier. The applicant must have a qualifying individual who has satisfied an examination accepted by the Board.
  5. Use the NASCLA examination for the Building classification when applicable. North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for this classification.
  6. Meet the applicable financial and application requirements. Requirements vary depending on the license limitation requested.
  7. Complete the Board's review process. Licensing is issued only after the applicant satisfies the North Carolina application and qualification requirements.

State Requirements

General contractor licensing in North Carolina is administered by the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors. North Carolina licenses are organized by both classification and limitation. The classification determines the type of work the contractor may perform, while the limitation determines the size of projects the contractor may undertake.

The North Carolina Building classification covers a broad range of building construction and demolition activity, including commercial, industrial, institutional, and residential building construction within the scope established by the Board.

The Board accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the Building classification. Candidates who have already passed the NASCLA examination can use that examination toward the applicable North Carolina trade-exam requirement, but passing NASCLA does not automatically issue a North Carolina contractor license.

Applicants must still follow the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors application process and meet the applicable classification, limitation, financial, qualification, and other licensing requirements.

North Carolina law generally requires a general contractor license when undertaking construction work meeting the state's statutory contractor licensing threshold. Candidates should select the classification and limitation that correspond with the type and size of work they intend to perform.

Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions and allows 330 minutes for completion. Candidates must answer 81 questions correctly to achieve the 70% minimum passing score.

Study by subject rather than by book. Organize your preparation into construction management, safety, site construction, concrete, masonry, steel, wood construction, roofing, accessibility, building codes, and building systems. Then connect each subject to the references most likely to support it.

Develop reference associations. Accessibility questions should immediately bring the ICC accessibility standard to mind. OSHA questions should direct you toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318, while broad commercial building requirements may involve the International Building Code.

Use the highlighted and tabbed preparation actively. The tabs can help you identify important areas quickly, but repeated practice is what builds familiarity. Work with the books regularly instead of waiting until the final days before the examination.

Practice timed navigation. Complete practice questions while using a clock. Train yourself to recognize when a lookup is becoming too long and move to another question when necessary.

Understand before searching. Start by determining what the question is asking. If you know the answer, avoid an unnecessary search. If confirmation is needed, choose the correct reference and look for the specific information rather than scanning multiple books.
