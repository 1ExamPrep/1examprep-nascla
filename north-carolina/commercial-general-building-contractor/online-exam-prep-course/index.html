Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors accepts the NASCLA examination for the Building classification.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • North Carolina Classification: Building

The exam measures broad commercial general building knowledge. Candidates should expect to prepare across multiple construction disciplines rather than concentrating on one specialty. Major areas include general requirements, site construction, structural concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, doors and glazing, finishes, mechanical and plumbing systems, electrical systems, and procurement and contracting requirements.

The 115-question format also makes pacing important. Candidates should practice answering questions they understand directly while recognizing when a reference is needed to confirm a code requirement, safety provision, technical standard, table, construction detail, or other specific information.

With 330 minutes available, an inefficient lookup strategy can still consume valuable exam time. Good preparation includes understanding the material well enough to identify the subject quickly and knowing which reference is most likely to contain the information needed.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Candidates may use approved references during the examination in accordance with the applicable testing requirements.

An open-book exam should not be approached as an opportunity to search for every answer. The strongest advantage comes from knowing the material and using the books selectively. When a candidate already understands the construction concept, the reference can be used to confirm a specific detail rather than becoming the starting point for every question.

Reference navigation should therefore be part of the study process. Candidates should become familiar with each major book's table of contents, index, terminology, chapter structure, technical tables, definitions, standards, and code organization.

Practice associating common subjects with specific references. Accessibility questions should lead you toward the applicable accessibility standard. OSHA safety questions should direct you toward construction safety regulations. Structural concrete questions may require concrete codes and reinforcing references, while broader commercial building requirements may require navigation of the International Building Code.

A practical open-book strategy is to identify the subject, choose the most relevant reference, locate the needed information, confirm the answer, and continue. Developing this find-and-confirm habit during practice can help reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

  1. Select the appropriate North Carolina classification. North Carolina licenses general contractors according to classifications and limitations. The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is accepted for the Building classification.
  2. Select the appropriate license limitation. North Carolina license limitations determine the maximum value of projects that may be undertaken under the license.
  3. Complete the North Carolina general contractor application. Applicants submit the applicable new-license application to the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors.
  4. Identify the qualifier. The applicant must identify a qualifier who satisfies an examination accepted by the Board for the requested classification.
  5. Use the NASCLA examination when applicable. Candidates choosing the NASCLA pathway complete the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the accepted Building classification examination requirement.
  6. Complete applicable financial and qualification requirements. The applicant must satisfy the requirements associated with the requested license limitation and classification.

State Requirements

General contractor licensing in North Carolina is administered by the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors. The Board regulates licenses according to both classification and limitation.

The classification determines the type of construction work a contractor may perform. The North Carolina Building classification covers a broad range of private, public, commercial, industrial, institutional, and residential building construction and includes additional work within the scope defined by the Board.

The limitation determines the dollar size of projects the contractor may undertake. Applicants must therefore select both the classification appropriate to their work and the limitation appropriate to the project values they intend to pursue.

The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the Building classification. Candidates choosing NASCLA generally complete that examination before applying for the North Carolina general contractor license and then provide the applicable NASCLA information as part of the licensing process.

Passing the NASCLA exam does not automatically create a North Carolina general contractor license. The examination satisfies an accepted testing component. Applicants must still complete the applicable North Carolina application and meet the Board's remaining classification, limitation, financial, qualifier, and licensing requirements.

North Carolina law requires general contractor licensing for construction activity meeting the state's applicable statutory project-cost threshold. Candidates should select the license classification and limitation that correspond with the work they intend to perform.

Test Information and Study Materials

Preparing for the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is easier when the reference library is connected to a structured study plan. Candidates should focus on understanding the construction subjects first and learning how individual references support those areas.

Study by knowledge area. Divide preparation into project management, safety, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, mechanical systems, electrical systems, and contracting requirements. This approach can make a large syllabus easier to manage.

Build reference associations. Learn which books support each subject. When a question involves accessibility, you should know where to begin. The same applies to OSHA safety, structural concrete, steel deck, masonry, roofing, carpentry, and commercial building-code questions.

Practice finding and confirming information. Begin each practice question by analyzing what is being asked. Answer from your knowledge when appropriate. When a technical detail needs confirmation, use the most relevant reference rather than searching the entire library.

Use timed practice. With 115 questions and 330 minutes, candidates should become comfortable maintaining a consistent pace. Timed practice helps develop the discipline to move forward when a reference search becomes too long.

Review management and contracting topics. The NASCLA exam is not limited to physical construction methods. Contractors should also prepare for questions involving project administration, procurement, contracting, scheduling, financial management, and other responsibilities associated with operating and managing commercial construction projects.

Practice applying concepts. Exam preparation should go beyond memorizing definitions. Candidates should work on recognizing construction situations, identifying applicable principles, comparing possible answers, and using references when additional confirmation is required.
