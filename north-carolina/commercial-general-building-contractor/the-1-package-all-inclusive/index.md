## Licensing Steps

The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors administers general contractor licensing. Applicants using the NASCLA examination must still complete the Board's application and qualification requirements before receiving a North Carolina license.

1. **Determine the appropriate classification.** North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination for the Building classification.
2. **Select the desired license limitation.** Applicants choose Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited based on the size of projects the business intends to undertake and the financial qualification it can meet.
3. **Identify a qualifier.** The applicant must identify an individual who holds an examination credential accepted by the Board and who will serve as the qualifier for the license.
4. **Complete the NASCLA examination.** Candidates choosing the NASCLA pathway take the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination before using the result for North Carolina qualification.
5. **Submit the North Carolina license application.** New license applications are submitted through the Board's licensing system with the required business, qualifier, financial, and supporting information.

7. **Meet financial responsibility requirements.** The applicant must satisfy the financial standards associated with the selected Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited limitation.
8. **Complete applicable background requirements.** New applicants and qualifying individuals are subject to the background-check provisions established by the Board.
9. **Complete Board review.** The North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors reviews the application and supporting qualifications before issuing the license.

## State Requirements

North Carolina requires a general contractor license when the value of a covered construction project is **$40,000 or more**. The licensing requirement applies to covered building, highway, public utilities, grading, and other qualifying construction work.

The North Carolina **Building Contractor** classification includes commercial, industrial, institutional, public, private, and residential building construction. Its scope also includes qualifying sitework, grading, paving, storm drainage, retaining and screen walls, accessory structures, recreational facilities, and several specialty construction areas identified by Board rules.

North Carolina licenses are also divided by monetary limitation. A **Limited License** permits projects valued up to **$750,000** each. An **Intermediate License** permits projects valued up to **$1,500,000** each. An **Unlimited License** does not restrict the value of an individual project.

Financial requirements differ by limitation. Limited applicants must have current assets exceeding current liabilities by at least **$17,000** or total net worth of at least **$80,000**. Intermediate applicants must demonstrate at least **$75,000** in working capital. Unlimited applicants must demonstrate at least **$150,000** in working capital. North Carolina rules also provide qualifying surety-bond alternatives for these financial requirements.

Applicants must be at least **18 years old**, demonstrate financial responsibility, submit the appropriate application, and satisfy the Board's character and background requirements.

NASCLA qualification does not itself authorize contracting in North Carolina. The examination credential is used as part of the licensing process, and the contractor must receive the appropriate North Carolina license before undertaking work that requires licensure.
