Exam Details

The NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination is administered by PSI through the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

North Carolina accepts passing results from this examination for several electrical contractor classifications, including Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors (Master Electricians/Unlimited Electricians).
  • Accrediting Organization: National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA).
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services.
  • North Carolina Licensing Authority: North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.
  • Scored Questions: 100.
  • Additional Questions: 10 unscored experimental questions.
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes, or 4 hours and 30 minutes.
  • Passing Score: 75 correct answers out of 100 scored questions.
  • Examination Format: Open book.
  • Testing Method: Computer-based.
  • Electrical Code Reference: National Electrical Code, 2020 or 2023 Edition.

The examination covers electrical installation principles, technical calculations, equipment, wiring methods, special occupancies, project management, and safety.

Examination content update: NASCLA has published a revised subject outline effective January 1, 2027. Candidates testing through December 31, 2026, use the earlier outline described below.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring designated technical publications into the PSI examination center.

Open book testing requires familiarity with the National Electrical Code, safety standards, electrical reference materials, and the organization of technical information.

Approved reference publications must be supplied by the candidate. The testing center does not provide these books.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before examination day. Permanent tabs are permitted, but handwritten notes, temporary tabs, and additional loose papers are prohibited.

Optional electrical code indexes are permitted under separate restrictions and must not contain highlighting, markings, or tabs.

PSI supplies a physical diagram and blueprint packet for applicable examination questions. Candidates may also use a permitted silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator.

Practice locating conductor tables, grounding requirements, equipment provisions, and calculation procedures. Efficient reference navigation supports better use of the examination time.

Licensing Steps

North Carolina recognizes NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination results as an alternative route for satisfying the applicable technical examination requirement.

  1. Review NASCLA examination requirements: Understand the electrical examination content, reference publications, and testing rules.
  2. Apply through NASCLA: Submit the examination application through NASCLA's National Examination Database.
  3. Receive examination authorization: Obtain approval to schedule through PSI.
  4. Prepare for the examination: Review electrical codes, calculations, installation procedures, and safety requirements.
  5. Schedule with PSI: Select an examination appointment after receiving authorization.
  6. Complete the examination: Take the NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination and meet the passing score.
  7. Submit your transcript: Arrange electronic delivery of the passing NASCLA examination transcript to the North Carolina electrical licensing Board.
  8. Select your license classification: Apply for the appropriate North Carolina electrical contractor classification.
  9. Complete state-specific requirements: Submit qualifying information, character statements, and the North Carolina Laws, Rules and Business Practices examination.
  10. Complete licensing procedures: Provide the additional documentation applicable to the selected classification before license issuance.

NASCLA examination approval provides one year of eligibility, with up to three examination attempts during that period.

Passing the NASCLA examination satisfies an accepted technical examination requirement but does not independently issue a North Carolina electrical contractor license.

North Carolina State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors regulates electrical contractor licensing under Chapter 87, Article 4 of the North Carolina General Statutes.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for its Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and qualifying special restricted electrical contractor classifications.

Applicants must satisfy the experience, age, character, and other licensing requirements applicable to their requested classification.

North Carolina distinguishes its principal electrical classifications as follows:

  • Limited (L): Electrical contracting within the applicable project-value and installation limitations.
  • Intermediate (I): Electrical contracting for projects within the Intermediate classification's authorized limits.
  • Unlimited (U): Electrical contracting without an individual project-value limitation.

Applicants using the NASCLA examination must also complete the North Carolina Laws, Rules and Business Practices examination. This separate open book examination contains 25 questions and requires at least 18 correct answers.

The Board's NASCLA licensing application also requires electronic examination transcripts, a qualified individual affidavit, character statements, and classification-specific documentation.

Applicants seeking an Unlimited license must provide additional supervision and financial qualification information. Intermediate applicants must meet their classification's applicable financial requirements.

Reference Books

The NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination uses the following approved reference publications for the examination outline effective through December 31, 2026.

  • National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023 Edition
    Provides requirements covering electrical circuits, conductors, grounding, bonding, equipment, wiring methods, and special installations.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR 1926 – OSHA Construction Industry Regulations, 2024 Edition
    Addresses construction-site safety, worker protection, equipment hazards, and occupational safety requirements.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR 1910 – OSHA Occupational Safety and Health Standards, 2024 Edition
    Provides workplace safety regulations relevant to electrical work and occupational hazards.
  • A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge, 7th Edition, 2021
    Introduces project management concepts, planning, coordination, and organizational responsibilities.
  • ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022, Energy Standard for Sites and Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential Buildings, I-P Edition
    Addresses building energy efficiency, including provisions affecting electrical and lighting systems.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic, 14th Edition, 2024
    Explains contractor operations, contracts, project administration, business management, and professional responsibilities.
  • NFPA 70E: Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024 Edition
    Addresses electrical workplace hazards, risk assessment, protective measures, and safe electrical work practices.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023 Edition
    Contains electrical formulas, technical tables, calculations, and quick-reference information.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications, Mike Holt
    Explains electrical theory and its application to electrical installations and NEC requirements.

Exam Room Approved Books

The nine publications listed above are approved for examination-room use, subject to NASCLA and PSI reference-material rules.

The examination also permits the following optional indexing references:

  • Ferm's Fast Finder Index
    An optional electrical code index intended to help locate NEC information efficiently.
  • Key Word Index by Tom Henry, Based on the National Electrical Code

Electrical theory provides the foundation for understanding circuits, equipment, and installation requirements.

Study voltage, current, resistance, power, alternating current, and circuit relationships. Review Ohm's law, power calculations, and methods for interpreting electrical quantities.

Practice identifying the appropriate formulas and solving problems involving electrical loads and system characteristics.

National Electrical Code Requirements

The National Electrical Code is a central preparation resource for the NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor examination.

Study NEC definitions, installation provisions, conductor requirements, electrical equipment, and special conditions.

Practice locating articles, tables, and exceptions while developing a structured approach to code questions.

Grounding, Bonding, and Electrical Protection

Grounding and bonding help establish effective fault-current paths and support electrical system safety.

Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, and conductor sizing.

Review overcurrent protection, circuit breakers, fuses, and electrical protection principles. Practice identifying applicable code requirements for different installation situations.

Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials

Electrical contractors must understand appropriate wiring methods for different environments and applications.

Study raceways, conduits, cable assemblies, conductors, fittings, electrical boxes, and terminations.

Review conductor ampacity, raceway fill, physical protection, and installation methods.

Practice interpreting wiring scenarios and selecting the relevant NEC provisions.

Electrical Equipment and Motor Systems

Electrical equipment installation involves technical requirements for services, panelboards, transformers, motors, and other systems.

Study equipment ratings, disconnecting means, motor circuits, and protective devices.

Review the relationship between electrical loads, conductors, and equipment capacity.

Become familiar with the technical requirements associated with different equipment applications.

Special Occupancies and Electrical Installations

Some electrical installations require additional provisions because of their location, equipment, or operating conditions.

Study hazardous locations, special occupancies, specialized equipment, and electrical systems requiring specific installation procedures.

Practice identifying applicable NEC chapters and understanding how special requirements affect electrical work.

Electrical Safety and Workplace Protection

Electrical safety is a major responsibility for master-level electrical professionals.

Study OSHA construction standards, general industry safety requirements, and NFPA 70E.

Review electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, safe work practices, and jobsite safety responsibilities.

Practice locating relevant requirements in the approved safety references.

Electrical Project Management and Plan Reading

Electrical contractors coordinate materials, labor, construction documents, schedules, and project requirements.

Study electrical drawings, specifications, estimating concepts, and project administration.

Review the relationship between construction plans, electrical installations, and contractor responsibilities.

Practice interpreting simplified diagrams and identifying the technical information required for electrical planning.
