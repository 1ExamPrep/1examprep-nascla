Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and is accepted by participating state and local licensing agencies.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractor / Master Electrician / Unlimited Electrician
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The examination is developed around electrical trade knowledge and professional responsibilities. Current examination topics include electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design, estimating and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and special equipment installations, tools and test equipment, circuit protection and installation, wiring methods and practices, equipment for general use, and energy-related concepts.

The broad subject range means preparation should involve multiple references rather than relying only on the National Electrical Code. Candidates should practice recognizing whether a question involves Code application, safety, electrical theory, project management, or another reference category before beginning a search.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for applicants seeking eligible Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited classifications. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book examination. Approved reference materials may be used during testing according to current NASCLA and testing-provider requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Technical books can contain hundreds of pages of information, and searching every question without knowing how the references are organized can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should practice identifying the subject first and then moving to the reference most likely to contain the answer.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable with the NEC index, definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study areas should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and specialized systems.

Electrical theory should be studied through active calculations. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related electrical problems. Develop a consistent process for identifying the information provided, determining what must be solved, selecting the correct formula, completing the calculation, and then checking whether a Code requirement affects the final answer.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning how each book is organized can make safety-related research more efficient.

State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted licenses and establishes the experience and qualification standards that applicants must satisfy.

North Carolina became a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program in 2023. Applicants who pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination may apply for eligible Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited classifications.

The Limited classification requires at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 2,000 hours of primary experience involving direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires at least 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 5,000 hours of primary electrical experience. The Limited and Intermediate classifications are also subject to project-value limits established under North Carolina law and Board rules.

The Unlimited classification requires at least 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary electrical experience. Unlimited applicants must also submit written statements from at least two individuals knowledgeable about the applicant's electrical experience and ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring and installation work.

North Carolina currently limits Limited license holders to qualifying electrical projects not exceeding $60,000 in value. Intermediate license holders may engage in qualifying projects up to $150,000, while Unlimited license holders may undertake electrical contracting projects regardless of project value.

Applicants using the NASCLA examination route must have their examination transcript sent to the Board. Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create a North Carolina electrical contractor license. Applicants must still complete the state application and meet the experience, financial, business, and other requirements for the requested classification.

North Carolina also requires continuing education for listed qualified individuals. Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted Single-Family Dwelling qualified individuals are currently required to complete eight hours of approved continuing education for license renewal.
