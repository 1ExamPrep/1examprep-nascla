Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is a standardized professional electrical trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies, including the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Additional Experimental Questions: 10 non-scored questions may be included
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: PSI

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the North Carolina electrical contracting classification you want. North Carolina offers Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several Special Restricted electrical contracting classifications.
  2. Review the qualification requirements. Applicants must satisfy the experience and application requirements established by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors for the classification being pursued.
  3. Take the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. North Carolina accepts this NASCLA examination for applicants seeking eligible electrical contracting classifications.
  4. Prepare with the examination references. Study electrical theory, Code requirements, safety, wiring methods, equipment, grounding and bonding, circuit protection, project management, and supporting technical subjects.
  5. Pass the NASCLA examination. After passing, the examination result is maintained through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  6. Send the NASCLA transcript to the North Carolina Board. North Carolina requires NASCLA-qualified applicants to have their NASCLA examination transcript electronically transmitted to the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.
  7. Select the eligible North Carolina license classification. A candidate who has passed the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination may apply for eligible North Carolina classifications including Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and applicable Special Restricted classifications.
  8. Complete the North Carolina license application. Submit the applicable application, qualification information, business information, and other required documentation to the Board.
  9. Satisfy activation requirements. The business must meet North Carolina's requirements for listed qualified individuals and any financial or bonding ability requirements applicable to the license classification.

State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted electrical contracting licenses and establishes the experience and qualification standards associated with each classification.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for multiple license classifications. Applicants who have passed this NASCLA examination may apply for Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting licensure, provided they meet the requirements associated with the desired classification.

North Carolina requires at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience for the Limited classification. At least 2,000 of those hours must consist of primary experience. Primary experience generally involves direct electrical installation work governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires at least 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 5,000 hours of primary experience. The Intermediate license allows the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects within the project-value limitations established for that classification.

The Unlimited classification requires at least 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary experience. Unlimited applicants must also provide statements from two persons knowledgeable about the applicant's electrical experience that address the applicant's ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring or installation work performed by an electrical contracting business.

The Unlimited classification allows a licensed electrical contracting business to undertake electrical contracting projects without a project-value limitation. The Limited and Intermediate classifications operate under the project limits established by North Carolina law and Board rules.

North Carolina requires an electrical contracting licensee to have at least one listed qualified individual who is regularly employed and has responsibility and authority to supervise and direct the electrical contracting work performed under the license. Businesses with separate places of business must comply with the Board's requirements for qualified individuals.

Intermediate and Unlimited license applicants must also satisfy North Carolina's bonding ability or financial qualification requirements. These requirements are part of license activation and are separate from passing the NASCLA trade examination.

A passing NASCLA examination result is not itself a North Carolina electrical contracting license. The NASCLA examination can satisfy the accepted trade-examination component, but applicants must still send the required transcript and complete the North Carolina licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan uses the books actively rather than saving them for examination day. Begin by learning which reference is most useful for each major subject. This can help reduce time spent searching in the wrong book when working through practice questions.

Spend significant time with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice finding requirements involving services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and electrical equipment. Pay attention to definitions, exceptions, tables, and cross-referenced provisions.

Electrical theory should be practiced through calculations. Use the theory references to reinforce circuit relationships and calculation methods. A consistent process can include identifying the information given, determining what must be solved, selecting the appropriate formula, maintaining correct units, completing the calculation, and then checking for applicable Code requirements.

Safety preparation should involve both OSHA and NFPA 70E. Learn the function and organization of each reference so you can determine which source is appropriate when a question addresses jobsite safety, electrical hazards, or workplace electrical practices.

Project design, estimating, and management also deserve focused preparation. Electrical contractors work within larger construction projects involving planning, communication, scheduling, coordination, risk management, documentation, and business responsibilities. These areas extend examination preparation beyond installation skills alone.

Timed reference-navigation exercises can help bring the material together. Choose practice questions from different subjects, identify the appropriate reference, locate the controlling information, and measure the time required. Repeated practice can reveal which books or topics need additional study.
