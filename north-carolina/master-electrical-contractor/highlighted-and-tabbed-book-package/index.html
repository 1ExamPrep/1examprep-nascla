Exam Details

It is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program, which provides standardized trade examinations recognized by participating licensing agencies.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Pretest Questions: 10 non-scored questions may be included
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is developed around occupational analysis representing work performed by experienced electrical professionals. Questions are intended to evaluate practical electrical knowledge and application rather than simple memorization of isolated Code numbers.

State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted electrical contracting licenses and establishes the experience and qualification requirements associated with each classification.

North Carolina became a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program in 2023. Applicants who pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination may apply for eligible Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting classifications, provided all other requirements for the selected license are satisfied.

The Limited classification requires 3,000 hours of qualifying experience, with at least 2,000 hours consisting of primary experience gained through direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, with at least 5,000 hours consisting of primary electrical experience. Candidates pursuing this classification must document their experience according to the Board's application requirements.

The Unlimited classification requires 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary experience involving direct electrical installation work. Unlimited applicants must also provide the additional experience documentation required by the Board concerning their ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring and installation work.

The Unlimited classification allows a properly licensed electrical contracting business to undertake electrical contracting projects without the project-value limitation associated with the Limited and Intermediate classifications. The Limited and Intermediate classifications remain subject to the project limits established by North Carolina law and Board rules.

North Carolina requires licensed electrical contracting businesses to maintain at least one listed qualified individual who is regularly employed by the business and has the responsibility and authority to supervise and direct the electrical contracting work performed under the license.

The NASCLA examination does not itself create a North Carolina electrical contractor license. After passing the NASCLA examination, the applicant must have the examination transcript electronically transmitted to the North Carolina Board and complete the applicable state licensing process.

Intermediate and Unlimited applicants must also satisfy applicable financial or bonding ability requirements as part of the North Carolina licensing process. These state requirements are separate from the NASCLA trade examination and remain the responsibility of the applicant and contracting business.

Test Information and Study Materials

The highlighted and tabbed book package is most useful when the references become part of your regular study process. Candidates should use the books while reviewing technical subjects, solving calculations, and working through practice questions so navigation becomes familiar before exam day.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Practice following Code cross-references and locating applicable tables instead of relying exclusively on memorized article numbers.

Electrical theory should be studied through calculations and problem solving. Use the theory references to review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other fundamental electrical principles. Develop a repeatable method for identifying known values, selecting the correct formula, completing the calculation, and determining whether a Code provision affects the result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn which reference applies to the type of safety question being asked. Familiarity with the organization of both resources can help reduce unnecessary searching during an open-book examination.

Project-management preparation should include planning, estimating, coordination, communication, scheduling, documentation, risk, and other responsibilities associated with electrical contracting. The project-management and NASCLA contractor references broaden preparation beyond technical installation knowledge.

The energy-efficiency standard should also be incorporated into the study schedule. Learn how the reference is organized and become familiar with its terminology so energy-related material can be located efficiently when needed.

Use the tabs and highlighting during timed practice. Select a question, identify the subject, move to the relevant tabbed section, find the applicable highlighted or surrounding material, and verify the answer. Consistent practice can help candidates build a more confident and organized reference-navigation routine.
