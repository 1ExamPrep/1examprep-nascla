## State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted electrical contracting licenses and establishes the experience and qualification requirements associated with each classification.

North Carolina became a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program in 2023. Applicants who pass the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination may apply for eligible Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting classifications, provided all other requirements for the selected license are satisfied.

The Limited classification requires 3,000 hours of qualifying experience, with at least 2,000 hours consisting of primary experience gained through direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, with at least 5,000 hours consisting of primary electrical experience. Candidates pursuing this classification must document their experience according to the Board's application requirements.

The Unlimited classification requires 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary experience involving direct electrical installation work. Unlimited applicants must also provide the additional experience documentation required by the Board concerning their ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring and installation work.

The Unlimited classification allows a properly licensed electrical contracting business to undertake electrical contracting projects without the project-value limitation associated with the Limited and Intermediate classifications. The Limited and Intermediate classifications remain subject to the project limits established by North Carolina law and Board rules.

North Carolina requires licensed electrical contracting businesses to maintain at least one listed qualified individual who is regularly employed by the business and has the responsibility and authority to supervise and direct the electrical contracting work performed under the license.

The NASCLA examination does not itself create a North Carolina electrical contractor license. After passing the NASCLA examination, the applicant must have the examination transcript electronically transmitted to the North Carolina Board and complete the applicable state licensing process.

Intermediate and Unlimited applicants must also satisfy applicable financial or bonding ability requirements as part of the North Carolina licensing process. These state requirements are separate from the NASCLA trade examination and remain the responsibility of the applicant and contracting business.
