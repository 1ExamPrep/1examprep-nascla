## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Authorized references may be used according to the current NASCLA examination rules and testing-center requirements.

Open-book testing places a premium on familiarity with the reference material. Candidates who know how their books are structured can generally approach lookups more efficiently than candidates who begin searching an unfamiliar reference from scratch. Preparation should therefore include repeated navigation practice as well as technical study.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a major part of preparation. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC index, definitions, articles, chapters, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor requirements, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, electrical equipment, and special installations.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Candidates can strengthen preparation by practicing voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other electrical calculations. A useful approach is to identify what the question provides, determine what must be solved, select the correct formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the answer.

Safety study should include OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. These references address different areas of professional safety. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning where subjects are located within each book helps make open-book study more efficient.

Live virtual instruction can help candidates understand how these subjects connect, while self-study provides additional repetition. Candidates can return to Code sections reviewed during class, repeat calculation problems, practice safety lookups, and spend additional time on difficult concepts between scheduled sessions.

## State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board licenses electrical contractors in Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications and establishes qualification, experience, continuing-education, and licensing requirements.

The Limited classification requires at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 2,000 hours of primary experience involving direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires at least 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, with at least 5,000 hours consisting of primary electrical experience. Applicants must document the qualifying experience required by the Board.

The Unlimited classification requires at least 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary electrical experience. Unlimited applicants must also provide statements from individuals knowledgeable about their electrical experience attesting to their ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring or installation work performed by an electrical contracting business.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for applicants seeking Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited classifications. Candidates must have their NASCLA examination transcripts submitted to the Board as part of the licensing application.

The Unlimited classification permits the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects regardless of project value. The Limited and Intermediate classifications are subject to the project-value limits established under North Carolina law and Board rules.

North Carolina also requires continuing education for qualified individuals. Qualified individuals listed on Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted Single-Family Dwelling licenses are currently required to complete eight hours of approved continuing education for license renewal. Other Special Restricted classifications generally require four hours.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a North Carolina electrical contractor license. Applicants must complete the Board's application process, document the required experience, submit the NASCLA transcript, and satisfy all additional requirements associated with the license classification being requested.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should combine technical study with active use of the reference books. Live virtual instruction provides a structured way to work through important subjects, while self-study allows candidates to reinforce those subjects through repetition and practice.

Start with National Electrical Code navigation. Practice using the index instead of relying entirely on memorized article numbers. Follow Code cross-references, work with tables, locate definitions, and become familiar with how requirements are organized across the NEC.

Electrical calculations should also be practiced regularly. Work through circuit theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, motor, transformer, conductor, and other electrical problems. Focus on understanding the method rather than simply memorizing an answer.

Safety preparation should include direct practice with OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn which subjects belong in each reference and how those books are organized. This can help reduce unnecessary searching during open-book practice.

Project-management and contractor-business material should remain part of the study plan. Electrical contractors work within projects involving planning, communication, coordination, documentation, scheduling, risk, and professional management responsibilities. Energy-efficiency standards add another technical area candidates should learn to navigate.

Timed reference exercises can help connect all of these study areas. Select a practice question, identify the subject, determine which reference applies, locate the supporting information, and track the time required. This creates practical experience using the same decision-making process needed during an open-book examination.
