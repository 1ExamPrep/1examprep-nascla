## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. It is one of the open-book examinations offered through the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and can be accepted by participating licensing agencies in lieu of an agency-specific trade examination.

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors has participated in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program since 2023. The Board accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for applicants pursuing eligible Special Restricted, Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited classifications.

Current NASCLA examination content for Electrical Contractors includes Project Design and Management, Safety, Electrical Theory, General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, Equipment for General Use, Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions, and Communication Systems.

The published NASCLA content outline includes 8 items in Project Design and Management, 9 in Safety, 11 in Electrical Theory, 17 in General Code Requirements, 17 in Wiring and Protection, 16 in Wiring Methods and Materials, 13 in Equipment for General Use, 8 in Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions, and 1 in Communication Systems.

This broad subject distribution makes a complete reference-based study plan important. Candidates should prepare to move between the National Electrical Code and supporting references rather than expecting every answer to come from a single book.

North Carolina applicants using the NASCLA pathway must have their NASCLA examination transcript sent to the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors as part of the application process. Passing the examination does not automatically create a North Carolina electrical contractor license.

## State Requirements

Electrical contracting in North Carolina is regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted licenses and establishes the experience, application, examination, and continuing-education requirements associated with those classifications.

The Limited classification requires at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 2,000 hours of primary experience involving direct installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code.

The Intermediate classification requires at least 5,750 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 5,000 hours of primary electrical experience. The current Board licensing information limits an Intermediate license holder to a single electrical contracting project valued at no more than $150,000.

The Unlimited classification requires at least 9,000 hours of qualifying experience, including at least 8,000 hours of primary electrical experience. Unlimited applicants must also submit written statements from at least two people familiar with the applicant's electrical experience attesting to the applicant's ability to supervise and direct electrical wiring or installation work performed by an electrical contracting business.

The Unlimited classification allows the licensee to engage in electrical contracting projects regardless of project value. The Limited classification currently allows a single electrical contracting project valued at no more than $60,000 and subject to the applicable voltage limitation established by the Board.

Applicants for North Carolina electrical contractor examinations must be at least 18 years old, submit the required application, provide written statements from at least two people attesting to good character, and satisfy the experience requirements for the classification requested.

North Carolina's acceptance of the NASCLA examination does not eliminate these eligibility requirements. The NASCLA transcript can satisfy an accepted examination component, but the Board still evaluates the applicant's qualifying experience and license application.

Continuing education is also part of maintaining a North Carolina license. Qualified individuals listed on Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted Single-Family Dwelling licenses must complete eight hours of approved continuing education for renewal under current Board requirements.

Candidates pursuing the NASCLA pathway should therefore think beyond the examination itself. The process includes qualifying experience, the NASCLA transcript, the North Carolina application, applicable financial documentation, qualifying-individual responsibilities, and continuing obligations after licensure.
