Exam Details

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is administered by PSI through the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies.

The examination measures technical knowledge associated with residential electrical installations, troubleshooting, safety, equipment, and professional electrical contracting.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Residential Electrical Contractors.
  • North Carolina License Classification: Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling (SP-SFD).
  • Accrediting Organization: NASCLA.
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services.
  • Scored Questions: 100.
  • Unscored Experimental Questions: 10.
  • Passing Score: 70 correct answers from 100 scored questions.
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes, or 4 hours and 30 minutes.
  • Examination Format: Open book.
  • Testing Method: Computer-based.
  • Approved NEC Editions: 2020 or 2023 National Electrical Code.

Wiring methods and practices represent the largest examination category, followed by circuit protection, grounding and bonding, and electrical equipment.

Candidates should develop familiarity with electrical installation principles and efficient navigation of technical references.

Open Book Test

The North Carolina NASCLA Residential Electrician Contractor examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring the designated technical references into the PSI examination center.

Open book testing requires familiarity with the National Electrical Code, electrical safety standards, residential construction requirements, and technical electrical references.

Candidates must supply their own approved publications. The testing center does not provide reference books.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before examination day. Permanent tabs are permitted, but temporary tabs, handwritten notes, and additional loose papers are prohibited.

Optional electrical code indexes are permitted under separate restrictions and must remain free of marks, highlighting, and tabs.

PSI provides a physical diagram and blueprint packet for applicable questions.

Candidates may also use a silent, nonprinting, nonprogrammable calculator that complies with examination rules.

Practice navigating NEC articles, conductor tables, wiring requirements, grounding provisions, and electrical installation calculations.

Licensing Steps

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an alternative technical examination for the SP-SFD electrical contractor classification.

  1. Review examination requirements: Understand NASCLA's residential electrical examination content and approved references.
  2. Apply through NASCLA: Submit an examination application through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  3. Receive examination approval: Obtain authorization before scheduling with PSI.
  4. Prepare for testing: Study residential electrical systems, code requirements, safety, and calculations.
  5. Schedule with PSI: Arrange your examination appointment.
  6. Complete the examination: Take the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination and achieve the passing score.
  7. Submit examination transcripts: Arrange electronic transcript delivery to the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors through NASCLA's database.
  8. Apply for SP-SFD licensing: Complete the North Carolina licensing application and provide required supporting documentation.
  9. Complete North Carolina requirements: Submit the required character statements and North Carolina Laws, Rules and Business Practices examination.
  10. Complete license issuance: Satisfy the Board's remaining requirements before performing regulated electrical contracting.

NASCLA examination authorization is valid for one year from approval, with a maximum of three examination attempts during that period.

A passing NASCLA examination result does not independently provide a North Carolina electrical contractor license.

North Carolina State Requirements

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors regulates electrical contractor licensing under Chapter 87, Article 4 of the North Carolina General Statutes.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination specifically for the SP-SFD classification.

The SP-SFD classification authorizes installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical wiring and devices in or on single-family residential dwellings and associated ancillary structures.

Examples of ancillary structures include detached residential garages, carports, storage buildings, and other qualifying structures associated with a single-family residence.

The SP-SFD classification does not authorize unrestricted commercial or industrial electrical contracting.

Applicants must satisfy the Board's qualification requirements, including applicable electrical experience and character requirements.

North Carolina's examination qualification requirements for SP-SFD include 3,000 hours of qualifying electrical experience, with at least 2,000 hours of primary experience.

Applicants using a NASCLA examination transcript must also complete the North Carolina Laws, Rules and Business Practices examination as part of the licensing application.

This separate examination contains 25 questions and requires at least 18 correct answers.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for SP-SFD licensing only. Applicants pursuing Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting through NASCLA must use the qualifying Master Electrician/Electrical Contractor examination instead.

Reference Books

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination uses nine approved reference publications covering residential electrical systems, workplace safety, electrical theory, business practices, and construction codes.

  • NFPA 70: National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2020 or 2023 Edition
    Provides electrical installation requirements covering conductors, grounding, bonding, circuits, wiring methods, electrical equipment, and residential electrical systems.
  • Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926 – OSHA Construction Industry Regulations, 2024 Edition
    Addresses construction workplace safety, electrical hazards, equipment precautions, and worker protection requirements.
  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Basic, 14th Edition
    Explains business operations, estimating, project management, contracts, construction administration, and contractor responsibilities.
  • NFPA 70E: Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024 Edition
    Provides requirements and guidance involving electrical workplace hazards, protective measures, and safe work practices.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023 Edition
    Contains electrical formulas, calculations, technical tables, wiring information, and reference data useful for electrical professionals.
  • NFPA 72: National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2022 Edition
    Addresses fire alarm equipment, signaling systems, installation considerations, and related technical requirements.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications, Mike Holt
    Explains electrical theory and its relationship to practical applications of the National Electrical Code.
  • ANSI Z535.4-2023: Product Safety Signs and Labels
    Establishes requirements for product safety signs, warning labels, and hazard communication.
  • International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2024 Edition
    Provides residential building requirements and construction provisions associated with one- and two-family dwellings.

Exam Room Approved Books

The nine publications listed in the Reference Books section are approved for examination-room use under the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination reference policy.

The examination also permits two optional indexes:

  • Ferm's Fast Finder Index
    An optional electrical code index designed to assist with locating NEC topics.
  • Key Word Index by Tom Henry, Based on the National Electrical Code
    An optional keyword reference for navigating the National Electrical Code.

Optional indexes must contain no highlighting, annotations, or tabs. PSI supplies the physical diagram and blueprint packet at the testing center.
