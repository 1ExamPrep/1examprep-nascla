## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina is regulated by the **North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors**. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several Special Restricted electrical contracting licenses, including the SP-SFD classification.

Under current North Carolina rules, an applicant for the **SP-SFD Single-Family Detached Residential Dwelling classification** must have at least **two years of qualifying experience**. At least one year must consist of primary experience, while the remaining experience may be primary, secondary, or a combination of both.

North Carolina defines primary experience as work directly involving the installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code or closely related work activities. The Board evaluates the applicant's documented experience to determine whether the requirements have been met.

Applicants must also be at least 18 years old, submit the required application, and provide written statements from at least two people attesting to their good character.

The SP-SFD classification has a specific residential scope. It authorizes the license holder to **install, maintain, or repair electrical wiring and devices in or on a single-family residential dwelling or ancillary to that dwelling**.

North Carolina defines a single-family residential dwelling as a building or manufactured home designed and used for habitation by one family and not physically attached to another building or structure. Cabanas, porches, room additions, and qualifying ancillary structures can be included when they are designed for and used for residential purposes by the occupants of the dwelling.

Examples of ancillary structures identified in North Carolina's rules include detached residential garages or carports, household or farm equipment storage sheds, barns, pump houses, electric fences, and yard lighting associated with the single-family dwelling.

The SP-SFD classification is more limited than North Carolina's regular Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contracting classifications. Contractors should remain within the scope authorized by the SP-SFD credential.

North Carolina currently lists a **$100 license fee** for the SP-SFD classification. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is accepted specifically for this classification and does not qualify an applicant for the broader Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited licenses.

Applicants using the NASCLA route must ensure that their NASCLA examination transcript is sent to the Board as part of the application process. The Board maintains authority over licensing approval even when the accepted examination requirement has already been satisfied.
