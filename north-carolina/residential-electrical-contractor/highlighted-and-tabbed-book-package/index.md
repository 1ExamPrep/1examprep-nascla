## Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a North Carolina SP-SFD electrical contractor license. Applicants must complete the licensing process through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

1. **Identify the SP-SFD classification.** Confirm that the Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling classification matches the electrical work you intend to perform.
2. **Review North Carolina qualification requirements.** Applicants must satisfy the Board's current experience and eligibility rules for the SP-SFD classification.
3. **Meet the general application requirements.** Electrical contracting examination applicants must be at least 18 years old and submit the required application.
4. **Provide character statements.** North Carolina requires written statements from at least two people attesting to the applicant's good character.
5. **Complete the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination.** Prepare using the applicable residential electrical reference materials and pass the accepted NASCLA examination.
6. **Send the NASCLA transcript to the North Carolina Board.** NASCLA applicants must have their examination transcript transmitted as part of the licensing application process.
7. **Document qualifying electrical experience.** Provide the experience records required by the Board for the SP-SFD classification.
8. **Complete the licensing application.** Submit all additional documentation requested by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.
9. **Receive Board approval before contracting.** The applicant must be approved and properly licensed before performing work that requires the SP-SFD credential.

## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina is regulated by the **North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors**. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several Special Restricted classifications, including the SP-SFD classification associated with the accepted NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination.

North Carolina requires electrical contracting applicants to demonstrate qualifying electrical experience. The Board distinguishes between **primary experience** and **secondary experience**. Primary experience generally involves direct work installing electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code or work directly related to those installations.

Examples of work that may receive primary-experience credit under North Carolina rules include journeyman electrician or electrician mechanic work, electrical foreman work, electrical superintendent work, electrical estimating for a licensed contractor, qualifying electrical inspection, qualifying engineering supervision, electrical maintenance, and certain other electrical roles recognized by the Board.

Secondary experience can include electrical work or training related to electrical installations that does not receive the same level of credit as primary experience. Examples can include apprentice electrician experience, approved apprenticeship training, qualifying electrical education, electrical construction design, and certain other related activities.

The Board determines whether an applicant's documented experience satisfies the current eligibility requirement for the SP-SFD classification. Applicants should maintain clear employment and experience records so the Board can evaluate their qualifications.

Applicants for an electrical contracting examination must also be at least **18 years of age** and provide written statements from at least two individuals attesting to good character.

The **SP-SFD classification** authorizes qualifying electrical contracting work involving electrical wiring and devices located in or on single-family residential dwellings or ancillary to those dwellings. The classification is restricted in scope and does not provide the same authority as North Carolina's Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contractor licenses.

North Carolina law distinguishes the SP-SFD classification from broader contracting credentials. Candidates should understand the limitations of the residential classification and ensure that projects fall within the authorized scope before contracting for work.

Passing NASCLA does not replace North Carolina's experience, character, application, and licensing requirements. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination serves as an accepted trade-examination pathway, while the North Carolina Board retains control over the actual license.
