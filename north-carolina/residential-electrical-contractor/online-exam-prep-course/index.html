The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers three electrical trade examinations: Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor. North Carolina currently accepts the Residential Electrical Contractor examination for applicants pursuing the SP-SFD license classification.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around knowledge and skills associated with residential electrical installations, service, repair, and maintenance. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies all three examinations in its Accredited Electrical Examination Program as open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should learn how to identify what a question is asking, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the applicable code provision, table, formula, definition, or technical requirement, and then apply the information correctly.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial attention. Candidates should practice using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, chapter structure, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Residential questions may require candidates to connect general NEC rules with requirements specifically affecting dwelling units, branch circuits, feeders, services, wiring methods, equipment, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside code navigation. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, motors, transformers, electrical loads, and common calculations. Understanding the principle behind a problem often makes it easier to determine which formula or code provision applies.

Safety preparation should include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA supports study of construction-industry safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

A productive open-book study routine is to work practice-oriented questions while actively using the references. Even when an answer seems familiar, locate the supporting rule or formula. This reinforces technical knowledge and helps build familiarity with the organization of the reference library.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a North Carolina electrical contractor license. Applicants must complete the licensing process through the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.

  1. Identify the SP-SFD classification. North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination in place of the state's SP-SFD Single-Family Detached Residential Dwelling examination.
  2. Review the experience requirements. Applicants must satisfy the Board's current experience requirements for the SP-SFD classification.
  3. Prepare the required application. North Carolina requires an electrical contracting license application and supporting documentation.
  4. Provide character references. Examination applicants must submit written statements from at least two individuals attesting to the applicant's good character.
  5. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the NASCLA application through the National Examination Database.
  6. Prepare for the Residential Electrical Contractor examination. Study residential NEC requirements, electrical theory, calculations, safety, wiring methods, equipment, residential construction, fire alarm systems, and contractor business topics.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the current NASCLA and PSI examination rules.
  8. Send the NASCLA transcript to North Carolina. Applicants using the NASCLA pathway must have their examination transcript transmitted to the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors.
  9. Complete the licensing process. The Board determines whether the applicant has satisfied all experience, application, examination, and licensing requirements before issuing the SP-SFD license.

State Requirements

Electrical contracting licenses in North Carolina are regulated by the North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several special restricted classifications, including the Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling, or SP-SFD, license.

Under the Board's current examination requirements, an SP-SFD applicant must have at least 3,000 hours of qualifying experience. At least 2,000 hours must consist of primary experience, while the remaining qualifying experience may be primary, secondary, or a combination of both.

North Carolina defines primary and secondary electrical experience according to the duties performed and the percentage of credit allowed for different types of work. Direct electrical installation and several supervisory or professional electrical roles may receive qualifying credit under the Board's rules.

Applicants for any North Carolina electrical contracting examination must be at least 18 years of age, submit the required application, provide written statements from at least two persons attesting to good character, and meet the experience requirements established for the license classification requested.

The SP-SFD classification authorizes the licensee to install, maintain, or repair electrical wiring and devices in or on a single-family residential dwelling or ancillary to that dwelling.

North Carolina defines a single-family residential dwelling for this classification as a building or manufactured home designed and used for habitation by one family and not physically attached to another building or structure. Cabanas, porches, room additions, and qualifying ancillary structures may be considered part of the residential scope when used for residential purposes.

Ancillary structures can include items associated with a single-family dwelling such as detached residential garages or carports, household storage buildings, pump houses, electric fences, and yard lighting when they fall within the scope permitted by the SP-SFD classification.

The SP-SFD classification is a special restricted electrical contractor license. It is not equivalent to North Carolina's Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contracting classifications, which authorize broader electrical contracting work subject to their individual experience and project requirements.

North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination specifically for eligibility toward the SP-SFD classification. Applicants who pass NASCLA must still submit the required license application and have the NASCLA transcript sent to the Board.

Licensees must also comply with the Board's current renewal requirements and perform electrical contracting work only within the scope authorized by the classification held.
