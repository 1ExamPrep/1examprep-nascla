## State Requirements

The **North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors** regulates electrical contracting licenses in North Carolina. The Board issues Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and several Special Restricted classifications, including SP-SFD.

The **SP-SFD Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling** classification applies to electrical contracting within its restricted residential scope. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is accepted specifically for this classification.

Current North Carolina Board rules require an SP-SFD applicant to have at least **two years of qualifying experience**. At least **one year must be primary experience**. The remaining experience may consist of primary experience, secondary experience, or a combination of both.

Primary experience generally means work gained while directly engaged in installation of electrical wiring and equipment governed by the National Electrical Code or closely related activities. North Carolina rules recognize several categories of primary electrical experience, including journeyman electrician, electrical foreman, electrical superintendent, qualifying estimating work, and other approved electrical installation activities.

Applicants must also be at least **18 years old** and provide written statements from at least **two people** attesting to the applicant's good character.

North Carolina requires applicants using NASCLA to submit their official NASCLA examination transcripts to the Board. A passing NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination result does not automatically create an active license. The applicant must complete the licensing application and satisfy all additional Board requirements.

North Carolina's rules also provide an alternate-examination process involving NASCLA examinations. Applicants using a NASCLA-qualified pathway must still satisfy applicable experience, age, and character requirements under Board rules.

The SP-SFD classification is narrower than North Carolina's Limited, Intermediate, and Unlimited electrical contractor classifications. Candidates who intend to perform electrical contracting outside the SP-SFD scope should review the eligibility and examination requirements for the appropriate broader classification.

After satisfying examination requirements, an applicant must submit a New License Application for the desired classification and pay the applicable license fee. The license must then be activated before electrical contracting work requiring the credential is performed.

Business Formation included with The 1 Package establishes an LLC or Corporation, but business formation itself does not grant electrical contracting authority. The business and its qualified individual must still comply with North Carolina licensing, electrical code, permitting, inspection, tax, employment, and other applicable requirements.
