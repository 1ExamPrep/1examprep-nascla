## State Requirements

Electrical contractor licensing in North Carolina is regulated by the **North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors**. The Board administers several electrical contractor classifications, including Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited, and Special Restricted classifications.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is accepted specifically for the **SP-SFD Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling** classification. Candidates who have passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination may apply for SP-SFD licensure after satisfying the Board's other licensing requirements.

Current North Carolina rules require an SP-SFD applicant to have at least **3,000 hours of qualifying experience**. At least **2,000 hours must be primary experience**. The remaining experience may be primary experience, secondary experience, or a combination of the two.

Primary electrical experience generally includes work directly involving electrical wiring or electrical installation activities recognized by the Board. Secondary experience can include qualifying related training, education, or work that receives experience credit under Board rules. The Board evaluates the documentation submitted to determine whether the applicant meets the required experience threshold.

The SP-SFD classification is specifically restricted to electrical contracting work associated with qualifying single-family residential dwellings and applicable ancillary structures. It is not the same as North Carolina's Limited, Intermediate, or Unlimited electrical contractor licenses.

Applicants using the NASCLA pathway must have the NASCLA transcript sent to the Board as part of the license application. The Board's NASCLA application states that an applicant who passed the Residential Electrical Contractor examination may **only obtain the SP-SFD classification** through that specific NASCLA exam.

The Board maintains a separate fee schedule for electrical contractor licenses. The SP-SFD license is subject to the current Board fee in effect when the application is submitted. Candidates should use the current application when completing the licensing process.

North Carolina's SP-SFD credential is a contractor license, not simply an examination certificate. The license holder must comply with the Board's rules, remain within the authorized scope, maintain the license, and follow applicable permitting, inspection, electrical code, and business requirements.

License renewal applications and fees are due according to the Board's renewal schedule. Electrical contractors are responsible for keeping their license active and maintaining the qualifications required for continued licensure.
