## Exam Details

The **Oregon Construction Contractors Board** oversees contractor licensing for construction activities covered by Oregon law.

Oregon requires qualifying contractor businesses to have a Responsible Managing Individual (RMI) who completes the required pre-license education and passes the contractor examination.

The RMI is an owner or employee responsible for managing or supervising the construction activities of the business.

**Oregon Contractor Licensing Exam Information:**

- **Examination:** Oregon Construction Contractors Examination
- **Licensing Authority:** Oregon Construction Contractors Board

## Open Book Test

The **Oregon Construction Contractors Board examination is an open-book test**. Candidates may use the approved Oregon NASCLA 2nd Edition reference manual during the examination.

Although the examination is open book, candidates still need to prepare thoroughly. Understanding the terminology, recognizing the subject of each question, and locating relevant information efficiently are important to managing the available testing time.

**Oregon Open-Book Examination Rules:**

- Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference manual.
- The Oregon NASCLA Contractors Guide, 2nd Edition, is the designated examination reference.
- Both the English and Spanish editions may be brought into the examination.
- The reference manual may be highlighted or underlined before the examination.
- Tabs and indexing may be added before examination day.
- Handwritten notes are permitted within the approved reference manual.
- Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or add indexing to the reference during the examination.
- Additional loose papers are not permitted with the approved reference.

**Important:** This product is the standard Oregon NASCLA 2nd Edition reference book. Professionally highlighted or tabbed versions and separate tab accessories are different product options.

To prepare effectively, begin by learning the book's organization. Review the table of contents, identify the chapters associated with common examination topics, and practice locating important information.

## Licensing Steps

Obtaining an Oregon Construction Contractors Board license involves education, examination, business registration, bonding, insurance, and submission of the required application materials.

**Step 1: Select a Responsible Managing Individual**

Identify the owner or employee who will serve as the business's Responsible Managing Individual. This person manages or supervises construction activities and completes the required training and examination.

**Step 2: Complete Oregon Pre-License Education**

The RMI completes at least 16 hours of pre-license education through a provider approved by the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.

**Step 3: Prepare for the Oregon Contractor Examination**

Study the Oregon NASCLA 2nd Edition and review the contractor laws, business practices, contracts, and management subjects covered by the examination.

**Step 4: Pass the Required Examination**

After completing the approved education, follow the testing instructions provided through PSI and complete the open-book Oregon contractor examination.

**Step 5: Choose the Correct Contractor Endorsement**

Determine the endorsement that matches the type and scope of construction work the business intends to perform.

**Step 6: Register the Business**

Complete applicable registration requirements with the Oregon Secretary of State, including corporation, limited liability company, or assumed business name filings when appropriate.

**Step 7: Obtain the Required Bond and Insurance**

Secure the CCB surety bond and general liability insurance required for the selected endorsement. Workers' compensation coverage may also be required.

**Step 8: Submit the CCB License Application**

Complete the application and provide the examination results, required bond documentation, insurance certificate, and other supporting materials.

**Step 9: Obtain License Approval**

Complete the Board's application process before performing construction work that requires a CCB license.

**Application Deadline:** Candidates must apply for their CCB license within 24 months after passing the examination.

## State Requirements

Oregon requires contractors performing many types of construction work for compensation to hold the appropriate CCB license.

This includes activities such as roofing, siding, painting, carpentry, flooring, concrete work, and many other construction or repair services.

**Key Oregon Contractor Licensing Requirements:**

- **Minimum Age:** The individual completing the required pre-license education and examination must be at least 18 years old.
- **Required Education:** Complete 16 hours of approved pre-license training.
- **Examination:** Pass the Oregon contractor examination.
- **Responsible Managing Individual:** Designate an owner or employee who manages or supervises construction activities.
- **Contractor Endorsement:** Select the appropriate residential, commercial, or other applicable endorsement.
- **Business Registration:** Complete applicable Oregon business filings.
- **Surety Bond:** Provide the required CCB bond for the selected endorsement.
- **General Liability Insurance:** Maintain the required liability coverage.
- **Workers' Compensation:** Obtain coverage when required by the business's employment status.
- **Application:** Submit the required documentation and licensing fee to the CCB.

Bond and insurance requirements depend on the endorsement and type of construction business. Different classifications carry different responsibilities.

The CCB also distinguishes between residential and commercial contracting activities. Selecting the correct endorsement is important because the work a contractor may perform depends on the license authorization.

In addition to CCB licensing, certain specialized construction activities require credentials from other Oregon licensing authorities.

The NASCLA guide supports examination preparation by explaining the business and legal concepts that contractors need to understand while navigating these professional responsibilities.
