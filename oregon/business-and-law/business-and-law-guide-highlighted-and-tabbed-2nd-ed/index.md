## Exam Details

The **Oregon Construction Contractors Board** regulates construction contracting activities covered by Oregon law. Under the standard licensing pathway, a business identifies a Responsible Managing Individual (RMI) who completes the required pre-license education and passes the contractor examination.

The RMI must be an owner or employee of the business and is responsible for managing or supervising its construction activities.

Open-book testing requires more than having access to the manual. Candidates must recognize relevant topics, understand contractor terminology, and locate information within the permitted testing time.

**Oregon Open-Book Reference Rules:**

## Licensing Steps

Oregon contractor licensing involves pre-license education, examination, business registration, endorsement selection, bonding, insurance, and application requirements.

**Step 1: Identify the Responsible Managing Individual**

Select the owner or employee who will manage or supervise construction activities and satisfy the applicable education and examination requirements.

**Step 2: Complete Approved Pre-License Education**

Under the standard licensing pathway, the RMI completes 16 hours of pre-license education with an Oregon CCB-approved provider.

**Step 3: Study the Oregon NASCLA 2nd Edition**

Review business planning, contractor regulations, contracts, estimating, and project management. Use the installed chapter tabs to develop reference-navigation skills.

**Step 4: Complete the Contractor Examination**

After the required education, follow PSI scheduling instructions and complete the open-book Oregon contractor examination.

**Step 5: Select the Appropriate CCB Endorsement**

Choose the residential, commercial, or other applicable contractor endorsement corresponding to the business's intended construction activities.

**Step 6: Complete Business Registration**

Meet applicable registration requirements with the Oregon Secretary of State based on the business's structure and operating name.

**Step 7: Obtain Bonding and Insurance**

Secure the CCB surety bond and general liability insurance required for the selected endorsement, along with workers' compensation coverage when applicable.

**Step 8: Submit the License Application**

Provide the required application, examination information, bond, insurance documentation, and other supporting materials to the Construction Contractors Board.

**Step 9: Complete Licensing Approval**

Obtain the appropriate license before performing regulated construction work.

Oregon requires applicants to apply for their CCB license within two years after passing the examination.

## State Requirements

The **Oregon Construction Contractors Board** regulates many forms of construction, improvement, and repair work performed for compensation.

Contractor licensing applies to numerous construction activities, including residential building, commercial construction, roofing, siding, painting, carpentry, flooring, concrete work, and other regulated services.

**Important Oregon Contractor Licensing Responsibilities:**

- **Minimum Age:** The individual completing the standard education and examination requirements must be at least 18 years old.
- **Responsible Managing Individual:** Designate an owner or employee who manages or supervises construction activities.
- **Pre-License Education:** Complete the applicable 16-hour approved training requirement.
- **Examination:** Pass the Oregon contractor licensing examination.
- **Contractor Endorsement:** Select the authorization appropriate for the business's construction activities.
- **Business Registration:** Complete applicable business formation or registration requirements.
- **Surety Bond:** Maintain the bond required for the selected endorsement.
- **General Liability Insurance:** Obtain the required coverage.
- **Workers' Compensation:** Satisfy applicable coverage requirements.
- **Professional Compliance:** Follow Oregon contractor laws and CCB regulations.

Bonding, insurance, and other requirements depend on the endorsement and business circumstances. Specialized construction activities may also involve additional licensing requirements administered by other Oregon authorities.
