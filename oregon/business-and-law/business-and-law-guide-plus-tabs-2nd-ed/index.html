The Oregon Construction Contractors Board oversees contractor licensing for construction businesses covered by Oregon law. The Board's examination evaluates knowledge of construction business practices and state contractor requirements.

The examination is administered by PSI Services. The NASCLA Oregon Construction Contractors 2nd Edition is the authorized reference manual for this open-book examination.

Open Book Test

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board examination is an open-book test. Candidates are permitted to consult the approved Oregon NASCLA 2nd Edition manual during the examination.

The open-book format does not eliminate the need for thorough preparation. Candidates must recognize important terms, understand contractor responsibilities, and locate relevant information efficiently.

Oregon Open-Book Reference Rules:

  • Candidates must bring their own approved reference manual.

Licensing Steps

Obtaining an Oregon Construction Contractors Board license involves completing applicable education, examination, business registration, bonding, insurance, and application requirements.

Step 1: Identify a Responsible Managing Individual

Select the owner or employee who will serve as the business's Responsible Managing Individual (RMI). This individual is responsible for managing or supervising construction activities.

Step 2: Complete Required Pre-License Education

The standard licensing pathway requires the RMI to complete 16 hours of approved pre-license education.

Step 3: Study the Oregon NASCLA Manual

Review the Oregon 2nd Edition and use the matching chapter tabs to organize your study of business planning, contractor laws, contracts, estimating, and project management.

Step 4: Pass the Contractor Examination

Follow the examination scheduling instructions and complete the Oregon CCB examination through PSI.

Step 5: Select the Appropriate Contractor Endorsement

Determine which residential, commercial, or other applicable endorsement corresponds to the construction activities the business intends to perform.

Step 6: Complete Business Registration

Register the business with the Oregon Secretary of State when required for its organizational structure or business name.

Step 7: Obtain Bonding and Insurance

Secure the required CCB surety bond, general liability insurance, and applicable workers' compensation coverage.

Step 8: Submit the License Application

Provide the required application materials, examination results, bond, insurance documentation, and other supporting records to the CCB.

Step 9: Receive License Approval

Complete the licensing process before performing construction work that requires a CCB contractor license.

Oregon applicants must submit their license application within two years after passing the examination.

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board regulates many types of construction and repair work performed for compensation throughout the state.

Contractors working in fields such as residential construction, commercial construction, roofing, siding, painting, flooring, carpentry, and concrete work commonly fall within CCB licensing requirements.

Important Oregon Contractor Licensing Responsibilities:

  • Pre-License Education: Complete the applicable approved education requirement.
  • Contractor Examination: Pass the required Oregon CCB examination.
  • Appropriate Endorsement: Select the authorization corresponding to the business's intended work.
  • Business Registration: Meet applicable Oregon business registration requirements.
  • Surety Bond: Maintain the bond required for the selected endorsement.
  • General Liability Insurance: Maintain the applicable insurance coverage.
  • Workers' Compensation: Provide coverage when required.
  • Licensing Application: Submit the required forms and supporting documentation.
  • Professional Compliance: Follow Oregon contractor laws and CCB regulations.

Requirements vary according to the business structure, endorsement, employment arrangements, and type of construction work performed.

Certain specialized construction activities may also require credentials issued by other Oregon authorities.
