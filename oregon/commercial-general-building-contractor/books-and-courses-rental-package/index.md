## Exam Details

Oregon contractors using the NASCLA commercial examination pathway should understand that the NASCLA trade exam and the Oregon contractor exam are separate requirements. The **NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors** addresses broad commercial construction knowledge, while Oregon requires an additional examination focusing on Oregon laws, rules, and business practices.

- **NASCLA Trade Exam:** NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
- **NASCLA Questions:** 115
- **NASCLA Time Allowed:** 330 minutes
- **NASCLA Passing Requirement:** 81 correct answers, equivalent to 70%
- **Additional Oregon Requirement:** Oregon laws, rules, and business-practices examination
- **Oregon Exam Questions:** 80 multiple-choice questions
- **Oregon Exam Time:** 3 hours
- **Oregon Passing Score:** 70%, or 56 correct answers

The Oregon examination is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. The **NASCLA Oregon Construction Contractors, 2nd Edition** reference included in this rental package is therefore an important part of Oregon-specific preparation.

Candidates who have already passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors may provide their NASCLA transcript to the Oregon Construction Contractors Board to become eligible for the Oregon examination without completing the standard 16-hour pre-license training.

## Open Book Test

Both the NASCLA commercial exam and Oregon's contractor examination involve **Open Book Test** preparation. Approved references can be used according to the rules applicable to the examination being taken.

For Oregon's laws, rules, and business-practices examination, the Oregon Construction Contractors Board states that candidates may use the Oregon NASCLA contractor reference during testing. The manual may be highlighted, underlined, and tabbed before the examination. Handwritten notes may also be made in the Oregon manual before testing, but candidates may not write in the manual during the examination itself. Loose paper is not permitted in the testing facility.

Open-book preparation works best when you know the material well enough to identify the correct reference quickly. Searching through unfamiliar books for every question can consume time and make an open-book test harder than expected.

Use your study period to create strong reference associations. OSHA questions should direct you toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Accessibility questions should lead to ICC A117.1. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318 or concrete construction references, while broad building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books in this package provide an organized starting point. Continue working directly with the references throughout your study period so the chapter structure, indexes, tables, definitions, and terminology become familiar.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Pass the NASCLA commercial examination when using the NASCLA pathway.** Complete the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
2. **Provide your NASCLA transcript to the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.** Oregon uses the transcript to establish eligibility for the Oregon examination for NASCLA-qualified applicants.
3. **Prepare for the Oregon examination.** NASCLA-qualified applicants still need to pass Oregon's exam covering laws, rules, and business practices.
4. **Select a Responsible Managing Individual.** Oregon contractor businesses must have an RMI who manages or supervises the business's construction activities and satisfies the applicable examination requirement.
5. **Determine the appropriate Oregon endorsement.** Select the endorsement that corresponds with the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
6. **Register the business when applicable.** Complete the appropriate Oregon business registration for the corporation, LLC, or assumed business name.
7. **Obtain the required bond and liability insurance.** Oregon contractor applicants must maintain the bond and insurance required for their endorsement.
8. **Address workers' compensation and employer requirements when applicable.** Contractors hiring employees must satisfy applicable employment requirements.
9. **Submit the Oregon CCB license application.** Complete the application and provide the required supporting documents after satisfying the applicable examination requirements.

## State Requirements

Construction contractor licensing in Oregon is administered by the **Oregon Construction Contractors Board**. A contractor business must meet the applicable licensing requirements before operating under a CCB license.

Oregon generally requires a Responsible Managing Individual to satisfy the state's pre-license education and examination requirements. The RMI must be an owner or employee of the contractor business and must manage or supervise its construction activities.

An important exception applies to candidates who have already passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. Oregon states that these applicants do not have to complete the standard 16-hour pre-license training, but they must still pass the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

After satisfying the examination requirement, applicants continue through the Oregon licensing process. This includes determining the appropriate endorsement, establishing the business structure when necessary, providing the required surety bond and general liability insurance, addressing workers' compensation requirements when applicable, and submitting a complete CCB license application.

Oregon also requires candidates who pass the state examination to apply for their CCB license within 24 months of passing the test.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Use the rental books and online course together to create an organized study routine. This package includes **6 months of course access**, allowing time to review commercial construction topics while becoming familiar with the reference library.

**Separate NASCLA preparation from Oregon-specific preparation.** The NASCLA exam focuses on commercial construction trade knowledge, while Oregon's examination focuses on laws, rules, and business practices. Build study time for both requirements.

**Practice reference navigation.** Learn which book addresses each major subject. When a question involves OSHA safety, accessibility, structural concrete, roofing, steel systems, or building codes, you should already have an idea of which reference to use.

**Use a find-and-confirm strategy.** Understand the question first, identify the most relevant reference, locate the specific information, confirm your answer, and continue. Avoid searching multiple books without first identifying the subject.

**Study the Oregon manual directly.** Oregon's examination is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. Become comfortable navigating that manual before the state examination.

**Practice under timed conditions.** Both contractor examinations require candidates to manage their available testing time. Timed question sets can help you become more disciplined about deciding when a lookup is worthwhile and when it is better to move forward.
