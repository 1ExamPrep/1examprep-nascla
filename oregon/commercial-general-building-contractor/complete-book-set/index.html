Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Oregon is a participating jurisdiction through the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.

The examination evaluates knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects involving multiple construction trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than the work of one specialty contractor.

The listed references address project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

The examination library includes both broad construction textbooks and specialty technical references. Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management address field supervision, project planning, documentation, communication, cost control, quality management, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are distributed across ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These publications address structural concrete requirements, reinforcement, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, inspection, and quality control from different perspectives.

Additional specialty references cover precast erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, wood-truss restraint and bracing, masonry, excavation, gypsum systems, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, and mechanical and electrical systems.

After passing the NASCLA examination, a candidate can use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to Oregon or another participating jurisdiction. The examination result is then reviewed as part of the applicable state licensing process.

The NASCLA result does not independently issue an Oregon Construction Contractors Board license. Applicants must complete the Oregon application process, designate a Responsible Managing Individual, pass the Oregon laws, rules, and business-practices examination, select the proper endorsement and business structure, and satisfy applicable bonding and insurance requirements.

Oregon applicants who have passed the NASCLA commercial general building examination do not have to complete the standard 16-hour pre-license training. They are still required to pass the Oregon contractor examination based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law and Project Management.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete technical library.

Oregon’s examination covering laws, rules, and business practices is also open book. Candidates may use the approved Oregon reference manual during the examination under the current testing rules.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not remove the need for preparation. The NASCLA technical library contains thousands of pages across textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty construction guides.

A practical open-book method begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, diagrams, or technical tables to locate the applicable material.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing questions may require the BCSI guide.

Precast erection questions may be located in the Erectors’ Manual, while unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, and excavation or underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may require Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

Candidates should practice using the listed editions because page numbers, code provisions, technical requirements, illustrations, and publication organization may differ between editions. Working with the correct references helps build familiarity before examination day.

All books brought into the testing room must comply with current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, unapproved inserts, or other prohibited materials.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate Oregon contractor endorsement. Review the available residential, commercial, and combined endorsement options and choose the endorsement that matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Select the business structure. Determine whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another approved business form.
  3. Designate a Responsible Managing Individual. The RMI must be an owner or employee of the business and must manage or supervise its construction activities.
  4. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Study the commercial construction subjects and practice locating information throughout the listed technical reference library.
  5. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the current NASCLA application and examination-registration process.
  6. Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. The passing result may be transmitted to Oregon through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  7. Prepare for the Oregon examination. Study the Oregon Contractors’ Guide to Business Law and Project Management and review state laws, rules, licensing responsibilities, and business practices.
  8. Pass the Oregon laws, rules, and business-practices examination. Applicants using a NASCLA result remain responsible for completing this Oregon-specific examination.
  9. Register the business when applicable. File the corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or assumed business name with the Oregon Secretary of State when required.
  10. Obtain the required contractor bond. Secure the bond amount associated with the selected endorsement and business structure.
  11. Obtain general liability insurance. Provide a company-specific certificate showing the insurance coverage required for the selected endorsement.
  12. Submit the Oregon CCB application. Upload the examination records, bond, insurance certificate, business information, RMI information, and other required materials.

State Requirements

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board licenses and regulates construction contractors in the state. Contractors performing covered construction work must obtain the appropriate CCB license and endorsement before advertising, bidding, contracting, or performing work that requires licensure.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. The business must identify a Responsible Managing Individual who is connected with the company and manages or supervises its construction activities.

The RMI must satisfy the applicable education and examination requirements. Under the standard pathway, the RMI completes at least 16 hours of approved pre-license training and passes the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

An applicant who has passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is exempt from the 16-hour pre-license training requirement. The applicant must still pass the Oregon examination and complete the remaining licensing steps.

The Oregon examination is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. The examination is open book and focuses on state laws, rules, business practices, licensing responsibilities, contracts, employment matters, safety, insurance, and related contractor obligations.

Applicants must select the correct endorsement type. Oregon contractor endorsements distinguish between residential, commercial, and combined contracting activity and may also include different commercial levels based on the type and size of work performed.

The selected endorsement affects the contractor’s bond and insurance requirements. Applicants should obtain coverage in the exact business name shown on the CCB application and confirm that the policy and bond satisfy the current requirements for the requested endorsement.

Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, and businesses using an assumed business name must complete the applicable registration with the Oregon Secretary of State. The legal name and registration details should remain consistent throughout the licensing documents.

The CCB application may require proof of examination completion, RMI information, business-structure records, bond documentation, general liability insurance, ownership information, and other supporting materials.

Passing the NASCLA examination and purchasing this complete book set do not guarantee license approval. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board determines whether an applicant has completed the legal, examination, business, bonding, insurance, and administrative requirements for the requested license.
