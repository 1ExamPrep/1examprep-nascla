## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Oregon accepts the examination through the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.

The examination measures knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial construction projects involving multiple building trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than a contractor working in one specialty area.

Major subjects represented by the complete book set include project management, jobsite operations, estimating, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical systems.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support review of field supervision, communication, scheduling, documentation, cost control, quality management, risk, and project closeout. Principles and Practices of Commercial Construction provides broader coverage of materials, building systems, construction methods, sequencing, and field operations.

Concrete subjects are divided among ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These publications address structural requirements, reinforcement, field placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, inspection, and quality control from different perspectives.

Specialty structural references cover precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Additional publications cover masonry, gypsum systems, roofing, excavation, accessibility, stormwater controls, exterior insulation and finish systems, and building-service coordination.

After passing the NASCLA examination, a candidate may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to Oregon or another participating licensing agency. The receiving agency reviews the transcript as part of its contractor licensing process.

A passing NASCLA result does not independently issue an Oregon contractor license. Applicants must complete the Oregon Construction Contractors Board application process, designate a Responsible Managing Individual, pass the Oregon examination, select the proper endorsement, and satisfy applicable business-registration, bonding, insurance, and documentation requirements.

Applicants who have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination are not required to complete Oregon’s standard 16-hour pre-license training course. They must still pass the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical library.

The Oregon contractor examination covering laws, rules, and business practices is also open book. Candidates may use the approved Oregon reference manual under the current examination rules.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for study. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, standards, codes, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A productive navigation method begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the permanent tabs, index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, code provisions, diagrams, or technical tables to locate the applicable material.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate Oregon endorsement.** Review the residential, commercial, and combined endorsement options and select the one that matches the work the business intends to perform.
2. **Select the business structure.** Determine whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible business form.
3. **Designate a Responsible Managing Individual.** The RMI must be connected with the business and responsible for managing or supervising its construction activities.
4. **Prepare for the NASCLA examination.** Study the technical subjects and practice locating information throughout the highlighted and tabbed reference set.
5. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the current NASCLA application and examination-registration process.
6. **Pass the NASCLA examination.** The result may be transmitted to Oregon through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
7. **Prepare for the Oregon examination.** Study the Oregon NASCLA Business Book and review Oregon contractor laws, rules, licensing duties, and business practices.
8. **Pass the Oregon contractor examination.** NASCLA candidates remain responsible for completing the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.
9. **Register the business when required.** Complete the applicable corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or assumed business-name registration.
10. **Obtain the required contractor bond.** Secure the bond associated with the selected endorsement and business structure.
11. **Obtain general liability insurance.** Provide proof of the coverage required for the selected Oregon contractor endorsement.
12. **Submit the Oregon CCB application.** Include the required examination records, bond, insurance certificate, business information, RMI information, and supporting documents.

## State Requirements

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board licenses and regulates construction contractors in the state. Contractors performing covered construction work must obtain the appropriate CCB license before advertising, bidding, contracting, or performing work that requires licensure.

The license is issued to the business entity. The applicant must identify a Responsible Managing Individual who is an owner or employee and who manages or supervises the company’s construction activities.

Under Oregon’s standard licensing pathway, the RMI completes approved pre-license education and passes the Oregon contractor examination. An applicant who has passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is exempt from the standard 16-hour pre-license training requirement.

The NASCLA exemption does not remove the Oregon examination requirement. The RMI must still pass the open-book Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

Applicants must select the endorsement that matches the work the company plans to perform. Oregon distinguishes between residential, commercial, and combined contracting activity, with additional endorsement levels and requirements based on the contractor’s scope.

The selected endorsement affects the contractor’s bond and insurance requirements. The bond and insurance certificate should use the same legal business name shown on the contractor application.

Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, and businesses using an assumed business name must complete the applicable Oregon business registration. Business names and organizational details should remain consistent across the application, bond, insurance, and registration records.

The CCB application may require examination documentation, RMI information, ownership details, business-structure records, bond documentation, general liability insurance, and additional materials related to the requested endorsement.

Passing the NASCLA examination and purchasing this complete book set do not guarantee license approval. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board determines whether the applicant has completed the examination, business, bonding, insurance, and administrative requirements for the requested license.
