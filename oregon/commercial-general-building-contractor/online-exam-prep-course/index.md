## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. It is designed to measure the knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects involving multiple construction trades.

The examination covers a broad range of topics rather than focusing on one specialty. Candidates should be prepared to review project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, documentation, scheduling, estimating, cost control, safety, risk, quality management, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are represented by ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. Together, these references address structural requirements, reinforcement, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, inspection, testing, durability, and quality control.

Specialty structural references address precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Other books cover masonry, gypsum construction, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, and building-service systems.

The International Building Code supports review of commercial building requirements involving occupancy, construction types, fire protection, means of egress, accessibility, structural provisions, and coordinated building systems. ICC A117.1 provides technical accessibility criteria for buildings and facilities.

Oregon is a participating jurisdiction for the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. After passing, a candidate may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.

A passing NASCLA result does not independently issue an Oregon contractor license. The applicant must still identify a Responsible Managing Individual, pass the Oregon contractor examination, choose the proper endorsement, register the business when required, obtain the applicable contractor bond and general liability insurance, and submit a complete CCB license application.

The Oregon examination is separate from the NASCLA technical examination. It is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law and Project Management and covers Oregon contractor laws, rules, licensing responsibilities, and business practices.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted references to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical library.

Oregon’s contractor examination covering laws, rules, and business practices is also open book. Candidates may use the approved Oregon reference manual during the examination under the current testing rules.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for study. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A practical reference-navigation method begins with identifying the primary subject of a question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the relevant information.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while questions involving reinforcing-bar placement may require Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the Steel Deck Institute Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Questions involving wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing may require the BCSI guide.

Precast concrete erection questions may be addressed in the Erectors’ Manual. Unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, while excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may involve Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

The online exam prep course helps candidates practice connecting these topics with the appropriate references. Students can build a repeatable process for recognizing a subject, selecting the correct publication, locating the supporting information, and evaluating the available answer choices.

All books brought into the testing room must comply with current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate Oregon contractor endorsement.** Review the residential, commercial, and combined endorsement options and select the endorsement that matches the work the business intends to perform.
2. **Select the business structure.** Decide whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible business form.
3. **Designate a Responsible Managing Individual.** The RMI must be an owner or employee of the business and must manage or supervise its construction activities.
4. **Prepare for the NASCLA examination.** Use the online course with the listed references to review technical construction subjects and practice open-book navigation.
5. **Apply for the NASCLA examination.** Complete the current NASCLA application and examination-registration process.
6. **Pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination.** The passing result may be transmitted to Oregon through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
7. **Prepare for the Oregon contractor examination.** Study the Oregon business-law reference and review contractor laws, rules, licensing duties, and business practices.
8. **Pass the Oregon examination.** NASCLA candidates remain responsible for completing the Oregon contractor examination even though they are exempt from the standard 16-hour pre-license training requirement.
9. **Register the business when required.** Complete the applicable corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or assumed business-name registration.
10. **Obtain the required contractor bond.** Secure the bond required for the selected endorsement and business structure.
11. **Obtain general liability insurance.** Provide proof of the coverage required for the selected Oregon contractor endorsement.
12. **Submit the Oregon CCB application.** Include the examination records, bond, insurance certificate, RMI information, business records, and other required documents.

## State Requirements

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board licenses and regulates construction contractors in the state. Contractors performing covered construction work must obtain the appropriate CCB license before advertising, bidding, contracting, or performing work that requires licensure.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. Each applicant must identify a Responsible Managing Individual who is an owner or employee and who manages or supervises the company’s construction activities.

Under the standard Oregon licensing pathway, the Responsible Managing Individual completes at least 16 hours of approved pre-license training and passes the Oregon contractor examination. A candidate who has passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is exempt from the standard 16-hour pre-license training requirement.

The NASCLA exemption does not eliminate the Oregon examination requirement. The Responsible Managing Individual must still pass the Oregon open-book examination covering contractor laws, rules, and business practices.

The Oregon contractor examination is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors’ Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. The examination covers licensing responsibilities, business organization, contracts, financial management, employment matters, insurance, safety, liens, and other contractor obligations.

Applicants must choose the correct endorsement for the work they intend to perform. Oregon distinguishes among residential, commercial, and combined contracting activity. Commercial endorsements may also have different levels based on the type and scope of projects performed.

The selected endorsement affects the required contractor bond and general liability insurance. Applicants should obtain the correct coverage and make sure the legal business name shown on the bond and insurance documents matches the CCB license application.

Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, and businesses operating under an assumed business name must complete the applicable registration with the Oregon Secretary of State. Organizational information should remain consistent throughout the application and supporting documents.

The CCB license application may require examination records, RMI information, business-structure details, ownership information, bond documentation, general liability insurance, and other materials associated with the requested endorsement.

Passing the NASCLA examination or completing this online course does not independently authorize contracting work. The business must complete the Oregon licensing process and receive the applicable CCB license before performing regulated construction services.
