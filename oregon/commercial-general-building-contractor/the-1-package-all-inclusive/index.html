Exam Details

Oregon contractor licensing is administered by the Oregon Construction Contractors Board, commonly known as the CCB. Most Oregon contractor licenses require a Responsible Managing Individual (RMI) who manages or supervises the construction activities of the business.

For most applicants, the RMI completes 16 hours of approved Oregon pre-license education and then passes the Oregon contractor examination. Candidates who have already passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors do not have to complete the 16-hour Oregon pre-license training. They must provide their NASCLA transcript to the CCB and still pass the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

The Oregon contractor examination contains 80 multiple-choice questions. Candidates have 3 hours to complete the exam and must achieve a 70% passing score, which equals 56 correct answers.

The Oregon examination is based on the Oregon edition of the contractor business, law, and project-management reference. Candidates should study subjects involving contractor licensing, business practices, contracts, employment responsibilities, financial management, insurance, bonds, project management, and Oregon construction requirements.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is a separate national trade examination covering broad commercial construction knowledge. Major preparation areas include:

  • General construction requirements
  • Planning and estimating
  • Procurement and contracting
  • Financial and project management
  • Construction safety
  • Site construction
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Wood construction
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors, windows, and glazing
  • Finishes
  • Mechanical and plumbing systems
  • Electrical systems

Candidates using NASCLA for Oregon licensing should treat the NASCLA trade examination and Oregon state examination as separate study areas. NASCLA focuses on commercial construction knowledge, while the Oregon examination focuses on Oregon contractor law, rules, and business practices.

Open Book Test

The Oregon contractor examination is an open book test. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board permits candidates to use the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide during the examination.

The Oregon reference manual may be highlighted, underlined, and tabbed before the examination. Handwritten notes may also be made in the book before testing. Loose paper is not permitted in the testing facility, and candidates may not add writing to the manual while taking the examination.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is also an open-book examination using its designated commercial construction reference library. Licensing Steps

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board establishes the licensing process for construction contractors operating in Oregon. NASCLA candidates still complete the Oregon-specific steps required for a CCB license.

  1. Identify the Responsible Managing Individual. Most Oregon contractor endorsements require an RMI who manages or supervises the company's construction activities. The RMI may be an owner, officer, partner, or employee of the business.
  2. Use the NASCLA pathway when applicable. An applicant who has passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors can use that credential instead of completing Oregon's standard 16-hour pre-license education requirement.
  3. Provide the NASCLA transcript to the Oregon CCB. NASCLA candidates must provide proof of passing the accredited commercial examination so they can become eligible for Oregon's state examination.
  4. Pass the Oregon contractor examination. NASCLA applicants must still pass the Oregon examination covering contractor laws, rules, and business practices.
  5. Choose the correct Oregon endorsement. Oregon offers residential and commercial endorsements with different scopes and requirements. Commercial contractors should select the endorsement that matches their intended business activities.
  6. Register the business entity. Corporations, LLCs, and assumed business names must be properly filed through Oregon's business registration system when applicable.
  7. Obtain the required CCB surety bond. The bond amount depends on the endorsement selected.
  8. Obtain general liability insurance. Oregon requires proof of general liability coverage in an amount corresponding with the contractor's endorsement.
  9. Obtain workers' compensation coverage when applicable. Contractors hiring employees must comply with Oregon workers' compensation requirements.
  10. Complete and submit the CCB license application. The application must include the required bond, insurance, business, RMI, and supporting information.

Applicants must apply for the Oregon CCB license within 24 months of passing the Oregon contractor examination. The Application Service included with The 1 Package supports candidates with organizing the licensing process while they complete exam preparation and business setup.

State Requirements

Oregon requires construction contractors performing qualifying work for compensation to hold the appropriate Construction Contractors Board license. The license is issued to the contracting business and must correspond with the endorsement under which the contractor intends to operate.

Most licenses require a Responsible Managing Individual. The RMI must be an owner or employee of the business and have management or supervisory authority over the company's construction activities. An individual may not serve as an RMI employee for multiple CCB licenses at the same time.

Oregon contractors must select an appropriate residential or commercial endorsement. Commercial general and commercial specialty endorsements are divided into Level 1 and Level 2 categories, with requirements that vary according to the business and endorsement selected.

A CCB surety bond is required, and the applicable bond amount depends on the endorsement. Contractors must also maintain the general liability insurance required for their endorsement and provide the necessary certificate of coverage with the licensing application.

Businesses employing workers must comply with Oregon workers' compensation requirements. Contractors may also need state and federal tax accounts and employer identification numbers depending on their business structure and workforce.

Commercial contractors have continuing education obligations during the license cycle. Oregon bases commercial continuing education requirements on whether the contractor is Level 1 or Level 2 and, for Level 1 contractors, the number of key employees.
