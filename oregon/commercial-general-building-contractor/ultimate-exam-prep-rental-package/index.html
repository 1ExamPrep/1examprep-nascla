Exam Details

Oregon candidates using the NASCLA pathway should prepare for two distinct areas: the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors and Oregon's separate contractor examination covering state laws, rules, and business practices.

  • NASCLA Exam: Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • NASCLA Questions: 115
  • NASCLA Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • NASCLA Minimum Passing Score: 81 correct answers, equivalent to 70%
  • Oregon Exam: Laws, rules, and business practices examination
  • Oregon Exam Questions: 80 multiple-choice questions
  • Oregon Exam Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Oregon Passing Score: 70%, or 56 correct answers

Applicants who have already passed the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination can provide their NASCLA transcript to the Oregon Construction Contractors Board. This allows them to become eligible for the Oregon examination without completing the standard 16-hour pre-license training.

The Oregon examination is based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management. The NASCLA Oregon Construction Contractors, 2nd Edition included with this rental package therefore plays an important role in Oregon-specific preparation.

Open Book Test

The Oregon contractor examination is an Open Book Test. Candidates may use the approved Oregon NASCLA contractor manual during the examination.

The Oregon manual may be highlighted, underlined, tabbed, and indexed before the examination. Handwritten notes may also be made in the manual before testing. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, or index material during the examination itself, and loose papers are not permitted in the testing facility.

Open-book testing rewards preparation rather than random searching. Knowing that a book is available does not help if you cannot locate the information efficiently. Candidates should practice navigating the manual before test day so they understand the chapter structure, terminology, index, and placement of important Oregon laws and business-practice topics.

The same navigation principle applies to NASCLA commercial preparation. When a question concerns OSHA requirements, you should immediately know which reference to consult. Accessibility questions should point you toward ICC A117.1. Structural concrete questions may require ACI materials, while building-code questions may require navigation of the International Building Code.

The highlighted and tabbed books included with this Ultimate package provide an organized starting point. Repeated practice with the reference library can help you develop a faster find-and-confirm method instead of searching through multiple unfamiliar books for every question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Pass the NASCLA commercial examination when using the NASCLA pathway. Complete the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors.
  2. Provide your NASCLA transcript to the Oregon Construction Contractors Board. NASCLA-qualified candidates submit their passing transcript to establish eligibility for the Oregon examination.
  3. Prepare for the Oregon laws, rules, and business-practices exam. NASCLA-qualified candidates are exempt from the standard 16-hour pre-license training but must still pass Oregon's contractor examination.
  4. Select the Responsible Managing Individual. Most Oregon endorsements require an RMI who manages or supervises the business's construction activities and satisfies the applicable examination requirement.
  5. Determine the appropriate endorsement. Choose the Oregon endorsement that matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  6. Establish the business structure. Register the applicable corporation, LLC, or assumed business name when required.
  7. Obtain the required surety bond. Oregon contractors must provide the bond amount applicable to their endorsement.
  8. Obtain general liability insurance. Applicants must provide proof of the required liability coverage.
  9. Address workers' compensation requirements when applicable. Contractors with employees must meet applicable workers' compensation obligations.
  10. Submit the Oregon CCB license application. Complete the application and submit the required supporting materials after satisfying the applicable examination requirements.

State Requirements

Construction contractor licensing in Oregon is administered by the Oregon Construction Contractors Board. Contractors must complete the applicable CCB licensing requirements before operating under an Oregon contractor license.

Most endorsements require a Responsible Managing Individual. The RMI must be an owner or employee of the business and have management or supervisory authority over the business's construction activities.

Oregon provides a specific pathway for applicants who have already passed the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. These candidates are exempt from the standard 16-hour Oregon pre-license training requirement but must still complete the Oregon examination covering laws, rules, and business practices.

After passing the applicable Oregon examination, candidates continue through the licensing process. This includes selecting an endorsement, establishing the business entity when applicable, obtaining the required surety bond and general liability insurance, meeting workers' compensation requirements when applicable, and submitting the CCB application.

Oregon requires candidates to apply for the CCB license within 24 months of passing the Oregon examination.
