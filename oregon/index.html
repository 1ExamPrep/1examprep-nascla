Oregon NASCLA Exam: How It Fits Your CCB License, and How to Prepare

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 9 min read · 7 official sources cited

Oregon handles the NASCLA exam differently from most states. The Construction Contractors Board (CCB) does not treat it as a replacement for a state trade exam. Instead, passing the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors excuses your Responsible Managing Individual from the 16 hours of pre-license training. The Oregon law and business exam is still required.

Does Oregon accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board appears on NASCLA's list of agencies that accept the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. NASCLA's note for Oregon is specific: the exam is accepted in lieu of the 16-hour pre-licensure training requirement.

That is the whole benefit, and it is worth being clear about its limits. A NASCLA pass does not license you. It does not waive the Oregon exam on contractor laws, rules and business practices. It does not reduce the bond, insurance, experience or business registration rules for your endorsement. The CCB says NASCLA candidates send their NASCLA transcript to ccbeducation@ccb.oregon.gov and then sit for the Oregon exam.

So who benefits? Mostly contractors who already passed NASCLA for another state, such as a commercial builder licensed elsewhere who is expanding into Oregon. You skip the training hours and go straight to the Oregon test. If you have no NASCLA pass yet and only plan to work in Oregon, the 16-hour course is usually the simpler route. The NASCLA exam makes the most sense when you also want it for other states that accept it.

Who issues contractor licenses in Oregon

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board licenses and regulates construction contractors. If your work requires a license, you need it before you advertise, bid, contract for or perform that work.

Oregon licenses the business, not just the person. Each licensed business names a Responsible Managing Individual (RMI), an owner or employee who manages or supervises the company's construction work. The RMI is the person who completes the training (or presents the NASCLA exemption) and passes the Oregon exam.

Business filings go to a second agency. Corporations, LLCs, partnerships and companies using an assumed business name register with the Oregon Secretary of State, Corporation Division, before the CCB application. The legal name on your bond and insurance documents needs to match your CCB application.

Oregon CCB license endorsements and which exam each needs

Every CCB license carries one or more endorsements that define the work you can do. All of them run through the same RMI requirement and the same Oregon exam. The NASCLA exemption applies only to the 16-hour training, and only if your RMI passed the NASCLA commercial general building exam. The CCB endorsement chart (November 2024) lists these main types:

  • Commercial General Contractor Level 1: unlimited unrelated trades on small or large commercial projects; key employees need 8 years of experience.
  • Commercial General Contractor Level 2: the same scope with a lower bond and insurance tier; key employees need 4 years of experience.
  • Commercial Specialty Contractor Level 1 and Level 2: one or two unrelated trades on commercial projects, with the same 8 and 4 year experience tiers.
  • Commercial Developer: arranges commercial construction through licensed general contractors on property it plans to sell.
  • Residential General, Specialty, Limited and Developer: residential and small commercial work at different scopes.
  • Restricted residential endorsements: home services, locksmith, home inspector, home energy performance score and restoration contractors.

How to get an Oregon CCB license, step by step

The CCB publishes a short checklist. Here is the same path with the NASCLA option built in.

  1. Pick your endorsement based on the work you plan to do. It sets your bond, insurance and experience needs.
  2. Choose your business structure and designate an RMI who is at least 18 and is an owner or employee.
  3. Either complete 16 hours of training with a CCB-approved provider, or pass the NASCLA commercial general building exam and email your NASCLA transcript to the CCB.
  4. Pass the Oregon contractor exam at PSI.
  5. Register your corporation, LLC, partnership or assumed business name with the Secretary of State.
  6. Buy a CCB surety bond in the required amount and get the power of attorney that comes with it.
  7. Get general liability insurance at the required level, with the CCB named as certificate holder.
  8. Set up workers compensation if you will have employees, plus state and federal tax account numbers.
  9. Submit the CCB application with your bond, insurance certificate, signatures form and fee. The CCB says processing can take six to eight weeks.

The NASCLA commercial general building exam

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions and a 330 minute time limit, and you need 70 percent to pass (81 correct answers out of the 115 scored questions). It may also include some unscored pretest items. It is open book, and you bring your own references, since the testing center does not supply the library.

You apply through NASCLA for pre-approval before you can schedule, using the NASCLA National Examination Database. After you pass, your result sits in that database, which is how other agencies can see it.

The reference list is long. It runs from project and jobsite management texts through ACI 318-19 and reinforcing steel, precast erection, post-tensioning, steel deck and joists, wood truss bracing, masonry, excavation, roofing, the International Building Code and ICC A117.1 accessibility. Most of the work is learning which book answers which kind of question and getting to the page quickly.

The Oregon CCB exam on business and law

Every RMI must pass this exam, NASCLA or not. According to the CCB, it is given by PSI at PSI test sites, has 80 multiple-choice questions, allows three hours and is open book. You need 70 percent, which is 56 correct answers. The published test fee is $60 per attempt.

The reference is the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 2nd Edition. It mixes national business content with Oregon contractor law. Expect questions on licensing duties, business organization, contracts, financial management, employment, insurance, safety and construction liens.

Once you pass, apply for your license within 24 months, or you may need to test again. Eighty questions in three hours is a bit over two minutes each, so a tabbed book matters here just as much as it does on the NASCLA exam.

Oregon experience, bond and insurance requirements

Commercial endorsements carry experience rules for key employees: 8 years for Level 1 and 4 years for Level 2, based on the CCB endorsement chart. Key employees include owners, officers, managers, superintendents, forepersons and others who supervise construction.

The CCB publishes these bond and liability insurance amounts. Confirm them with the board before you buy coverage.

  • Commercial General Contractor Level 1: $80,000 bond, $2,000,000 aggregate insurance.
  • Commercial General Contractor Level 2: $25,000 bond, $1,000,000 aggregate insurance.
  • Commercial Specialty Contractor Level 1: $55,000 bond, $1,000,000 aggregate insurance.
  • Commercial Specialty Contractor Level 2: $25,000 bond, $500,000 per occurrence.
  • Commercial Developer: $25,000 bond, $500,000 per occurrence.
  • Residential General Contractor: $25,000 bond, $500,000 per occurrence.
  • Residential Specialty Contractor: $20,000 bond, $300,000 per occurrence.
  • Residential Limited Contractor and restricted endorsements: $15,000 bond, $100,000 per occurrence.

Oregon contractor license costs

These are the published figures from the CCB site. Check the board for current amounts before you pay.

  • CCB license application: $400 for a two-year license, effective July 1, 2025.
  • Renewal: $400 every two years.
  • Oregon PSI exam: $60 each time you test.
  • 16-hour training: set by each approved provider, with ranges listed on the CCB provider page. NASCLA-exempt RMIs skip this cost.
  • NASCLA exam: NASCLA sets its own exam fees, so check at application.

After you pass: CCB application, renewal and continuing education

With both exams handled, you file the CCB application with your bond, insurance certificate and fee. Licenses run two years. Continuing education must be done before you submit a renewal, and your bond and insurance must stay current.

For commercial contractors, CE hours depend on the license level. Level 2 needs 32 hours per two-year cycle, completed by key employees. Level 1 runs from 16 hours with one key employee up to 80 hours with five or more, and hours from several key employees add together. Residential contractors licensed under six years need 16 hours (3 of them CCB law and business classes), and those licensed six years or more need 8 hours. If you hold both residential and commercial endorsements, you follow the commercial rules only.

Oregon NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does passing NASCLA get me an Oregon contractor license?

No. In Oregon, a NASCLA commercial general building pass only replaces the 16 hours of pre-license training. Your RMI still has to pass the Oregon exam, and the business must meet every bond, insurance and registration rule.

Do I still have to take the Oregon CCB exam if I passed NASCLA?

Yes. The CCB requires every RMI to pass the Oregon exam on contractor laws, rules and business practices. Email your NASCLA transcript to the CCB education team, then schedule the Oregon exam with PSI.

Is the Oregon CCB exam open book?

Yes. It is an 80-question, three-hour, open book exam based on the Oregon version of the NASCLA Contractors Guide, 2nd Edition. You need 70 percent, or 56 correct answers.

How much does an Oregon CCB license cost?

The CCB lists a $400 application fee for a two-year license starting July 1, 2025, and $400 to renew. The Oregon exam is $60 per attempt. Bond and insurance costs are extra and depend on your endorsement.

What bond does an Oregon commercial general contractor need?

The CCB lists an $80,000 bond for Commercial General Contractor Level 1 and $25,000 for Level 2. Insurance is $2,000,000 aggregate for Level 1 and $1,000,000 aggregate for Level 2.

What is a Responsible Managing Individual in Oregon?

The RMI is an owner or employee who manages or supervises the business's construction work. The RMI completes the training or NASCLA exemption and passes the Oregon exam for the company.

How long is my Oregon exam result good for?

The CCB says to apply for your license within 24 months of passing the Oregon test. After that, plan on testing again.

Is the NASCLA exam worth it for Oregon only?

If you only plan to work in Oregon, the 16-hour course is usually the simpler route. NASCLA pays off when you also want a license in other states that accept it, or when you already passed it elsewhere.

How many continuing education hours do Oregon commercial contractors need?

Level 2 commercial contractors need 32 hours every two years. Level 1 needs between 16 and 80 hours depending on the number of key employees. Finish CE before you renew.
