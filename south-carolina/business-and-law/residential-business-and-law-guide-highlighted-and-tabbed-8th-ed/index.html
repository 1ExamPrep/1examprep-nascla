During the examination, candidates may encounter questions covering several different business and project-management subjects. Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should combine reading, review, practice questions, and reference-book navigation. Reading the book once without returning to the material may not provide enough familiarity for an open-book examination. Repetition helps you recognize subjects and remember where information is located.

Begin with the table of contents and the tab labels. Review the overall structure of the guide before studying individual chapters. Understanding how the book is organized helps you create a mental map of the reference and makes it easier to predict where a topic will appear.

Study in manageable sessions rather than attempting to cover the entire book at once. Select a chapter or subject area, read the material, review the highlighted passages, and summarize the main ideas. Then answer questions related to that subject before moving to the next section.

Use the tabs actively during every study session. When reviewing a topic, close the book and practice reopening it to the correct tab. Repeat this process until major sections become familiar. The objective is to reduce hesitation when moving between subjects.

Practice identifying keywords in exam-style questions. A question may not use the same heading found in the reference. Determine whether the issue relates to contracts, project administration, financial management, business operations, safety, scheduling, employees, subcontractors, customers, or another major subject. Then use the book’s organization to narrow your search.

When a practice answer is incorrect, do more than record the correct choice. Locate the supporting information in the book and determine why the original answer was wrong. Review the surrounding text and identify the tab that should lead you back to the subject in the future.

Create a list of topics that require additional review. Candidates often bring different strengths to the examination. Someone with extensive field experience may need additional study in business administration, contracts, accounting, or legal terminology. A business owner may need more practice with construction scheduling or project-management concepts. Your practice results should guide the amount of time assigned to each area.

Use timed practice to develop pacing. Open-book tests can create a false sense that every answer can be researched without time pressure. In practice sessions, set a reasonable time limit and monitor how long you spend on individual questions. Learn when to confirm an answer quickly and when to mark a difficult question for later review.

Do not overuse the reference book on questions you already understand. Answer from your knowledge when possible, then verify only when needed. This approach reserves more time for difficult questions and reduces unnecessary page searching.

Review highlighted information in context. Highlighting helps prioritize material, but nearby paragraphs may explain exceptions, definitions, relationships, or examples that are necessary to select the best answer. Reading only isolated highlighted phrases may lead to misunderstanding.

Build a consistent search routine. Read the question carefully, identify the subject, select the relevant tab, scan the section headings, review the highlighted material, and confirm the answer. Using the same routine during practice can make exam-day navigation more natural.

In the final stages of preparation, focus on weak topics and reference speed. Continue answering mixed-subject practice questions so you become comfortable switching between sections. Review all tabs regularly, including those connected to subjects you believe you already know.

On exam day, read every question carefully before opening the book. Determine exactly what the question is asking and pay attention to words that affect the meaning. Then use the reference strategically. The goal is not to find a sentence that resembles the question, but to locate the information that supports the correct answer.
