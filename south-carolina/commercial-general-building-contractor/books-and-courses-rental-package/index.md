## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **Open Book Test**. South Carolina commercial contractor examinations administered through PSI are open book, with candidates responsible for bringing permitted references to the testing center.

Open-book testing should be approached strategically. The availability of reference books does not remove the need to understand the construction material. Searching through unfamiliar books for every answer can consume a substantial portion of the testing period.

A better approach is to determine what the question is testing, identify the reference most likely to contain the information, locate the relevant section, confirm the technical detail, and move forward. Practicing this process repeatedly can help make reference use more deliberate and efficient.

Reference materials used in South Carolina open-book commercial contractor examinations may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed according to applicable testing rules. References must otherwise remain free of prohibited writing and additional loose or attached papers. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing, while temporary removable tabs are not accepted.

Build familiarity with tables of contents, indexes, definitions, technical tables, code chapters, and terminology before exam day. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1. OSHA safety questions should lead to 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI materials, while commercial building-code questions may involve the International Building Code.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate South Carolina classification or subclassification.** South Carolina commercial contractor licenses are issued according to regulated construction classifications and subclassifications.
2. **Identify the Primary Qualifying Party.** Applicants must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification for which licensure is requested.
3. **Meet the experience requirement.** Applicants seeking qualifying-party certification by examination generally must document at least two years of applicable work experience performed within the previous five years.
4. **Complete the technical examination requirement.** For classifications requiring technical testing, candidates must satisfy the accepted examination requirement. The NASCLA commercial examination provides a national commercial building trade exam option recognized by South Carolina.
5. **Pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law exam.** Individuals seeking qualifying-party certification must complete this state-specific examination.
6. **Prepare the required licensing documentation.** The application process includes the applicable contractor and qualifying-party documentation.
7. **Provide the required financial documentation or surety bond.** South Carolina license group limitations are supported by applicable financial documentation or a qualifying surety bond.
8. **Complete the licensing application.** After examination and qualification requirements are satisfied, submit the required materials to the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board.

## State Requirements

Commercial contractor licensing in South Carolina is administered by the **South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board** through the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. A General Contractor license is required for regulated commercial construction when the work falls within the state's applicable licensing requirements.

Applicants must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification requested. The Primary Qualifying Party is the principal individual responsible for directing or reviewing the work performed by the licensee in that classification.

To qualify through examination, applicants must complete the applicable technical examination requirement and the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam. Candidates generally must also provide at least two years of relevant work experience completed within the previous five years for the requested classification or subclassification.

South Carolina contractor licenses also use financial group limitations. The applicable project and bid limitation depends on the financial documentation or surety bond submitted with the license application.

After examination requirements have been completed, applicants submit the appropriate general contractor and qualifying-party applications, required financial documentation or bond, evidence of authority to conduct business in South Carolina when applicable, examination pass documentation, and the applicable licensing materials.

Passing the NASCLA examination is an important examination credential, but it does not by itself issue a South Carolina contractor license. The contractor business and its qualifying party must satisfy the remaining South Carolina licensing requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions and provides 330 minutes for completion. Candidates need at least 81 correct answers to reach the 70% passing requirement. Effective preparation should combine construction knowledge with repeated reference-navigation practice.

**Study by construction subject.** Break the reference library into areas such as project management, safety, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, mechanical and electrical systems, building codes, and contracting requirements.

**Connect each subject to the correct reference.** Learn where to begin when you encounter a particular type of question. Strong topic-to-book associations reduce the time spent deciding which reference to search.

**Practice find-and-confirm navigation.** Analyze the question first. Use your knowledge to determine what is being tested and narrow the possible answers. Use a reference when you need to confirm a specific standard, rule, table, code section, or technical detail.

**Prepare separately for South Carolina Business Management and Law.** The South Carolina-specific exam is a separate requirement from the NASCLA commercial building trade exam. Spend dedicated study time with the South Carolina Commercial Contractors reference rather than assuming the NASCLA trade material covers the state examination.

**Train for pacing.** Timed practice helps develop the discipline to move on when a difficult question or lookup begins consuming too much time. A strong open-book strategy protects your available time across the entire exam.
