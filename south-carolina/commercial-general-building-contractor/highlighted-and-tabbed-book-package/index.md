## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate South Carolina classification.** South Carolina commercial contractor licensing is organized according to classifications and subclassifications.
2. **Identify the Primary Qualifying Party.** An applicant must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification for which licensure is requested.
3. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** Applicants seeking qualifying-party certification by examination generally must document at least two years of applicable work experience performed within the previous five years.
4. **Complete the technical examination requirement.** The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors can be used toward the applicable technical examination requirement for South Carolina's Unlimited Building classification.
5. **Pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law examination.** Individuals requesting qualifying-party certification must also pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam.
6. **Submit qualifying-party documentation.** Complete the applicable Primary Qualifying Party or Qualifying Party application materials.
7. **Complete the contractor license application.** Submit the required commercial contractor licensing documents to the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board.
8. **Meet the applicable financial requirements.** South Carolina license group limitations are supported by the required financial documentation or qualifying surety bond.

## State Requirements

Commercial contractor licensing in South Carolina is administered by the **South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board**. Applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with the classification or subclassification in which they plan to operate.

A South Carolina contractor applicant must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for the applicable classification. The Primary Qualifying Party is the principal individual responsible for directing or reviewing work performed by the licensee within that classification.

Applicants qualifying by examination must pass the applicable technical examination and must also pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam. South Carolina also generally requires qualifying-party applicants using the examination route to submit at least two years of relevant work experience performed within the previous five years.

South Carolina uses license group limitations that affect the size of projects a contractor may undertake. Applicants must provide the applicable financial documentation or surety bond supporting the group limitation they request.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create a South Carolina contractor license. NASCLA is an examination credential. Applicants must still satisfy South Carolina's qualifying-party, Business Management and Law, experience, financial, business, and application requirements before a license is issued.
