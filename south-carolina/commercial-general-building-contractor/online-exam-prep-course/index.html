Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial building trade examination used by participating contractor licensing jurisdictions. South Carolina recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam as the technical examination equivalent for its Unlimited Building classification.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, requiring 81 correct answers
  • South Carolina Classification: Unlimited Building
  • Additional South Carolina Requirement: South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam
  • South Carolina Testing Provider: PSI

The current NASCLA examination content outline includes 25 questions on General Requirements, 15 on Site Construction, 6 on Concrete, 4 on Masonry, 6 on Metals, 5 on Wood, 5 on Thermal and Moisture Protection, 4 on Doors, Windows and Glazing, 5 on Finishes, 6 on Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, 3 on Electrical Systems, and 31 on Procurement and Contracting Requirements.

This distribution makes it important to prepare across the entire commercial construction scope. Procurement, contracting, and general requirements account for a substantial share of the examination, while technical trade subjects remain important throughout the test.

With 115 questions and 330 minutes available, time management should be part of your preparation. Candidates should practice recognizing which questions can be answered from knowledge and which require a focused reference lookup. Spending too much time researching one question can reduce the time available for the remainder of the examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination according to the applicable examination and testing-center requirements.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity especially important. Candidates should not approach the examination with the expectation that every answer can simply be searched from scratch. The reference library is extensive, and inefficient searching can consume valuable testing time.

A stronger strategy begins with understanding the construction topic. Read the question carefully, identify what subject is being tested, select the most relevant reference, locate the needed information, confirm the answer, and continue. This find-and-confirm process is more efficient than moving randomly between several books.

During preparation, become familiar with each reference's table of contents, index, chapter organization, technical tables, definitions, and terminology. Build direct associations between subjects and books. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1-2017. OSHA questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

Practice is especially useful because reference-navigation speed improves with repetition. The more frequently you work with the same books while studying, the easier it becomes to determine where to begin when a lookup is required.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the South Carolina contractor classification you need. Commercial contractor licenses are organized by classifications and subclassifications. The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is recognized as the technical exam equivalent for Unlimited Building.
  2. Identify the Primary Qualifying Party. South Carolina applicants must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification requested.
  3. Meet the applicable experience requirement. Applicants seeking qualifying-party certification by examination generally must document at least two years of applicable work experience performed within the previous five years.
  4. Complete the technical examination requirement. Candidates pursuing Unlimited Building may use the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors toward this requirement.
  5. Pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law examination. All individuals must pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam before requesting qualifying-party certification.
  6. Complete the qualifying-party certification process. Submit the applicable qualifying-party information and supporting documentation.
  7. Complete the contractor license application. The contractor business must submit the required licensing application to the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board.
  8. Meet applicable financial requirements. South Carolina uses license groups and project limitations supported by required financial documentation or qualifying surety bonds.
  9. Complete remaining business and licensing requirements. Submit the documentation applicable to the business entity and requested license group.

State Requirements

Commercial contractor licensing in South Carolina is administered by the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board. The Board regulates commercial general and mechanical contractor classifications and establishes the licensing requirements that apply to each classification and subclassification.

For the Unlimited Building classification, South Carolina recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as an accepted technical examination equivalent. This allows candidates to use their NASCLA examination result toward the applicable technical exam requirement rather than completing a separate South Carolina Unlimited Building technical exam.

South Carolina still requires the Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam. The Board states that all individuals must pass this examination before requesting qualifying-party certification, regardless of how they are applying for licensure.

The contractor business must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each applicable classification. Candidates qualifying by examination generally must also demonstrate at least two years of relevant work experience performed during the previous five years.

South Carolina commercial contractor licenses use financial group limitations. These groups determine the project-value range available to the license holder. Applicants must satisfy the financial requirements associated with the group they request through applicable financial documentation or an approved surety bond.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a South Carolina contractor license. The examination satisfies an accepted technical testing component. Applicants must still complete the qualifying-party, Business Management and Law, experience, financial, application, and other requirements established for South Carolina licensure.

Test Information and Study Materials

A successful NASCLA preparation routine should combine knowledge review with reference navigation and timed practice. The exam's 115 questions cover enough subjects that studying one trade in isolation is not sufficient.

Organize your study by content area. Divide preparation into general requirements, site work, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, building systems, procurement, and contracting. This makes the overall syllabus easier to manage.

Pay attention to high-volume content areas. General Requirements and Procurement and Contracting Requirements represent a significant portion of the exam. Technical subjects are still important, but candidates should not overlook construction administration and contracting knowledge.

Practice with the reference materials. Use practice questions to develop subject-to-book associations. When a question involves accessibility, safety, concrete, steel deck, roofing, masonry, or building codes, you should have a clear idea of which reference is most likely to help.

Practice find-and-confirm navigation. Read the question first, determine the construction issue, identify the proper reference, locate the relevant information, and confirm the answer. Avoid searching randomly through multiple books.

Train under timed conditions. The NASCLA exam provides 330 minutes for 115 questions. Timed practice can help candidates recognize when a lookup is taking too long and when it is better to continue and return later.

Prepare separately for South Carolina Business Management and Law. The state-specific Business Management and Law examination remains a separate South Carolina requirement. Candidates should maintain a separate study plan for that exam in addition to NASCLA commercial trade preparation.
