Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies, including the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination contains 115 questions. Candidates must achieve a 70% passing score, or 81 correct answers. The examination provides 330 minutes, or 5 hours and 30 minutes, for completion.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • General Requirements: 25 questions
  • Site Construction: 15 questions
  • Concrete: 6 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 6 questions
  • Wood: 5 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 5 questions
  • Doors, Windows, and Glazing: 4 questions
  • Finishes: 5 questions
  • Mechanical and Plumbing Systems: 6 questions
  • Electrical Systems: 3 questions
  • Procurement and Contracting Requirements: 31 questions

The examination requires candidates to move between practical construction knowledge and technical reference material. Subjects can involve construction planning, jobsite operations, contracts, OSHA safety, building codes, concrete, masonry, structural systems, accessibility, building envelopes, mechanical systems, electrical systems, and project-management responsibilities.

South Carolina commercial contractor applicants must also pass the S.C. Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors examination. This state-specific examination is separate from the NASCLA trade exam and focuses on business, law, licensing, financial, contractual, employment, and project-management responsibilities applicable to contractors operating in South Carolina.

Candidates should prepare for the NASCLA trade exam and South Carolina Business Management and Law exam as separate but complementary parts of the licensing process. The national examination tests commercial construction competency, while the state examination addresses the regulatory and business responsibilities associated with South Carolina licensure.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open book test. Candidates may use the reference materials authorized for the examination under current testing-provider rules.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation a major part of preparation. Licensing Steps

The South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board regulates commercial general and mechanical contractor licensing. Passing an approved trade examination is one part of the process, but applicants must complete the Board's qualification and licensing requirements before operating under the applicable South Carolina contractor license.

  1. Determine the appropriate South Carolina classification or subclassification. Applicants should select the classification corresponding with the type of commercial construction work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Primary Qualifying Party. Each applicant must designate a Primary Qualifying Party, or PQP, for every classification or subclassification requested. The PQP is responsible for directing or reviewing work performed under that classification.
  3. Satisfy the technical examination requirement. The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is accepted by the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board for applicable commercial general building trade qualification.
  4. Pass South Carolina Business Management and Law. Applicants must satisfy the S.C. Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors examination requirement.
  5. Document qualifying experience. Applicants qualifying through examination are required to document at least two years of work experience performed within the previous five years for the requested classification or subclassification.
  6. Establish and register the business. Business entities must be authorized to conduct business in South Carolina when applicable.
  7. Complete the contractor license application. Applicants submit the required business, PQP, examination, classification, financial, and supporting documentation to the Contractors' Licensing Board.
  8. Meet financial qualification requirements. South Carolina requires financial documentation or an acceptable surety bond supporting the group limitation requested for the contractor license.
  9. Complete Board review. The Board evaluates the licensing application and supporting materials before issuing the applicable South Carolina contractor license.

State Requirements

South Carolina generally requires a General or Mechanical Contractor license for regulated commercial construction work valued at more than $10,000. The applicable classification must correspond with the type of construction work the contractor intends to perform.

The South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board requires applicants to designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification requested. Additional Qualifying Parties may also be certified when they satisfy the same examination requirements, but the PQP is the principal individual responsible for directing or reviewing work performed under the classification.

Applicants qualifying through examination must generally provide at least two years of applicable work experience performed within the previous five years for the requested classification or subclassification. Approved waiver or reciprocity applicants may be treated differently under Board rules.

Commercial contractor applicants must pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors examination in addition to satisfying the applicable technical examination or approved technical-exam waiver requirement.

After the qualifying individual has satisfied examination requirements, the contractor business must submit its licensing application. Business entities must provide authorization to conduct business in South Carolina when required, along with the applicable financial statement or surety bond supporting the requested license group limitation.

The NASCLA examination is not a national contractor license. It is an examination credential accepted by participating state agencies. Contractors using a NASCLA result in South Carolina must still obtain approval and licensure from the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board.
