Exam Details

South Carolina commercial contractor applicants using the NASCLA pathway should prepare for two distinct examination requirements. The first is the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors, which can satisfy the applicable technical examination requirement for South Carolina's Unlimited Building classification. The second is the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam, which is required for qualifying-party certification.

  • NASCLA Trade Examination: Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • NASCLA Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 81 correct answers
  • South Carolina Requirement: Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam
  • Testing Provider for South Carolina Commercial Contractor Exams: PSI

The NASCLA commercial exam measures broad knowledge rather than one construction specialty. Candidates should be prepared to work across areas involving general requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, finishes, building systems, safety, project management, procurement, and contracting responsibilities.

Because the exam covers so many disciplines, preparation should balance trade knowledge with reference familiarity. A contractor may be highly experienced in concrete, framing, roofing, or jobsite supervision while still needing additional review in accessibility, structural standards, OSHA regulations, steel systems, building codes, or project administration.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is an Open Book Test. South Carolina commercial contractor examinations administered through PSI also use approved reference materials according to the applicable testing requirements.

Open-book testing rewards organization and familiarity. Having access to a large number of books is useful only when you know which reference applies to the subject being tested. Candidates who search through several unfamiliar books for every question can lose valuable testing time.

The rental books included with this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These features provide an organized starting point for study, but candidates should still work directly with the books throughout the preparation period. Learn how each index works, where major chapters begin, what terminology the publication uses, and where important tables, definitions, standards, and code sections are located.

A useful open-book workflow is to identify the subject, select the most relevant reference, locate the needed information, confirm the answer, and move forward. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1. OSHA safety questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI materials, while broader building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

The goal is not to memorize every page. It is to become familiar enough with the reference library that you know where to begin when a technical lookup is necessary.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the applicable South Carolina classification. Commercial contractor licensing is organized by classifications and subclassifications. The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is accepted as the technical examination equivalent for Unlimited Building.
  2. Identify the Primary Qualifying Party. A South Carolina applicant must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each classification or subclassification requested.
  3. Complete the applicable technical exam requirement. Candidates applying by examination must satisfy the technical examination associated with the requested classification.
  4. Pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law exam. Qualifying-party applicants must pass this state-specific examination before requesting certification.
  5. Document applicable experience. Applicants seeking qualifying-party certification by examination must generally submit at least two years of relevant work experience performed within the previous five years.
  6. Complete the qualifying-party application. Submit the applicable Primary Qualifying Party or Qualifying Party certification documentation.
  7. Submit the contractor license application. The contractor business completes the applicable general contractor licensing application.
  8. Provide financial documentation or a surety bond. South Carolina uses license groups with project-value limits, supported by applicable financial statements or surety bonds.
  9. Complete remaining business requirements. Provide applicable South Carolina business-registration information and other licensing materials.

State Requirements

Commercial contractor licensing in South Carolina is administered by the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board. A General or Mechanical Contractor's license is required for regulated commercial construction exceeding the applicable statutory threshold within classifications governed by South Carolina contractor law.

Applicants must designate a Primary Qualifying Party for each requested classification or subclassification. The Primary Qualifying Party is responsible for directing or reviewing work performed by the licensed entity within that classification.

Qualifying-party candidates applying by examination must complete the appropriate technical examination requirement and pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors Exam. Applicants generally must also document at least two years of applicable work experience performed during the previous five years.

For the Unlimited Building classification, South Carolina recognizes the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as an accepted technical exam equivalent. This allows candidates with an accepted NASCLA result to use that examination toward the applicable South Carolina technical exam requirement.

South Carolina contractor licenses also use financial group limitations. Each group establishes a maximum project value and corresponding financial requirements. Applicants may support the requested group using applicable financial documentation or an approved surety bond.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically create a South Carolina contractor license. The contractor business and its qualifying party must still complete the Business Management and Law exam, experience, application, financial, and other licensing requirements that apply.
