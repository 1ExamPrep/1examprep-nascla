South Carolina NASCLA Exam and Contractor License Guide

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 13 min read · 10 official sources cited

South Carolina is one of the states where the NASCLA exam can do real work for you. The South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam and the NASCLA electrical contractor exam, and the Residential Builders Commission accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam as a way to skip its own technical test. If you plan to work in more than one state, that is a strong reason to take the national exam instead of a South Carolina only trade test.

This page walks you through which agency licenses what, which exam each license needs, the Business Management and Law exam that nobody gets to skip, the financial groups and bond options, published fees, and how to pick study materials. Does South Carolina accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes, through two separate agencies. NASCLA lists the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board (CLB) as a participating agency for the NASCLA Commercial Building exam. On the electrical side, NASCLA lists the CLB as accepting the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam and the Residential Builders Commission (RBC) as accepting the Residential Electrical Contractor exam.

On the commercial side, the CLB's licensure page names the National Commercial Building Contractor exam and the NASCLA accredited electrical contractor exam as options for people who want a multi-state credential, and it says NASCLA transcripts can be sent straight to the Board. The PSI bulletin for the CLB describes the NASCLA commercial exam as built for the general commercial building classification or a similar one. Before you register, ask the CLB which building classification your NASCLA result will support, since South Carolina splits building work into Building, Limited Building and Unlimited Building.

On the residential side, the RBC waives its Residential Electrical technical exam if you passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam within the past three years. You attach the NASCLA transcript to your application.

In every case the NASCLA exam replaces only the trade portion. You still pass the South Carolina Business Management and Law exam, document your experience, and meet the agency's financial and application rules.

Who issues contractor and electrical licenses in South Carolina

Both agencies sit under the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR). The Contractor's Licensing Board regulates general contractors, mechanical contractors, construction managers, burglar and fire alarm contractors, fire sprinkler contractors and the boiler safety program. You need a CLB license for regulated commercial work when the total cost of construction is more than $10,000.

Electrical contracting on commercial jobs falls under the CLB as a mechanical contractor classification called Electrical (EL). The number after the code sets your per-job limit, so an EL4 license covers jobs up to $400,000.

The Residential Builders Commission licenses residential builders and residential specialty contractors, including Residential Electricians. Its page says residential electrical work over $500 requires a license, while commercial electrical work over $10,000 belongs with the CLB. An entity that does only residential construction is licensed by the RBC, not the CLB.

Your city or county may also require its own business license and permits, so check locally once your state license is in hand.

South Carolina contractor license types and which exam each needs

The CLB issues licenses by classification and by group. Each license needs a Primary Qualifying Party (PQP) for every classification. That person passes the exams and carries the experience. Here is how the main paths line up with the exams we prepare you for:

  • Building (BD), Limited Building (LB) and Unlimited Building (UB): general contractor classifications that need a technical exam plus Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors. Limited Building is capped at three stories and Groups 1 to 3; Unlimited Building has no story cap and can apply for any group. The NASCLA Commercial General Building exam is the national route here; confirm with the CLB which classification it supports for you.
  • Electrical (EL), a CLB mechanical classification: you can take the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam or the South Carolina state electrician exam, plus Business Management and Law. NASCLA lists this as the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician acceptance.
  • Residential Electrician (RBC): the SC Residential Electrical exam, or a waiver if you passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam within three years, plus the RBC Business Management and Law exam.
  • Classifications with no technical exam, such as Masonry, Boring and Tunneling, Highway Incidental, Miscellaneous Metals and Railroad Lines, still need the experience affidavit and the business exam.

How to get a South Carolina general contractor license, step by step

This is the CLB path for a commercial building license using the NASCLA exam. The residential electrician path follows the same idea, except you apply to the RBC first and it approves the waiver.

  1. Pick your classification and group. Match the classification to the work and the group to the largest job you will bid.
  2. Name your Primary Qualifying Party, the person who will sit for the exams and show at least 2 years of experience within the past 5 years.
  3. Apply to NASCLA for the Commercial General Building exam at ned.nascla.org, get approved, then schedule with the testing vendor.
  4. Pass the NASCLA exam and have your transcript sent to the CLB.
  5. Register with PSI and pass Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors. The CLB says no Board pre-approval is needed to schedule PSI exams.
  6. Gather your financial statement or surety bond for your group, Secretary of State proof that the entity can do business in South Carolina (not needed for sole proprietors), and the Work Experience Affidavit.
  7. File the initial application with the $350 license fee by mail, email or in person, and wait for the Board to issue the license before you bid regulated work.

The NASCLA exam itself

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions, a 330 minute time limit, and a 70 percent passing mark. It may also include unscored pretest items. It is open book, so your job on test day is to find answers fast in a large reference library, not to recall every rule from memory. You apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org and need pre-approval before you can schedule.

PSI's South Carolina commercial bulletin lists the NASCLA commercial exam at $110 and the NASCLA electrical contractor exam at $116. The electrical exam runs 100 questions in 270 minutes, and PSI hands out a printed diagram or blueprint packet at the test site. Those are published figures; check the current bulletin before you pay.

Book rules matter. PSI allows highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs, meaning tabs that would tear the page if pulled off. Sticky notes and other temporary tabs must come out before the exam starts, books cannot contain loose papers, and proctors inspect every book before and after. Reference books are not sold at the test center, so bring your own.

For the Residential Electrical Contractor exam, NASCLA treats it as an open book electrical trade exam. Plan on heavy National Electrical Code work, theory and calculations, and safety standards.

The South Carolina Business Management and Law exam

Every CLB applicant must pass Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors, and the NASCLA exam does not waive it. PSI's bulletin lists it at 50 questions, 125 minutes, 35 correct to pass, and a $78 fee. It is open book. The reference is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, South Carolina Commercial Contractors, 9th Edition. The CLB lets you retake this business exam as often as you need within 12 months, and once you pass it you cannot sit for it again.

The RBC runs its own Business Management and Law exam through PSI. The Commission lists 50 multiple choice questions with a scale score of 68 to pass, and you schedule with PSI after the Commission approves your application. It still applies when your technical exam is waived through NASCLA.

Watch the edition. NASCLA's store now lists a South Carolina Residential Builders 9th Edition, and we also carry the Residential Builders 8th Edition. Before you buy, check the current PSI bulletin or ask the RBC which edition your exam uses. For the commercial exam, the 9th Edition is the one PSI names.

South Carolina experience, financial and bond requirements

Experience: CLB applicants need at least 2 years of experience within the past 5 years in the classification requested, documented on the Work Experience Affidavit (Doc. 167). Experience should be mostly commercial, except for Nonstructural Renovation, which can rest on residential work alone. For the RBC Residential Electrician license, the Commission has to verify and approve your work experience, and the NASCLA waiver does not remove that step.

Financial: the CLB sets five groups. You meet either the working capital or the net worth figure for your group, or you post a surety bond in the group amount instead. Published general contractor figures:

  • Group 1: jobs up to $100,000; $10,000 working capital or $20,000 net worth; $20,000 bond.
  • Group 2: up to $400,000; $40,000 or $60,000; $60,000 bond.
  • Group 3: up to $1,000,000; $100,000 or $150,000; $150,000 bond.
  • Group 4: up to $3,000,000; $175,000 or $250,000; $250,000 bond.
  • Group 5: unlimited; $250,000 or $350,000; $350,000 bond.
  • Mechanical (including Electrical) groups run from $35,000 per job at Group 1 to unlimited at Group 5, with their own lower capital and bond figures. On a first application, Groups 1 and 2 may use an owner prepared statement with affidavit, Groups 3 and 4 need a CPA compiled statement, and Group 5 needs an audited statement, unless you bond instead.

South Carolina contractor license costs and fees

These are the published figures at the time of writing. Confirm on the CLB fee page and the RBC Residential Electrician page before you pay, because fees can change.

CLB general and mechanical contractors: a $350 initial license fee and a $135 biennial renewal if postmarked by October 31. Late renewal adds $100 in November, $150 in December and $200 in January. Reinstatement is $350.

RBC Residential Electrician: a $135 application fee and a $220 license fee. Renewal is $220, a late renewal adds $50, and reinstatement within three years of lapse is $480. Past three years you reapply and retest.

Exam fees go to PSI, not the board. PSI's commercial bulletin lists $78 for Business Management and Law, $110 for the NASCLA commercial exam and $116 for the NASCLA electrical contractor exam. Add the cost of your books, any CPA work on your financial statement, or bond premiums if you bond instead.

Reciprocity and working in other states

The biggest reason to choose the NASCLA exam is portability. Your passing score is stored in NASCLA's National Examination Database, and you can send transcripts to other participating agencies. Each state still makes you apply under its own rules, and many will want their own business and law exam.

South Carolina also has its own exam waiver route. If you hold a license in good standing in a jurisdiction on the CLB's Technical Exam Waiver Agreement List, the board that licensed you completes a waiver form and mails it in. Licenses earned by waiver or grandfathering do not count, and photocopies of licenses or scores are not accepted. Waiver applicants are exempt from the experience requirement. For Residential Electricians, the RBC also accepts licenses from North Carolina or Mississippi earned by exam and held for at least a year, among other routes.

Going the other way, Tennessee lists the South Carolina CLB among the agencies whose commercial licenses can earn a trade exam waiver there.

After you pass: applying, renewal and keeping your license

Passing is the middle of the process, not the end. CLB applicants submit the application package with PSI pass sheets or an approved waiver, the financial statement or bond, entity paperwork and the fee. Do not bid regulated work until the license is issued.

CLB general contractor licenses expire on October 31 of even-numbered years, and mechanical contractor licenses, including Electrical, expire on October 31 of odd-numbered years. Renewal is biennial, and at renewal the financial documentation rules ease for most groups, though Group 5 still needs a CPA reviewed statement or a bond. If you want to move to a higher group or add a classification later, the CLB has change forms for that.

On the residential side, the RBC's online application is for first-time applicants, and once you submit a complete application you have one year to send in everything the Commission asks for. Keep your email current with the Commission, since that is how it reaches you. The pages we reviewed do not list a continuing education rule for either license, so ask the board at renewal time.

South Carolina NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does South Carolina accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. NASCLA lists the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board as a participating agency, and the Board accepts NASCLA transcripts sent directly to it. It covers the technical exam only; you still pass Business Management and Law for Commercial Contractors and meet the experience and financial rules.

Do I still have to take the South Carolina Business and Law exam if I pass NASCLA?

Yes. Both the CLB and the Residential Builders Commission require their Business Management and Law exam no matter how you satisfy the technical exam. The commercial version uses the NASCLA South Carolina Commercial Contractors 9th Edition guide and is open book.

When do I need a contractor license in South Carolina?

The CLB requires a license for regulated commercial work where the total construction cost is more than $10,000. The Residential Builders Commission requires a license for residential electrical work over $500. Local permits and business licenses are separate.

How much experience do I need for a South Carolina general contractor license?

The CLB asks for at least 2 years of experience within the past 5 years in the classification you request, shown on the Work Experience Affidavit. Applicants who qualify through an exam waiver or reciprocity are exempt from that rule.

Can I use the NASCLA Residential Electrical exam for a South Carolina residential electrician license?

Yes, if you passed it within the past three years. The Residential Builders Commission waives its technical exam when you include your NASCLA transcript, but you still need verified work experience and the Business Management and Law exam.

What does a South Carolina contractor license cost?

The CLB's published initial license fee is $350 and biennial renewal is $135. The RBC's published Residential Electrician fees are a $135 application fee and a $220 license fee. PSI exam fees and financial statement or bond costs come on top, so check the boards' fee pages for current numbers.

Do I need a bond or a financial statement?

One or the other. CLB applicants either show the working capital or net worth for their group or post a surety bond in the group amount, from $20,000 at Group 1 to $350,000 at Group 5 for general contractors. The bond must name the State of South Carolina as obligee.

Can I bring tabbed and highlighted books into the South Carolina exam?

PSI allows highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs. Temporary tabs such as sticky notes must be removed, books cannot hold loose papers, and proctors check every book. Our highlighted and tabbed packages use permanent tabs for that reason.

Which edition of the South Carolina business and law book do I need?

For the commercial exam, PSI names the South Carolina Commercial Contractors 9th Edition. NASCLA now lists a 9th Edition for residential builders too, so confirm with the Residential Builders Commission or the PSI bulletin which edition your residential exam uses before you order.
