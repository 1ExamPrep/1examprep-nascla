Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical trade examinations accepted by participating state agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

South Carolina's Residential Builders Commission accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination as a technical-exam waiver option for applicants seeking the Residential Electrician license. To use this pathway, the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination must have been passed within the previous three years, and a copy of the examination transcript must be included with the application.

The accepted NASCLA examination addresses the technical examination requirement. It does not replace the separate South Carolina Business Management and Law examination, nor does it replace the required work experience or other licensing documentation.

For candidates who do not use an accepted technical-exam waiver, South Carolina's Residential Electrician technical examination currently consists of 60 multiple-choice questions and requires a passing score of 73.3%. The separate Business Management and Law examination contains 50 multiple-choice questions and requires a scale score of 68.

South Carolina requires applicants to obtain examination eligibility from the Residential Builders Commission before scheduling a state examination. Once approved, the Commission notifies the applicant and PSI, and the candidate can then contact PSI to schedule the required examination.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program identifies its Residential Electrical Contractor examination as one of its open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice determining which reference applies to a question, locating the applicable code section, table, definition, formula, or safety requirement, and applying the information correctly.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a South Carolina Residential Electrician license. Candidates must complete the licensing process through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.

  1. Confirm the Residential Electrician classification. Make sure the South Carolina residential electrical license matches the work you intend to perform.
  2. Meet the experience requirement. Applicants must have the qualifying residential electrical experience required by the Commission.
  3. Submit the Residential Electrician application. Apply through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission with all required supporting documentation.
  4. Use the NASCLA technical-exam waiver when eligible. Applicants who passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination within the previous three years may submit that examination result for the technical-exam waiver pathway.
  5. Provide the NASCLA transcript. Include a copy of the applicable NASCLA examination transcript with the application.
  6. Complete the Business Management and Law examination. This South Carolina examination remains required even when the technical exam is waived through NASCLA.
  7. Provide work-experience documentation. Submit the Residential Electrician Experience Affidavit or other supporting documentation required by the Commission.
  8. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. Provide any additional business, identity, financial, or licensing documentation applicable to the application.
  9. Receive Commission approval. The South Carolina Residential Electrician license must be issued before performing work that requires the credential.

State Requirements

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission regulates residential specialty contractors, including Residential Electricians. South Carolina states that residential electrical work exceeding $500 requires a license. Commercial electrical work that exceeds the applicable commercial licensing threshold is regulated separately through the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board.

Applicants seeking South Carolina Residential Electrician licensure by examination must have at least one year of Commission-approved experience under the supervision of a licensee in the trade before becoming eligible for the licensing examination.

Work experience must be verified as part of the application process. The Commission reviews the applicant's experience and documentation before determining eligibility. Detailed descriptions of actual electrical work are important because the Commission evaluates whether the experience relates directly to the trade classification being requested.

South Carolina provides several technical-exam waiver pathways. For the Residential Electrician license, an applicant may qualify through an eligible existing South Carolina electrical contractor credential, a qualifying license from North Carolina or Mississippi, an accepted master electrician certification, or the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination passed within the previous three years.

The Business Management and Law examination remains required for applicants using the technical-exam waiver pathway. NASCLA acceptance does not waive the business portion of South Carolina's licensing examination process.

For candidates taking the standard state Residential Electrical technical examination, the current test contains 60 multiple-choice questions and requires a passing score of 73.3%. The Business Management and Law examination contains 50 multiple-choice questions and requires a scale score of 68.

Applicants must receive an Examination Eligibility Letter from the Commission before scheduling the applicable state examination with PSI. Examination fees are paid directly to PSI.

A South Carolina Residential Electrician license is intended for residential electrical work. Contractors planning to perform regulated commercial electrical work must review the separate commercial licensing requirements administered through the Contractors' Licensing Board.

South Carolina Residential Electrical licenses renew on June 30 of every odd-numbered year. Licensees are responsible for maintaining active status and satisfying applicable renewal and business requirements.
