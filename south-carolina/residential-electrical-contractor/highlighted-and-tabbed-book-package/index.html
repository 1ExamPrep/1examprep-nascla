Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized electrical trade examinations for participating regulatory agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission currently accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination for its Residential Electrician technical-exam waiver pathway. To qualify under the NASCLA option, the examination must have been passed within the previous three years, and the applicant must provide a copy of the NASCLA examination transcript with the application.

South Carolina applicants should understand that the NASCLA examination addresses the technical examination requirement. The Business Management and Law examination remains required, along with the Commission's work-experience and application requirements.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a South Carolina Residential Electrician license. Applicants must complete the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission licensing process.

  1. Review the Residential Electrician classification. Confirm that the South Carolina residential specialty electrical license matches the scope of work you intend to perform.
  2. Meet the work-experience requirement. Applicants must demonstrate the residential electrical experience required by the Commission.
  3. Complete the Residential Electrician application. Submit the current application and required supporting documentation to the Commission.
  4. Use the NASCLA technical-exam waiver when eligible. Applicants who passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam within the previous three years may use that result to waive the technical examination.
  5. Provide the NASCLA transcript. Include a copy of the applicable NASCLA examination transcript with the application.
  6. Complete the Business Management and Law examination. The NASCLA waiver does not eliminate this South Carolina examination requirement.
  7. Provide experience documentation. Submit the applicable Work Experience Affidavit or other qualifying documentation accepted by the Commission.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Provide any additional identification, credit, business, or supporting documentation required by the Commission.
  9. Receive the license before performing regulated work. The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission must approve the application and issue the Residential Electrician license.

State Requirements

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission regulates residential builders and residential specialty contractors, including Residential Electricians. The Residential Electrician license applies to qualifying electrical work within the residential specialty contracting scope established by South Carolina law and Commission rules.

Current Commission instructions require residential specialty applicants to document qualifying work experience. Applicants generally must provide a Work Experience Affidavit supporting one year of residential work experience within the previous five years for the requested trade classification or qualifying notarized reference letters accepted under the Commission's instructions.

For examination-based licensure, the Commission also states that applicants must have one year of Commission-approved experience under the supervision of a licensee in the trade before becoming eligible to take the required examination.

The Commission looks for detailed descriptions of the work performed by the individual applicant rather than general descriptions of the company's services. For residential electrical applicants, relevant experience can include hands-on residential electrical installation, service-panel work, residential wiring, new-construction wiring, estimating, bidding, plan review, and other qualifying electrical tasks.

Applicants may also be required to provide supporting employment records such as W-2 or 1099 documentation when applicable. The Commission uses this information together with experience affidavits and other records to evaluate whether the applicant satisfies the experience requirement.

South Carolina provides several technical-exam waiver pathways for Residential Electricians. One option is the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination when passed within the previous three years. Other qualifying pathways can include certain existing South Carolina mechanical electrical licenses, qualifying licenses from eligible states, or designated electrical certifications recognized by the Commission.

The accepted NASCLA result waives only the technical portion. The work-experience requirement and the Business Management and Law examination remain applicable.

The Residential Builders Commission currently requires applicants to obtain a scale score of 68 to pass the Business Management and Law examination. Applicants seeking examination approval must first submit their application to the Commission. Once approved, the Commission notifies the applicant and PSI, and the applicant can then schedule the examination.

The Residential Electrician credential is separate from South Carolina's commercial electrical contractor licensing administered through the Contractors' Licensing Board. Contractors planning to perform commercial electrical work should review the separate licensing requirements applicable to that scope.

License holders must perform work only within the scope authorized by their credential and comply with applicable electrical codes, permitting rules, inspection requirements, and local business licensing requirements.
