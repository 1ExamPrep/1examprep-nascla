## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes three electrical trade examinations: Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and **Residential Electrical Contractor**. NASCLA developed these examinations to provide participating licensing agencies with standardized trade examinations addressing electrical knowledge and practical trade responsibilities.

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission accepts the Residential Electrical Contractor examination. NASCLA examination scores must be within **three years** for South Carolina acceptance. South Carolina's own licensing information likewise states that an applicant who has passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam within the previous three years may obtain the Residential Electrician license without taking the state's technical examination, provided the applicant satisfies the remaining requirements.

South Carolina applicants using the NASCLA exam-waiver pathway must also complete the **Business Management and Law portion** of the state's licensing examination requirements. Passing NASCLA waives the technical portion; it does not waive Business Management and Law.

For comparison, applicants who use South Carolina's standard examination pathway take a Residential Electrical technical examination containing 60 multiple-choice questions and must obtain a passing score of 73.3%. The Business Management and Law examination contains 50 multiple-choice questions. NASCLA candidates who qualify for the technical exam waiver remain responsible for the Business Management and Law requirement.

Open-book testing makes reference-navigation skills an important part of preparation. ## Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a South Carolina Residential Electrician license. Applicants must complete the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission licensing process.

1. **Review South Carolina Residential Electrician requirements.** Confirm that you meet the Commission's experience and application requirements.
2. **Document qualifying work experience.** South Carolina requires verifiable experience under appropriate supervision in the electrical trade.
3. **Apply through the Residential Builders Commission.** Submit the Residential Electrician license application and required supporting documentation.
4. **Prepare for the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination.** Study residential NEC requirements, electrical theory, calculations, safety, wiring methods, equipment, fire alarm systems, residential construction, and business topics.
5. **Take and pass the NASCLA examination.** The passing examination must be within three years when used for South Carolina's technical examination waiver.
6. **Submit the NASCLA examination transcript.** Include the required examination documentation with the South Carolina application.
7. **Complete the South Carolina Business Management and Law examination.** This portion remains required even when the NASCLA examination is accepted in place of the technical exam.
8. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** Submit any additional documentation requested by the Residential Builders Commission.
9. **Receive the license before performing regulated work.** The Commission must approve the application and issue the Residential Electrician license.

## State Requirements

The **South Carolina Residential Builders Commission** regulates Residential Electricians under the state's residential specialty contracting laws. South Carolina requires a Residential Electrician license for qualifying residential electrical work exceeding **$500**.

Residential electrical applicants must have qualifying experience. South Carolina's current licensing instructions require applicants using the examination pathway to demonstrate at least **one year of verifiable experience under the supervision of an appropriately licensed individual** in the trade for which the applicant is seeking licensure.

The Residential Builders Commission verifies experience through documentation from previous or current supervisors or employers. The Residential Electrician Experience Affidavit is used to document the applicant's work, and the person verifying the experience must have been appropriately licensed during the period of supervision and have direct knowledge of the applicant's work.

The Commission prefers hands-on experience and identifies residential electrical activities such as service-panel installation and upgrades, general residential wiring, new-construction wiring, estimating, bidding, and plan review among the types of experience that can be documented on the current Residential Electrician Experience Affidavit.

Applicants are also instructed to provide proof of employment history, including applicable W-2 or 1099 records, along with experience affidavits and other supporting documentation. The Commission may verify the experience claimed by contacting the employers or supervisors identified in the application.

For the NASCLA pathway, South Carolina accepts a passing **NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors exam completed within the previous three years**. This permits the applicant to waive the required technical portion of the South Carolina examination. The work-experience requirements and South Carolina Business Management and Law examination remain applicable.

South Carolina also recognizes technical-exam waiver pathways for qualifying licensees from North Carolina and Mississippi, as well as certain South Carolina mechanical contractors holding the Electrical classification. These pathways are separate from NASCLA acceptance.

The current South Carolina Residential Electrician application fee is **$135**, and the current license fee is **$220**. The current renewal fee is also $220. These government licensing fees are separate from this online exam prep product and from NASCLA examination charges.

South Carolina law also establishes limitations on the scope of residential specialty contracting. A Residential Electrician must operate within the scope authorized by the electrical license and comply with applicable county and municipal business licensing and regulatory requirements.

If a firm practices residential specialty contracting and the licensee does not own at least 51% of the business, a **Residential Business Certificate of Authorization** is required under the Commission's current licensing rules.

Commercial electrical contracting is regulated separately. South Carolina directs individuals planning to perform commercial electrical work exceeding $10,000 to the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board for information about the applicable commercial contractor license.
