Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical trade examinations recognized by participating regulatory agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

South Carolina currently allows an applicant for the Residential Electrician license to obtain a waiver of the required technical examination when the applicant has passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination within the previous three years. A copy of the NASCLA examination transcript must be included with the South Carolina application.

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination focuses on knowledge associated with electrical installation, maintenance, service, repair, safety, code compliance, electrical theory, calculations, and residential electrical systems. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program identifies its Residential Electrical Contractor examination as an open-book electrical trade examination.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should know how to identify a question's subject, determine which reference contains the relevant material, locate the applicable code section, definition, table, formula, or safety requirement, and apply that information correctly.

All rental books included in this Ultimate package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting can help draw attention to important material during review, while permanent tabs provide visible navigation points throughout larger codes, standards, manuals, and reference books.

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2023 should receive substantial study attention. Candidates should practice using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, chapter organization, individual articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Residential study should include services, feeders, branch circuits, load calculations, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, appliances, motors, and other residential electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be reviewed alongside NEC navigation. Important areas include voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, motors, transformers, and practical electrical calculations. Understanding the electrical principle behind a problem can help candidates determine which formula or code provision applies.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-industry safety preparation, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system preparation. The International Residential Code provides broader residential construction context, and ANSI Z535.4-2023 addresses product safety signs and labels.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide and NASCLA South Carolina Residential Builders, 8th Edition support business-focused preparation. Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination does not automatically issue a South Carolina Residential Electrician license. Applicants must complete the licensing process through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.

  1. Confirm the Residential Electrician classification. Make sure the South Carolina Residential Electrician license matches the work you intend to perform.
  2. Complete the Residential Electrician application. Submit the required application, documentation, and applicable fees to the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission.
  3. Document qualifying work experience. Work experience must be verified and approved by the Commission before examination eligibility or waiver processing is completed.
  4. Use the NASCLA technical-exam waiver when eligible. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination must have been passed within the previous three years.
  5. Provide the NASCLA examination transcript. Include a copy of the applicable exam transcript with the South Carolina application.
  6. Prepare for Business Management and Law. The South Carolina Business Management and Law examination remains applicable even when the technical examination is waived.
  7. Use the Ultimate preparation materials. Study with the highlighted and permanently tabbed rental books and the included 1 year of course access.
  8. Use the included Application Service. Application Service is included with this package to support the licensing application process.
  9. Complete remaining Commission requirements. Submit any additional documentation required to complete the application.
  10. Receive the Residential Electrician license. The Commission must approve the application before the applicant performs work requiring the license.

State Requirements

The South Carolina Residential Builders Commission regulates Residential Electricians and other residential specialty contractors in South Carolina. Individuals seeking a Residential Electrician license must satisfy the Commission's application, experience, and examination requirements.

South Carolina currently requires a license for residential electrical work exceeding $500. Commercial electrical work exceeding the applicable commercial licensing threshold is handled separately through the South Carolina Contractors' Licensing Board.

Applicants seeking licensure through the NASCLA exam-waiver pathway must first submit the appropriate application and required documentation to the Residential Builders Commission. The Commission reviews the application and determines whether the applicant qualifies for the waiver.

The technical examination may be waived when an applicant has passed the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractors examination within the previous three years. The applicant must submit a copy of the NASCLA examination transcript.

The waiver does not remove the work-experience requirement or the Business Management and Law examination. Both remain part of the South Carolina licensing process.

Work experience must be documented and approved by the Commission. Applicants should provide complete and accurate information describing the electrical work performed so that the Commission can evaluate whether the experience satisfies the requirements for Residential Electrician licensure.

South Carolina also recognizes certain other technical-exam waiver pathways, including qualifying licenses from North Carolina and Mississippi and qualifying electrician certifications. These pathways are separate from the NASCLA option and have their own eligibility conditions.

The current South Carolina Residential Electrician application fee is $135. The current Residential Electrician license fee is $220. These state licensing fees are separate from the Ultimate Exam Prep Rental Package price.

South Carolina introduced a new electronic application platform for Residential Builders Commission applications in 2026. Applicants completing a new license application should use the current Commission application process and submit all required information within the applicable application period.

A South Carolina Residential Electrician license is separate from commercial electrical contractor licensing. Contractors should remain within the scope authorized by their credential and comply with applicable building, electrical, permitting, inspection, and local licensing requirements.
