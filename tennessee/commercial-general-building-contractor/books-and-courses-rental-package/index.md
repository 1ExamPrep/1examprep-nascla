## Open Book Test

Tennessee contractor examinations are **Open Book Test** examinations. The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors identifies the Business and Law exam as open book, and candidates use approved reference materials according to the applicable examination rules.

Open-book testing makes preparation with the actual reference materials especially important. Having access to books during an examination does not eliminate the need to understand the material. Candidates who are unfamiliar with their references may spend too much time trying to determine which book contains a particular answer.

The rental books included with this package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These features provide an organized starting point for study and navigation. Candidates should still work with the references regularly so they become familiar with tables of contents, indexes, chapters, technical tables, code sections, terminology, and major subject areas.

A useful open-book strategy is to first identify what a question is testing. From there, select the reference most likely to contain the information, perform a focused lookup, confirm the answer, and move forward. For example, accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1, OSHA safety questions toward 29 CFR Part 1926, and structural concrete questions toward ACI 318 and related concrete references.

The same principle applies to the Tennessee Business and Law examination. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the Tennessee edition of the NASCLA business and law guide so they can quickly locate state-specific material involving contractor responsibilities, business practices, law, and project management.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate Tennessee contractor classification.** Candidates pursuing commercial building work generally review the BC-B Commercial Building classification and its scope.
2. **Identify the Qualifying Agent.** Tennessee uses a Qualifying Agent to complete required examinations for the business seeking licensure. The QA may be an owner, officer, or full-time employee of the applicant entity.
3. **Complete the Tennessee Business and Law examination.** All new contractor license applicants must pass Tennessee's Business and Law exam.
4. **Satisfy the commercial trade examination requirement.** For BC-B, Tennessee accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as an alternative to the Tennessee commercial trade exam.
5. **Prepare the contractor license application.** The applicant entity submits the required licensing documentation to the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.
6. **Provide the applicable financial documentation.** Tennessee contractor licensing includes financial requirements that affect the monetary limit assigned to the license.
7. **Address the business structure and required registrations.** Complete applicable business registration and entity requirements before licensing.
8. **Submit the completed application for Board review.** The Board reviews the application, examination results, financial information, experience, and other required materials before issuing a license.

## State Requirements

Contractor licensing in Tennessee is administered by the **Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors**. Tennessee requires a contractor license before contracting, bidding, offering to engage, or negotiating a price on projects that meet the state's applicable contractor licensing threshold.

The Board issues contractor licenses with both a **classification** and a **monetary limit**. The classification determines the type of work the contractor may perform, while the monetary limit determines the maximum project amount authorized under the license.

The BC-B Commercial Building classification applies to commercial construction and includes buildings for public use and occupancy, residential projects with more than four units, and buildings exceeding three stories. Tennessee accepts the NASCLA National Commercial examination for BC-B.

All new Tennessee contractor applicants must pass the Business and Law examination. A separate trade examination is required for classifications that carry a technical testing requirement. The same person does not have to take both examinations; Tennessee allows the applicant entity to designate the applicable Qualifying Agent for the required testing.

The Board also reviews financial information as part of the licensing process. Beginning July 1, 2026, Tennessee added a contractor surety bond option as an alternative to certain CPA-reviewed or CPA-audited financial statement requirements, subject to the Board's applicable rules.

Passing NASCLA does not automatically issue a Tennessee contractor license. Candidates must still complete Tennessee's Business and Law requirement and satisfy the application, classification, monetary limit, financial, business, and other licensing requirements established by the Board.
