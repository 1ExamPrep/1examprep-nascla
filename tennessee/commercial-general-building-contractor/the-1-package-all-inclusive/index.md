## Exam Details

Tennessee contractor licensing is administered by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. New contractor applicants must complete the **Tennessee Business and Law examination**, and a trade examination is required for classifications that carry a technical exam requirement.

Tennessee accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the **BC-B Commercial Building Contractor** classification. The BC-B classification covers commercial building construction, including buildings intended for public use and occupancy, residential construction involving more than four units, and buildings exceeding three stories.

The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination covers a broad range of construction subjects, including:

## Licensing Steps

Tennessee contractor licenses are issued to the contracting entity by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors. A company may designate a Qualifying Agent to complete the examinations required for the license.

1. **Determine the appropriate Tennessee classification.** Contractors pursuing commercial building work generally use the BC-B Commercial Building Contractor classification when that classification matches their intended scope.
2. **Identify the Qualifying Agent.** The QA is the person designated to complete the required examinations for the licensing entity. The Qualifying Agent may be an owner, officer, or full-time employee meeting Tennessee requirements.
3. **Pass the Tennessee Business and Law examination.** All new Tennessee contractor applicants must satisfy the Business and Law requirement.
4. **Satisfy the commercial building trade-exam requirement.** Tennessee accepts the NASCLA National Commercial examination for the BC-B classification.
5. **Prepare the required financial qualification.** Tennessee evaluates financial information when assigning the contractor's monetary limit.
6. **Establish the contracting entity.** The business must be properly organized and ready to apply under its legal business name.
7. **Complete the contractor license application.** The applicant submits its business information, examination documentation, classification request, financial qualification, and other required materials.
8. **Complete Board review.** The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors evaluates the applicant before issuing the classification and monetary limit approved for the business.

## State Requirements

A Tennessee contractor license is generally required before bidding, offering to perform, negotiating, or contracting for qualifying projects valued at **$25,000 or more**. This requirement applies to prime contractors as well as certain subcontractors and construction managers under Tennessee licensing law.

Tennessee contractor licenses contain both a **classification** and a **monetary limit**. The classification identifies the type of work the contractor may perform, while the monetary limit determines the maximum project value the contractor is authorized to undertake.

The **BC-B Commercial Building Contractor** classification covers commercial work involving buildings for public use and occupancy, residential buildings with more than four units, and buildings exceeding three stories. Tennessee accepts the NASCLA National Commercial examination for this classification.

The Board evaluates financial information when establishing the monetary limit. Tennessee has traditionally used CPA-reviewed or CPA-audited financial statements for financial qualification. Effective July 1, 2026, the Board also provides a Contractors' Surety Bond option as an alternative to a CPA-reviewed or audited financial statement. The qualifying surety bond must meet the Board's requirements and be at least 50% of the requested monetary limit.

Tennessee also evaluates contractor experience and equipment when determining qualifications. The Board indicates a preference for approximately three years of applicable experience when reviewing classifications.

The Qualifying Agent who takes the Business and Law examination does not have to be the same individual who completes the trade examination. Tennessee permits up to two people to take each required examination for the company under its qualifying-agent procedures.
