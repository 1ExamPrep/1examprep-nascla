## Licensing Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate Tennessee classification.** Candidates pursuing commercial building work should review the BC-B Commercial Building classification and its scope.
2. **Identify the Qualifying Agent.** Tennessee uses a Qualifying Agent to complete required examinations for the applicant entity. The QA may be an owner, officer, or full-time employee.
3. **Complete the Tennessee Business and Law examination.** New contractor applicants must satisfy this required state examination.
4. **Satisfy the trade examination requirement.** Tennessee accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the BC-B Commercial Building classification.
5. **Prepare the contractor license application.** The applicant entity submits the required licensing materials to the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.
6. **Provide applicable financial information.** Tennessee contractor licenses include a monetary limit tied to the applicant's financial qualification.
7. **Complete applicable business registration requirements.** The applicant entity must be properly structured and registered as required for its business type.
8. **Submit the completed application for review.** The Tennessee Board reviews the application, examination information, financial documentation, and other required materials before issuing a license.

## State Requirements

Contractor licensing in Tennessee is administered by the **Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors**. Tennessee requires a contractor license before contracting, bidding, offering to engage, or negotiating a price for projects that meet the state's applicable contractor licensing thresholds.

Each Tennessee contractor license includes both a classification and a monetary limit. The classification determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform, while the monetary limit determines the maximum project value the contractor may undertake.

The BC-B Commercial Building classification covers commercial building construction within the scope defined by the Tennessee Board. Tennessee specifically accepts the NASCLA National Commercial examination for the BC-B classification.

New Tennessee contractor license applicants are also required to pass the Tennessee Business and Law examination. The same person does not have to take both the Business and Law examination and the trade examination. Tennessee allows the applicant entity to designate the applicable Qualifying Agent for required testing.

Beginning July 1, 2026, Prov became the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors' provider for new contractor exam scheduling. Candidates who already had certain PSI appointments scheduled during the transition period could keep those appointments, but new scheduling beginning July 1 is handled through Prov.

Tennessee also evaluates financial qualifications when determining a contractor's monetary limit. Beginning July 1, 2026, the state provides a contractor surety bond option as an alternative to certain CPA-reviewed or CPA-audited financial statement requirements, subject to the Board's applicable rules.

Passing NASCLA does not automatically create a Tennessee contractor license. Candidates must still complete the applicable Tennessee Business and Law, application, financial, business, Qualifying Agent, and licensing requirements.
