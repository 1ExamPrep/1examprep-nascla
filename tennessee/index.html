Tennessee NASCLA Exam and Contractor License Guide

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 12 min read · 8 official sources cited

Tennessee is a NASCLA state. The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors accepts the NASCLA National Commercial exam as a trade exam, and NASCLA lists Tennessee for its electrical contractor and journeyman electrician exams as well. If you want one exam result you can carry into other participating states, the NASCLA route is worth a hard look.

This page covers when Tennessee requires a license, the classifications and which exam each needs, the open book Business and Law exam, the financial statement and monetary limit rules, published fees, the 2026 change in exam provider, and how to choose study materials. Does Tennessee accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors says it accepts the NASCLA National Commercial exam as a trade exam, and NASCLA lists Tennessee as a participating agency for the Commercial Building exam since March 2021. The Board's exam page describes the NASCLA program as available for Commercial General Building or Electrical candidates.

On the electrical side, NASCLA's agency list shows Tennessee administering and accepting the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam, and accepting the Journeyman Electrician exam as of March 2021.

What NASCLA replaces is the trade exam only. You still pass the Tennessee Business and Law exam, file the Board's application, provide a financial statement that sets your monetary limit, and show insurance. The Board also reminds candidates that you apply separately in each state that accepts NASCLA results.

One practical point: the PSI bulletin for Tennessee does not tie each NASCLA exam to a named Tennessee classification. For a commercial building license, the natural fit is the Commercial (BC-B) classification; for electrical, it is CE. Call the Board at the number on its site before you register so you are certain your NASCLA result covers the classification you plan to request.

Who issues contractor licenses in Tennessee

Contractor licenses come from the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, part of the Department of Commerce and Insurance. The same board oversees Limited Licensed Electricians, Limited Licensed Plumbers and home improvement licensing.

You need a contractor license before you bid, offer, or negotiate a price on a project of $25,000 or more, counting materials and labor. That applies to prime contractors and construction managers. Subcontractors doing electrical, mechanical, plumbing, HVAC or roofing work need a license when their portion reaches $25,000, and masonry subcontractors at $100,000.

Every license carries a classification and a monetary limit. You must bid and contract under the exact name on the license. The Board says new licenses typically take 4 to 6 weeks to issue once the file is complete.

Local governments add their own layer. You need a business tax license from the Department of Revenue for each city or county where you work, and electricians in particular should check whether a city requires its own electrical license for permits.

Tennessee contractor license types and which exam each needs

Every contractor applicant takes Business and Law. Some classifications also need a trade exam. These are the ones most NASCLA candidates care about:

  • Commercial (BC-B), Residential (BC-A), Industrial (BC-C) and combined building classifications: trade exam plus Business and Law. The NASCLA commercial exam is accepted as a trade exam; confirm which building classification it supports for you.
  • Res/SmCom (BC-A,b(sm)): a combined residential and small commercial classification for projects under $750,000.
  • Electrical (CE): Tennessee's full electrical contracting classification. You take a trade exam plus Business and Law. NASCLA lists Tennessee for the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam.
  • Mechanical (CMC), Plumbing (CMC-A), HVAC (CMC-C) and Fire Sprinkler (CMC-D): trade exams through the Board's provider. CMC and CMC-A need Board pre-approval for a $50 fee.
  • Limited Licensed Electrician (LLE): a separate credential for jobs under $25,000 in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections. Tennessee treats the LLE exam as distinct from journeyman and master exams, and trade exam waivers do not apply to it.
  • Restricted Residential Limited License: residential contractors can take an approved community college or vocational course instead of the BC-A trade exam and Business and Law, with jobs capped under $125,000.

How to get a Tennessee general contractor license, step by step

This is the path for a commercial building license using NASCLA. The CE electrical path is the same, swapping in the NASCLA electrical contractor exam.

  1. Pick your classification and target monetary limit, based on the work and the biggest single contract you plan to sign.
  2. Choose your Qualifying Agent. That person takes the exams for the company and can be an owner, full-time employee or officer. A non-owner needs the Board's Power of Attorney form.
  3. Apply to NASCLA at ned.nascla.org, get pre-approved, schedule, and pass the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam.
  4. Pass the Tennessee Business and Law exam with the Board's exam provider. Exams are generally valid for two years.
  5. Order a CPA or LPA financial statement in the exact business name being licensed, under 12 months old, reviewed or audited depending on your limit.
  6. Line up general liability and workers' compensation coverage (or proof of exemption), a Letter of Reference, the notarized Contractor's Affidavit, and Secretary of State registration if you are an entity.
  7. Submit the Initial New Contractor Application online at core.tn.gov with the $250 fee and your documents, attend a Board interview if one is requested, then wait for the license before you bid.

The NASCLA exam itself

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors has 115 scored questions, a 330 minute limit and a 70 percent passing standard, and may also include unscored pretest items. It is open book. You apply through NASCLA at ned.nascla.org and need pre-approval before you schedule. Topics run from general requirements and sitework through concrete, masonry, metals, wood, envelope, finishes, mechanical and electrical systems, with a large share on procurement and contracting.

The NASCLA electrical contractor exam has 100 questions with a 270 minute limit, and a printed diagram or blueprint packet is provided at the test site. PSI's Tennessee bulletin lists each NASCLA exam at $125. That is the published figure; check the current bulletin before paying.

Tennessee's own CE state electrical exam is a different test: 100 questions in 260 minutes with a 73 percent passing score, and its own reference list. Make sure you are preparing for the exam you actually registered for.

Book rules follow the testing vendor. PSI's Tennessee bulletin allows highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs, but temporary tabs like sticky notes must be removed, books cannot be written in, and loose papers are not allowed. References are not provided at the test center.

The Tennessee Business and Law exam

Every contractor applicant takes Business and Law, and a NASCLA pass does not waive it. It is open book. The PSI bulletin lists 50 questions, 140 minutes, and a passing score of 73 percent, which is 37 correct, with a published fee of $57. The reference is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Tennessee Edition, 4th Edition, which the Board points to on the NASCLA store.

Note the change in provider. The Board's exam page says Prov began administering its exams on July 1, 2026. PSI kept appointments already on its books through September 2026, and candidates holding a PSI date after July 1 could keep it or move to Prov. New candidates should schedule through Prov and read the Prov candidate bulletin for current fees, retake rules and book rules, since the figures above come from the PSI bulletin.

The same person does not have to take both exams. A company can have one person pass Business and Law and another pass the trade exam, and up to two people may take each exam for a company. Under the PSI rules, a candidate who failed could retest as early as two days later, depending on seat availability.

Tennessee financial, insurance and reference requirements

Tennessee sets your monetary limit from your finances rather than from fixed groups. The limit is 10 times the lesser of your working capital or net worth, plus the required experience. An unlimited license needs $300,000 in both. The limit applies per project, with a 10 percent tolerance, and splitting a job into phases to dodge it is not allowed.

The rules that trip people up:

  • The financial statement must come from a licensed, independent CPA or LPA, follow GAAP, be less than 12 months old at first application, and be in the exact name and form of the business being licensed.
  • A reviewed statement covers limits up to $3,000,000; above that, it must be audited.
  • You can add working capital with a line of credit or attach a guaranty agreement. A Board-format bond of $500,000, or $1,000,000 for an unlimited limit, is another option.
  • General liability insurance is required, with the amount tied to your monetary limit. Workers' compensation is required unless you prove an exemption.
  • A Letter of Reference from a past client, employer or code official who knows your construction work, not a family member, goes in with the application.
  • The Contractor's Affidavit must be notarized and signed by the Qualifying Agent and the owners and officers. Past complaints, convictions or unlicensed bidding trigger a Board interview.

Tennessee contractor license costs and fees

These are the published figures we found at the time of writing. Check the Board's requirements page and your exam provider's bulletin before you pay.

The Initial New Contractor Application fee is $250. Board pre-approval for the CMC and CMC-A exams costs $50. The PSI bulletin lists Business and Law at $57, the state trade exam portion at $57, and each NASCLA accredited exam at $125. Prov now runs the Board's exams, so confirm its current fees.

Plan for the costs outside the Board as well: the CPA or LPA review or audit of your financial statement, general liability and workers' compensation premiums, entity registration with the Secretary of State, local business tax licenses, and your study materials. For many applicants the financial statement and insurance end up costing more than the state fees. Exam fees under PSI were nonrefundable and lapsed if you did not test within a year of payment, so book your date only when you are ready.

Reciprocity and working in other states

Tennessee does not let you work here on another state's license. What it offers is a trade exam waiver. If you hold an active license in a participating state for a classification that required a trade exam, that state fills out the Board's License Verification form and you send it with your application. You still take Business and Law and meet every other requirement. Waivers apply to contractor licenses only, not home improvement, LLE or LLP.

The Board's published list includes Alabama, Arkansas (commercial and residential building), Georgia (electrical), Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina (building and electrical), Ohio (electrical, plumbing, HVAC), West Virginia, and the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board (commercial building, electrical, HVAC and plumbing). Coverage varies by classification, so read the list for yours.

The NASCLA route works the other way. Your passing score lives in NASCLA's National Examination Database, and you can send transcripts to any participating agency. That makes the NASCLA commercial exam useful if Tennessee is one stop among several.

After you pass: applying and keeping your Tennessee license

Keep an eye on the clock. Tennessee exam results are generally valid for two years, so file your application well inside that window. Once the Board has a complete file it typically issues the license in 4 to 6 weeks, and if your file has gaps or your affidavit raises an issue, you may be scheduled for a short informal interview with a Board member.

After you are licensed, bid only within your classification and monetary limit, and only under the exact name on your license. If you need a higher limit later, you will need updated financial documentation. Guaranty agreements and indemnities expire at renewal, and they cannot run longer than two years, so plan to refresh them.

The Board pages we reviewed did not spell out renewal fees or a continuing education rule for contractor licenses, so check with the Board when your renewal notice arrives. Keep your insurance active the whole time, since coverage is a condition of the license.

Tennessee NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does Tennessee accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors accepts the NASCLA National Commercial exam as a trade exam, and NASCLA lists Tennessee for its electrical contractor and journeyman electrician exams. You still take the Tennessee Business and Law exam and complete the full Board application.

When do I need a contractor license in Tennessee?

Before you bid or negotiate on a project of $25,000 or more, including materials and labor. Electrical, mechanical, plumbing, HVAC and roofing subcontractors need one when their portion reaches $25,000, and masonry at $100,000.

Is the Tennessee Business and Law exam open book?

Yes. It uses the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Tennessee 4th Edition. The PSI bulletin lists 50 questions in 140 minutes with 73 percent needed to pass.

Who gives the Tennessee contractor exams now?

The Board says Prov began administering its exams on July 1, 2026, with PSI honoring appointments already booked through September 2026. NASCLA exams are applied for through NASCLA itself.

How is my Tennessee monetary limit set?

It is 10 times the lower of your working capital or net worth, plus the required experience. An unlimited license needs $300,000 in both, and a reviewed CPA statement covers limits up to $3,000,000, with audited statements above that.

How much does a Tennessee contractor license cost?

The published application fee is $250. Exam fees are paid to the exam provider, and PSI's bulletin listed $57 for Business and Law and $125 for each NASCLA exam. Add the CPA statement, insurance and any local business tax licenses.

Can I use my South Carolina or other state license in Tennessee?

Not directly. Tennessee offers a trade exam waiver for active licenses from listed states, including the South Carolina Contractor's Licensing Board for commercial building, electrical, HVAC and plumbing. You still take Business and Law and meet all other requirements.

Is the Tennessee LLE the same as a journeyman license?

No. The Limited Licensed Electrician is a separate state credential for jobs under $25,000 in areas that use the state for permits or inspections, and Tennessee distinguishes its exam from journeyman and master exams. Check with your city, since some require their own electrical license.

Can I bring highlighted and tabbed books into the exam?

PSI's Tennessee rules allowed highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs, but not temporary tabs, writing, or loose papers. Our highlighted and tabbed packages use permanent tabs. Confirm the current Prov rules before test day.
