## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is designed around the competency expected of an individual performing electrical installation, maintenance, and repair work in accordance with applicable standards and codes. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis involving electrical subject matter experts and electricians from multiple jurisdictions.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains **100 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of **70**. The current candidate information published for the examination allows **300 minutes** for completion.

In addition to the scored questions, NASCLA may administer non-scored experimental questions. These questions are used for future examination development and are not included when calculating the candidate's final examination score.

The Journeyman Electrician examination content outline covers the following major subject areas:

- **Interpreting Plans and Specifications**
- **Basic Electrical Safety**
- **Electrical Theory and Principles**
- **Testing and Troubleshooting**
- **General Code Requirements**
- **Wiring and Protection**
- **Wiring Methods and Materials**
- **Equipment for General Use**
- **Special Occupancies and Special Equipment**
- **Special Conditions and Communication Systems**

The examination places substantial emphasis on code-based electrical work. Candidates should be prepared to locate and apply requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, special installations, and other National Electrical Code subjects.

Electrical theory, safety, plans, troubleshooting, and specialty systems remain important parts of preparation. A candidate who concentrates only on NEC lookup may leave significant areas of the examination insufficiently reviewed.

NASCLA examination applications are handled through the NASCLA National Examination Database. Once an application is approved, candidates receive examination scheduling information for the NASCLA testing program. NASCLA currently provides approved candidates with up to three examination attempts during the one-year eligibility period associated with an approved application.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**. Candidates may use authorized reference materials in accordance with NASCLA and testing-center rules.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for preparation. Candidates still need to identify the electrical concept involved, select the appropriate reference, locate the correct section or table, interpret the information, and apply it accurately within the available testing time.

The **2023 National Electrical Code** should become one of the most familiar references in your library. Practice using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapters, articles, tables, informational notes, and cross-references. Learn where commonly tested subjects are located rather than attempting to memorize every code number.

NASCLA testing rules permit qualifying reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs may be used under the published testing rules. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. References must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain additional loose or attached papers.

The reference books are not supplied at the examination center, so candidates using permitted books must bring their own approved materials. Preparing with the same references before testing helps build familiarity with each book's organization and reduces the need for random searching.

A useful practice technique is to read a question, identify the topic before opening a book, choose the reference most likely to contain the answer, and then locate the applicable information. Repeating that process develops both technical knowledge and practical reference-navigation skills.

## State Requirements

The **Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors** is the state agency responsible for contractor licenses and the Limited Licensed Electrician program. NASCLA currently lists the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors as an agency that accepts the Journeyman Electrician examination.

Tennessee's electrical licensing structure is different from states that issue a single statewide Journeyman Electrician license covering electrical work everywhere in the state. Candidates should distinguish between the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination and the particular Tennessee credential required for the work they plan to perform.

For larger contracting projects, Tennessee contractor licensing requirements may apply. A Tennessee contractor license is generally required before bidding, offering to perform, or contracting for projects of **$25,000 or more**. Electrical subcontractors are also subject to contractor licensing requirements when the electrical portion of the project meets the applicable threshold.

Tennessee's full electrical contractor classification is designated **CE – Electrical Contracting**. The CE classification covers electrical work for buildings and structures and requires a trade examination. Tennessee contractor applicants are also required to complete the state's Business and Law examination.

The **Limited Licensed Electrician, or LLE**, is a separate Tennessee credential. The LLE applies in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and covers qualifying electrical work costing less than $25,000 per project. Local governments may maintain their own licensing requirements, so the applicable local authority determines what credential is required in a particular area.

For this reason, a NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination result should not be confused with a Tennessee LLE license, a Tennessee CE contractor license, or a local journeyman credential. These are separate regulatory pathways.

Tennessee also requires contractors to hold the appropriate classification before performing work that falls within state contractor licensing requirements. The classification determines the type of contracting work the license authorizes, while the contractor license also carries a monetary limit.

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors changed its trade examination provider in 2026. Beginning **July 1, 2026**, Prov serves as the Board's new trade exam provider for Tennessee state contractor examinations. That state-provider change is separate from the national NASCLA Accredited Examination process.

Candidates planning to use NASCLA results in Tennessee should match the examination to the specific licensing or credentialing pathway they are pursuing and complete all remaining requirements imposed by the appropriate state or local authority.
