Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is designed around the knowledge and skills associated with journeyman-level electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work. NASCLA's electrical examination program was developed using occupational analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts.

The examination covers a broad range of electrical subjects rather than testing only National Electrical Code memorization. Candidates should be prepared to use code knowledge together with theory, calculations, troubleshooting skills, safety principles, plan interpretation, and practical trade understanding.

Major areas associated with NASCLA Journeyman Electrician preparation include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also understand how general NEC requirements interact with more specific rules for particular equipment and installation conditions.

Electrical theory remains an important part of journeyman-level preparation. Candidates should review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, loads, and common electrical calculations.

Testing and troubleshooting preparation should include the logical use of electrical test instruments, interpretation of voltage and continuity measurements, circuit behavior, and systematic methods for identifying electrical faults. Safety preparation should include construction safety, general-industry workplace requirements, and electrical-specific safe-work practices.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not by itself create a Tennessee electrical contractor license or Limited Licensed Electrician credential. Tennessee and applicable local authorities continue to determine the licensing requirements for electrical work performed within their jurisdictions.

Licensing Steps

Tennessee's electrical licensing structure includes state contractor licensing, the Limited Licensed Electrician program, and local licensing requirements. Candidates should determine which credential applies to the work they intend to perform before beginning the licensing process.

  1. Identify the required Tennessee or local electrical credential. Determine whether the work requires a Tennessee CE Electrical Contractor license, Limited Licensed Electrician credential, or another license issued by a local jurisdiction.
  2. Review the applicable licensing requirements. Tennessee contractor licensing and Limited Licensed Electrician licensing have separate requirements, and municipalities may maintain their own electrical licensing rules.
  3. Prepare for the appropriate trade examination. Study electrical code requirements, theory, calculations, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  4. Complete the applicable examination registration process. Follow the instructions for the examination and licensing pathway being pursued.
  5. Take the required examination. Follow current testing rules regarding identification, authorized books, permanent tabs, calculators, and prohibited materials.
  6. Complete the applicable licensing application. Passing a trade examination does not replace the separate application and qualification requirements established by Tennessee or a local jurisdiction.
  7. Obtain the applicable license or credential before performing regulated work. Electrical contracting and limited electrical work must be performed within the scope authorized by the applicable credential.
  8. Maintain the credential. Follow the renewal and continuing compliance requirements associated with the specific Tennessee or local license held.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors regulates contractors, Limited Licensed Electricians, Limited Licensed Plumbers, and other contractor-related programs. Tennessee's electrical licensing framework differs from states that issue one statewide Journeyman Electrician license covering all electrical work.

A Tennessee contractor license is generally required before bidding, offering to perform, or contracting for a project of $25,000 or more when the person or business is acting as a prime contractor, qualifying subcontractor, construction manager, or consultant. Electrical subcontractors are also subject to contractor licensing requirements when the electrical portion of a qualifying project reaches the applicable state threshold.

Tennessee's full electrical contractor classification is identified as CE – Electrical Contracting. The CE classification covers electrical work for buildings and structures and requires a trade examination. Tennessee contractor applicants are also required to complete the state's Business and Law examination.

Tennessee also offers a separate Limited Licensed Electrician, or LLE, credential. The LLE applies to electricians performing qualifying work in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and is limited to projects costing less than $25,000.

The LLE credential is not the same as a journeyman or master electrician examination. Tennessee's own licensing materials specifically distinguish the Limited Licensed Electrician examination from journeyman and master electrician credentials.

Local requirements also matter. Tennessee advises electricians to check with the local government where the work will be performed to determine whether that jurisdiction accepts the state LLE credential or requires a separate local electrical license for permits and inspections.

Electrical contractors should also distinguish the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination from Tennessee's state CE contractor trade examination. A trade-examination result does not by itself grant authority to contract for electrical work. The applicable Tennessee contractor license must be issued before performing work that requires state contractor licensure.

Beginning July 1, 2026, Prov became the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors' new trade examination provider. New Tennessee Board trade-exam scheduling beginning on that date is handled through Prov. This change applies to Tennessee Board examinations and should not be confused with the separate administration of NASCLA Accredited Examinations.

Tennessee's contractor examinations are open book. Contractor candidates are required to take the Business and Law examination, and trade examinations are required for specified classifications, including the full CE Electrical Contracting classification.

Because Tennessee uses several regulatory pathways for electrical work, candidates should match their examination preparation to the specific credential required for the type, value, and location of the work they intend to perform.
