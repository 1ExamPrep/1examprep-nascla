Candidates should be prepared to use code knowledge together with theory, calculations, troubleshooting skills, safety principles, plan interpretation, and practical trade understanding.

## State Requirements

The **Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors** regulates contractors, Limited Licensed Electricians, Limited Licensed Plumbers, and other contractor-related programs. Tennessee's electrical licensing framework differs from states that issue one statewide Journeyman Electrician license covering all electrical work.

A Tennessee contractor license is generally required before bidding, offering to perform, or contracting for a project of **$25,000 or more** when the person or business is acting as a prime contractor, qualifying subcontractor, construction manager, or consultant. Electrical subcontractors are also subject to contractor licensing requirements when the electrical portion of a qualifying project reaches the applicable state threshold.

Tennessee's full electrical contractor classification is identified as **CE – Electrical Contracting**. The CE classification covers electrical work for buildings and structures and requires a trade examination. Tennessee contractor applicants are also required to complete the state's Business and Law examination.

Tennessee also offers a separate **Limited Licensed Electrician, or LLE**, credential. The LLE applies to electricians performing qualifying work in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and is limited to projects costing less than $25,000.

The LLE credential is not the same as a journeyman or master electrician examination. Tennessee's own licensing materials specifically distinguish the Limited Licensed Electrician examination from journeyman and master electrician credentials.

Local requirements also matter. Tennessee advises electricians to check with the local government where the work will be performed to determine whether that jurisdiction accepts the state LLE credential or requires a separate local electrical license for permits and inspections.

Electrical contractors should also distinguish the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination from Tennessee's state CE contractor trade examination. A trade-examination result does not by itself grant authority to contract for electrical work. The applicable Tennessee contractor license must be issued before performing work that requires state contractor licensure.

Beginning **July 1, 2026**, Prov became the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors' new trade examination provider. New Tennessee Board trade-exam scheduling beginning on that date is handled through Prov. This change applies to Tennessee Board examinations and should not be confused with the separate administration of NASCLA Accredited Examinations.

Tennessee's contractor examinations are open book. Contractor candidates are required to take the Business and Law examination, and trade examinations are required for specified classifications, including the full CE Electrical Contracting classification.

Because Tennessee uses several regulatory pathways for electrical work, candidates should match their examination preparation to the specific credential required for the type, value, and location of the work they intend to perform.
