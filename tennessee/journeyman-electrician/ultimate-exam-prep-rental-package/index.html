Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program provides standardized electrical trade examinations that participating licensing agencies may administer or accept as part of their licensing procedures.

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors is a participating NASCLA electrical agency. NASCLA identifies Tennessee as accepting the Journeyman Electrician examination. Passing a NASCLA examination does not itself create a Tennessee license or authorize electrical work; applicants must still satisfy the requirements of the applicable state or local licensing authority.

Preparation for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination should include the major knowledge areas associated with professional electrical work, including:

  • Interpreting plans and specifications
  • Basic electrical safety
  • Electrical theory and principles
  • Testing and troubleshooting
  • General electrical code requirements
  • Wiring and protection
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Equipment for general use
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Special conditions and communication systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also develop confidence with electrical calculations, theory, safety, testing, troubleshooting, and plan interpretation.

Licensing Steps

Tennessee regulates electrical work through a combination of state contractor licensing, the Limited Licensed Electrician program, and local electrical licensing requirements. Candidates should identify the credential required for their intended scope and location of work before completing the licensing process.

  1. Identify the electrical credential needed. Determine whether your work requires a Tennessee CE Electrical Contractor license, a Limited Licensed Electrician license, or a locally issued journeyman, master, or other electrical credential.
  2. Review the applicable licensing requirements. State contractor licensing, LLE licensing, and local electrical licensing have different requirements and scopes.
  3. Determine how the NASCLA examination applies. Tennessee's Board for Licensing Contractors accepts the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination, but the applicable licensing authority determines how an examination result is used for the credential being pursued.
  4. Prepare for the examination. Study electrical code, theory, calculations, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, plans, and specialty electrical systems.
  5. Complete the applicable examination process. Follow the registration and testing requirements associated with the NASCLA examination and your licensing pathway.
  6. Submit the required licensing application. Provide the documentation and application materials required by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors or applicable local government.
  7. Complete additional requirements. Contractor applicants may have separate Business and Law, financial, qualifying-agent, and business licensing requirements.
  8. Obtain the required credential before contracting or performing regulated work. Work must remain within the scope authorized by the applicable license.

State Requirements

Electrical licensing in Tennessee exists at both state and local levels. The state does not rely on a single journeyman credential for every jurisdiction and every type of electrical project. Local governments may maintain their own electrical journeyman or master licensing requirements.

At the state contractor level, Tennessee uses the CE – Electrical Contracting classification. A Tennessee contractor license is generally required before bidding, offering to contract, or contracting as a prime contractor on projects valued at $25,000 or more. Electrical subcontractors contracting directly with a contractor are also subject to contractor licensing requirements when the electrical portion of the project is $25,000 or more, including labor, materials, and equipment.

The CE Electrical Contracting classification is comparable to a master-level electrical contractor credential and covers electrical work performed within the authorized contractor classification and monetary limit.

Tennessee also issues the Limited Licensed Electrician, or LLE, credential. The LLE applies to qualifying electrical work valued at less than $25,000 in municipalities that use the Tennessee Division of Fire Prevention for permits or inspections and recognize the LLE license.

Local requirements remain an important part of Tennessee electrical licensing. Many municipalities and counties administer their own electrical licensing or inspection programs and may require a local journeyman, master, or other electrical credential instead of accepting the state LLE license.

Tennessee directs electricians to check with the local government where work will be performed to determine which license is required for permits and inspections. Jurisdictions that do not participate in the State Electrical Program maintain their own permitting and inspection procedures.

The Tennessee LLE license has its own examination and application process and should not be confused with the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Tennessee specifically states that the LLE examination is not reciprocal with another state's electrical license examination.

Tennessee also accepts NASCLA electrical examinations through the Board for Licensing Contractors. NASCLA acceptance can satisfy an applicable trade examination component when used under the Board's licensing process, but an examination is not itself a license. Applicants must complete the remaining Tennessee requirements for the credential sought.

Contractor applicants must also consider Tennessee's Business and Law examination and the other requirements associated with contractor licensure. A contractor license is issued with both a classification and a monetary limit, which determines the scope and maximum project value authorized by the license.
