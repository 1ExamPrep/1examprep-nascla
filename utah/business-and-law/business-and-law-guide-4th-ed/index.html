Exam Details

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL), within the Utah Department of Commerce, regulates contractor licensing and establishes examination requirements for applicable construction classifications.

Prov, Inc. administers the Utah Contractor Business and Law examination.

Utah Contractor Business and Law Examination Information:

  • Examination: Utah Contractor Business and Law
  • Licensing Authority: Utah Division of Professional Licensing
  • Testing Provider: Prov, Inc.
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Approved Reference: NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Utah, 4th Edition

The Business and Law examination evaluates a candidate's understanding of important construction business and legal responsibilities.

Some classifications also involve additional technical examinations or professional qualifications. For example, the H100 HVAC Contractor classification requires both the Utah Business and Law examination and the applicable HVAC examination.

Licensing Steps

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing administers contractor licensing through a process involving classification selection, education, qualifications, examinations, business documentation, and licensing approval.

Step 1: Identify Your Contractor Classification

Determine which Utah contractor license classification corresponds to the construction services your business intends to provide.

Step 2: Review Qualification Requirements

Identify the experience, education, examination, and professional qualifications associated with your selected classification.

Step 3: Complete Applicable Pre-Licensure Education

Complete the approved contractor pre-licensure education required for the license classification, unless an applicable exemption or alternative qualification applies.

Step 4: Prepare for the Business and Law Examination

Study the NASCLA Utah 4th Edition, focusing on contractor licensing, estimating, contracts, project management, financial administration, and legal responsibilities.

Step 5: Complete Required Examinations

Register with Prov and complete the Utah Business and Law examination and any additional examination required for your classification.

Step 6: Register Your Business

Complete applicable Utah business entity registration and organizational requirements.

Step 7: Obtain Required Insurance

Arrange the liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage or waiver applicable to your business.

Step 8: Prepare Your Contractor Application

Gather examination results, education documentation, business information, and other required application materials.

Step 9: Submit the Licensing Application

Complete the application process through Utah DOPL and satisfy the remaining requirements for licensing approval.

Obtaining a contractor license involves meeting all applicable requirements for the requested classification, not simply completing an examination.

State Requirements

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing regulates contractor licensing under Utah's construction trades licensing laws and administrative rules.

The state recognizes multiple contractor classifications, including general building, residential and small commercial, general engineering, specialty, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC classifications.

Important Utah Contractor Licensing Requirements:

  • Appropriate Classification: Apply for the license corresponding to the construction work you intend to perform.
  • Pre-Licensure Education: Complete the applicable approved education requirement or qualify for an accepted alternative.
  • Business and Law Examination: Pass the examination when required for your classification.
  • Qualifying Experience: Meet applicable experience requirements for general contractor classifications.
  • Business Registration: Register the applicable business entity with the Utah Division of Corporations.
  • Federal Tax Identification: Provide applicable federal employer identification information.
  • Liability Insurance: Meet Utah's contractor insurance requirements.
  • Workers' Compensation: Obtain applicable coverage or an authorized waiver.
  • Additional Qualifications: Meet technical examination, certification, or professional licensing requirements associated with particular classifications.

General contractor applicants in the B100, E100, and R100 classifications generally must demonstrate at least two years, or 4,000 hours, of qualifying paid construction industry experience, unless an accepted alternative applies.

Utah's contractor pre-licensure education requirements generally include a 25-hour course for specialty contractors and a 30-hour program for general contractor classifications.

Applicable alternatives may include qualifying education, previous licensure, or other qualifications recognized by DOPL.

The Utah Business and Law examination is an important requirement for specified general and trade classifications. However, some applicants may satisfy examination requirements through recognized prior qualifications or licensing pathways.
