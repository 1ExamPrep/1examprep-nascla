## Licensing Steps

## State Requirements

The **Utah Division of Professional Licensing** regulates contractor licensing under Utah's construction trades licensing laws and administrative rules.

The state recognizes multiple contractor classifications, including general building, residential and small commercial, general engineering, specialty, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC classifications.

**Important Utah Contractor Licensing Requirements:**

- **Appropriate Classification:** Apply for the license corresponding to the construction work you intend to perform.
- **Pre-Licensure Education:** Complete the applicable approved education requirement or qualify for an accepted alternative.
- **Business and Law Examination:** Pass the examination when required for your classification.
- **Qualifying Experience:** Meet applicable experience requirements for general contractor classifications.
- **Business Registration:** Register the applicable business entity with the Utah Division of Corporations.
- **Federal Tax Identification:** Provide applicable federal employer identification information.
- **Liability Insurance:** Meet Utah's contractor insurance requirements.
- **Workers' Compensation:** Obtain applicable coverage or an authorized waiver.
- **Additional Qualifications:** Meet technical examination, certification, or professional licensing requirements associated with particular classifications.

General contractor applicants in the B100, E100, and R100 classifications generally must demonstrate at least two years, or 4,000 hours, of qualifying paid construction industry experience, unless an accepted alternative applies.

Utah's contractor pre-licensure education requirements generally include a 25-hour course for specialty contractors and a 30-hour program for general contractor classifications.

Applicable alternatives may include qualifying education, previous licensure, or other qualifications recognized by DOPL.

The Utah Business and Law examination is an important requirement for specified general and trade classifications. However, some applicants may satisfy examination requirements through recognized prior qualifications or licensing pathways.
