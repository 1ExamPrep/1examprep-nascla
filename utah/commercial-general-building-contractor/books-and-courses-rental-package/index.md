## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **Open Book Test**. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination according to the applicable testing requirements.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing. They must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing, while temporary tabs such as removable notes are not permitted.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books in this package provide an organized starting point for preparation. Candidates should still practice with the books regularly so they become familiar with chapter organization, indexes, tables of contents, technical standards, definitions, and code provisions.

A practical open-book method is to identify the construction subject first. Once you understand what the question is asking, select the reference most likely to contain the information, locate the relevant section, confirm the answer, and continue. This is usually more efficient than searching several books without first identifying the topic.

Build strong subject-to-reference associations during your study period. OSHA questions should lead you toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Accessibility questions should point toward ICC A117.1-2017. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19 or related concrete references, while broad commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

## State Requirements

Contractor licensing in Utah is administered by the **Utah Division of Professional Licensing** within the Department of Commerce. Utah licenses contractors by classification, which determines the construction work the license holder is authorized to perform.

The **B-100 General Building** classification is one of Utah's general contractor classifications. Contractors seeking a general classification must satisfy requirements applicable to the business and the qualifying individual before licensure.

Utah's current contractor application requires each qualifier to satisfy the applicable pre-licensure education requirement or meet one of the recognized alternatives. For a general classification, the standard education pathway includes a 25-hour pre-licensure course plus a 5-hour Business and Law course.

General classification qualifiers must also meet Utah's Business and Law examination requirement unless they qualify under an alternative recognized by the Division. The application additionally requires general classification qualifiers to demonstrate at least two years of paid construction-industry experience or satisfy another accepted qualification.

Utah's contractor application recognizes passing the NASCLA Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors as one of the accepted ways a general classification qualifier may satisfy the experience section of the application. NASCLA also lists the Utah Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing, Construction Trades Bureau as a participating agency in the Commercial Exam Program.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not automatically issue a Utah contractor license. NASCLA is an examination credential rather than a contractor license. Applicants must still satisfy Utah's applicable qualifier, education, Business and Law, application, classification, and other licensing requirements.
