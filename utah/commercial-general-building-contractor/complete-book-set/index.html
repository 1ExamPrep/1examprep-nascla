Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Utah is a participating jurisdiction through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing.

The examination is designed to evaluate knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects involving multiple construction trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than a contractor working in one specialty area.

The listed references address project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, scheduling, documentation, cost control, quality management, risk, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are represented by ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These references address structural concrete requirements, reinforcing-bar placement, field procedures, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, inspection, and quality control.

Specialty structural references cover precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Other books address masonry, gypsum systems, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation and finish systems, and building-service coordination.

Utah accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result as part of its contractor licensing framework. The examination result may also be used by other participating jurisdictions that accept the NASCLA examination.

After passing the examination, candidates may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to Utah or another participating licensing agency. The agency pairs the transcript with the candidate’s contractor license application.

A passing NASCLA result does not independently issue a Utah contractor license. Applicants must complete the state application process and satisfy the applicable business, ownership, experience, classification, insurance, financial-responsibility, and supporting-document requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for preparation. The NASCLA reference library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A practical open-book strategy begins with identifying the primary subject of a question. The candidate should then choose the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the relevant information.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while questions involving reinforcing-bar placement may require Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the Steel Deck Institute Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Questions involving wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing may require the BCSI guide.

Precast concrete erection questions may be addressed in the Erectors’ Manual. Unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, while excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may involve Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

Candidates should practice using the listed editions because code provisions, page numbers, technical requirements, illustrations, and publication organization may differ between editions. Working directly with the correct books helps build familiarity before examination day.

All books brought into the testing room must comply with current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Utah classification. Candidates seeking broad commercial building authority should review the B100 General Building Contractor classification and confirm that it matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Choose the business structure. Determine whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible business entity.
  3. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor business must identify the person whose experience and qualifications support the requested classification.
  4. Prepare for the Utah Business and Law examination. Study the Utah Contractors’ Guide to Business Law and Project Management and review state contractor responsibilities.
  5. Complete the Utah Business and Law requirement. Follow the current examination and application procedures established for Utah contractor applicants.
  6. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Study the technical construction subjects and practice locating information throughout the complete reference library.
  7. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the current NASCLA examination application and testing-registration process.
  8. Pass the NASCLA examination. The passing result may be transmitted to Utah through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  9. Document the applicable experience. Provide the employment and construction-experience records required for the requested Utah classification.
  10. Complete the contractor application. Submit the business, ownership, qualifying-individual, classification, and contact information requested by Utah.
  11. Provide supporting documents. Submit the applicable insurance, financial-responsibility, registration, and business records required for the license.
  12. Receive the license before contracting. The business must obtain the applicable Utah contractor license before performing work that requires state licensure.

State Requirements

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing regulates contractor licensing through its Construction Trades Bureau. Businesses performing work that requires a Utah contractor license must obtain the appropriate classification before offering or completing regulated contracting services.

Utah’s B100 General Building Contractor classification covers general building work within the scope established by the state’s contractor licensing laws and rules. Applicants should review the classification and confirm that it corresponds with the projects the company intends to undertake.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. The application must identify the company’s legal structure, ownership, qualifying individual, requested classification, and other information required by the Division.

Utah does not use the NASCLA examination as a mandatory state trade examination for every general building applicant. A passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result may be applied toward Utah’s experience-related licensing pathway under current state acceptance policies.

The NASCLA examination does not replace the Utah Business and Law requirement. Applicants should prepare from the Utah contractor business reference and complete the applicable state examination or approved requirement connected with their license application.

Contractor applicants must document the experience required for the requested classification. Records should accurately describe the qualifying individual’s work history and connection with the applicant business.

The application process may also include business registration, financial responsibility, insurance, ownership information, qualifying-individual records, and other supporting documents. Requirements may vary according to the classification and business structure.

The Utah business name should remain consistent across the contractor application, registration records, insurance materials, and other supporting documents. Incomplete or inconsistent information may delay the licensing review.

Passing an examination or purchasing this complete book set does not independently authorize contracting work. The applicant must complete the Utah licensing process and receive the appropriate contractor license before performing regulated construction services.
