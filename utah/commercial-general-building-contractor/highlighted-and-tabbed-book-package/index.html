Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Utah is a participating jurisdiction through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing.

The examination evaluates knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial construction projects involving multiple building trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than the work of a contractor operating in one specialty area.

Major subjects represented by this complete book set include project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, documentation, scheduling, cost control, quality management, risk, safety, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are distributed across ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These references address structural concrete requirements, reinforcing-bar placement, field procedures, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, inspection, and quality control from different perspectives.

Specialty structural references cover precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Other publications address masonry, gypsum construction, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, and building-service coordination.

Utah does not require a state trade examination for the general contractor classifications addressed by its current contractor licensing framework. Utah accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result toward the experience requirement connected with applicable contractor licensing.

After passing the examination, candidates may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to Utah. The transcript is paired with the contractor license application and supporting records submitted through the state licensing process.

A passing NASCLA result does not independently issue a Utah contractor license. Applicants must complete the Utah application process and satisfy the applicable business, ownership, qualifying-individual, experience, classification, insurance, financial-responsibility, and documentation requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for preparation. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A productive open-book method begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the permanent tabs, index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the applicable material.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Questions involving wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing may require the BCSI guide.

Precast erection questions may be located in the Erectors’ Manual, while unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, and excavation or underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may require Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

The highlighting and permanent tabs in this package support faster navigation, but candidates should practice with the prepared books before examination day. All references brought into the testing room must comply with current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid loose pages, removable notes, unauthorized inserts, or other prohibited materials.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Utah classification. Candidates seeking broad commercial building authority should review the B100 General Building Contractor classification and confirm that it matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Choose the business structure. Determine whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible business form.
  3. Identify the qualifying individual. The applicant business must identify the person whose experience and qualifications support the requested contractor classification.
  4. Prepare for Utah Business and Law. Study the Utah NASCLA Business Book and review contractor licensing, contracts, business management, insurance, employment, safety, and project-administration responsibilities.
  5. Complete the applicable Utah Business and Law requirement. Follow the current examination procedures established for Utah contractor applicants.
  6. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Study the technical construction subjects and practice locating information throughout the highlighted and tabbed reference set.
  7. Apply for the NASCLA examination. Complete the current NASCLA application and examination-registration process.
  8. Pass the NASCLA examination. A passing result may be used within Utah’s contractor experience pathway.
  9. Transmit the examination result. Send the official transcript to the Utah Division of Professional Licensing through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  10. Document the required experience. Provide the employment and construction-experience records required for the requested Utah classification.
  11. Complete the contractor application. Submit the business, ownership, qualifying-individual, classification, and contact information requested by Utah.
  12. Provide supporting records. Submit the applicable insurance, financial-responsibility, registration, and business documentation required for the license.
  13. Receive the license before contracting. The business must obtain the applicable Utah contractor license before performing work that requires state licensure.

State Requirements

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing regulates contractor licensing through its Construction Trades Bureau. Businesses performing work that requires a Utah contractor license must obtain the appropriate classification before offering or completing regulated contracting services.

The B100 General Building Contractor classification covers broad general building work within the scope established by Utah’s contractor licensing laws and rules. Applicants should review the official classification description and confirm that it corresponds with the projects the company intends to undertake.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. The application must identify the legal business structure, owners, qualifying individual, requested classification, and other information required by the Division.

Utah does not require trade examinations for its general contractor classifications under the current licensing framework. A passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result may be accepted toward the two-year experience requirement associated with applicable contractor licensing.

The NASCLA examination does not replace Utah’s separate Business and Law requirement. Applicants should prepare from the Utah contractor business reference and complete the applicable state-specific examination process.

Applicants who do not use a NASCLA result must document the qualifying experience required for the classification through the standard state pathway. Applicants using NASCLA must still provide the records and information requested during licensing review.

The Utah application process may include business registration, qualifying-individual records, financial-responsibility documentation, insurance information, ownership details, and other supporting materials. Requirements may differ according to classification and business structure.

The legal business name should remain consistent across the contractor application, registration records, insurance materials, and other supporting documents. Incomplete or inconsistent information may delay the licensing review.

Passing the NASCLA examination and purchasing this complete book set do not independently authorize contracting work. The business must complete the Utah licensing process and receive the appropriate contractor license before performing regulated construction services.
