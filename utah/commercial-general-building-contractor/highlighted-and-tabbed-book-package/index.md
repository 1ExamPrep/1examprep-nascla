## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Utah is a participating jurisdiction through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing.

The examination evaluates knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial construction projects involving multiple building trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than the work of a contractor operating in one specialty area.

Major subjects represented by this complete book set include project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, commercial building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, documentation, scheduling, cost control, quality management, risk, safety, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are distributed across ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These references address structural concrete requirements, reinforcing-bar placement, field procedures, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, inspection, and quality control from different perspectives.

Specialty structural references cover precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Other publications address masonry, gypsum construction, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, and building-service coordination.

Utah does not require a state trade examination for the general contractor classifications addressed by its current contractor licensing framework. Utah accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination result toward the experience requirement connected with applicable contractor licensing.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The examination center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for preparation. The NASCLA library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A productive open-book method begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the permanent tabs, index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the applicable material.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Questions involving wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing may require the BCSI guide.

Precast erection questions may be located in the Erectors’ Manual, while unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, and excavation or underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may involve 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may require Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.
