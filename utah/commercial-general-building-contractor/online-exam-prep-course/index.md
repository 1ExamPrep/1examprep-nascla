## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical examination designed for candidates pursuing commercial general building contractor classifications or similar licenses in participating jurisdictions.

The examination measures knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial building projects involving multiple construction trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a general building contractor rather than a contractor working within one specialty.

The current examination includes questions involving general requirements, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood, thermal and moisture protection, doors, windows and glazing, finishes, mechanical and plumbing systems, electrical systems, and procurement and contracting requirements.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project planning, estimating, scheduling, communication, documentation, contracts, cost control, quality management, safety, risk, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are represented by ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. Together, these references address structural requirements, reinforcing-bar placement, field preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, inspection, durability, and quality control.

Specialty structural references address precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Other publications cover masonry, excavation, gypsum construction, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation systems, and building-service coordination.

The International Building Code supports review of commercial building requirements involving occupancy, construction types, fire protection, means of egress, accessibility, structural provisions, and coordinated building systems. ICC A117.1 provides technical accessibility criteria for buildings and facilities.

Utah is a participating jurisdiction for the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination. A passing result may be applied toward Utah’s two-year contractor experience requirement under the state’s NASCLA acceptance policy.

After passing the examination, candidates can use the NASCLA National Examination Database to electronically send an official transcript to Utah or another participating jurisdiction. The receiving agency reviews the transcript with the candidate’s contractor license application.

A passing NASCLA result does not independently issue a Utah contractor license. Applicants must follow the licensing procedures established by the Utah Division of Professional Licensing and satisfy all requirements connected with the requested classification.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for focused preparation. The examination library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

Candidates who are unfamiliar with the references may spend too much time searching unrelated books. A practical open-book method begins with identifying the primary subject of the question and selecting the publication most likely to contain the answer.

After choosing a reference, the candidate should use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the supporting information. This process should be practiced repeatedly before examination day.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while questions involving reinforcing-bar placement may require Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the Steel Deck Institute Manual of Construction with Steel Deck. Questions involving steel joists and joist girders may require Technical Digest No. 9, while wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing questions may involve the BCSI guide.

Precast concrete erection questions may be addressed in the Erectors’ Manual. Unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, while excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may involve Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

The online exam prep course helps candidates practice connecting these subjects with the appropriate references. Students can develop a consistent process for recognizing the topic, selecting the correct publication, locating the supporting material, and evaluating the available answer choices.

All books brought into the examination room must comply with current testing rules. Candidates should avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## State Requirements

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing regulates contractor licensing through its Construction Trades Bureau. Businesses performing work that requires a Utah contractor license must obtain the appropriate classification before offering or completing regulated contracting services.

The B100 General Building Contractor classification covers broad general building work within the scope established by Utah contractor licensing laws and rules. Applicants should review the classification and confirm that it matches the projects the company intends to undertake.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity. The application must identify the legal business structure, owners, qualifying individual, requested classification, and other information required by the Utah Division of Professional Licensing.

Utah does not require a state trade examination for the general contractor classifications covered by its current licensing framework. However, Utah accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors toward the two-year experience requirement.

The NASCLA examination does not replace Utah’s complete contractor application. Applicants must still satisfy the applicable business registration, financial-responsibility, insurance, ownership, classification, and supporting-document requirements.

The legal business name should remain consistent throughout the contractor application, registration records, insurance materials, and other supporting documents. Missing information or inconsistent business records may delay the licensing review.

Applicants should provide accurate qualifying-individual information and clearly identify the classification requested. The state reviews the complete application before issuing a contractor license.

Passing the NASCLA examination or completing this online course does not independently authorize contracting work. The business must receive the appropriate Utah contractor license before offering or performing services that require state licensure.
