## Exam Details

Utah contractor licensing is administered by the Utah Department of Commerce, Division of Professional Licensing. The state's general contractor classifications include the **B100 General Building Contractor** classification.

For general-classification applicants, Utah recognizes several ways a qualifier may demonstrate the required qualification. One accepted option is passing the **NASCLA Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors**. Utah's contractor application specifically identifies the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as an accepted qualification method for general-classification applicants.

The NASCLA examination covers commercial construction topics across a broad range of disciplines, including:

- Planning and estimating
- Financial and project management
- Construction safety
- Site construction
- Concrete construction
- Masonry
- Metals
- Wood foundations and framing
- Thermal and moisture protection
- Doors, windows, and glazing
- Finishes
- Specialty equipment and related commercial construction subjects

Utah also requires general-classification applicants such as B100 applicants to meet the state's **Business and Law examination requirement**. The Utah Business and Law examination currently uses the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Utah, 4th Edition as an approved reference.

The Utah Business and Law examination contains **60 questions**, provides **2 hours** for completion, and requires a **70% passing score**. Exam subjects include business organization, licensing, estimating and bidding, contract management, project management, labor law, lien law, financial management, risk management, tax law, and safety.

Candidates should prepare for the NASCLA construction examination and Utah Business and Law examination as separate but complementary components. NASCLA emphasizes commercial construction knowledge and technical references, while Utah Business and Law concentrates on the business and regulatory knowledge required to operate a contracting company in Utah.

## Licensing Steps

Utah contractor licenses are issued through the Division of Professional Licensing. Candidates pursuing a B100 General Building Contractor license must complete the requirements applicable to both the qualifying individual and the contractor business.

1. **Select the B100 General Building Contractor classification.** Applicants should confirm that the B100 classification matches the scope of construction work the business intends to perform.
2. **Identify the qualifier.** Utah contractor businesses designate a qualifier who meets the requirements associated with the requested general classification.
3. **Meet the general-classification qualification requirement.** Passing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination is one method Utah recognizes for a general-classification qualifier.
4. **Complete Utah prelicensure education.** Initial B100 applicants must complete the required contractor prelicensure education, including the general prelicensure course and the Business and Law course applicable to B100 applicants.
5. **Satisfy the Utah Business and Law exam requirement.** General-classification applicants must provide documentation demonstrating completion of the required Utah Business and Law examination.
6. **Establish the contracting business.** The applicant must organize the business entity and provide the ownership, officer, director, and related entity information required by DOPL.
7. **Obtain general liability insurance.** Utah requires active general liability insurance meeting the state's coverage requirements.
8. **Obtain workers' compensation coverage when applicable.** Businesses with employees must provide the required workers' compensation documentation.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** Applicants provide the required business, qualifier, classification, education, examination, insurance, and supporting information.

## State Requirements

The Utah **B100 General Building Contractor** classification is one of Utah's primary general contractor classifications. It is distinct from the R100 Residential/Small Commercial Contractor classification and the E100 General Engineering Contractor classification.

A qualifier for a general classification generally must demonstrate at least **two years of paid construction-industry experience**, equivalent to at least 4,000 hours, or satisfy another qualification standard recognized by Utah law and DOPL. Utah's application also recognizes passing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as one of the qualifying methods available to general-classification applicants.

Initial B100 applicants must complete Utah's required prelicensure education. The current DOPL application identifies a **25-hour prelicensure course** for general-classification applicants and an additional **5-hour Business and Law course** for applicants seeking classifications including B100.

Utah also requires documentation that the Business and Law examination requirement has been satisfied. The Utah Business and Law reference provided with this package supports preparation for that portion of the licensing process.

Contractor applicants must maintain general liability insurance. Utah licensing materials require coverage of at least **$100,000 for each incident and $300,000 in total**. Businesses with employees must also maintain workers' compensation insurance as required.

Utah's contractor application requires detailed information about the licensing entity, including owners, officers, directors, parent companies, subsidiaries, and qualifying individuals when applicable. Business Formation assistance included with The 1 Package can help establish an LLC or corporation, while EIN Filing with the IRS supports business banking, taxes, hiring employees, and professional operation.
