## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an **Open Book Test**. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination according to the applicable testing requirements.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the reference library. Having many books available does not automatically make the examination easier. Candidates who search several unfamiliar references for every question can lose significant testing time.

The books included in this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These navigation aids provide an organized starting point, but candidates should still work directly with the references throughout their study period. Become familiar with tables of contents, indexes, chapter structures, technical tables, definitions, standards, and code sections.

A practical strategy is to identify the construction subject first, select the most relevant reference, locate the specific information needed, confirm the answer, and continue. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1-2017. OSHA safety questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while broader building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

Reference materials used for NASCLA open-book testing may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Candidates should follow the testing provider's rules regarding markings, tabs, and permitted materials when preparing their books for test day.

## State Requirements

Contractor licensing in Utah is administered by the **Utah Division of Professional Licensing** within the Utah Department of Commerce. Utah licenses contractors by classification and requires both the contractor business and its qualifying individual to satisfy applicable licensing requirements.

The **B-100 General Building** classification is one of Utah's general contractor classifications. Applicants pursuing B-100 or another general classification must complete the requirements associated with that classification before licensure.

Utah currently does not require a trade examination for general contractor classifications. Instead, the state evaluates education, Business and Law requirements, experience or accepted alternatives, business qualifications, and other licensing documentation.

For general classification applicants, Utah's standard education pathway includes a 25-hour pre-licensure course. Applicants for classifications including B-100 must also complete a 5-hour Business and Law course unless an applicable alternative qualification is accepted.

The qualifier must also satisfy Utah's Business and Law examination requirement or an accepted alternative. General classification applicants must document the required paid construction experience or meet another qualification recognized by the state.

Utah specifically recognizes a passing score on the **NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors** as one way to satisfy the two-year experience requirement for a general classification qualifier. This is an important distinction: NASCLA is not functioning as a required Utah trade exam. Instead, it can serve as an accepted alternative for the experience requirement.

Passing NASCLA does not automatically create a Utah contractor license. Applicants must still complete Utah's applicable education, Business and Law, qualifier, application, insurance, business, and classification requirements.
