Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. The Virginia Board for Contractors participates in the NASCLA program, with the NASCLA commercial examination recognized as comparable to the Virginia Commercial Building classification.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, requiring 81 correct answers
  • Virginia Participation: Virginia Board for Contractors accepts the NASCLA commercial examination for the comparable Commercial Building classification.

The current NASCLA content outline includes General Requirements, Site Construction, Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Wood, Thermal and Moisture Protection, Doors, Windows and Glazing, Finishes, Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, Electrical Systems, and Procurement and Contracting Requirements.

General Requirements and Procurement and Contracting Requirements represent substantial portions of the examination, while the remaining questions cover technical construction subjects. Candidates should therefore prepare for both field-oriented trade knowledge and the administrative responsibilities associated with managing commercial construction projects.

The exam's 115-question format also makes pacing important. Candidates should practice recognizing which questions can be answered from construction knowledge and which require a reference lookup. Developing this judgment before test day can help protect valuable examination time.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an Open Book Test. Approved references may be used during the examination according to the applicable NASCLA and testing-provider rules.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the reference materials. Having a large library available does not remove the need to study. Candidates who are unfamiliar with the books may spend too much time deciding which reference to use or searching for a specific provision.

The rental books in this package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These features provide an organized starting point, but candidates should still work with the books regularly. Learn how each table of contents is structured, how the indexes are organized, where important technical tables appear, and what terminology is used throughout the reference.

A practical exam strategy is to identify the subject first, select the appropriate reference, locate the relevant information, confirm the answer, and continue. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1-2017. OSHA questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while broad commercial building questions may require the International Building Code.

The goal of reference preparation is not to memorize every page. It is to develop enough familiarity that you know where to begin when a technical lookup is necessary.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia licenses contractor businesses as Class A, Class B, or Class C depending on the applicable licensing requirements and scope of contracting activity.
  2. Select the applicable classification or specialty. The business must identify the classification or specialty that corresponds with the construction work it intends to perform.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. Every licensed contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the license.
  4. Meet the Qualified Individual experience requirement. The QI must have the amount of experience required for the classification or specialty being requested.
  5. Satisfy the applicable trade examination requirement. Virginia accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors for the comparable Commercial Building classification.
  6. Identify the Designated Employee for Class A or Class B. Class A and Class B applicants must designate an eligible individual who satisfies Virginia's business examination requirements.
  7. Complete the Virginia Designated Employee examination when required. Class B requires the Virginia and General portions, while Class A also includes the Advanced portion.
  8. Complete the required pre-license education. A Designated Employee or member of Responsible Management for a new contractor business must complete the Board-approved eight-hour pre-license business course.
  9. Submit the Virginia contractor license application. Complete the application and provide the required business, qualifier, examination, and supporting information to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in Virginia is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities rather than simply to the individual who takes an examination.

Every licensed contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the license. The QI is responsible for meeting the experience and, where applicable, examination requirements associated with that classification or specialty. The QI must be a full-time employee or a member of Responsible Management of the applicant business.

Virginia also uses a Designated Employee requirement for Class A and Class B contractor applicants. The Designated Employee must be a full-time employee or member of Responsible Management, must be at least 18 years old, and must complete the required business examination.

The Class B Designated Employee examination consists of a Virginia portion addressing Virginia contractor laws and regulations and a General portion addressing general administrative and business knowledge. The Class A examination includes those two portions plus an Advanced portion covering additional business and administrative knowledge associated with Class A contracting.

New contractor businesses must also satisfy Virginia's pre-license education requirement. A Designated Employee or member of Responsible Management must successfully complete an eight-hour business course approved by the Board for Contractors.

Virginia's examination vendor is PSI for state contractor examinations. Candidates must successfully complete required examinations before applying for licensure.

Passing the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. NASCLA can satisfy the applicable trade examination component for the comparable Commercial Building classification, while the applicant must still meet Virginia's business examination, education, Qualified Individual, Designated Employee, license-class, business, and application requirements.
