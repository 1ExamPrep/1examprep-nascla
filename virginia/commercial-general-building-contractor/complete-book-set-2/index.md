## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination accepted by participating contractor licensing agencies. Virginia accepts the examination for use with the Commercial Building Contractor classification.

The examination evaluates knowledge used to plan, supervise, coordinate, and complete commercial construction projects involving multiple building trades. Its broad scope reflects the responsibilities of a commercial general building contractor rather than the work of a contractor operating within one specialty.

Major subject areas represented by this complete book set include project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, safety, structural concrete, reinforcing steel, precast construction, post-tensioning, steel decking, steel joists, wood trusses, masonry, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, building codes, environmental controls, exterior systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, documentation, cost control, quality management, safety, risk, scheduling, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are distributed across ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. These references address structural requirements, reinforcing-bar placement, concrete preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, inspection, and quality control from different perspectives.

Specialty structural references cover precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Additional publications address masonry, gypsum systems, excavation, carpentry, roofing, accessibility, stormwater planning, exterior insulation and finish systems, and building-service coordination.

After passing the NASCLA examination, candidates may use the NASCLA National Examination Database to send an official transcript to the Virginia Board for Contractors. The Board reviews the transcript as part of the contractor licensing application.

A passing NASCLA result addresses the accepted technical examination component. It does not independently issue a Virginia contractor license or replace the state’s pre-license education, business examination, designated employee, qualified individual, experience, financial, classification, and application requirements.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued in Class A, Class B, and Class C. The license class determines the applicable project and financial limitations, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the licensed business may perform.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification applies to qualifying construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition work involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures. Applicants should review the official classification scope before selecting it for the license application.

## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical library.

Open-book testing allows candidates to consult approved publications, but it does not eliminate the need for focused preparation. The examination library contains thousands of pages across construction textbooks, codes, standards, manuals, and specialty technical guides.

A productive open-book strategy begins with identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then choose the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code sections, or technical tables to locate the relevant information.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while steel-joist and joist-girder questions may involve Technical Digest No. 9. Questions involving wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing may require the BCSI guide.

Precast concrete erection questions may be addressed in the Erectors’ Manual. Unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, while excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Federal construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may involve Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

Candidates should practice using the listed editions because code provisions, technical requirements, page numbers, illustrations, and publication organization may differ between editions. Working directly with the correct references helps build familiarity before examination day.

All materials brought into the testing room must comply with current examination rules. Candidates should review the applicable candidate bulletin and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, unapproved inserts, or other prohibited materials.

## Licensing Steps

1. **Select the appropriate Virginia license class.** Review the differences among Class A, Class B, and Class C and choose the class that matches the business’s intended contracting activity.
2. **Select the Commercial Building Contractor classification.** Confirm that the CBC classification corresponds with the commercial construction work the company intends to perform.
3. **Choose the business structure.** Determine whether the contractor will operate as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible entity.
4. **Identify responsible management.** List the owners, officers, managers, members, or other individuals who meet Virginia’s definition of responsible management for the business.
5. **Complete pre-license education.** A member of responsible management or another eligible individual must complete the Board-approved contractor pre-license education requirement.
6. **Identify the designated employee when required.** Class A and Class B applicants must designate the individual responsible for completing the applicable Virginia contractor business examinations.
7. **Identify the qualified individual.** The business must designate a qualified individual for the Commercial Building Contractor classification.
8. **Prepare for the NASCLA examination.** Study the technical construction subjects and practice locating information throughout the complete reference library.
9. **Apply for and pass the NASCLA examination.** Complete the current NASCLA application and testing process for the Commercial General Building Contractor examination.
10. **Transmit the examination result.** Send the official NASCLA transcript to the Virginia Board for Contractors through the National Examination Database.
11. **Complete the Virginia business examination requirement.** The designated employee for an applicable Class A or Class B application must complete the required portions of the Virginia contractor examination.
12. **Submit the contractor license application.** Provide the requested business, ownership, class, classification, designated employee, qualified individual, financial, and supporting information.

## State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors, operating through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, licenses contractor businesses and regulates contracting classifications and specialties within the Commonwealth.

Virginia contractor licenses contain two primary elements: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the applicable limits on contract and project activity, while the classification determines the type of work the contractor may perform.

Class A, Class B, and Class C licenses have different application, financial, examination, and experience requirements. Applicants should select the class that accurately represents the size and nature of the projects the company intends to undertake.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification covers qualifying work involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures. The classification does not automatically authorize every regulated specialty trade, so contractors should review the official scope carefully.

Every new contractor applicant must complete the Board-approved pre-license education requirement. The course covers contractor laws, regulations, and basic business responsibilities and is separate from the technical classification examination.

Class A and Class B applicants must identify a designated employee who completes the applicable contractor business examination. Class C applicants follow the examination requirements assigned to their selected classification or specialty.

The business must also identify a qualified individual for every classification or specialty shown on the license. The qualified individual must satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements and remain properly associated with the licensed entity.

Virginia requires different minimum experience periods for qualified individuals based on the license class. Applicants should submit complete experience information related to the classification requested.

Virginia accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination as comparable to the Commercial Building Contractor classification. The NASCLA transcript supports the technical qualification but does not replace the remaining Virginia licensing requirements.

The application process may include business-entity records, financial documentation, responsible-management information, designated-employee records, qualified-individual information, examination results, fees, and other supporting materials requested by the Board.

Passing the examination or purchasing this complete book set does not independently authorize contracting work. The business must complete the Virginia licensing process and receive the appropriate license before performing work that requires contractor licensure.
