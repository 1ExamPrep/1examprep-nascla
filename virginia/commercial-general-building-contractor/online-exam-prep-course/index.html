Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized technical trade examination used by participating contractor licensing agencies. It evaluates knowledge involved in planning, supervising, coordinating, and completing commercial building projects that include multiple construction trades.

The examination has a broader scope than a single-trade test. Candidates should be prepared to review project management, jobsite operations, estimating, scheduling, contracts, structural systems, safety, accessibility, building codes, site construction, exterior systems, finishes, and building-service coordination.

Construction Jobsite Management and Construction Project Management support preparation involving field supervision, project organization, communication, documentation, scheduling, estimating, cost control, quality management, risk, safety, and project closeout.

Concrete subjects are represented by ACI 318-19, Placing Reinforcing Bars, and The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. Together, these references address structural concrete requirements, reinforcement, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, inspection, and quality control.

Specialty structural references address precast concrete erection, unbonded post-tensioning, steel deck installation, steel joist handling, and metal plate connected wood-truss restraint and bracing. Candidates may also encounter subjects involving excavation, masonry, carpentry, gypsum construction, roofing, accessibility, stormwater controls, exterior insulation systems, and mechanical and electrical coordination.

The International Building Code supports review of commercial building requirements involving occupancy, construction types, fire protection, means of egress, accessibility, structural provisions, and coordinated building systems. ICC A117.1 provides technical criteria for accessible and usable buildings and facilities.

Federal construction-safety material is addressed through 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates may use this reference when reviewing fall protection, excavation, scaffolding, electrical safety, equipment, personal protective measures, and other construction-site requirements.

Virginia contractor licenses are divided into Class A, Class B, and Class C. The class determines the level of contracting activity permitted under the license, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of construction work the business is authorized to perform.

The Virginia Commercial Building Contractor classification applies to qualifying commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and related building work. Applicants should select the license class and classification that correspond with the business’s intended contracting activity.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not complete the entire Virginia licensing process. The contractor business must satisfy the applicable requirements of the Virginia Board for Contractors, including the requirements related to responsible management, pre-license education, business examinations, the designated employee, the qualified individual, experience, financial responsibility, and the license application.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for obtaining and bringing permitted reference materials to the testing location. The testing center does not provide the complete technical reference library.

Open-book testing allows approved publications to be used during the examination, but it does not remove the need for technical study. The reference library contains thousands of pages, and searching every book for every question is not an effective testing strategy.

A productive reference-navigation process begins by identifying the primary subject of the question. The candidate should then select the publication most likely to contain the answer and use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, diagrams, code provisions, or technical tables to find the relevant information.

Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while reinforcing-bar placement questions may involve Placing Reinforcing Bars. Concrete field-practice questions involving preparation, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, or quality control may be addressed in The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

Steel-deck questions may require the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck. Questions involving steel joists and joist girders may require Technical Digest No. 9, while wood-truss handling, installation, restraint, and bracing questions may involve the BCSI guide.

Precast concrete erection questions may be addressed in the Erectors’ Manual. Unbonded post-tensioning questions may require the Level 1 Field Fundamentals publication. Masonry questions may involve Modern Masonry, while excavation and underground utility questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting.

Accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1, while broader commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code. Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and stormwater-planning questions may involve Developing Your Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan.

The course helps candidates practice making these connections before examination day. Students can work on recognizing key terms, selecting the appropriate publication, locating the supporting information, and evaluating the available answer choices.

All materials brought into the testing room must comply with the current examination rules. Candidates should use the approved editions and avoid unauthorized loose pages, removable notes, inserts, or other prohibited materials.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate Virginia license class. Review the requirements for Class A, Class B, and Class C and choose the class that matches the business’s intended level of contracting activity.
  2. Select the Commercial Building Contractor classification. Confirm that the classification corresponds with the commercial construction work the company intends to perform.
  3. Establish the business entity. Organize the contractor as a sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or another eligible business form.
  4. Identify responsible management. List the owners, officers, managers, members, partners, or other individuals who meet Virginia’s responsible-management requirements.
  5. Complete contractor pre-license education. An eligible member of the applicant organization must complete the Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
  6. Identify the designated employee when required. Class A and Class B applicants must designate the individual responsible for satisfying the applicable Virginia contractor business-examination requirement.
  7. Identify the qualified individual. The business must designate a qualified individual whose experience and examination credentials support the Commercial Building Contractor classification.
  8. Prepare for the NASCLA examination. Use the online course with the listed references to review commercial construction topics and practice open-book navigation.
  9. Apply for and take the NASCLA examination. Complete the current application, approval, registration, and scheduling procedures.
  10. Transmit the examination result. Follow the applicable process for providing the official NASCLA result to the Virginia Board for Contractors.
  11. Complete the Virginia business requirements. Satisfy the business-examination and application requirements associated with the selected license class.
  12. Submit the contractor license application. Provide the requested business, ownership, classification, financial, examination, experience, and organizational information.
  13. Receive the license before contracting. The business must obtain the appropriate Virginia contractor license before performing work that requires state licensure.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses and regulates contractor classifications and specialties within the Commonwealth. The license belongs to the business entity rather than solely to the individual who passes an examination.

A Virginia contractor license includes both a class and a classification or specialty. The class establishes the permitted level of contracting activity, while the classification identifies the type of work the business may perform.

Class A, Class B, and Class C applicants have different application, financial, experience, and examination requirements. Candidates should select the class that accurately reflects the scope and size of the company’s intended projects.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification applies to qualifying construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and related buildings or structures. This classification does not automatically authorize every separately regulated specialty trade.

Virginia requires contractor pre-license education for new license applicants. This education addresses contractor regulations and business responsibilities and is separate from the technical preparation provided by this NASCLA online course.

Class A and Class B applicants must identify a designated employee who satisfies the applicable Virginia business-examination requirements. The designated employee must remain properly associated with the licensed business.

The applicant must also identify a qualified individual for the Commercial Building Contractor classification. The qualified individual is the person whose experience and examination qualifications support the classification shown on the license.

Experience requirements vary according to the license class. The applicant should provide complete and accurate records describing the qualified individual’s relevant construction experience.

The licensing application may require organizational records, financial information, responsible-management details, designated-employee information, qualified-individual information, examination records, and other supporting documentation.

Passing the NASCLA examination or completing this course does not independently authorize construction work. The business must complete the Virginia licensing process and receive the appropriate contractor license before performing regulated contracting services.
