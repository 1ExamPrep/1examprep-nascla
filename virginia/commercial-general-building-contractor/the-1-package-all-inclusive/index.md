## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating state agencies, including the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination provides a way for qualifying contractors to satisfy an accepted commercial trade-examination requirement while still completing Virginia's state-specific licensing process.

The NASCLA examination covers broad commercial construction knowledge, including planning and estimating, project management, construction safety, site construction, concrete, masonry, metals, wood construction, thermal and moisture protection, doors and glazing, finishes, and other commercial building subjects.

Virginia also has separate business examination requirements for Class A and Class B contractor firms. A Virginia **Class B** applicant must designate an employee who has successfully completed the **General and Virginia portions** of the contractor business examination. A **Class A** applicant must designate an employee who has successfully completed the **Advanced, General, and Virginia portions**.

The Designated Employee must be either a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Virginia uses these business examination portions to evaluate knowledge of contractor laws, regulations, general administration, and business practices appropriate to the license class.

Virginia also requires successful completion of applicable examinations before the contractor license application is submitted. Candidates should therefore coordinate NASCLA trade preparation with the Virginia business examination requirements that apply to the class of license being pursued.

## Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors rather than to individuals acting independently from the licensed business. Applicants must select the appropriate license class and classification and identify the individuals who satisfy Virginia's management and qualification requirements.

1. **Choose the appropriate license class.** The business selects Class A, Class B, or Class C according to the monetary level of projects it intends to perform.
2. **Select the appropriate classification.** Commercial building contractors may pursue the CBC classification when it matches the scope of work the company intends to perform.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** A Qualified Individual must be designated for each classification or specialty and meet Virginia's applicable experience and examination requirements.
4. **Identify a Designated Employee for Class A or B.** Class A and Class B businesses must designate an individual who satisfies Virginia's contractor business examination requirements.
5. **Complete pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee.
6. **Satisfy the trade-examination requirement.** Virginia participates in the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor examination program for accepted commercial trade qualification.
7. **Complete the required Virginia business examinations.** Class B requires the General and Virginia portions, while Class A requires the Advanced, General, and Virginia portions.
8. **Meet financial requirements.** Class A and Class B applicants must document the applicable net worth or equity requirement or use an accepted surety bond option.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business provides the required ownership, Responsible Management, Designated Employee, Qualified Individual, financial, examination, and classification information.

## State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses are divided into **Class A, Class B, and Class C**. The class determines the monetary size of work the business may undertake.

**Class A** applies when a single contract or project is valued at **$150,000 or more**, or when the total value of construction undertaken during a 12-month period is **$1 million or more**.

**Class B** applies when a single contract or project is valued at **$30,000 or more but less than $150,000**, or when the total value of construction during a 12-month period is **$250,000 or more but less than $1 million**.

**Class C** applies to covered work valued at **more than $1,000 but less than $30,000** for a single contract or project and within the applicable annual limitation established by Virginia law.

Class A and Class B applicants must demonstrate financial responsibility. A Class A applicant must document at least **$45,000 in net worth or equity**, while a Class B applicant must document at least **$15,000**. Virginia permits applicants to satisfy the financial verification requirement using the applicable financial statement or an accepted surety bond option.

The Qualified Individual must meet the minimum experience requirement for the license class. Virginia currently requires at least **five years of experience for Class A**, **three years for Class B**, and **one year for Class C**. The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee working at least 30 hours per week or a member of Responsible Management.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor applicants. This education is separate from the NASCLA commercial trade examination and forms part of Virginia's business licensing process.
