Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA developed its electrical examination program through national occupational analysis and input from electrical subject matter experts to represent the knowledge and skills used by electricians across multiple jurisdictions.

The Journeyman Electrician examination focuses on technical knowledge associated with electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, repair, safety, and code compliance. Candidates should prepare for questions that require National Electrical Code navigation as well as questions involving electrical theory, calculations, safety practices, equipment, troubleshooting, plans, and specialty systems.

Major areas of preparation for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination include:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications
  • Basic Electrical Safety
  • Electrical Theory and Principles
  • Testing and Troubleshooting
  • General Code Requirements
  • Wiring and Protection
  • Wiring Methods and Materials
  • Equipment for General Use
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, electrical equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems. Candidates should also be prepared to work with calculations, theory concepts, safety requirements, test instruments, and practical troubleshooting situations.

Virginia's own tradesman licensing system requires Board pre-approval before candidates sit for Virginia-administered tradesman examinations. PSI currently serves as Virginia's examination administrator for tradesmen. The NASCLA Accredited Examination is a separate examination program recognized by participating jurisdictions, while the Virginia Board for Contractors remains responsible for determining licensing eligibility and issuing the actual Journeyman Electrician license.

Licensing Steps

Passing an accepted electrical examination does not automatically issue a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license. Individual tradesman licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors.

  1. Review Virginia Journeyman Electrician qualification pathways. Determine which combination of practical electrical experience, vocational training, apprenticeship, or qualifying education applies to your background.
  2. Gather supporting documentation. Virginia requires applicants to document the practical experience and education used to establish eligibility.
  3. Complete the applicable Virginia application process. Submit the information required by the Virginia Board for Contractors for Journeyman Electrician licensure.
  4. Prepare for the electrical examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, OSHA requirements, NFPA safety standards, calculations, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Complete the accepted examination requirement. Follow the current procedures associated with the examination pathway used for your Virginia application.
  6. Submit the required examination documentation. Provide the Board with the examination information required to complete the licensing process.
  7. Complete any remaining Virginia licensing requirements. The Board evaluates the applicant's qualifications before issuing the Journeyman Electrician license.
  8. Maintain the license. Complete the required continuing education and renewal process for your Virginia electrical tradesman license.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen, including Journeyman and Master Electricians. Virginia provides several combinations of formal education or vocational training and practical experience that can qualify an applicant for Journeyman Electrician licensure.

An applicant with a bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the electrical trade may qualify with one year of practical experience in the trade.

An applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also recognizes the following combinations of formal vocational training and practical experience for Journeyman Electrician applicants:

  • 240 hours of formal vocational training plus four years of practical experience.
  • 160 hours of formal vocational training plus five years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training plus six years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training plus seven years of practical experience.

Virginia regulations also include additional pathways involving approved apprenticeship and other recognized training or experience. Applicants must provide documentation that supports the specific qualification method being used.

Virginia requires pre-approval from the Board before a candidate can sit for a Virginia-administered tradesman examination. Applicants should submit complete experience and education documentation so the Board can determine eligibility.

After licensure, Virginia Journeyman Electricians must complete Board-approved continuing education as a condition of renewal. Electricians are required to complete an approved three-hour electrical continuing-education course during each applicable renewal period.

Continuing-education courses must be approved by the Virginia Board for Contractors. The course must be completed before the license expiration date for it to satisfy the renewal requirement.

Virginia's Journeyman Electrician license is an individual tradesman credential. It is separate from a Virginia contractor business license. A company or individual contracting for work that falls under Virginia's contractor licensing laws must separately satisfy the applicable contractor licensing requirements.
