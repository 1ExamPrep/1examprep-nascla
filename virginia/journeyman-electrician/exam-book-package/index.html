Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized electrical trade examination created around the knowledge and skills used by journeyman electricians. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis involving electrical subject matter experts from multiple jurisdictions.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains 100 scored questions. Candidates must answer at least 70 questions correctly to achieve the current minimum passing score. Candidates are allowed 300 minutes, or 5 hours, to complete the examination.

NASCLA also administers 10 non-scored experimental questions. These questions are used for examination development and do not count toward the candidate's final score.

The current scored examination content is distributed across the following subject areas:

  • Interpreting Plans and Specifications: 6 questions
  • Basic Electrical Safety: 8 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Principles: 10 questions
  • Testing and Troubleshooting: 7 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 15 questions
  • Wiring and Protection: 18 questions
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 questions
  • Equipment for General Use: 10 questions
  • Special Occupancies and Special Equipment: 8 questions
  • Special Conditions and Communication Systems: 2 questions

Wiring and Protection represents the largest portion of the scored examination, followed by Wiring Methods and Materials and General Code Requirements. Those categories make strong National Electrical Code navigation especially important. Candidates should also devote meaningful study time to theory, troubleshooting, safety, equipment, plans, and specialty systems.

Virginia is currently listed by NASCLA as an accepting agency for both the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the Journeyman Electrician examination. Passing a NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted examination requirement, but the Virginia Board for Contractors remains responsible for determining whether an applicant qualifies for the Virginia Journeyman Electrician license.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Candidates may use authorized reference materials that comply with the current examination rules.

The examination format makes reference-navigation practice a major part of preparation. Candidates should be able to identify the subject of a question, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, locate the appropriate section or table, and correctly apply the information.

The 2023 National Electrical Code deserves substantial study time. Practice using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, individual articles, tables, notes, and cross-references. Become comfortable moving between general provisions and rules that apply to a particular wiring method, occupancy, piece of equipment, or installation condition.

Current NASCLA examination rules allow qualifying reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. Books must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain additional loose or attached papers.

The authorized reference materials are not supplied by the testing center, so candidates using permitted books must bring their own qualifying references. Preparing with your books in advance helps make the reference library familiar before examination day.

A useful study technique is to work practice questions with the books beside you. First determine what the question is testing. Then select the appropriate reference and locate the supporting information. This method helps develop electrical knowledge and reference-navigation skills at the same time.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license. Virginia licenses individual electrical tradesmen through the Board for Contractors, and applicants must satisfy the state's qualification requirements.

  1. Review the Virginia Journeyman Electrician qualification pathways. Determine which combination of practical experience, vocational education, apprenticeship training, or qualifying education applies to your background.
  2. Gather your supporting documentation. Virginia requires documentation verifying the practical experience and education used to establish eligibility.
  3. Complete the applicable Virginia application process. Submit the required information to the Virginia Board for Contractors for the Journeyman Electrician licensing pathway.
  4. Prepare for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Study electrical code requirements, theory, calculations, safety, wiring methods, troubleshooting, plans, equipment, and specialty systems.
  5. Take and pass the accepted examination. Follow NASCLA and testing-provider requirements for scheduling, identification, authorized references, and examination procedures.
  6. Complete the remaining Virginia licensing requirements. Passing the examination is one component of eligibility for the individual tradesman license.
  7. Receive the applicable Virginia credential. The Board for Contractors issues the license after the applicant has met the applicable statutory and regulatory requirements.
  8. Maintain the license. Virginia Journeyman Electricians must comply with renewal and continuing-education requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen who work in electrical, plumbing, HVAC, gas fitting, and several other regulated trades. A Virginia Journeyman Electrician license is issued to an individual rather than a business entity.

Virginia provides multiple education-and-experience pathways for candidates seeking authorization to take the Journeyman Electrician examination. One pathway requires a qualifying bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade together with one year of practical experience.

Another pathway allows an applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program to qualify with two years of practical experience in the trade.

Virginia also recognizes combinations of formal vocational training and practical electrical experience. These pathways include:

  • 240 hours of formal vocational training plus four years of practical experience.
  • 160 hours of formal vocational training plus five years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training plus six years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training plus seven years of practical experience.

Virginia's rules also provide pathways involving qualifying apprenticeship training and other recognized education or training. Applicants must furnish the documentation required to support the pathway they use.

The Virginia Board for Contractors currently requires pre-approval before a candidate sits for the state-administered tradesman examination. The Board reviews the application and qualification documentation before examination eligibility is granted.

Virginia's state tradesman examinations are currently administered by PSI. The NASCLA Accredited Examination is a separate examination program that Virginia accepts for Journeyman Electrician purposes. Candidates should make sure the examination pathway selected matches the licensing application they intend to use.

Virginia Journeyman Electricians are also subject to continuing education. Electrical tradesmen must complete an approved three-hour continuing-education course covering the applicable electrical code as part of the renewal requirements.

A Virginia individual Journeyman Electrician license should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. Contractors are licensed separately by the Board for Contractors, and businesses performing work that falls under contractor licensing laws must satisfy the applicable contractor-class and specialty requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia NASCLA Journeyman Electrician preparation plan should be based on the examination blueprint rather than random reading. The content outline provides a practical guide for deciding where to invest study time.

Wiring and Protection accounts for 18 scored questions. Review branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, conductor requirements, grounding, bonding, and related NEC provisions. Practice locating code requirements quickly and recognizing when multiple articles apply to the same problem.

Wiring Methods and Materials accounts for 16 scored questions. Study conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, conduit systems, installation requirements, fill calculations, and the rules associated with specific wiring methods.

General Code Requirements contains 15 scored questions. Become familiar with broadly applicable NEC requirements, including definitions, installation rules, equipment requirements, working spaces, and other provisions used throughout electrical installations.

Electrical Theory and Principles contains 10 scored questions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, conductor relationships, and electrical calculations. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications and Ugly's Electrical References can support this portion of preparation.

Equipment for General Use also contains 10 scored questions. Candidates should review the NEC requirements that apply to commonly used electrical equipment and understand how equipment rules connect with wiring, protection, grounding, and installation requirements.

Basic Electrical Safety accounts for 8 scored questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, OSHA 29 CFR 1910, and NFPA 70E provide resources for studying construction safety, general workplace safety, and electrical safe-work practices.

Testing and Troubleshooting contains 7 questions. Review voltage, resistance, current, continuity, equipment behavior, test instruments, and systematic troubleshooting. These questions may require practical reasoning rather than simply locating a single code rule.

Plans and specifications, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm systems should also be included in the study schedule. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling-system topics.

The best way to become comfortable with an open-book examination is to use the references continuously. Practice identifying the subject, selecting the correct book, finding the relevant information, and applying it to the problem. Repeating this process can make reference navigation more organized and deliberate.
