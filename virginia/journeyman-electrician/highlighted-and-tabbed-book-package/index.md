## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized electrical trade examination developed around the knowledge and skills expected of journeyman electricians performing electrical installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and repair work. NASCLA developed the examination through a national occupational analysis with input from electrical subject matter experts.

The current NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination contains **100 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of **70**. The current examination allows **270 minutes, or 4.5 hours**, for completion.

The current examination content outline includes:

- **Interpreting Plans and Specifications:** 6 questions
- **Basic Electrical Safety:** 8 questions
- **Electrical Theory and Principles:** 10 questions
- **Testing and Troubleshooting:** 7 questions
- **General Code Requirements:** 15 questions
- **Wiring and Protection:** 18 questions
- **Wiring Methods and Materials:** 16 questions
- **Equipment for General Use:** 10 questions
- **Special Occupancies and Special Equipment:** 8 questions
- **Special Conditions and Communication Systems:** 2 questions

Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and General Code Requirements represent a significant portion of the examination. Candidates should therefore develop strong National Electrical Code navigation skills while also reviewing electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, plans, equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems.

NASCLA examination applications are submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. An approved NASCLA examination application is valid for one year from the approval date. After passing, candidates can use the National Examination Database to send their examination transcript electronically to participating licensing agencies such as Virginia.

The NASCLA examination itself is not a license. Virginia remains responsible for determining whether an applicant satisfies the state's Journeyman Electrician licensing requirements.

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license. Individual tradesman licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

## State Requirements

The **Virginia Board for Contractors** regulates individual tradesmen, including Journeyman and Master Electricians. Virginia provides several education-and-experience combinations that can qualify an applicant for Journeyman Electrician licensure.

One pathway allows an applicant with a **bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the electrical trade** to qualify with one year of practical experience in the trade.

An applicant with an **associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field** may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also provides the following vocational-training and experience combinations:

- **240 hours of formal vocational training** plus four years of practical experience.
- **160 hours of formal vocational training** plus five years of practical experience.
- **80 hours of formal vocational training** plus six years of practical experience.
- **40 hours of formal vocational training** plus seven years of practical experience.

Virginia regulations also recognize qualifying apprenticeship and other approved pathways. Applicants must provide the supporting documentation required for the qualification method they use, including experience verification and education or training records when applicable.

Applicants pursuing Virginia's state-administered tradesman examination must receive Board approval before testing. Virginia currently uses PSI for its state tradesman examinations. The NASCLA Accredited Examination is a separate testing pathway that Virginia accepts for Journeyman Electrician purposes.

After licensure, Virginia electricians are subject to continuing-education requirements. Journeyman and Master Electricians must complete an approved **three-hour electrical continuing-education course** as a condition of license renewal.

A Virginia Journeyman Electrician license is an individual tradesman credential and is separate from a Virginia contractor business license. Businesses performing contracting work that falls within Virginia contractor licensing laws must separately comply with the applicable contractor licensing requirements.
