Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is a standardized electrical trade examination developed from a national occupational analysis of journeyman electrical work. NASCLA's electrical program was developed with input from electrical subject matter experts and is designed to represent the knowledge and skills used in jurisdictions across the United States.

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination covers major areas of professional electrical work, including interpreting plans and specifications, basic electrical safety, electrical theory and principles, testing and troubleshooting, general code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment for general use, special occupancies and equipment, and special conditions and communication systems.

Virginia is listed by NASCLA as an accepting agency for the Journeyman Electrician examination. A passing NASCLA result may be transmitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database for use by participating agencies. The examination result itself is not a license; Virginia retains authority over the actual Journeyman Electrician licensing process.

Virginia's Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen, including electricians. The Board requires pre-approval before candidates sit for Virginia-administered tradesman examinations. PSI is Virginia's current examination vendor for individual tradesman testing, and the Board's current testing program includes Journeyman Electrician and Master Electrician examinations.

Candidates preparing specifically through the NASCLA pathway should study the NASCLA examination blueprint and the reference materials associated with the accredited Journeyman Electrician exam. The strongest preparation plans balance National Electrical Code work with electrical theory, safety, calculations, troubleshooting, and practical application.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. Authorized reference materials may be used according to the examination rules in effect for the NASCLA testing appointment.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates need to recognize what a question is asking, decide which reference contains the relevant information, locate the appropriate section, and apply the rule or technical concept efficiently. Simply having the books available does not eliminate the need to understand the material.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should become one of your most familiar study tools. Practice using the NEC index, Article 100 definitions, tables, article organization, chapter structure, informational notes, and cross-references. Learn how general requirements interact with more specific rules for equipment, occupancies, and wiring systems.

Safety references should also be used throughout preparation. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 focuses on construction-industry safety requirements, while OSHA 29 CFR 1910 addresses general-industry workplace requirements. NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace.

NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References provides formulas, tables, calculations, and practical electrical information. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications supports the electrical principles behind many code and calculation questions.

A useful study routine is to identify the topic before opening a reference. Decide which book should contain the answer, navigate to the correct section, and then confirm the solution. Repeating that process helps develop a more efficient open-book testing strategy.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates must complete the licensing requirements administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

  1. Choose the Journeyman Electrical designation. Virginia licenses Journeyman Electricians as individual tradesmen through the Board for Contractors.
  2. Review Virginia's qualification pathways. Determine which combination of practical experience, vocational training, education, apprenticeship, or other approved pathway applies to you.
  3. Document your experience and education. Virginia requires supporting documentation such as experience verification forms and training or education records when applicable.
  4. Complete the applicable exam and license application process. Submit the information required by the Virginia Board for Contractors for the licensing pathway you are using.
  5. Prepare for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Study the 2023 NEC, electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies, fire alarms, and communication systems.
  6. Take and pass the accepted examination. Follow the current NASCLA and testing-provider rules for scheduling, identification, authorized references, and examination-day procedures.
  7. Complete Virginia licensure. After satisfying the examination requirement, complete the Board's remaining licensing requirements before working under the Virginia Journeyman Electrician designation.
  8. Maintain the license. Virginia Journeyman Electricians must comply with renewal and continuing-education requirements.

State Requirements

Virginia Journeyman Electrician licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board regulates individual tradesmen engaged in electrical, plumbing, HVAC, gas fitting, and other regulated trades.

Virginia provides several qualification combinations for applicants seeking a Journeyman tradesman license by examination. One pathway requires four years of practical experience in the trade plus 240 hours of formal vocational training.

Another pathway allows five years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training. Applicants may also qualify with six years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training, or seven or more years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.

Virginia also recognizes qualifying education-based pathways. An applicant may qualify with an associate degree or certificate from at least a two-year tradesman-related program from an accredited community college or technical school together with two years of practical trade experience.

A qualifying bachelor's degree or certificate of completion from an accredited college or university in an engineering curriculum related to the trade can also be used with the applicable practical-experience requirement.

Journeyman applicants who successfully complete an approved apprenticeship program may qualify through that pathway. Virginia's application also provides procedures for applicants who completed an apprenticeship program in another state, provided the required documentation from the regulating entity and apprenticeship sponsor is submitted.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old. Virginia requires supporting documentation that verifies the experience, training, education, or apprenticeship pathway being used to qualify for licensure.

Virginia also requires pre-license education for tradesmen. Candidates should complete the Board-approved education requirements that apply to their licensing method before the license can be issued.

After licensure, Virginia Journeyman Electricians are subject to continuing-education requirements. Electricians must complete an approved three-hour continuing-education course in the applicable electrical code as a condition of renewal.

Virginia Journeyman Electrician licensure applies to an individual tradesman. Contractor business licensing is a separate matter. An individual who performs work that requires a Virginia contractor license must also comply with the Board for Contractors' business licensing rules.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine National Electrical Code navigation with electrical theory, safety, troubleshooting, calculations, plans, equipment, and specialty electrical work. The NASCLA examination blueprint covers a wide range of journeyman responsibilities, so preparation should not be limited to memorizing NEC articles.

Start with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice locating requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, equipment, and special occupancies. Learn to use the index efficiently and practice following cross-references between related articles.

Electrical theory should receive regular study time. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, series circuits, parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, conductor calculations, and other principles commonly used in electrical work. Understanding the theory behind a calculation can make unfamiliar questions easier to solve.

Testing and troubleshooting are also important journeyman-level skills. Practice interpreting voltage, resistance, current, continuity, circuit behavior, and equipment operation. A systematic troubleshooting method can help with questions that require reasoning rather than direct code lookup.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction standards, OSHA general-industry standards, and NFPA 70E. Study both general workplace safety requirements and electrical-specific hazard controls.

Plans and specifications should be part of the study schedule. Practice recognizing symbols, circuits, equipment, locations, and installation requirements from electrical drawings. Journeyman electricians must often translate plans into compliant field installations.

Special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, and fire alarm systems should also receive attention. These subjects may involve code sections that candidates encounter less frequently, making advance navigation practice especially valuable.

Use the references while studying rather than waiting until the final review period. When you work a practice question, identify the subject first, select the most likely reference, locate the applicable section, and then confirm the answer. This reinforces both trade knowledge and open-book exam skills.
