Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program provides participating licensing agencies with a standardized trade examination designed around the knowledge and skills associated with journey-level electrical work.

NASCLA currently lists the Virginia Board for Contractors, Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation as a participating agency that accepts both the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination and the Journeyman Electrician examination.

Preparation for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination should include a broad range of electrical trade knowledge, including:

  • Interpreting plans and specifications
  • Basic electrical safety
  • Electrical theory and principles
  • Testing and troubleshooting
  • General electrical code requirements
  • Wiring and protection
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Equipment for general use
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Special conditions and communication systems

National Electrical Code preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and communication systems.

Candidates should also be prepared to apply electrical theory to practical problems. Important areas include voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current concepts, transformers, motors, loads, electrical calculations, test instruments, and systematic troubleshooting.

Passing the NASCLA examination is not the same as receiving a Virginia license. NASCLA examination acceptance satisfies an examination component for participating agencies, while the Virginia Board for Contractors determines whether the applicant meets all qualifications for Journeyman Electrician licensure.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license. Applicants must complete the licensing process administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

  1. Review Virginia's Journeyman Electrician qualification pathways. Determine which combination of education, formal vocational training, and practical experience applies to your background.
  2. Document practical electrical experience. Virginia requires experience verification supporting the specific eligibility pathway selected by the applicant.
  3. Document formal education or vocational training. Provide certificates, transcripts, or other qualifying records where required.
  4. Complete the applicable licensing application. Submit the required information to the Virginia Board for Contractors.
  5. Complete the accepted examination requirement. Virginia accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians.
  6. Prepare for the examination. Study electrical code, theory, calculations, safety, troubleshooting, wiring methods, equipment, plans, and specialty systems.
  7. Submit examination documentation. Follow the applicable NASCLA and Virginia procedures for reporting the examination result.
  8. Complete any remaining licensing requirements. The Board reviews the applicant's education, experience, examination record, and application before issuing a license.
  9. Maintain the license. Complete the required electrical continuing education before renewal.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesman licenses, including Journeyman and Master Electricians. Virginia provides several different combinations of practical experience and formal education or vocational training that can qualify an applicant for a Journeyman Electrician license.

An applicant with a bachelor's degree or certificate of completion from an accredited college or university in an engineering curriculum related to the electrical trade may qualify with one year of practical experience in the trade.

An applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program at an accredited community college or technical school may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also recognizes several combinations of formal vocational training and practical electrical experience:

  • 240 hours of formal vocational training plus four years of practical experience.
  • 160 hours of formal vocational training plus five years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training plus six years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training plus seven or more years of practical experience.

Applicants using one of these pathways must provide the supporting experience verification and training or education documentation required by the Board.

Virginia also provides additional routes to eligibility through recognized apprenticeship and other qualifying education or experience established in the Board's regulations. Applicants should select the pathway that accurately reflects their training and practical electrical background.

Virginia's individual Journeyman Electrician license is different from a Virginia contractor business license. The tradesman license identifies the individual's qualification in the electrical trade. Businesses contracting for regulated construction work must separately comply with Virginia's contractor licensing requirements when those requirements apply.

Virginia electrician licenses are currently issued on a three-year license cycle. As a condition of renewal, Journeyman and Master Electricians must complete an approved three-hour electrical continuing-education course. The continuing-education requirement must be completed before the license expires.

The required continuing education must be provided by a Board-approved provider and relate to the applicable electrical code changes. Electricians holding both Journeyman and Master designations in the same electrical trade need only complete the applicable electrical continuing-education requirement for that trade during the renewal period.
