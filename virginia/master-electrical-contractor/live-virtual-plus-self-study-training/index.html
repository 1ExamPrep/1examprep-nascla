Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for experienced electrical professionals preparing for contractor, master electrician, or unlimited electrician examination requirements in participating jurisdictions. The examination emphasizes practical electrical knowledge, the application of Code requirements, electrical calculations, safety, and professional responsibilities.

The examination preparation material spans multiple technical areas. Candidates should be comfortable with electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, electrical equipment, special systems, testing equipment, and energy-efficiency concepts.

The broad range of subjects means effective preparation should include several different references instead of focusing exclusively on the National Electrical Code. Candidates should practice recognizing which source is most appropriate for the question before beginning a lookup.

Electrical calculations are another important part of preparation. Candidates should be comfortable working through voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, conductor sizing, loads, and other electrical calculations while identifying where Code rules affect the final result.

Virginia licensing requirements remain separate from the NASCLA trade examination. A successful examination result does not by itself issue a Virginia Master Electrician credential or a Virginia contractor-business license. Applicants must satisfy the requirements established by the Virginia Board for Contractors for the credential or license they are seeking.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book electrical trade examination. Candidates may use authorized references during testing in accordance with the current examination and testing-center requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity a major component of preparation. Technical electrical books contain extensive information, and attempting to research every question without knowing the organization of the references can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should practice identifying the subject first and then selecting the most appropriate reference.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive extensive navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC index, definitions, article structure, chapters, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Important study areas can include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and specialty electrical systems.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Understanding which reference applies to a particular situation helps reduce unnecessary searches.

Electrical theory resources should be used for active problem solving. Practice circuit relationships, voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, and related calculations. A useful approach is to identify the known information, determine what must be solved, choose the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and then check whether a Code requirement modifies the result.

Live virtual training can help demonstrate how different subjects and references connect. Self-study sessions then allow candidates to reinforce those concepts through repetition, practice questions, Code research, and timed lookup exercises.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors, operating through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, regulates contractor businesses and individual tradesmen in the electrical trade. Virginia distinguishes between a contractor-business license and an individual Master Electrician tradesman credential.

Virginia contractor firms are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C contractors. These licenses are issued to business entities or sole proprietorships rather than serving as individual electrical tradesman licenses. The contractor license also includes classifications or specialties that define the work the business is authorized to perform.

Every contractor business must identify a qualified individual for each classification or specialty on the license. Virginia's current Board for Contractors regulations identify a Master Electrician tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors as the prerequisite for the Electrical Contractors specialty.

The qualified individual must also have the amount of experience applicable to the contractor class being requested. Virginia's current contractor application lists a minimum of one year of qualifying experience for a Class C license, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applications, the course must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

The contractor pre-license education requirement is a business-focused course covering Virginia regulations, statutes, and contractor responsibilities. It is separate from electrical trade preparation and separate from the technical examination material covered in this NASCLA-focused training.

For Class A and Class B contractor licenses, Virginia also has designated-employee examination requirements relating to contractor business knowledge and Virginia laws and regulations. These requirements are distinct from the individual Master Electrician credential and from an electrical trade examination.

Virginia regulates individual Master Electricians through its Tradesmen Program. The Board for Contractors requires continuing education for Journeyman and Master Electricians as part of the credential-renewal process. Electrical tradesmen currently complete three hours of continuing education covering the applicable electrical Code.

Passing an electrical trade examination is only one component of the licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable Virginia application, meet experience and qualification requirements, satisfy contractor-business requirements when relevant, and receive approval before performing work under the requested license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A balanced study plan should combine live instruction with consistent independent review. Live virtual classes provide structure and help candidates work through technical subjects in an organized sequence. Self-study allows candidates to repeat difficult material and spend additional preparation time on the areas where they need the most improvement.

Start with National Electrical Code navigation. Instead of reading the NEC from beginning to end, practice using it as a working reference. Look up realistic questions, use the index, follow cross-references, identify applicable tables, and learn how individual Code sections relate to the overall electrical installation.

Electrical calculations should also be part of every study schedule. Work through theory problems involving circuit relationships, loads, voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, and other electrical concepts. Repetition can help candidates recognize which calculation method applies and where the NEC may influence the final answer.

Safety study should involve both OSHA and NFPA 70E. Learn which subjects belong to construction safety and which relate specifically to electrical workplace safety. Becoming familiar with the organization of both references can make open-book research more efficient.

Project-management preparation should include planning, coordination, communication, risk, documentation, and other responsibilities associated with professional construction projects. Energy-efficiency material should also be incorporated so candidates are familiar with the structure and terminology of ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1-2022.

Self-study sessions are especially useful for timed reference exercises. Select a practice question, identify the subject, decide which book applies, locate the relevant information, and record how long the process takes. This type of practice helps develop both technical understanding and practical navigation habits.

Live instruction can then be used to reinforce difficult concepts and place individual subjects into a broader preparation structure. Candidates can return to independent study afterward and repeat the material until the concepts and reference locations become more familiar.
