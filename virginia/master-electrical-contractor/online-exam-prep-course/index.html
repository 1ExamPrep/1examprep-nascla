Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. NASCLA defines this examination classification around professionals conducting business involving the installation, maintenance, and repair of electrical work in accordance with applicable standards and codes.

The Electrical Contractors examination is organized around nine major content domains. The published NASCLA examination blueprint distributes 100 scored items across the following areas:

  • Project Design and Management: 8 items
  • Safety: 9 items
  • Electrical Theory: 11 items
  • General Code Requirements: 17 items
  • Wiring and Protection: 17 items
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 16 items
  • Equipment for General Use: 13 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions: 8 items
  • Communication Systems: 1 item

The content distribution makes Code-related preparation especially important. General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Equipment for General Use account for a substantial portion of the scored examination. Candidates should be comfortable identifying applicable NEC provisions and applying those rules to realistic electrical situations.

Electrical Theory also deserves focused preparation. Candidates may need to apply electrical principles, formulas, and calculations rather than simply locate a Code section. Project Design and Management and Safety add professional responsibilities beyond installation rules, while Special Occupancies, Equipment and Conditions require familiarity with electrical provisions that may not be encountered every day.

The NASCLA electrical examinations are computer-based examinations administered in secure testing locations through approved examination providers. Candidates should follow their current examination authorization and scheduling instructions when arranging a test appointment.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Approved references may be used during testing in accordance with the current examination-provider and testing-center rules.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for preparation. The examination covers numerous technical subjects, and the reference collection contains thousands of pages of information. Candidates who open a reference for the first time during the examination may lose valuable time determining how the book is organized and where relevant information is located.

Study sessions should therefore include regular reference-navigation practice. Learn how the National Electrical Code is organized and become comfortable using its index, chapters, articles, tables, definitions, exceptions, notes, and related provisions. Practice identifying the key topic in a question before searching so you can select the correct reference and begin in the most likely section.

Navigation practice should extend beyond the NEC. Safety questions may require familiarity with OSHA construction requirements or NFPA 70E. Electrical theory questions may require a textbook or calculation reference. Project-management questions may involve material found in contractor or project-management publications. Energy-related questions may draw from the applicable energy standard.

Working through questions with the references in front of you is one of the most practical ways to prepare. Read the question, determine what information is being requested, select the appropriate book, locate the supporting material, and then answer the question. Repeated practice builds familiarity with both the subject and the location of important information.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the Virginia credential you need. Virginia distinguishes between individual master electrician licensure and contractor firm licensure. Electrical professionals should identify whether they are pursuing an individual master license, serving as a contractor's qualified individual, or completing both pathways.
  2. Meet the applicable master electrician qualification standard. Virginia requires master tradesman applicants to satisfy one of the experience pathways established by the Board for Contractors.
  3. Complete the applicable examination process. Virginia participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and recognizes the NASCLA electrical examination within its participating-agency framework.
  4. Prepare for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination. Study electrical theory, NEC requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment, safety, project management, special conditions, and reference navigation.
  5. Take and pass the applicable NASCLA examination. Successful examination results are maintained through the NASCLA National Examination Database for transmission to participating regulatory agencies.
  6. Complete the Virginia application process. Submit the required application and supporting documentation through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation for the credential being pursued.
  7. Complete contractor licensing requirements when operating a contracting business. A contractor firm must satisfy Virginia's separate contractor licensing requirements, including the appropriate contractor class, specialty designation, qualified individual requirements, and other applicable conditions.

State Requirements

Virginia individual tradesman licensing and contractor business licensing are administered through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Virginia Board for Contractors. Master electrician candidates must satisfy the qualification standards established in Virginia's tradesman regulations.

Under Virginia's current master tradesman examination qualification standards, an applicant for master licensure may qualify by having held a valid journeyman license in the applicable trade for at least one year or by documenting nine years of practical experience in the trade. Virginia regulations also provide a qualifying pathway for certain holders of residential tradesman licenses in applicable trades.

Experience and license documentation must satisfy the Board's application requirements. Candidates using prior licensing or certifications to support qualification may be required to provide documentation from the applicable licensing authority.

Virginia also distinguishes an individual's master electrician license from an electrical contractor firm's license. For the Electrical Contractors specialty, Virginia contractor regulations identify a master electrician tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors as the required qualification for the applicable qualified individual.

Every licensed Virginia contractor must identify a qualified individual for each specialty listed on the contractor license. For electrical contracting, this connection between the contractor business and the appropriately qualified master electrician is an important part of the state's licensing structure.

Virginia contractor firms may be licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C contractors. Contractor applicants are subject to requirements that are separate from the individual electrical trade examination, including requirements tied to business licensing, responsible management, qualified individuals, and the contractor classification being requested.

Virginia requires eight hours of pre-license education for applicants seeking a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license. For Class A and Class B applicants, the required pre-license education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee.

Virginia also maintains continuing education requirements for licensed master electricians. Master electrical license holders must complete an approved electrical continuing education course as a condition of renewal. For electrical tradesmen, the required continuing education course is three hours and addresses the applicable National Electrical Code update.

Passing the NASCLA examination does not by itself create a Virginia license. NASCLA examination results can satisfy an accepted trade examination requirement, but applicants must still follow Virginia's application procedures and meet the state requirements applicable to their individual or contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

Preparation for the Virginia NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor exam should combine technical knowledge, calculations, Code application, and reference navigation. Reading alone may not provide enough practice for a professional open-book examination. Candidates benefit from actively solving questions and locating supporting information.

Begin with the highest-volume Code subjects. General Code Requirements, Wiring and Protection, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Equipment for General Use represent a large portion of the examination. Review topics involving branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and commonly encountered equipment.

Electrical Theory should be studied through problem solving. Practice identifying the values provided in a question, selecting the appropriate relationship or formula, maintaining correct units, completing the calculation, and determining whether an NEC requirement affects the final answer. Understanding the underlying electrical principle makes it easier to recognize how a problem should be approached.

Safety preparation should include familiarity with both construction workplace requirements and electrical safety practices. Learn which reference is likely to contain the information requested so that you can navigate directly to the appropriate source during study and testing.

Project Design and Management should also be part of the study schedule. Electrical contractors work within broader construction projects that involve planning, coordination, documentation, scheduling, risk management, communication, and project execution. These professional responsibilities are represented in the NASCLA examination blueprint.

Timed practice can help bring these skills together. Set aside blocks of time to answer questions while using the reference materials. Track how long you spend on calculations and searches. If a question becomes time-consuming, practice moving forward and returning to it later. Developing a consistent approach to pacing can make the examination environment more manageable.
