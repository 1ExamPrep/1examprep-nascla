Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes a standardized examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. Virginia is a participating jurisdiction in the program through the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is designed to assess knowledge used by experienced electrical professionals. The program is based on a national job analysis and covers a wide range of trade subjects, including electrical theory, project design and management, safety, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special systems, and other technical areas associated with electrical contracting.

Virginia accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination as a trade-examination pathway. Candidates who pass can use their NASCLA examination results when completing the applicable Virginia licensing process. NASCLA examination results are stored in the National Examination Database and can be transmitted electronically to participating state agencies.

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Successful completion of required examinations occurs before applying for the applicable contractor license. Virginia uses PSI as its contractor examination vendor for state-administered contractor examinations.

The business and licensing portion of this package is intended to support preparation beyond the trade examination. The included NASCLA Virginia, 11th Edition and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018 provide resources for reviewing Virginia-specific contractor law, regulation, and building-code material associated with the licensing process.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Electrical Examination Program includes open-book electrical trade examinations for Electrical Contractors/Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation a major part of effective preparation. Candidates need to recognize the subject being tested, determine which reference contains the relevant material, locate the applicable section, and apply the information efficiently. A large reference library can become difficult to use under exam conditions without regular practice.

All books included with The 1 Package are highlighted and tabbed, giving you an organized starting point for study. Highlighting can direct attention to important material, while permanent tabs can make major sections easier to locate as you practice navigating the books.

Study sessions should include repeated practice with the actual references rather than relying entirely on memorization. Work on identifying where different types of information are located, using indexes, finding code sections, following cross-references, and becoming familiar with the layout of each book.

State Requirements

Virginia contractor licensing is regulated by the Virginia Board for Contractors under the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The licensing process is separate from NASCLA examination approval, which means candidates must satisfy Virginia's own rules even after passing the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

Virginia recognizes NASCLA as an accepted examination pathway for the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician trade examination. NASCLA identifies the Virginia Board for Contractors as a participating electrical examination agency. Once a candidate passes the NASCLA examination, the result may be sent electronically to the participating regulatory agency through the NASCLA National Examination Database.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued according to classifications and specialties established by the Board for Contractors. Candidates must determine the appropriate licensing structure for the work their business will perform and satisfy the corresponding examination, application, business, and other regulatory requirements.

The Virginia Board for Contractors also administers contractor business examinations through PSI. Virginia's business examination requirements are separate from the NASCLA electrical trade examination, so candidates should prepare for both technical electrical knowledge and the business, regulatory, and legal subjects relevant to Virginia contractor licensing.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor preparation should combine electrical knowledge with consistent reference navigation. The examination is not limited to one book or one category of electrical work, so a balanced study plan should cover technical theory, code requirements, safety, project management, wiring systems, equipment, and specialty topics.

Make the 2023 National Electrical Code a central part of your study routine. Practice locating major articles, using the index, following cross-references, reading tables, and understanding how different sections of the NEC work together. Familiarity with the structure of the code can reduce the amount of time spent searching during an open-book examination.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside NEC navigation. Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to reinforce concepts involving circuits, electrical quantities, power, conductors, loads, and calculations. Understanding why a formula or principle applies is more useful than memorizing isolated answers.

Safety preparation should include both construction workplace requirements and electrical safety practices. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports jobsite safety study, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical workplace safety concepts.

Project management and contractor business knowledge should also receive focused attention. Electrical contractors are responsible not only for technical work but also for planning, coordinating, estimating, and managing projects. The NASCLA Contractors Guide and project-management references support preparation in these areas.

Virginia-specific study should be incorporated into your schedule separately from the NASCLA electrical trade material. The included NASCLA Virginia, 11th Edition and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018 give you dedicated resources for reviewing state contractor business, law, licensing, and building-code subjects.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books throughout your preparation. When answering practice questions, identify the likely reference before searching for an answer. Practice moving from question to book, from book to section, and from section to conclusion. Repeating that process can build a more efficient reference-navigation routine.
