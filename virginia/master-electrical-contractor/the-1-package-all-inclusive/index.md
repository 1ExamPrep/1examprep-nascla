## Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes a standardized examination for **Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians**. Virginia is a participating jurisdiction in the program through the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is designed to assess knowledge used by experienced electrical professionals. The program is based on a national job analysis and covers a wide range of trade subjects, including electrical theory, project design and management, safety, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, equipment, special systems, and other technical areas associated with electrical contracting.

Virginia accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination as a trade-examination pathway. Candidates who pass can use their NASCLA examination results when completing the applicable Virginia licensing process. NASCLA examination results are stored in the National Examination Database and can be transmitted electronically to participating state agencies.

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors**. Successful completion of required examinations occurs before applying for the applicable contractor license. Virginia uses PSI as its contractor examination vendor for state-administered contractor examinations.

Make the **2023 National Electrical Code** a central part of your study routine. Practice locating major articles, using the index, following cross-references, reading tables, and understanding how different sections of the NEC work together. Electrical theory should be studied alongside NEC navigation. Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to reinforce concepts involving circuits, electrical quantities, power, conductors, loads, and calculations. Project management and contractor business knowledge should also receive focused attention. Electrical contractors are responsible not only for technical work but also for planning, coordinating, estimating, and managing projects.
