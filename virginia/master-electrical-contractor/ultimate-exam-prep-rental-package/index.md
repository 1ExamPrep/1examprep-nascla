The program offers an open-book examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians that is recognized by participating licensing agencies.

The Virginia Board for Contractors is a participating agency in the NASCLA electrical program. A passing NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination result can be used in connection with participating-state licensing requirements, but the NASCLA examination itself is not a Virginia license.

Virginia contractor licensing is structured around both a contractor license class and a classification or specialty. The contractor license class determines the monetary level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work authorized. Electrical contracting is identified under the Electrical specialty.

This means candidates preparing for the NASCLA electrical examination should distinguish between the individual Master Electrician credential and the contractor business license issued to the company or sole proprietorship.

## State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietorships engaged in regulated contracting work. Virginia contractor licenses consist of two primary components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class is Class A, Class B, or Class C, while the specialty defines the type of construction work authorized.

Electrical contracting is regulated under the Electrical specialty. Every contractor business applying for an Electrical specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who satisfies the Board's requirements for that specialty.

The Qualified Individual for the Electrical specialty must hold a current Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license. This individual credential is separate from the contractor business license and is issued through the Virginia Board for Contractors' Tradesmen Program.

Virginia also establishes experience requirements for Qualified Individuals based on the contractor license class. Current Board materials require at least one year of appropriate experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, at least three years for Class B, and at least five years for Class A.

New contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

The Virginia contractor pre-license course is a business-focused course covering statutes, regulations, and responsibilities relevant to operating a contracting business. It is separate from the technical electrical examination and separate from the Class A and Class B designated-employee examinations.

Class A and Class B businesses must declare a Designated Employee who satisfies the applicable examination requirement. The Class B contractor-business examination contains a Virginia portion and a general portion. Class A includes those components plus an advanced portion addressing higher-level administrative and business knowledge.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants must also satisfy Virginia's applicable financial-verification requirement. The Board provides financial-statement and surety-bond options for applicants subject to this requirement.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination may satisfy an accepted trade-examination component through Virginia's participation in the NASCLA electrical program, but it does not replace the Virginia Master Electrician credential required for the Electrical Qualified Individual or the contractor-business requirements associated with Class A, B, or C licensure.

Candidates should therefore view the licensing pathway as a combination of individual electrical qualifications and business licensing requirements. The technical electrical examination addresses trade knowledge, while Virginia separately regulates the business entity, Qualified Individual, Designated Employee, pre-license education, experience, and financial requirements.
