The District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection is listed as an accepting agency for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. This allows qualifying candidates to use the NASCLA examination as an accepted trade-examination pathway while still completing the District's other licensing requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity a major part of preparation. Candidates should know how each book is organized and how to move efficiently from a technical question to the applicable code section, formula, safety rule, table, definition, or technical requirement.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Washington DC electrician license. Candidates must complete the licensing process administered by the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection and the Board of Industrial Trades.

  1. Review the Washington DC journeyman electrician pathway. Determine which experience and application requirements apply to your background.
  2. Document qualifying electrical experience. The District requires journeyman applicants to establish qualifying work experience under its licensing rules.
  3. Prepare required supporting documents. Depending on the licensing pathway, applicants may need employment verification, identification, licensing records, NASCLA documentation, or a letter of good standing.
  4. Apply for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Submit the applicable NASCLA examination application.
  5. Study with the rental books and course. Use the highlighted and tabbed references together with the included 6 months of course access.
  6. Practice open-book navigation. Work through technical questions using the same types of references associated with the NASCLA examination.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow current testing procedures and authorized-reference requirements.
  8. Submit the applicable documentation to Washington DC. Complete the journeyman electrician application process with the Board of Industrial Trades.
  9. Complete licensing review. The Board determines whether the applicant satisfies all remaining licensing requirements before issuing the credential.

State Requirements

Electricians in Washington DC are regulated through the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades. The Board oversees electrician apprentice, journeyman, master, and contractor credentials along with several other regulated trades.

One District qualification pathway for a journeyman electrician requires at least 8,000 hours of apprentice electrician experience completed over at least four years. The District also recognizes additional qualification pathways for applicants who meet certain education and practical-experience requirements.

An education-based pathway is available to applicants who graduate from an accredited college or university with a degree in electrical engineering and obtain the required practical electrical experience certified by a licensed master electrician.

District law also includes requirements involving completion of approved instruction addressing the applicable District electrical code or an equivalent code for certain journeyman applicants. Candidates should incorporate local code awareness into their licensing preparation in addition to NASCLA examination study.

The District's electrician application materials identify documents that may be required depending on the applicant's pathway, including employment verification letters, letters of good standing, NASCLA accreditation documentation, and valid state-issued electrician licenses.

Applicants are also instructed to provide government-issued identification and a passport-style photograph. Applicable application, examination, and licensing charges are handled separately from this rental package.

Washington DC also has a reciprocity pathway for qualifying Journeyman and Master Electricians licensed in Maryland or Virginia. The qualifying out-of-state license must have been obtained by examination and must remain current, active, and in good standing. Reciprocity is separate from NASCLA examination acceptance.

Electrical work performed in the District must stay within the scope of the credential held and comply with applicable permitting, inspection, construction-code, and supervision requirements.
