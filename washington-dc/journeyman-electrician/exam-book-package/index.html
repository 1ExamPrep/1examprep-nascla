Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA's Accredited Electrical Examination Program identifies its Journeyman Electrician examination as one of its open-book electrical trade examinations.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity especially important. Having the correct books available is only one part of preparation. Candidates should also know how the books are organized and how to move efficiently from a technical problem to the appropriate provision, table, formula, definition, or safety requirement.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code 2023 is a central reference. Candidates should practice using Article 100 definitions, the NEC index, article organization, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Strong preparation should include services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, motors, transformers, and special systems.

The two OSHA references address different work environments. 29 CFR Part 1926 focuses on construction-industry safety, while 29 CFR 1910 contains general-industry occupational safety requirements. Candidates should understand the basic organization and purpose of each reference so they can identify which source is appropriate for a safety-related question.

NFPA 70E focuses specifically on electrical safety in the workplace. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system preparation. Ugly's Electrical References provides frequently used electrical formulas, calculations, tables, and practical information. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications supports the principles behind voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, and NEC-related calculations.

A useful open-book study routine is to answer a technical question first by identifying the subject. Then select the most appropriate book, locate the supporting requirement, and solve or confirm the answer. Repeating this approach develops reference-navigation habits while strengthening trade knowledge.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Washington DC journeyman electrician license. The licensing process is administered through the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection and the Board of Industrial Trades.

  1. Confirm the journeyman electrician license pathway. Review the District's requirements for the type of electrician credential being requested.
  2. Document qualifying electrical experience. District journeyman examination instructions identify four years or 8,000 hours of qualifying work experience for the examination pathway.
  3. Prepare employment verification. Applicants using the examination pathway are instructed to document employment and electrical experience through qualifying verification materials.
  4. Gather application documents. Current District requirements include government-issued identification, a passport-style photograph, and applicable electrician documentation.
  5. Apply for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Submit the applicable NASCLA examination application through the National Examination Database.
  6. Study the examination references. Prepare using NEC 2023, OSHA construction and general-industry regulations, NFPA 70E, NFPA 72, electrical calculations, and electrical theory.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the examination provider's current testing and reference-material rules.
  8. Provide NASCLA documentation to Washington DC. The District lists NASCLA accreditation among the electrician application materials associated with licensing.
  9. Complete Board review and licensing. The Board of Industrial Trades determines whether all applicable licensing qualifications have been satisfied before issuing the credential.

State Requirements

Electricians in Washington DC are regulated by the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades through the Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection. The Board regulates apprentice, journeyman, master, and contractor electrical credentials along with other industrial trades.

District journeyman electrician application instructions identify four years or 8,000 hours of qualifying work experience for applicants using the examination pathway. Employment verification documentation is used to establish qualifying experience.

The District also recognizes an apprenticeship-based journeyman examination waiver pathway. Applicants who have completed a qualifying nationally certified apprenticeship program may submit the applicable completion records, designation documentation, or official transcripts for review under that pathway.

Washington DC also provides electrician reciprocity for qualifying licensees from Maryland and Virginia. Journeyman and Master Electricians seeking reciprocity must hold a current, active license in good standing that was originally obtained through examination. Reciprocity is a separate licensing pathway from NASCLA examination acceptance.

Current District electrician application materials include employment verification letters, a letter of good standing when applicable, NASCLA accreditation documentation, and a valid state-issued electrician license when relevant to the applicant's pathway. All applicants must also provide a 2-inch by 2-inch passport-style photograph, government-issued identification, and payment information for applicable fees.

The current District electrician application fee is $65. The current journeyman electrician license fee is $110, and the credential is issued for a term of up to two years. Washington DC also lists a temporary journeyman electrician license at $110 for a period of up to 60 days.

District electrician licenses expire on November 30 of odd-numbered years. Journeyman electrician renewal is currently listed at $110. Government application and licensing fees are separate from this exam book package and from NASCLA examination charges.

Electrical work in the District must be performed within the scope allowed by the license or registration held and in accordance with applicable District electrical permitting, inspection, construction-code, and supervision requirements.

    An electrical workplace safety standard addressing practices and procedures designed to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while working on or near electrical systems and equipment.
  • Ugly's Electrical References, 2023
    A compact electrical field reference containing frequently used formulas, tables, calculations, conversions, and technical information for practical electrical problem solving.
  • NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2022
    A reference supporting preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems, initiating devices, notification functions, circuits, detection equipment, and associated system requirements.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference focused on connecting fundamental electrical theory with practical NEC applications involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, calculations, and electrical equipment.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Washington DC NASCLA Journeyman Electrician preparation plan should combine NEC navigation, electrical theory, calculations, safety study, fire alarm knowledge, and regular use of the reference books.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review conductor ampacity, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, and special installations. Learn the structure of the NEC rather than attempting to memorize every section.

Electrical theory should be studied throughout preparation. Review Ohm's law, voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current fundamentals, motors, transformers, electrical loads, and common calculations. Theory helps candidates understand why electrical systems behave the way they do and improves their ability to solve code-related problems.

Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to study construction-site safety and 29 CFR 1910 to study general-industry safety. Practice recognizing which set of regulations applies to different workplace situations.

NFPA 70E should be reviewed for electrical safe-work practices and hazard concepts. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References provides a convenient source for commonly used formulas and practical electrical information.

Regular practice should include selecting the correct book before searching for an answer. This develops the ability to distinguish between an NEC installation question, an OSHA construction question, a general-industry safety question, an NFPA 70E electrical-safety question, or a theory calculation.

Journeyman-level questions often combine subjects. A motor problem may require calculations, NEC motor provisions, conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, and grounding. A workplace-safety question may require deciding whether OSHA construction requirements, general-industry regulations, or NFPA 70E applies. An organized reference library helps candidates practice making those distinctions.
