Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers standardized examinations for participating electrical licensing agencies. The program includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

The District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection is listed by NASCLA as an accepting agency for the Journeyman Electrician examination. An accepting jurisdiction can recognize the applicable NASCLA examination in place of its state- or jurisdiction-specific trade examination while continuing to enforce its own licensing requirements.

Journeyman-level preparation should cover a broad range of technical subjects, including:

  • Electrical theory and calculations
  • General National Electrical Code requirements
  • Services and service equipment
  • Feeders and branch circuits
  • Conductor sizing and ampacity
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Raceways, cables, boxes, and enclosures
  • Motors and motor circuits
  • Transformers and electrical equipment
  • Special occupancies and special conditions
  • Construction-industry safety
  • General-industry workplace safety
  • Electrical safe-work practices
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Electrical testing and practical theory

NASCLA examination applications may be submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The current NASCLA examination application fee is $65. Applications are generally processed within seven business days, and an approved application is valid for one year from the approval date.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not create a national electrician license. The examination result can be used by participating jurisdictions as an accepted trade-examination credential, while the candidate remains responsible for meeting local licensing requirements.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies its Journeyman Electrician examination as one of the open-book electrical trade examinations within the Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes familiarity with the reference library a major part of preparation. Candidates should know more than the general content of each book. They should develop a consistent process for recognizing a question's subject, selecting the most appropriate reference, locating the applicable provision, table, formula, definition, or safety requirement, and applying that information to the problem.

The highlighted and tabbed format of this package supports that process. The two OSHA references address different types of workplaces. 29 CFR Part 1926 covers construction-industry safety, while 29 CFR 1910 addresses general-industry occupational safety. Learning to distinguish these references can be useful when working through questions involving worker protection, electrical hazards, equipment, and safe workplace practices.

NFPA 70E supports preparation involving electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system study. Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Washington DC electrician license. Licensing is administered through the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection and the Board of Industrial Trades.

  1. Review the Washington DC journeyman electrician requirements. Determine which application pathway applies to your electrical experience and existing credentials.
  2. Document qualifying experience. The District requires journeyman applicants to establish qualifying electrical training and experience under the applicable licensing rules.
  3. Gather application documentation. Electrician applicants may need employment verification letters, identification, licensing records, NASCLA accreditation information, or letters of good standing depending on the pathway used.
  4. Apply for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Submit the applicable examination application through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  5. Prepare with the examination references. Study NEC 2023, OSHA construction and general-industry regulations, NFPA 70E, NFPA 72, electrical theory, and calculations.
  6. Practice open-book reference navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed books while working through technical questions to strengthen familiarity with the reference library.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow the current testing provider rules concerning examination procedures and authorized reference materials.
  8. Provide the applicable NASCLA documentation to Washington DC. The District identifies NASCLA accreditation among the documents used in its electrician licensing process.
  9. Complete the licensing review. The Board of Industrial Trades determines whether the applicant has satisfied all applicable licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Electricians in Washington DC are regulated through the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades. The Board oversees electrician apprentice, journeyman, master, and contractor license categories along with several other regulated industrial trades.

District law provides several qualification pathways for a journeyman electrician. One pathway requires an applicant to have worked as an apprentice electrician for at least 8,000 hours over at least four years. The District also recognizes an education-based pathway for applicants who graduate from an accredited college or university with a degree in electrical engineering and obtain at least two years of practical electrical experience certified by a licensed master electrician.

District law also requires a journeyman electrician applicant, except where another statutory provision applies, to establish completion of an approved class addressing the applicable District electrical code or an equivalent code within the required period before submitting the application.

The Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection currently identifies several materials associated with electrician applications, including employment verification letters, letters of good standing, NASCLA accreditation, and valid state-issued electrician licenses. All applicants are also instructed to provide a 2-inch by 2-inch passport-style photograph, government-issued identification, and payment information.

The current District electrician application fee is $65. The current journeyman electrician license fee is $110 for a license term of up to two years. The District also lists a temporary journeyman electrician license at $110 for a term of up to 60 days.

Washington DC electrician licenses currently expire on November 30 in odd-numbered years. The current journeyman renewal fee is $110.

The District also maintains an electrician reciprocity pathway for qualifying Journeyman and Master Electricians licensed in Maryland or Virginia. For reciprocity, the Maryland or Virginia license must have been obtained by examination and must remain current, active, and in good standing. Reciprocity is a separate pathway from NASCLA examination acceptance.

Electrical work must be performed within the scope allowed by the District license or registration held and in accordance with applicable permitting, inspection, supervision, and construction-code requirements.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Study branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special systems.

Use the permanent tabs as navigation points, but also learn the NEC's overall organization. Practice locating information using the index and article structure so that you can approach questions from several directions.

Electrical theory should remain part of the study routine. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 for construction-safety topics and 29 CFR 1910 for general-industry workplace requirements. NFPA 70E should be studied for electrical safe-work practices, while NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system preparation.

Ugly's Electrical References can support quick review of formulas and practical electrical information. Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications can be used to reinforce calculation methods and connect electrical principles with code-based applications.

Work a question, identify the technical subject, decide which book applies, locate the supporting information, and then solve the problem. How much experience is required for the Washington DC journeyman pathway?
