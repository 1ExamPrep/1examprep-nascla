Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program offers three electrical examination categories: Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician, Journeyman Electrician, and Residential Electrical Contractor. Washington DC currently accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Journeyman Electricians.

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is designed to evaluate knowledge associated with the work performed by journeyman electricians across participating jurisdictions. Preparation should include a broad range of electrical subjects rather than focusing on one portion of the National Electrical Code.

Important areas of preparation include:

  • Electrical theory and practical calculations
  • General National Electrical Code requirements
  • Services and service equipment
  • Feeders and branch circuits
  • Load calculations
  • Conductor sizing and ampacity
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Grounding and bonding
  • Wiring methods and materials
  • Raceways, cables, boxes, and enclosures
  • Motors, transformers, and electrical equipment
  • Special occupancies and special equipment
  • Electrical workplace safety
  • Construction and industrial safety
  • Fire alarm and signaling systems
  • Electrical testing and troubleshooting concepts

NASCLA examination applications may be submitted through the NASCLA National Examination Database. The current NASCLA application fee is $65. Approved applications are valid for one year, and candidates currently receive up to three opportunities to take and pass the examination during that eligibility period.

After the NASCLA application is approved, candidates receive scheduling information from PSI. NASCLA candidates may schedule at PSI locations offering the applicable examination.

After passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination, candidates can use the NASCLA examination record as part of the licensing process with participating jurisdictions, including Washington DC. The District still reviews the applicant's licensing qualifications before issuing a journeyman electrician license.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies the Journeyman Electrician examination, Electrical Contractor or Master Electrician examination, and Residential Electrical Contractor examination as open-book electrical trade examinations.

Because the examination is open book, reference navigation should be a central part of preparation. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question, selecting the appropriate reference, locating the applicable code provision, table, definition, formula, or safety requirement, and applying that information correctly.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code 2023 should receive substantial attention. Candidates should become comfortable with Article 100 definitions, the index, chapter organization, individual articles, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. A single question may require knowledge of more than one NEC requirement, especially when calculations, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding, equipment, or special conditions are involved.

Journeyman preparation should include regular practice with services, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, ampacity, voltage drop concepts, grounding and bonding, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, appliances, equipment, special occupancies, and special systems.

The two federal safety references in this study list serve different purposes. 29 CFR Part 1926 addresses construction-industry safety, while 29 CFR 1910 addresses general industry workplace requirements. Understanding how to navigate both references can strengthen preparation for questions involving worker protection, electrical hazards, equipment, and workplace safety practices.

NFPA 70E adds another layer of electrical safety preparation by addressing electrical safe-work practices. NFPA 72 supports preparation involving fire alarm and signaling systems. Ugly's Electrical References and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications support formulas, calculations, and the underlying electrical principles used throughout the trade.

A productive open-book study method is to use the books actively while answering practice-oriented questions. Even when you believe you know the answer, locate the supporting information. This helps reinforce knowledge while building familiarity with the organization of each reference.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Washington DC journeyman electrician license. Applicants must complete the licensing process administered through the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection and the Board of Industrial Trades.

  1. Determine the appropriate electrician license type. Washington DC licenses electricians through the Board of Industrial Trades, including journeyman and master classifications.
  2. Review the current application requirements. Electrician applicants must provide the documentation requested by the District for the license type being pursued.
  3. Gather identification documents. Current application requirements include a passport-style photo and government-issued identification.
  4. Prepare employment and licensing documentation. Electrician applicants may be required to provide employment verification letters, a letter of good standing, NASCLA accreditation documentation, or a valid state-issued electrician license, depending on the pathway being used.
  5. Apply for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. Complete the NASCLA examination application through the National Examination Database.
  6. Prepare for the examination. Study the 2023 NEC, OSHA construction and general-industry regulations, NFPA 70E, electrical calculations, theory, fire alarm systems, and practical electrical installation topics.
  7. Take and pass the NASCLA examination. Follow current NASCLA and PSI testing procedures and authorized-reference rules.
  8. Submit the required licensing application to Washington DC. Provide the NASCLA examination documentation and all other materials required by the Board of Industrial Trades.
  9. Complete District review. The Board determines whether all licensing requirements have been met before the journeyman electrician license is issued.

State Requirements

Electrician licensing in Washington DC is regulated through the Board of Industrial Trades under the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection. The Board regulates electricians along with several other construction and industrial trades.

Current District application instructions identify several items that electrician applicants may need to provide, including employment verification letters, a letter of good standing, NASCLA accreditation documentation, and a valid state-issued electrician license. The exact combination of documents depends on the applicant's licensing pathway.

All applicants are also instructed to provide a 2-inch by 2-inch passport-style photograph, government-issued identification, and payment information for applicable application, examination, or licensing fees.

The District currently lists an electrician application fee of $65. The journeyman electrician license fee is currently $110, with a license term of up to two years. These government licensing fees are separate from this online exam prep product and from NASCLA examination fees.

Washington DC also offers a temporary journeyman electrician license for qualifying applicants. The District currently lists the temporary journeyman license fee as $110, with the temporary credential valid for up to 60 days.

The District requires electrical work that requires a permit to be performed by appropriately licensed or registered electrical personnel. District construction requirements identify licensed master electricians, licensed journeyman electricians, and registered apprentice electricians working under the required supervision among the classifications authorized to perform regulated electrical work.

Washington DC also maintains a reciprocity pathway for qualifying journeyman electricians and master electricians licensed in Maryland or Virginia. To qualify for the District's reciprocity process, the Maryland or Virginia license must have been obtained by examination and must be current, active, and in good standing.

Applicants using reciprocity must obtain a letter of good standing or license verification from the applicable Maryland or Virginia licensing authority and submit it with the District application. Reciprocity is a separate pathway from NASCLA examination acceptance.

The NASCLA pathway allows the District to recognize an accredited Journeyman Electrician examination in place of its applicable state-specific trade examination. The licensing authority still evaluates the applicant's qualifications and documentation before issuing a license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Washington DC NASCLA Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine National Electrical Code review, electrical theory, calculations, workplace safety, construction safety, fire alarm systems, and consistent reference navigation.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Review services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, equipment, motors, transformers, and special systems. Learn the structure of the Code so you can move from a technical problem to the appropriate article or table efficiently.

Electrical theory should be studied throughout the preparation process. Practice Ohm's law, voltage, current, resistance, power, series and parallel circuits, alternating-current principles, transformers, motors, and electrical loads. Understanding the theory helps with calculations and improves the ability to interpret NEC requirements.

Use 29 CFR Part 1926 for construction-safety preparation and 29 CFR 1910 for general-industry workplace safety. Learn how the two federal references are organized so you can recognize which one is most relevant to a particular safety question.

NFPA 70E should be reviewed for electrical safe-work practices and electrical hazard concepts. NFPA 72 should be used for fire alarm and signaling-system preparation. Ugly's Electrical References provides quick access to common formulas and technical information, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications reinforces the principles behind code calculations and electrical behavior.

Open-book preparation should involve frequent use of all seven references. Avoid relying only on memorization. Instead, practice identifying the subject first, selecting the correct reference, finding the applicable provision or formula, and applying that information to the question.

This approach is especially helpful for questions involving multiple technical concepts. A motor problem, for example, may require theory, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, motor-specific NEC rules, and grounding requirements. A jobsite-safety question may require determining whether the applicable standard is found in OSHA construction rules, OSHA general-industry rules, or NFPA 70E.

Does Washington DC accept the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination?
