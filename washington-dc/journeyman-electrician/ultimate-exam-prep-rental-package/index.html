Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized trade examinations for participating electrical licensing agencies. NASCLA currently offers examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors.

Washington DC is an accepting jurisdiction for the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination. As an accepting agency, the District can recognize a passing NASCLA examination as a waiver in place of its jurisdiction-specific trade examination. The NASCLA examination itself is not an electrician license, and applicants must still complete the District licensing process.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination is an open book test. NASCLA identifies its Journeyman Electrician examination as one of the open-book electrical trade examinations within the Accredited Electrical Examination Program.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should develop the ability to identify the subject of a question, choose the correct reference, locate the applicable section, table, formula, definition, or safety requirement, and apply that information accurately.

All seven rental books in this Ultimate package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighting helps draw attention to important study material, while permanent tabs create visible navigation points within the references. Candidates should use the tabs as part of a broader navigation strategy that also includes the index, table of contents, article organization, and cross-references.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code 2023 is a central reference for journeyman-level preparation. Study Article 100 definitions, services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, motors, transformers, and special systems.

The two OSHA references address different work environments. 29 CFR Part 1926 covers construction-industry occupational safety, while 29 CFR 1910 covers general-industry workplace safety. Candidates should know which reference applies to the type of work environment described in a question.

NFPA 70E supports preparation involving electrical workplace safety. NFPA 72 supports fire alarm and signaling-system study. Ugly's Electrical References provides formulas, conversions, calculations, and practical technical information, while Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications reinforces the electrical principles behind calculations and code applications.

A productive open-book study routine involves working questions with the books available. Identify the topic first, select the most appropriate reference, locate the supporting provision, and then apply that information. Repeating this process helps build both technical knowledge and navigation skill.

Licensing Steps

Passing the NASCLA Journeyman Electrician examination does not automatically issue a Washington DC electrician license. Applicants must complete the licensing process through the District of Columbia Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection and the Board of Industrial Trades.

  1. Review the Washington DC journeyman electrician licensing requirements. Determine which qualification pathway applies to your education, apprenticeship, and electrical experience.
  2. Document qualifying experience. One District pathway requires 8,000 hours of qualifying apprentice electrician experience completed over at least four years.
  3. Gather application documents. Electrician applicants may need employment verification letters, a letter of good standing, NASCLA accreditation documentation, a valid electrician license, government-issued identification, and a passport-style photograph, depending on the pathway used.

State Requirements

Electrician licensing in Washington DC is regulated through the District of Columbia Board of Industrial Trades under the Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection. The Board regulates electrical apprentices, journeymen, masters, and contractors along with other industrial trades.

One qualification pathway for a Washington DC journeyman electrician requires at least 8,000 hours of apprentice electrician experience completed over at least four years. The District also provides additional qualification pathways for applicants with specified electrical education and practical experience.

Applicants may be required to provide employment verification letters and other documentation supporting the experience claimed. The District's current electrician application information also identifies letters of good standing, NASCLA accreditation documentation, and valid state-issued electrician licenses among the materials relevant to electrician applicants.

All applicants are instructed to provide a 2-inch by 2-inch passport-style photograph, government-issued identification, and payment information for applicable application, examination, or licensing fees.

The current District electrician application fee is $65. The current Journeyman Electrician license fee is $110 for a license term of up to two years. Government fees are separate from the price of this Ultimate Exam Prep Rental Package.

Washington DC also maintains a reciprocity pathway for qualifying Journeyman and Master Electricians licensed in Maryland or Virginia. The qualifying license must have been obtained through examination and must be active and in good standing. Reciprocity is separate from NASCLA examination acceptance.

Electrical work performed in Washington DC must remain within the scope authorized by the credential held and comply with applicable permitting, inspection, supervision, and District construction-code requirements.
