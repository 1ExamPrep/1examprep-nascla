Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination recognized by participating contractor licensing agencies. The West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board is listed as a participating agency in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, requiring 81 correct answers
  • West Virginia Requirement: Contractors must also satisfy the West Virginia Business and Law examination requirement.
  • West Virginia Testing Agent: ProV

The NASCLA examination covers a broad commercial construction scope. Candidates should prepare for General Requirements, Site Construction, Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Wood, Thermal and Moisture Protection, Doors, Windows and Glazing, Finishes, Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, Electrical Systems, and Procurement and Contracting Requirements.

The examination is designed to measure knowledge across multiple construction disciplines rather than expertise in one specialty. Contractors with extensive field experience in framing, concrete, roofing, supervision, or another area may still need additional preparation in building codes, accessibility, OSHA safety, project administration, structural systems, contracts, or other subjects represented by the exam.

West Virginia separately requires contractors to pass its Business and Law examination in addition to applicable trade examination requirements. That exam focuses on the business-management and legal responsibilities involved in operating a construction business in West Virginia.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during testing according to the applicable examination rules.

West Virginia contractor examinations also permit applicable approved references. The current West Virginia contractor examination materials identify specific references that candidates may bring for their examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing permitted materials and following the testing provider's rules concerning book preparation and use.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity rather than random searching. Having access to a large collection of books does not eliminate the need to understand the construction material. Candidates who spend too much time searching multiple unfamiliar references can quickly lose valuable testing time.

The books in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Use those navigation aids throughout your study period. Learn how each index works, where major chapters begin, how technical tables are organized, and what terminology is used by the different publications.

A practical open-book strategy is to identify the subject first, select the most relevant reference, locate the needed information, confirm the answer, and move forward. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1-2017. OSHA questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19 or another concrete reference, while broad building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate West Virginia contractor classification. Contractor licensing is classification-based, so applicants should identify the classification that corresponds with the work they intend to perform.
  2. Complete the applicable trade examination requirement. West Virginia participates in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program and accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors through the participating-agency process.
  3. Pass the West Virginia Business and Law examination. West Virginia requires contractors to pass the Business and Law exam in addition to applicable trade examinations.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Complete the applicable West Virginia contractor licensing forms and provide the required applicant information.
  5. Provide examination documentation. Submit the applicable examination results or NASCLA transcript through the process required for the license application.
  6. Complete applicable business requirements. Contractor businesses must satisfy the state requirements that apply to their business structure and licensing application.
  7. Submit the completed licensing materials. The West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board reviews the required information before issuing the contractor license.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in West Virginia is administered by the West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board with support from the West Virginia Division of Labor. Contractor licenses are issued according to classifications that correspond with the type of construction work being performed.

West Virginia requires all contractors to pass the state's Business and Law examination in addition to applicable trade examinations. The Business and Law examination addresses business management and the legal responsibilities involved in establishing and operating a construction business, including contractor laws and regulations.

West Virginia uses ProV as its contractor licensing examination testing agent. Candidates preparing for a West Virginia-specific examination should follow the current ProV candidate bulletin for the examination they intend to take and use the approved references identified for that examination.

West Virginia is also a participating jurisdiction in the NASCLA Accredited Examination Program. Candidates who pass the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors can use their NASCLA examination result through the participating-state process instead of retaking the equivalent state-specific trade examination.

A NASCLA passing result is an examination credential rather than a contractor license. Candidates must still apply for the West Virginia contractor license and satisfy the additional state requirements applicable to the requested classification.

The West Virginia General Building Contractor classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves structures built for the support, shelter, or enclosure of people, animals, movable property, or other qualifying uses and requires work involving more than two contractor classifications or supervision of such construction. Specialized electrical, plumbing, HVAC, piping, or general engineering work may require additional applicable classifications.
