Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is a standardized commercial construction trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. The West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board participates in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program.

  • Exam: NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors
  • Number of Questions: 115
  • Time Allowed: 330 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, requiring 81 correct answers
  • West Virginia Requirement: Business and Law examination in addition to applicable trade examination requirements
  • West Virginia Testing Agent: ProV

The NASCLA commercial examination covers a broad range of construction knowledge. Candidates should prepare for General Requirements, Site Construction, Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Wood, Thermal and Moisture Protection, Doors, Windows and Glazing, Finishes, Mechanical and Plumbing Systems, Electrical Systems, and Procurement and Contracting Requirements.

This broad content means that field experience in one construction specialty may not cover everything represented on the exam. A contractor who is highly experienced in framing, roofing, concrete, project supervision, or another area may still need focused review in accessibility, OSHA requirements, structural standards, procurement, building codes, project administration, and other commercial construction subjects.

Time management is also important. With 115 questions and 330 minutes, candidates should develop a method for determining which questions can be answered directly and which require a focused reference lookup. Efficient use of the reference library can help prevent one difficult question from consuming too much testing time.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors is an Open Book Test. Approved reference materials may be used during the examination according to the applicable testing requirements.

Open-book testing makes familiarity with the reference library an important part of preparation. Access to a large number of books is useful only when you understand which reference applies to the subject being tested and how that reference is organized.

The rental books included with this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These navigation aids provide an organized starting point, but candidates should still work directly with the books throughout the study period. Learn how the indexes are organized, where major chapters begin, how technical tables are structured, and what terminology is used in each reference.

A practical open-book approach is to identify the construction topic first, choose the reference most likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, confirm the information, and move forward. Accessibility questions should direct you toward ICC A117.1-2017. OSHA construction safety questions should point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Structural concrete questions may require ACI 318-19, while broader building-code questions may require the International Building Code.

Repeated reference practice can help candidates develop these subject-to-book associations. The objective is not to memorize every page. The objective is to know where to begin when a specific technical lookup is necessary.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the appropriate West Virginia contractor classification. Contractor licensing is classification-based, so applicants should identify the classification that matches the type of work they intend to perform.
  2. Complete the applicable trade examination requirement. West Virginia participates in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program and accepts the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors through the participating-state process.
  3. Pass the West Virginia Business and Law examination. Contractors seeking West Virginia licensure must pass the Business and Law exam in addition to the applicable trade examination requirement.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Complete the applicable West Virginia contractor licensing forms and provide the required applicant and business information.
  5. Provide examination documentation. Submit the applicable examination results or NASCLA transcript according to the requirements of the licensing process.
  6. Complete applicable business requirements. Contractor applicants must satisfy the requirements that apply to their business entity and requested license classification.
  7. Submit the completed licensing materials. Provide the required documents to the West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board for review.

State Requirements

Contractor licensing in West Virginia is administered by the West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board through the West Virginia Division of Labor. Contractor licenses are issued according to classifications that correspond with the scope of construction work performed.

West Virginia requires contractor applicants to pass the state's Business and Law examination in addition to applicable trade examinations. The Business and Law exam addresses contractor business responsibilities and the laws and regulations that affect operating a construction business in West Virginia.

ProV serves as the testing agent for West Virginia contractor licensing examinations. ProV administers the Business and Law exam as well as numerous state-specific trade examinations, including General Building Contractor.

West Virginia also participates in the NASCLA Commercial Exam Program. This allows candidates who pass the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors to use that examination through the participating-state process rather than retaking the equivalent trade examination.

The NASCLA examination itself is not a national contractor license. It is an accepted trade examination credential. After passing NASCLA, the applicant must still apply to West Virginia and satisfy the additional licensing requirements established by the Contractor Licensing Board.

The West Virginia General Building Contractor classification addresses contractors whose principal business involves structures built for the support, shelter, or enclosure of people, animals, movable property, or other qualifying uses, including construction involving multiple contractor classifications or supervision of such work. Additional specialty classifications may be required for specialized work outside the general building scope.
