West Virginia NASCLA Exam Guide for Commercial and Electrical Contractors

By Pass Your NASCLA Exam Prep · 11 min read · 10 official sources cited

West Virginia requires a license for most construction contracting, and the state's Contractor Licensing Board accepts two NASCLA exams toward it: the Commercial General Building Contractor exam and the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam. If you pass one of them, you can use the result in place of the matching West Virginia trade exam. You still take the state's Contractor Licensing Act exam (the business and law test) and meet the Board's registration, insurance and wage bond rules.

Does West Virginia accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes, for two exams. NASCLA lists the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor, as a participating agency in its commercial exam program since September 1, 2013. The Board will not take NASCLA scores or transcripts dated before that day. NASCLA also lists the Board in its electrical program, accepting the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam as of December 2020. West Virginia is not listed for the NASCLA Journeyman or Residential electrical exams.

In practice, the NASCLA commercial exam stands in for the state's General Building Contractor trade exam, and the NASCLA electrical contractor exam stands in for the state's Electrical Contractor trade exam. Ask the Board to confirm that the NASCLA result matches the classification you are applying for before you schedule.

Acceptance covers the trade exam only. Every West Virginia contractor applicant also passes the Contractor Licensing Act exam, registers the business, carries the required coverage, and files the Board's application and wage bond affidavit.

Who issues contractor licenses in West Virginia

The West Virginia Contractor Licensing Board issues contractor licenses under the Contractor Licensing Act, Chapter 30, Article 42 of the state code. NASCLA lists the Board's office at 1900 Kanawha Boulevard East, State Capitol Complex, Building 3, Room 200, in Charleston. The current Prov candidate bulletin points applicants to the Board at wvclboard.wv.gov and 304-356-3928 for application questions.

State law defines a contractor as someone paid to build, alter, repair or demolish a structure, road or similar project when the job costs at least $5,000 for residential work or $25,000 for commercial work, as West Virginia Code 30-42-3 reads when we checked. Construction managers paid a professional fee are covered too. Several groups are exempt, including owners working on their own property.

Individual electrician licenses (master, journeyman and specialty electricians) are a separate credential issued by the Office of the State Fire Marshal through its Regulatory and Licensing Division. The NASCLA exam applies to the contractor license from the Board, not to the Fire Marshal's personal electrician license.

West Virginia contractor license types and which exam each needs

The Board's rule lists 17 exam classifications: concrete, electrical, excavation, general building, general engineering, heating/ventilating/cooling, manufactured home installation, masonry, multifamily, piping, plumbing, remodeling and repair, residential, sprinkler and fire protection, structural steel erection, and utilities. The Board can add specialties. Here is how the two NASCLA-eligible classifications work.

  • General Building Contractor: for structures that shelter people, animals or property, where the work needs more than two classifications or the contractor supervises the construction. It excludes electrical, plumbing, HVAC, piping and general engineering work. The trade exam is the state General Building exam or the NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam, plus the Contractor Licensing Act exam.
  • Electrical Contractor: for installing, repairing or altering electrical equipment. The trade exam is the state Electrical Contractor exam or the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam, plus the Contractor Licensing Act exam.
  • Remodeling and repair: for general remodeling, additions and repairs using two or more classifications on jobs that do not exceed $40,000, under the Board's definitions. This one uses state exams only.
  • Residential, multifamily and specialty classifications: state exams through Prov, plus the Contractor Licensing Act exam.

How to get a West Virginia contractor license, step by step

The Board's own checklist asks you to finish the first several steps before you send the application. Here is the order that fits a NASCLA candidate.

  1. Confirm your classification. Email the Board your scope of work and ask which classifications and exams apply.
  2. Pick your qualifier. The person who tests must be an officer, member or owner of the company, or a full-time employee, and the Board may ask for payroll records to prove employment.
  3. Apply for the NASCLA exam at ned.nascla.org, pay NASCLA's $65 fee, then schedule at PSI once you are approved.
  4. Schedule the Contractor Licensing Act exam with Prov, the Board's current testing vendor for state exams.
  5. Pass both exams, then buy a NASCLA transcript ($45 per agency) so the Board receives your result electronically.
  6. Get a West Virginia Business Registration tax number from the State Tax Department and register with the Secretary of State if your business type requires it.
  7. Set up coverage: workers' compensation, unemployment compensation through WorkForce West Virginia, and general liability insurance.
  8. Complete the wage bond status affidavit, then file the Board's application and affidavit signed by an owner, officer or managing member, and pay the license fee.

The West Virginia NASCLA exams: format and references

Both NASCLA exams are given by PSI, and you can test at any PSI site that offers them, even outside West Virginia. The NASCLA Commercial General Building Contractor exam has 115 scored questions and a 330 minute time limit, and you need 70 percent to pass (81 correct answers out of the 115 scored questions). It also carries 10 unscored pretest questions, with their time added. It is open book, and PSI's bulletin lists the 2021 or 2024 International Building Code among the references along with project management, concrete, steel, masonry, roofing, excavation, accessibility and OSHA books. PSI lists the exam fee at $130.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam has 100 scored questions plus 10 unscored items, 270 minutes, and a published minimum passing score of 75. Its references are the 2020 or 2023 NEC, OSHA 29 CFR 1926 and 1910, the PMBOK Guide (7th edition), ASHRAE 90.1-2022, the NASCLA Contractors Guide (Basic, 14th edition), NFPA 70E 2024, Ugly's Electrical References 2023 and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications. General code requirements and wiring and protection carry the most questions (17 each).

On both exams you may highlight, underline and add permanent tabs. Handwritten notes, loose papers and sticky tabs are not allowed. Your NASCLA approval lasts one year, with up to three attempts in that year.

The West Virginia Contractor Licensing Act exam (business and law)

Every West Virginia contractor applicant takes this exam on top of any trade exam, including NASCLA candidates. It used to be called the Business and Law exam. Prov's August 2026 candidate bulletin lists 90 questions, 3 hours and a 70 percent cut score. It is open book, and the required reference is the West Virginia Contractor Licensing Act Study Guide sold by Prov's bookstore, which the bulletin says covers every question. If you already own the West Virginia Business and Law Study Guide, 6th edition, the bulletin says you may use it.

NASCLA published a West Virginia edition of its Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management (1st edition, 2022), but Prov allowed it only as an alternate reference through March 31, 2025. Do not plan on it for test day. The broader business topics covered on this exam include contracts, labor law, tax law, licensing, lien law, financial management, project management and safety.

Prov lists the state exam fee at $59.95 per attempt, with no waiting period to retake a failed exam. You can test at Prov centers in Charleston, Martinsburg, Morgantown and Wheeling, at other Prov sites, or by remote proctoring. Cancel or reschedule at least three business days ahead to keep your fee.

West Virginia contractor license requirements: insurance, bond and registration

West Virginia does not set a years-of-experience rule for its contractor license in the sources we reviewed. Instead it relies on the exams and on proof that your business is set up correctly. Before the Board issues a license, state law requires you to swear by affidavit that you comply with the business franchise tax rules, that you are registered with the workers' compensation and employment security programs, and that you meet the state's wage bond rules.

The Board's checklist asks for a Business Registration tax number, Secretary of State registration for most entities, workers' compensation, unemployment compensation and general liability insurance. If your company is exempt from West Virginia coverage because it is based elsewhere, you submit proof of coverage from your home state. If you do commercial construction with employees, check your wage bond status with the Division of Labor's Wage and Hour Section, which the affidavit asks about.

Once licensed, you must post the license at job sites and show your license number on ads and contracts. Any job of $10,000 or more requires a signed written contract describing the work and cost. Building permit offices must see a valid license, or an affidavit that one is not required, before issuing a permit.

West Virginia contractor license costs

State law caps the contractor license fee at $150, and the Board sets the actual figure by rule. The Board's application pages did not show a dollar amount when we checked, so confirm the current license fee with the Board before you pay. Here are the exam-related figures we could confirm.

  • NASCLA exam application: $65, paid at ned.nascla.org.
  • NASCLA commercial exam fee at PSI: $130, valid one year from payment.
  • NASCLA transcript to the Board: $45.
  • Contractor Licensing Act exam at Prov: $59.95 per attempt.
  • Contractor license: up to $150 under state law, renewed yearly.

Out-of-state contractors and reciprocity

The NASCLA exam is the main tool for out-of-state contractors. If you passed the NASCLA commercial or electrical contractor exam for another state, buy a transcript for West Virginia instead of taking the state trade exam, as long as your exam date is on or after September 1, 2013 for the commercial exam. NASCLA says passing results do not expire.

We did not find a general reciprocity agreement in the Board's published materials. State law does let an out-of-state contractor that is not doing business in West Virginia get a conditional license for bidding purposes only. Ask the Board how that works for your project before you bid.

After you pass: your West Virginia license and renewal

Once both exams are passed and your transcript is sent, file the Board's application with your registration, coverage and wage bond paperwork. The Board will not process an incomplete file, so gather everything first. A license is tied to one business entity at a time, though the Board can transfer it to a new entity you own or lead without a new exam.

Under state law a West Virginia contractor license expires one year from the day it is issued, and the Board sends renewal notices at least 30 days ahead. The statute lets the Board set continued-competency rules for renewal; ask the Board whether any apply to your classification. If you also need a personal electrician license, contact the State Fire Marshal separately.

West Virginia NASCLA exam and license FAQ

Does West Virginia accept the NASCLA commercial exam?

Yes. NASCLA lists the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board as accepting the Commercial General Building Contractor exam since September 1, 2013. Scores or transcripts dated before then are not accepted.

Does West Virginia accept the NASCLA electrical exam?

Yes, the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam, as of December 2020. West Virginia is not listed for the NASCLA Journeyman or Residential electrical exams.

Do I still take the West Virginia business and law exam if I pass NASCLA?

Yes. Every contractor applicant passes the Contractor Licensing Act exam, the renamed Business and Law exam, in addition to any trade exam. Prov lists it at 90 questions in 3 hours with a 70 percent cut score.

Who gives the West Virginia contractor exams?

Prov administers the state exams, including the Contractor Licensing Act exam, at $59.95 per attempt. NASCLA exams are given by PSI after NASCLA approves your application.

When do I need a West Virginia contractor license?

State law covers paid construction work costing $5,000 or more on residential jobs or $25,000 or more on commercial jobs, with some exemptions. Ask the Board if you are unsure whether your work qualifies.

How much is a West Virginia contractor license?

State law caps the license fee at $150 a year, and the Board sets the exact amount. Add NASCLA's $65 application, the PSI exam fee, the $45 transcript and Prov's $59.95 state exam fee.

How long is a West Virginia contractor license good for?

One year from the date it is issued. The Board sends a renewal notice at least 30 days before it expires.

Is the West Virginia electrical contractor license the same as a master electrician license?

No. The Contractor Licensing Board issues the contractor license, while the State Fire Marshal licenses individual electricians. The NASCLA exam applies to the contractor license.

Can I use highlighted and tabbed books on the NASCLA exam?

Yes. PSI allows highlighting, underlining and permanent tabs, but no handwritten notes, loose papers or removable sticky tabs.
