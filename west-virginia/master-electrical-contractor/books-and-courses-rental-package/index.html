Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program offers an open-book electrical trade examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians and is accepted by participating state and local licensing agencies.

West Virginia's Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor participates in the NASCLA electrical examination program. Candidates pursuing the NASCLA route apply through the NASCLA National Examination Database and, after approval, receive scheduling instructions for the examination through PSI.

The NASCLA electrical examination is designed around knowledge used by experienced electrical professionals. The examination contains 100 scored items and may include 10 additional pretest items. Candidates should be prepared for questions that require application of electrical principles, Code requirements, calculations, safety practices, and professional electrical knowledge.

Preparation should include electrical theory, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, equipment, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, special electrical systems, test equipment, and energy-related requirements. Candidates should also practice moving between multiple references rather than relying only on the National Electrical Code.

West Virginia contractor licensing includes a separate Business and Law requirement. Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the West Virginia contractor classification you need. Contractor licensing is administered through the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board within the Division of Labor.
  2. Review the applicable licensing requirements. Identify the contractor classification, business documentation, examinations, and additional requirements associated with your intended scope of work.
  3. Apply for the NASCLA electrical examination when using the NASCLA pathway. Candidates apply through the NASCLA National Examination Database.
  4. Prepare for the electrical trade examination. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental references together with the included course access to study electrical theory, Code application, calculations, safety, project management, and reference navigation.
  5. Take and pass the applicable electrical examination. Complete the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination according to current NASCLA and PSI testing procedures.
  6. Complete the West Virginia Business and Law examination. West Virginia contractors must satisfy the state Business and Law examination requirement in addition to the applicable trade examination.
  7. Submit the contractor licensing application. Complete the required West Virginia Division of Labor application and provide the required business and qualification documentation.
  8. Complete additional business requirements. Meet the registration, insurance, tax, workers' compensation, or other requirements that apply to the contractor business.
  9. Receive licensing approval. Passing the examinations does not itself issue the contractor license; the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board must approve the application.

State Requirements

West Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Contractors Licensing Board within the West Virginia Division of Labor. The Board regulates contractor licensing and establishes the examination and application requirements associated with contractor classifications.

West Virginia participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. NASCLA examination results are not themselves a contractor license. After satisfying the examination requirement, applicants must still complete the West Virginia licensing process.

The West Virginia Division of Labor requires contractors to pass the Business and Law examination in addition to applicable trade examinations. The Business and Law test covers business-management and contractor-law subjects such as business organization, contracts, estimating and bidding, financial management, labor law, licensing, lien law, project management, risk management, safety, and tax laws.

West Virginia's contractor Business and Law examination is separate from the NASCLA electrical trade examination. Candidates preparing for both components should divide their study plan between technical electrical preparation and West Virginia-specific contractor law and business requirements.

Individual electrician licensing is also separate from contractor licensing. West Virginia individual Master Electrician and other electrician credentials are administered through the West Virginia Office of the State Fire Marshal rather than the Contractors Licensing Board. Candidates should distinguish between the individual electrician credential and the contractor business license required for the work they intend to perform.

The NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted trade-examination component, but applicants remain responsible for completing the West Virginia application, Business and Law examination, and all other licensing requirements associated with their contractor classification.
