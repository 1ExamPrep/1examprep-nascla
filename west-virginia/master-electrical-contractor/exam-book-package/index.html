State Requirements

West Virginia regulates electrical contractor businesses and individual electricians through separate agencies. The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor, administers contractor licensing, including electrical contractor classifications. Individual electrician licenses are administered statewide by the West Virginia State Fire Marshal's Office, Regulatory and Licensing Division.

The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. West Virginia accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for the applicable contractor licensing examination pathway.

Passing the NASCLA examination is not the same as receiving a West Virginia contractor license. NASCLA describes its examination program as a standardized trade-examination system rather than a national license. Candidates must still complete West Virginia's contractor application process and satisfy the additional requirements established by the state.

Individual master electrician licensing follows a separate process. West Virginia's current electrician licensing rules require a master electrician applicant to have qualifying journeyman-level electrical experience. The rules establish a standard of two years of experience as a journeyman electrician involving electrical work of sufficient breadth, independence, and quality to demonstrate competency to perform electrical work and direct other electricians.

The State Fire Marshal's Office also announced that, beginning January 1, 2026, West Virginia state electrical examinations are based on the 2023 edition of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code. The state Master, Journeyman, and Single Family Dwelling electrical examinations use a 50-question multiple-choice format under the 2026 update. These State Fire Marshal examinations are separate from the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination used through the contractor licensing program.

Candidates should therefore distinguish between the NASCLA contractor examination pathway and the individual West Virginia master electrician examination pathway. Depending on the work to be performed and the business structure involved, an electrical professional may need to satisfy requirements under both regulatory programs.

  • NFPA 70E - Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace, 2024
    An electrical workplace-safety standard addressing practices designed to reduce exposure to electrical hazards while performing work on or near electrical systems and equipment.
  • Ugly's Electrical References
    A compact electrical field reference containing commonly used formulas, calculations, conversions, tables, and technical information for electrical work and exam preparation.
  • Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications
    A study reference connecting electrical theory and calculation principles with practical applications of National Electrical Code requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A complete reference package is most useful when the books become active study tools rather than materials reserved for exam day. Candidates should work with the references throughout their preparation so the location and organization of important information become familiar.

Start with the National Electrical Code. Review services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and commonly encountered equipment. Practice using the NEC index and following cross-references when more than one section affects the answer.

Electrical theory should be studied through repeated calculations. Use Delmar's Standard Textbook of Electricity, Ugly's Electrical References, and Understanding Electrical Theory for NEC Applications to reinforce fundamental relationships and practical problem-solving methods. Build a consistent process for identifying known values, choosing a formula, calculating accurately, and checking whether a Code provision affects the final result.

Safety preparation should include both OSHA construction requirements and NFPA 70E. These references address different aspects of safety, so candidates should learn how to recognize which source applies to a particular question. Becoming familiar with the structure of both books can reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Project-management study should include planning, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, and contractor responsibilities. The NASCLA Contractors Guide and the project-management reference can help candidates become more familiar with the professional management concepts that supplement technical electrical knowledge.

The ASHRAE/IES energy standard adds another specialized reference to the collection. Candidates should become comfortable with its layout and terminology so energy-efficiency questions can be approached without searching blindly through an unfamiliar standard.

Timed lookup practice can tie these materials together. Choose a practice question, determine which book applies, locate the relevant information, and measure how long the search takes. When a topic repeatedly requires too much time, return to that reference and learn its organization more thoroughly.
