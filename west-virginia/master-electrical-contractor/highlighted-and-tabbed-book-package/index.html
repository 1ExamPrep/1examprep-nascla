Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. NASCLA developed the examination using occupational analysis and input from electrical subject-matter experts to represent the work performed by experienced electrical professionals.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The examination content includes Theory, Personal and Jobsite Safety, Project Design, Estimating and Management, Grounding and Bonding, Special Systems and Special Equipment Installations, Tools and Test Equipment, Circuit Protection and Installation, Wiring Methods and Practices, Equipment for General Use, and Energy Efficiency or Management.

The exam requires candidates to combine trade knowledge with reference research. Some questions may be answered through electrical principles and practical experience, while others require calculations or precise information from the National Electrical Code, OSHA regulations, NFPA 70E, or another approved reference.

The 270-minute testing period also makes time management important. Candidates should practice recognizing what a question is asking before opening a reference. Identifying the subject first can help determine which book to use and reduce unnecessary searching.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is an open-book examination. Candidates are permitted to use approved reference materials in accordance with NASCLA examination rules.

NASCLA permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and permanently indexed. The reference material must otherwise remain unmarked and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Permanent indexing tabs are permitted, while temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

State Requirements

West Virginia uses separate systems for contractor licensing and individual electrician licensing. The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor, regulates contractor businesses and contractor classifications. The West Virginia State Fire Marshal's Office regulates individual electricians throughout the state.

The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. NASCLA examination acceptance allows the examination to serve as an accepted trade-examination component within the participating contractor licensing process.

The NASCLA examination itself is not a contractor license or national electrical license. Applicants must complete the West Virginia contractor licensing process and satisfy the requirements associated with the contractor classification they intend to obtain.

Individual Master Electrician licensing follows a different regulatory pathway. West Virginia's electrician licensing rules define a Master Electrician as an individual with at least two years of qualifying electrical work experience who holds a Master Electrician license issued by the West Virginia State Fire Marshal and is competent to instruct and supervise Journeyman and Assistant or Helper Electricians.

The State Fire Marshal's examination application describes the Master Electrician experience requirement as at least two years, or 4,000 hours, of qualifying hands-on electrical work experience. The qualifying experience must meet the requirements established by the State Fire Marshal's licensing program.

Beginning January 1, 2026, West Virginia State Fire Marshal electrical examinations are based on the 2023 edition of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code. The Master, Journeyman, and Single Family Dwelling state examinations now use a 50-question multiple-choice format, with calculation questions incorporated into the multiple-choice examination.

Those State Fire Marshal examinations are separate from the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination addressed by this package. Electrical professionals may encounter requirements from both regulatory systems depending on the individual license and contractor business activities they intend to pursue.

Test Information and Study Materials

A highlighted and tabbed reference package is most effective when candidates use the books throughout their preparation. The purpose of the organization is to support quicker navigation, but repeated study is what develops familiarity with where information is located and how different references work together.

Begin with the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice locating requirements for branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and equipment. Pay attention to definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-references.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Use the theory references to strengthen your understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, and other fundamental concepts. For calculations, identify the information provided, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, and then determine whether an NEC requirement affects the answer.

Safety study should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Learn which reference applies to the type of safety question presented. This helps prevent spending time searching an unrelated reference during an open-book examination.

Project-management material should remain part of the study schedule. Electrical contractors may be tested on subjects involving project planning, estimating, coordination, communication, risk, documentation, and management responsibilities in addition to technical electrical installation.

Use timed reference exercises as the exam approaches. Select questions from different subject areas and practice moving directly to the relevant tabbed section, finding the highlighted material that supports the subject, and confirming the answer from the reference. This combines technical learning with practical navigation practice.
