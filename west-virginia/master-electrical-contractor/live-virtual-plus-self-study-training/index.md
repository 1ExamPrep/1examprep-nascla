## Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program consists of open-book electrical trade examinations, including the Electrical Contractor examination. Approved references may be used during testing according to the applicable NASCLA and testing-provider rules.

Open-book does not mean that preparation can be limited to looking up every answer. Electrical references are extensive, and searching unfamiliar books can consume valuable time. Candidates should know the basic concepts well enough to identify the likely reference and narrow the search before opening a book.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should be a central part of preparation. Candidates should practice using the index, definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Study should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, electrical equipment, and other areas associated with professional electrical work.

Safety preparation should incorporate both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. These resources serve different purposes. OSHA Part 1926 addresses federal construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Learning the organization of each reference can make it easier to determine where a safety-related answer is most likely to be found.

Electrical theory should be studied with active problem solving. Candidates can strengthen preparation by working through voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, motors, transformers, and related calculations rather than simply reading formulas. The goal is to understand the steps required to reach an answer and recognize when an NEC provision must also be applied.

Live virtual instruction can help demonstrate how these subjects connect, while self-study provides additional time for repetition. Candidates can review the material covered in class, repeat calculations, revisit difficult Code sections, and practice locating information until the reference process becomes more familiar.

## State Requirements

West Virginia's contractor licensing program is administered through the Contractors Licensing Board within the Division of Labor. NASCLA lists the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor as a participating agency that accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

The contractor license and an individual West Virginia electrician license are separate credentials administered by different agencies. Individual electrician licensing is handled by the West Virginia Office of the State Fire Marshal, Regulatory and Licensing Division. The State Fire Marshal administers licenses for Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and applicable specialty electricians.

West Virginia's current State Fire Marshal rules require a person seeking a Master Electrician license through the state electrician-licensing process to have qualifying electrical experience before examination. The state's individual electrician examination is distinct from the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination used in the contractor-licensing context.

Beginning January 1, 2026, West Virginia's State Fire Marshal electrical examinations are based on the 2023 National Electrical Code. The State Fire Marshal also changed its Master, Journeyman, and Single Family Dwelling examination format to 50 multiple-choice questions. Those state electrician examinations should not be confused with the separate NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors.

This distinction is important for candidates planning their licensing path. A person may encounter contractor-licensing requirements through the West Virginia Division of Labor as well as individual electrician-licensing requirements through the State Fire Marshal. Applicants should complete every requirement associated with the specific credential or business license they intend to obtain.

Passing the NASCLA electrical examination can satisfy an accepted trade-examination component for participating agencies, but candidates still need to meet the applicable West Virginia application and licensing requirements before performing work under the requested license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation strategy should combine instructor-led explanation with repeated independent practice. Live training can help candidates work through difficult subjects in a structured sequence, while self-study gives them the opportunity to reinforce those concepts between classes.

Begin by building familiarity with the National Electrical Code. Do not limit study to reading individual articles. Practice using the index, following cross-references, locating tables, interpreting definitions, and recognizing which chapter or article is likely to address a particular electrical problem.

Calculations should become another regular part of preparation. Work through electrical theory problems in a consistent sequence: identify the known values, determine what must be solved, select the appropriate formula, complete the calculation, and then determine whether a Code rule affects the final answer.

Safety study should include both construction safety and electrical workplace safety. Candidates should become comfortable distinguishing between OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E so they know which reference is appropriate when working through a particular question.

Project-management and contractor-focused material should not be overlooked. Electrical contractors operate within projects that require planning, coordination, communication, documentation, risk management, and professional organization. These topics complement technical electrical knowledge and should remain part of the study plan.

Live virtual instruction can provide structure for these study areas, while self-study preparation allows candidates to repeat difficult topics. A candidate who needs more work on calculations can dedicate additional independent time to theory problems, while another candidate may spend more time practicing NEC navigation, safety lookups, or project-management material.

The most useful open-book preparation involves answering realistic questions while using the actual references. Instead of simply reading a chapter, select a question, identify the subject, determine the appropriate reference, locate the relevant information, and confirm how it applies. This creates a practical connection between subject knowledge and reference navigation.
