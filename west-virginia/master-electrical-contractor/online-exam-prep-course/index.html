Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. It is intended for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians whose work involves electrical installation, maintenance, repair, and related responsibilities governed by electrical codes and industry standards.

The examination content is based on an occupational analysis developed with electrical industry subject matter experts. Candidates should expect the exam to evaluate both technical knowledge and the practical application of electrical standards rather than simple memorization of Code article numbers.

Major examination areas include:

  • Project Design and Management: Planning, coordination, professional responsibilities, and project-related electrical work.
  • Safety: Construction safety and electrical workplace safety requirements.
  • Electrical Theory: Electrical principles, relationships, calculations, and practical applications.
  • General Code Requirements: Broad National Electrical Code requirements applying to electrical installations.
  • Wiring and Protection: Branch circuits, feeders, services, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and related requirements.
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: Conductors, cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, installation methods, and permitted applications.
  • Equipment for General Use: Motors, transformers, appliances, luminaires, equipment connections, and other commonly installed electrical equipment.
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: NEC requirements applying to specialized locations, systems, and installations.
  • Communication Systems: Requirements associated with applicable communications wiring and systems.

A strong exam preparation plan should address every content area while giving additional attention to topics that require frequent Code navigation or multi-step calculations. Candidates should be comfortable determining whether a problem calls for direct trade knowledge, a mathematical calculation, an NEC lookup, or information from another reference.

State Requirements

West Virginia uses separate regulatory systems for contractor businesses and individual electricians. A business performing construction work that meets the state's contractor licensing threshold must obtain the appropriate contractor license through the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor. Electrical contractor is one of the classifications regulated through that program.

The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. This allows an applicant to use an accepted NASCLA examination result in place of the applicable state trade examination requirement for the participating contractor licensing pathway.

NASCLA examination acceptance should not be confused with individual master electrician licensure. The West Virginia State Fire Marshal's Office, Regulatory and Licensing Division, administers electrician licenses statewide. Its programs include master electrician, journeyman electrician, and specialty electrician licensing.

West Virginia's electrician licensing rules establish experience requirements for individuals seeking a master electrician license. The current rules require a master electrician applicant to have two years of experience as a journeyman electrician in electrical work demonstrating the breadth, independence, and quality necessary to perform electrical work and direct other electricians.

The State Fire Marshal's current electrical examination application describes the master electrician experience standard as at least two years, or 4,000 hours, of qualifying hands-on electrical work experience. Individual applicants pursuing the West Virginia master electrician credential must follow the State Fire Marshal's licensing and examination requirements.

As of January 1, 2026, West Virginia's state-administered electrical examinations through the State Fire Marshal are based on the 2023 edition of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code. This state examination update is separate from the NASCLA contractor examination program but is important for applicants who also need an individual West Virginia electrician license.

Passing a NASCLA examination does not issue a West Virginia contractor license, master electrician license, or other professional credential. Applicants must complete the relevant state applications and satisfy the licensing requirements associated with the work they intend to perform.

Test Information and Study Materials

Successful preparation should combine technical review with active problem solving. Electrical contractor examinations require candidates to interpret information, select appropriate Code rules, perform calculations, and apply standards to realistic situations. Reading alone may not develop the speed and decision-making needed for an open-book professional exam.

Start with fundamental NEC subjects such as branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, cables, motors, transformers, and equipment requirements. Work through questions that require locating and applying specific Code provisions rather than simply memorizing article numbers.

Electrical theory should be practiced through calculations. Build a consistent process for identifying known values, determining what must be solved, selecting the correct formula, performing the calculation, and confirming whether a Code requirement affects the final answer. Understanding the theory behind the calculation helps when a question is presented in an unfamiliar way.

Safety preparation should incorporate both construction safety and electrical workplace safety. OSHA construction regulations and NFPA 70E address different aspects of workplace protection, so candidates should learn which reference is appropriate for the type of safety question being asked.

Project management and energy standards should also remain part of the study plan. Contractors are responsible for more than installation work. Planning, coordination, documentation, risk, schedule considerations, and energy-related requirements can all contribute to professional electrical contracting responsibilities.

As your preparation progresses, use timed practice sessions. Keep the reference materials nearby and measure how quickly you can identify the subject, select the correct book, and locate supporting information. If you consistently spend too much time searching for a particular topic, return to that reference and study its organization more closely.

Does West Virginia accept the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician exam?

Yes. The West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor, participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

Is the NASCLA exam the same as a West Virginia master electrician license?

No. The NASCLA examination is a trade examination accepted by participating licensing agencies. West Virginia individual electrician licenses are administered separately through the State Fire Marshal's Office.

Who licenses electrical contractors in West Virginia?

Contractor businesses are licensed through the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor. Electrical contractor is one of the contractor classifications regulated by that agency.

Who licenses individual master electricians in West Virginia?

The West Virginia State Fire Marshal's Office, Regulatory and Licensing Division, administers individual electrician licensing, including master electrician licenses.

What experience is required for a West Virginia master electrician license?

West Virginia's electrician licensing rules require a master electrician applicant to have two years of qualifying journeyman-level electrical experience demonstrating the ability to perform and supervise electrical work. Does passing the NASCLA exam automatically give me a West Virginia contractor license?

Can NASCLA examination results be used in other participating states?
