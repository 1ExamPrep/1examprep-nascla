Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program includes a standardized trade examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. West Virginia is a participating jurisdiction that accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

The NASCLA electrical examination program is designed to evaluate knowledge expected of experienced electrical professionals across participating jurisdictions. Major knowledge areas include electrical theory, project design and management, safety, National Electrical Code requirements, wiring and protection, wiring methods and materials, general-use equipment, special occupancies and equipment, and communication systems.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination is administered through the NASCLA Accredited Examination Program. Candidates first complete the applicable NASCLA examination application process and then schedule the examination through the approved testing provider after receiving authorization.

Passing the NASCLA examination can satisfy the electrical trade examination component for participating agencies such as the West Virginia Contractors Licensing Board. Candidates must still complete West Virginia's own contractor licensing requirements before a license is issued.

West Virginia also requires contractors to pass the state's Business and Law examination in addition to an applicable trade examination. The current West Virginia Business and Law examination contains 50 questions with 3 hours allowed for completion. The examination covers business management and legal topics associated with running a contracting business, including business organization, contracts, estimating and bidding, financial management, labor law, licensing, lien law, project management, risk management, safety, and tax law.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the applicable West Virginia contractor classification. Confirm that the electrical contractor classification matches the work your business intends to perform.
  2. Prepare for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Study electrical theory, safety, NEC requirements, wiring, project-management material, special systems, and other tested areas using the included course and references.

State Requirements

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive NASCLA Master Electrical Contractor study plan should combine electrical knowledge with regular reference-navigation practice. The examination spans multiple subject areas, so candidates should avoid focusing entirely on the NEC while neglecting theory, safety, project management, or specialized electrical subjects.

Begin by becoming familiar with the layout of the 2023 National Electrical Code. Practice locating articles through the index, moving between referenced sections, using tables, and recognizing where major requirements are organized. Open-book testing rewards candidates who understand the structure of the reference instead of searching randomly for individual words.

Electrical theory should be studied alongside code work. Use the electrical theory references to strengthen understanding of voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, calculations, conductors, loads, and practical applications. Knowing the principles behind a calculation can make it easier to recognize which formula and code requirements apply.

Safety preparation should include both construction safety and electrical workplace safety. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and NFPA 70E provide different but complementary perspectives on workplace hazards, electrical safety practices, and contractor responsibilities.

Project design, estimating, management, and business knowledge should also be incorporated into the study schedule. This is especially important because West Virginia requires a separate Business and Law examination. Candidates pursuing the full contractor license need to understand both the technical side of electrical contracting and the responsibilities involved in operating a contracting business.
