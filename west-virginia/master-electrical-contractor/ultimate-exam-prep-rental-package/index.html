Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. It is designed for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians and is recognized by participating state and local licensing agencies.

West Virginia's Contractors Licensing Board, Division of Labor participates in the NASCLA electrical program. Candidates who choose the NASCLA pathway apply through the NASCLA National Examination Database. After an application is approved, scheduling information is provided for the examination through PSI.

The electrical examination covers professional trade knowledge across several areas rather than focusing exclusively on the National Electrical Code. Candidates should prepare for electrical theory, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, circuit protection, electrical equipment, safety, project design and management, special installations, and other technical topics associated with electrical contracting.

The broad reference list means candidates should practice using several books. An NEC question may require Code navigation, while a safety question may involve OSHA Part 1926 or NFPA 70E. Theory and calculation questions may require electrical textbook knowledge, while project-management subjects can lead to the project-management and contractor-business references.

West Virginia also requires contractors to complete the applicable Business and Law examination in addition to a trade examination when required. Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use approved reference materials during the examination according to current NASCLA and testing-provider rules.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for technical preparation. Candidates must still understand electrical concepts, determine what a question is asking, identify the correct reference, and locate the relevant information within the available testing period.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive significant navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable locating definitions, branch-circuit requirements, feeders, services, conductor sizing information, ampacity provisions, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, transformers, special occupancies, and specialized electrical systems.

Electrical theory should be reinforced through calculations. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, motors, transformers, circuit relationships, and related concepts. When working through a problem, identify the values provided, determine what must be calculated, choose the appropriate formula, and then determine whether an NEC provision affects the answer.

Safety study should include both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA addresses construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E focuses on electrical safety in the workplace. Becoming familiar with the organization and purpose of each reference can make open-book research more efficient.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books are designed to support this preparation style. Candidates can practice navigating the tabs during course exercises, reference drills, and practice questions instead of waiting until examination day to learn where information is located.

State Requirements

West Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Contractors Licensing Board within the West Virginia Division of Labor. The Board regulates contractor classifications and establishes the examination and application requirements contractors must satisfy before performing regulated work.

West Virginia is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. NASCLA examination results can satisfy an accepted trade-examination component, but NASCLA itself does not issue a West Virginia contractor license.

The West Virginia contractor licensing process also requires the applicable Business and Law examination. This requirement is separate from the technical NASCLA electrical examination and focuses on business management and legal responsibilities associated with contracting.

Business and Law subjects include contractor licensing concepts, business organization, contracts, estimating and bidding, financial management, project management, labor requirements, safety, risk management, taxation, and other responsibilities involved in operating a contracting business.

Individual electrician licensing is separate from contractor business licensing in West Virginia. Individual electrical credentials are administered through the West Virginia Office of the State Fire Marshal, while contractor licenses are administered through the Contractors Licensing Board. Candidates should determine which individual and business credentials apply to the work they intend to perform.

The NASCLA pathway can reduce duplication of trade testing in participating jurisdictions, but it does not replace West Virginia's application process or other state requirements. Applicants remain responsible for completing the licensing steps associated with their contractor classification.
